Defense of the Pentatuech as a Work of Moses

Mention the abundant evidence that the history was written by Moses, and refer to my treatise on this subject.1

The continuance of the KENITES to the captivity, a monument. See 1 Chronicles 2:55.

KENITES. The posterity of Jethro, which remained in a known, visible distinction among the children of Israel, even to the captivity, was one among many other standing monuments of the truth of the Mosaic history. See. 1 Chronicles 2:55.

He that adds to the Law is severely threatened.

The keeping in a known and visible distinction the Kenites, of which manner the Rechabites, even till the captivity, is one among many evidences of their keeping up their GENEALOGIES.

There were many devout persons that used to write out the Law, and therefore are called SCRIBES, as the Kenites, 1 Chronicles 2:55. There are other passages in Scripture that show that these Kenites gave themselves very much up to a contemplative life.

There was an order of men among them before the captivity, who made it their business to write the Law, as appears by Jeremiah 8:8, "How do ye say, We are wise, the law of the Lord is with us? Lo, certainly in vain made he it; the pen of the SCRIBES is in vain."

Many of the Levites made that their business; see 2 Chronicles 34:13 parenthetical

Such was the nature of the Law, so many and so punctilious the services and ceremonies to be observed by ALL THE PEOPLE, that it naturally lead, and as it were obliged and compelled, the whole people to look into the Law, to study it and take care to get a good acquaintance with it.

LAW PUBLICLY KNOWN. The book of the Law was commonly known among the people through their generations. It appears to have been a custom to give the king a copy of it, when he was crowned and enthroned, 2 Kings 11:12.

If any would palm this book containing the laws and history of facts, they must make the people believe that they always had those laws and this account of facts among them, and all those things in their state dependent on the laws and facts from the beginning, though the senses of the whole nation would contradict it, and all would know that it was now new, and that they never had heard of it before. Because the book in many places plainly supposes it would always remain, and be in being from thenceforth: a book pretended to be laid up in a most public repository, in the most public building they had in the nation, to which all the nation were to resort three times in the year, Deuteronomy 31:24-26; commanded to be read before the whole nation once in seven years, their sabbatical year, Deuteronomy 31:10.

Moses was commanded to write a memorial of the oppression of Amalek, that it might remain forever, to put the children in mind of it through all generations, Exodus 14:17 to the end.

Moses delivered the book in a public manner to be laid up in the side of the ark, that it might be there to be a witness against 'em, Deuteronomy 31:26; which supposes it would always be known of.

The king was commanded to write out a copy of the Law parenthetical.2 The people were required to be continually talking of it, to wear it for a sign on their heads, to write it on the post of their houses, etc.

THE FACTS PUBLICLY KNOWN. Concerning those great, miraculous facts, it is written in the book of the Law, Deuteronomy 4:9-12, "Only take heed to thyself, and keep thy soul diligently, lest thou forget the things that thine eyes have seen, and lest they depart from thine heart all the days of thy life: but teach them thy sons, and thy sons' sons; specially the day when thou stoodst before the Lord thy God in Horeb,. .. and the mountain burnt with fire unto the midst of heaven, with darkness, clouds, and thick darkness. And the Lord spake unto you out of the midst of the fire." It would be natural to expect from hence that the history of these great miracles should all along be universally known among the people, and not that there should be any time when the account of 'em should be new in the nations, the people in general not having received the account from their forefathers.

If the history had been forged, it must be introduced to the people's belief and reception with very great SUBTILTY, and those subtil forgers of the history would not be likely to put into it such things as would be likely to lead people to suspect the truth, such as those which have been already mentioned, which naturally awaken people's admiration and inquiry why they never heard those things before, and such precepts as those, where they were commanded to lay up a part of their manna to be kept for their generations, that their posterity might see what bread their forefathers lived upon in the wilderness; which naturally, whenever the history first came abroad, would excite such inquiries as these: Where is this pot of manna? what is become of it? how came it to be lost? And so of Aaron's rod that budded, in memory of the many great miracles done by that rod, and particularly the miraculous destruction of Korah and his company.

The book of the Law contained some prayers, wherein all the people, every head of a family, were required every year, in a very public manner, to rehearse some of the principal facts and expressly mention the great miracles,3 as the law of him that offered the firstfruits; and a public profession was to be made at the offering the tithes that included a reference to the same things. See concerning both, in Deuteronomy 26. And care was taken to erect monuments of miraculous events in Joshua's time, after Moses' death, as the monument at Gilgal, and in the midst of Jordan.

And there was a law, Deuteronomy 6:20 to the end, for every head of a family to declare the chief facts to their children.

The Law requires the priests and Levites to teach the people the Law on all occasions, which supposes 'em to be thoroughly acquainted with the book of the Law parenthetical.4 And the Law also supposes the elders of the land, and their judges and civil rulers in general, to be well acquainted with the book of the Law; and it supposes the people in general to be sufficiently taught in the Law, to enable them to be well acquainted with it, by its being said concerning all in general, the word is not "far off," out of sight and out of reach, but is "nigh thee, in thy mouth, and in thy heart, that thou mayst" hear it, and "do it." See that very remarkable place, Deuteronomy 30:11-14. Also, such places as these suppose that sufficient means are provided to make 'em known fully to all; Deuteronomy 6:6-8, and ch. Deuteronomy 11:18-20 and ch. Deuteronomy 4:9.

PASSOVER. Concerning the law of the Passover, to be kept in remembrance of the greatest plague in Egypt: it was appointed to be kept forever by the nation, every year from that time, Exodus 12:14, Exodus 24-26, ch. Exodus 13:10. Every man, woman and child were to be active in this memorial; every household was to kill the lamb, and was to strike the blood on the post and lintel of the door, and were to eat it with unleavened bread, and to eat unleavened bread seven days. And in the law by which it was commanded, it was commanded that their children should be instructed in the history of the facts that it was in remembrance of, Exodus 12:27, ch. Exodus 13:8-10.

And the fact, which is the ground of the Law, is much insisted upon and repeated in the Law, as what they should very carefully keep in remembrance, Exodus 12:2, Exodus 11-14, Exodus 17, Exodus 42-43, Exodus 47-49; ch. Exodus 13; Exodus 34:1-10, and Exodus 23, Exodus 25; Deuteronomy 16:1 , Deuteronomy 16:3, Deuteronomy 16:6.

And the injunction of this observance was so strict, that whosoever should neglect it was to be cut off from Israel; and this is urged and repeated with great strictness, Exodus 12:15, Exodus 12:19, and Numbers 9:13, Exodus 23:15 and Exodus 34:18.

And concerning the law of the first-born, in like manner the reason and reference to the fact is contained in the law itself, with a particular injunction to teach their children in all future generations this reason, rehearsing to 'em the principal circumstances of the facts as we find them in the history. See Exodus 13:1-2, Exodus 13:11-16, Numbers 8:17, Numbers 3:12-13. Here Exodus 13, in the 16th verse, is an injunction that those facts shall be continually kept in remembrance, as a token on their hand, and frontlets between their eyes, v. Exodus 13:9, and vv. Exodus 13:15-16; that there might always a continual, universal and perfect remembrance of this fact, and constant and great notice taken of it, and that it should never be out of mind.

MONUMENTS. One of the laws of Moses was to set up great stones as an altar in the land of Canaan, and engrave the words of the Law very plainly, Deuteronomy 27:1, Deuteronomy 27:8; which we have an account of the fulfillment of, in Joshua 8:30-32.

STANDING MIRACLES. The Law of Moses, which we have now, giving an account of the facts, not only appoints the ark, etc., as standing monuments, but speaks of and promises a standing miracle, which would be a confirmation of these facts that should remain in after-ages; such as God's appearing over the mercy seat, and miraculously giving answers from above the mercy seat in their tabernacle, Exodus 25:22-23, and ch. Exodus 29:42, ch. Exodus 30:36, Numbers 17:4, Leviticus 16:2, Leviticus 9:6.

ANOTHER STANDING MIRACLE promised, was God's manifesting his mind by Urim and Thummim, if that was different, as Exodus 28:30, Numbers 27:21.

And5 another standing miracle promised was the spirit of prophecy, or foretelling things to come, with that mark given by which to know false prophets, viz. if their prophecies did not come to pass, they were to be rejected; with that severe law annexed, that if any presumed to prophecy and God had not sent him, that man should die, Deuteronomy 18.

Another strange law with a promise of a great standing miracle annexed, was that all their males should go three times in the year to the sanctuary to worship, and that at those times their enemies should not desire their land, Exodus 34:23-24.

Now 'tis not at all likely that any cunning impostors that should forge the Pentateuch afterwards, would insert such things as these for its confutation, or to raise suspicions of their forgery.

Another STANDING MIRACLE was the causing the land to be so extraordinarily fruitful every sixth year, that it produced enough for three years, Leviticus 24:20-21.

Another standing miracle was the effect of the bitter water, Numbers 5.

LAW PUBLIC. It appears that DAVID was thoroughly acquainted with the word of God, as appears by the 19th Psalm, and also the 119th Psalm, throughout.

Psalms 18:22, "For all his judgments were before me, and I did not put away his statutes from me." Psalms 19:11.

Especially the following verses in the 119th Psalm, show David's intimate acquaintance with the Law: v. Psalms 119:11, "Thy word have I hid in my heart, that I might not sin against thee"; v. Psalms 119:13, "With my lips have I declared all the judgments of thy mouth"; v. Psalms 119:15, "I will meditate in thy precepts, and have respect to thy ways"; v. Psalms 119:23, "thy servant did meditate in thy statutes"; v. Psalms 119:24, "Thy testimonies are my delight and my counselors"; v. Psalms 119:30, "thy judgments have I laid before me"; v. Psalms 119:48, "I will meditate in thy statutes"; v. Psalms 119:78, "I will meditate in thy precepts"; v. Psalms 119:93, "I will never forget thy precepts"; v. Psalms 119:97, "O how I love thy law! it is my meditation all the day."

DAVID THOROUGHLY ACQUAINTED WITH THE LAW. Psalms 119:98, "Thou through thy commandments hast made me wiser than mine enemies: for they are ever with me"; v. Psalms 119:99, "I have more understanding than all my teachers: for thy testimonies are my meditation"; v. Psalms 119:105, "Thy word is a light unto my feet, and a lamp to my path"; v. Psalms 119:148, "Mine eyes prevent the night watches, that I might meditate in thy word"--by this it seems as though David often sat up late to read in the Law of God, or else rose up very early before day to that end; v. Psalms 119:176, "I do not forget thy commandments."

LAW PUBLICLY KNOWN. This is implied in Jeremiah 9:13, "they have forsaken my law which I SET BEFORE THEM"; in the original, Natti Leniphneikem, "which I set before their faces."

King AMAZIAH appears to have been acquainted with the Law of Moses, by 2 Kings 14:6.

What we have in Jeremiah 11:1-8, supposes the Law of Moses open to the view of the people in general.

The FIRST PSALM supposes that the people in general had opportunity to know the Law, and if so, the priests could not alter it parenthetical.7 So Psalms 37:31, Psalms 119:1-8, Psalms 94:12, and Psalms 119:165, and Psalms 119:19, Psalms 119:139, Psalms 25:8-10, Psalms 25:12, Psalms 25:14. In Psalms 119:19, 'tis mentioned as the character of those that fear God, that they have known God's testament.

There seems to be an allusion to the custom of INQUIRING IN THE TEMPLE, when the tribes went up thither to get acquaintance with the Law of God, in that, Isaiah 2:3,8 "And many people shall go and say, Come ye, and let us go up to the mountain of the Lord, to the house of the God of Jacob; and he will teach us of his ways, and we will walk in his paths: for out of Zion shall go forth the law, and the word of the Lord from Jerusalem." So Micah 4:2.

'Tis said, Psalms 48:9, "we have thought of thy lovingkindness in the midst of thy temple." In which words, 'tis probable there is respect to God's people having the history of Moses read to 'em there in the temple, giving an account of God's wonderful works of mercy to his people, in bringing them out of Egypt, etc. And this is confirmed by Psalms 77:10-13, "And I said, This is my infirmity: but I will remember the years of the right hand of the most High. I will remember the works of the Lord: surely I will remember thy wonders of old. I will meditate also of thy works, and talk of thy doings. Thy way, O Lord, is in the sanctuary"; i. e. "There is the place where thou declarest and manifestest these thy great works." And the context shows that the works meant, are those of the Mosaic history: "Thy way is in the sanctuary," i. e. "The way of thy commands, the way of holiness, and the way in which thou proceeds, there thy way, or thy goings, are, and wonderful works are seen"; agreeable to Psalms 68:24. What goings these were, we may learn by the preceding part of the psalm, vv. Psalms 68:7-14, Psalms 68:16-17, Psalms 68:22. These ways of God, both the way agreeable to his nature and will, and the way of his wonderful acts and proceedings, are mentioned together as being alike made known to the children of Israel; see Psalms 119:15. In Psalms 103:7, "He made known his ways unto Moses, his acts unto the children of Israel": here is evidently respect to two things, viz. God's will revealed to Moses, and by him to Israel; and God's wonderful acts, written by Moses. And in the 111th Psalm, at the beginning, the Psalmist, exhorting the saints to praise God in the great congregation parenthetical, goes on to show the regard the saints show in that congregation to the works of God, attending the redemption of Israel. He says, v. Psalms 103:2, "The works of the Lord are great, sought out of all them that have pleasure in them." They are sought out and inquired after by God's people, when they inquire in the temple, who would well acquaint themselves with what is written in the Law or covenant. These works and his commandments, or righteousness, or covenant, are9 in like manner established, recorded, made to endure, being ever remembered, made sure, standing fast. "His WORK is honorable and glorious: his RIGHTEOUSNESS ENDURETH FOR EVER. He hath made his wonderful WORKS to be REMEMBERED: the Lord is gracious, and full of compassion. He hath given meat unto them that fear him: he will be EVER MINDFUL of his COVENANT. He hath showed the people the power of his WORKS, that he may give them the heritage of the heathen. The WORKS of his hands are verity and judgment; and all his COMMANDMENTS are SURE. They stand fast for ever and ever, they are done in truth and righteousness. He sent redemption unto his people: he hath commanded his COVENANT for ever" Psalms 11:3-9.

The LAW taught in the TEMPLE. Psalms 122:3-4, "Jerusalem is builded," etc., "whither the tribes go up,. .. to the TESTIMONY of Israel."

PRIESTS, LEVITES AND PEOPLE KNEW THE LAW; LAW PUBLICLY KNOWN. Their priests in general were acquainted with the Law parenthetical,10 Leviticus 10:9-11. The priests were forbidden to drink wine, etc., that they might teach the children of Israel all the statutes which the Lord had spoken unto them by the hand of Moses. It was absolutely necessary that they all should have a very perfect acquaintance with it; otherwise it is impossible they should know what to do, how to wait on their priest's office.

The laws about the leprosy, and their sacrifices, do all plainly suppose that the priests must have their rule always before 'em parenthetical. That the priests and Levites in general are well acquainted with the Law, is supposed in Deuteronomy 33:10, speaking of the Levites: "They shall teach Jacob thy judgments, and Israel thy law." See 2 Chronicles 17:7-9 and 2 Chronicles 35:2-3, Micah 3:11, Deuteronomy 17:8-13, Isaiah 30:20 and Isaiah 43:27-28, 2 Chronicles 15:3, Jeremiah 18:18, Ezekiel 7:26, Hosea 4:4, Hosea 4:6, Malachi 4-7, Jeremiah 2:8, Haggai 2:11-13, Deuteronomy 24:8, Lamentations 2:9.

And the LEVITES. 2 Chronicles 30:22 and 2 Chronicles 34:13, and 2 Chronicles 35:3, "and said unto the Levites that taught ALL ISRAEL"; Ezra 8:8-9, Ezra 8:13; 2 Chronicles 19:8 to the end, 2 Chronicles 31:4, Jeremiah 8:8-9, Psalms 50:16, "But to the wicked God saith, What hast thou to do to declare my statutes, and to take my covenant into thy mouth?" By this, 'tis manifest there were many who made it their business to declare God's statutes and his covenant.

THE LAW PUBLIC. It is supposed the elders of every city-state knew the Law, for the words they should say on some occasions are therein prescribed, as Deuteronomy 21:6-8.

That direction given for all to try what any shall set up for teachers by the Law, in Isaiah 8:18-20, seems to suppose the Law open to all: "To the law and to the testimony: if they speak not according to this word, it is because there is no light in them."

The same is supposed, Hosea 8:12, "I have written to them the great things of my law."

The LAW PUBLIC. Psalms 74:8, "they have burnt up all the synagogues of God in the land." 'Tis pretty manifest from hence that there were many houses in the land, where God's people statedly met for religious purposes. The word in the original translated "synagogues," signifies "congregation," and what is meant is the houses built for these congregations. 'Tis manifest by this, that burning up the congregations is meant, as the foregoing verse speaks of burning up the temple.

And 'tis evident that 'tis religious congregations are spoken, for it was things of this nature that the Psalmist is speaking of, as appears by the foregoing and following verse. And 'tis highly probable that in those congregations, in various parts of the land, the people were instructed in the Law by the priests and Levites, who were dispersed through all parts of the land to that purpose.

It was the manner of the people, especially such as were devout, when they went up to the temple every year to the three great feasts, to spend their time there very much in endeavors to get knowledge in the Law, which was called "inquiring in God's temple." Psalms 27:4, "One thing have I desired of the Lord, that will I seek after; that I may dwell in the house of the Lord all the days of my life, to behold the beauty of the Lord, and to INQUIRE in his temple."

Psalms 122:3-4, "Jerusalem is builded as a city compact together: whither the tribes go up, the tribes of the Lord unto the testimony of Israel." This place is a great confirmation, that one great end of the tribes' going up to the sanctuary at their three great feasts, was to acquaint themselves with the Law or testimony.

Such charges as are often to all the people universally, and with utmost care, diligence, strictness and universality to observe the Law, suppose 'em well acquainted with it. Exodus 23:13, "and in all things that I have said unto you be circumspect." See innumerable injunctions of this kind in the book of Deuteronomy parenthetical; see Deuteronomy 32:46-47. To take diligent heed in this matter, to neglect no command, statute, or judgment, and a strict injunction of their taking proper method to know them, and teach them with the facts to posterity, are intermixed, as Deuteronomy 6:3 to the end, and Deuteronomy 8:1-2, Deuteronomy 8:6, Deuteronomy 8:10 to the end parenthetical;11 and their being commanded to offer a sacrifice whenever they happened through inadvertence, IGNORANCE, or forgetfulness in any particular to omit or transgress, either priest, or prince, or the whole people, or particular persons, which was a fine or punishment.

That awful threatening, Deuteronomy 27:26, "Cursed is every one that confirmeth not all the words of this law to do them," supposes those words very public.

Those words, Deuteronomy 33:2-4, suppose the Law public to the view of all: "from his right hand went a fire" parenthetical "of law for them. Yea, he loved the people; all his saints are in thy hand: and they sat down at thy feet; every one shall receive of thy words. Moses commanded us a law, even the inheritance of the congregation of Jacob."

See laws concerning SINS OF IGNORANCE. See Leviticus, chs. 4 and 5; Numbers 15:22-29.

So much said in the history against the notion {that their} their FAULTS related in such a manner.

They had a regular, orderly history of their nation up to that very time, giving an account of the state of things from age to age, as gradually and naturally proceeding from that miraculous state of things which Moses gave account of.

'Tis abundantly manifest from that history of their nation, continued through all succeeding times, that they had this history of Moses in being among them from the very times that the history gives an account of.

The history is blended with the great Law, on which their whole constitution was built, so that the book of history and book of laws was the same.

UNIVERSAL. The whole constitution of the nation, in every part of it, civil and ecclesiastical, appears to be founded on the facts, and even the very being, and all the public and important things belonging to the nation; even their possession of their land, and possession of all their rights and privileges; their religion, and all the innumerable rites belonging to it; and the distinction of the tribes, and the division of the land among them, and the bounds of each tribe; and more than that, even the distinction of families, and particularly the extraordinary distinction and business of one whole tribe, even that of Levi, that were dispersed through all the nation, and yet were kept up distinct; and one remarkable family, even that of Aaron, also dispersed through the whole country in a very distinguishing business: all these were monuments and witnesses of the truth, as every law, every custom, every inheritance, the very being of the nation, and their possession of the country, and the names of the cities, everything belonging to the state of the nation, was a monument of the facts, and a standing witness of the truth of 'em.

UNIVERSAL. The things belonging to the religious and civil state and constitution of the nation, were so exceeding complicated, and withal so exceeding orderly, regular, exact and punctilious, and so steady and constant: as their elders, judges, officers, heads of thousands, hundreds, fifties, tens; the tribe of Levi, their divisions and services; the family of the priests, their exact order, their clothing, their beauteous, curious robes, wonderfully contrived; the tabernacle with its opulent utensils, so exceeding costly, exact, curious, convenient, useful and beautiful; and their rules and forms of worship; the services of the priests, of the Levites, of the whole nation, with their infinite varieties, without any confusion, all fixed and settled precisely without difference of different tribes; and all very different from all other nations, especially neighbor nations, so that it evidently had scarce anything that was akin to the customs of other people: I say, considering these things, there must be sometime wherein this constitution was made, and there must be some great establishment; and the establishment must be some very great and notable thing, by some remarkable authority, or some wonderful and strange and universal agreement of the nation.

And if so, it would be strange if nothing of it should be remembered among a nation, noted far above all nations in the world for keeping records of their affairs, herein vastly distinguished from all nations under heaven. And if there was some public account of the true fact, this would make it far more difficult for any imposters that should endeavor to palm on the nation a forged account of such an extremely strange original, altogether consisting of a series12 of prodigious miracles: appearances from heaven, leading the whole nation through seas and rivers on dry ground, God's appearing from heaven for so long a time in thunder, lightning, fire and earthquake on a mountain, etc., etc., etc., when all was mere fiction and nothing else.

LAWS UNIVERSAL. The laws of the Jews affected their concerns much more universally than the laws of any people: in their bodies, that painful rite of circumcision; their clothes, wearing fringes, not woolen and linen; their meat and drink, only clean things specified, and a multitude of things are mentioned--unclean animals and unclean parts, blood and fat forbidden; their women in their diseases, their lying-in, their purifications; their time, a multitude of days and years consecrated, and to be observed after a particular manner; their marriages; their funerals; their daily necessary occasions to ease nature; their behavior; their disposal of their money; their lands, their building battlements on them; their cities and suburbs; the possessions of every tribe, every family; their conversation with neighbor nations; their conjugal intercourse, Leviticus 15:18; the accidental emission of seed, Leviticus 15:16; the feast of the new moon, for the beginning of every month; their weekly sabbath to be strictly kept every seventh day by a strict rest; their sabbatical years, every seventh year, Leviticus 25; their year of jubilee, Leviticus 25:9, etc., once in fifty years, and then an universal release of all servants, all debts, return of all men's lands to their original owners: things greatly affecting the state and concerns of the people in general.

Besides the many universal feasts, there was their strict yearly feast for the whole nation.

The yearly feasts were much more affecting the state of the nation than ordinary public feasts among other nations, in that the whole nation were to travel so far, and in some of the feasts were to spend so many days. The seventh month was, great part of it, taken up in holy days, wherein great things were to be done.

Everything they did within doors and without, at home and abroad, day and night, in their beds and when up, they must not live, move or breath as it were but by law.

These things, all put together, were more than if they had erected thousands of brazen or marble monuments, obelisks, pyramids in all parts of the land, and had been obliged to visit them once every month in the year.

The law of worshipping one God only under their circumstances, was a law that required constant care, concern, watchfulness and resistance of others, and so occasioning a continual, sensible impression on the mind.

UNIVERSAL. The division of the land, distinction of tribes and family, the business of the posterity of Aaron and the business of the Levites, with all those many civil and religious observances, which concerned and constantly employed everybody, and was so blended in all their concerns, must have always been by some written law or constitution. The observances were too many and complicated, and exact and precise, and long-maintained to be known and kept, so that the whole nation should agree in them for any long time without a written constitution. Among so many observances, maintained only by tradition, there would soon be innumerable variations, and one part of the people would have things one way, and others another, and all would quickly run into confusion. 'Tis plain to a demonstration that, in order to an uniformity, there must be standard that all could appeal to, and that all errors might be rectified by. If it be said all were regulated by the priests, that would not be sufficient without a written rule: for they lived distant one from another, in all parts of the country.

We see, as to those things that depend on report without a written account, how quickly they vary, and very innumerable differences,13 as stories of strange events that are carried only by word of mouth and common fame.

And if there was a law, and a very particular law, appointing most of these things from the beginning, a law which all knew from generation to generation, which law had no reference to these facts, nor was dependent upon them: 'tis not likely that this law would be set aside for the sake of one agreeable to a new, forged history; or that it should be altered and have a set of new precepts added to it, requiring things to be performed by the whole nation, and having reference to the facts, with a pretence of those new precepts originally belonging to the law, and having been in it from the beginning; and this be done successfully so as to persuade the whole nation, from year to year, to comply with these new observances.

RECAPITULATION. A forgery of a great and wonderful fact pretended to be in SIGHT of a WHOLE NATION, every man, woman and child in it, is more easily detected than what is pretended to be seen only by a few; and a forgery published among that very nation, before whom the fact is pretended to be done, is more likely to be detected than that which was pretended to be before some other nation; and a forgery of such a fact pretended to be done on the spot where the forgery is first published, or in places in view, is more easily detected than of a fact pretended to be in some remote corner.

And a forgery of a fact pretended to be in the view of a whole nation--which fact, as it is related, vastly affects the state of the nation, and that not only for the present but their continued state, their perpetual constitution, and affects their state in every public and important interest--that all seems to be founded upon it, would be more difficulty palmed on a nation than a fact that was indifferent, and only is supposed to be seen and admired, and to excite the admiration of the nation,33 but to have no lasting effect. And the forgery of a long series of such facts for fifty years together, all in sight of the whole nation, and all in the highest degree affecting them for the present, and in the highest degree and most universal manner affecting their state, and all their interests in all future generations, must be more difficultly imposed on a people than one single facts, or facts for a very short time.

And such an imposture is least likely to take effect with a nation used to the use of letters and records than other nations that had not these things, especially a nation that had the most exact and precise records of their nation up to the very times of the facts, even so precise that every man could tell his ancestors that lived at that very time; and especially must it be hard to palm such an imposture on that nation within so short a time, as within two hundred years of the time that many of the eyewitnesses lived, or were pretended to live. And especially facts which were the ground of all their laws, etc., etc. And it must be more difficult still to make a forgery of such facts take effect, that were pretended to be done not only in sight of that nation, but all nations round about, and the most considerable nations in the world, etc., etc., etc. Here observe many other particulars: every man's farm in such a case must be a kind of monument of the truth of the history, for the Law makes it impossible to be alienated; and almost everything34 visible among such a people is a monument. And 'tis perhaps impossible to devise a state of things tending more to a continued, clear and certain evidence of a history.

If the history had been forged, the falsehood might have been the more easily detected for pretending to give so PARTICULAR an ACCOUNT of the whole state of the nation at the time: of their rulers, teachers, heads of tribes, the number of every tribe; all the important actions of the nation, such in particular as most affected the present and future state of the nation, the manner of their settlement in the land, the means and instruments, the cause and manner: introducing and establishing everything pertaining to the whole state of the nation, the names of the princes and heads of tribes which were appointed to divide the land, Numbers 34.

The after-history of their nation gives an account from time to time of facts that represent OTHER NATIONS as remembering and owning those facts, as the Ammonites that warred with Jephthah, and the Philistines when they took the ark.

THREE GREAT FEASTS. What made it infinitely more difficult to introduce such a fraud into the nation gradually, and as it were secretly, and by little and little, was this thing which attended the state of the nation, viz. that the whole nation met together three times in a year. This prevented all opportunity to introduce this fraud, to creep about by degrees from one place to another, as sometimes false reports do, till in great length of time it become generally established, that nation were together three times in a year.

THREE GREAT FEASTS. Very much at some of their feasts, they were together eight or ten days; and then, when they were coming down and going, it was in companies. No nation in the world was so united by a public union. They were all as one family, the whole acquainted with the affairs of all parts: no new thing, especially that greatly concerned the whole nation, could be set on foot and begun to be introduced, but it must be as it were with blowing a trumpet and proclaiming it about in the ears of the whole nation; and not only so, but the whole nation together having opportunity and necessity to consult one another, the poorer and weaker being under the eye and instruction of their great and wise men. This circumstance greatly tended to keep up the records of their nation with the greatest exactness, especially as to their religious affairs, and affairs greatly concerning the public as to its continued state.

HISTORY was CONTAINED IN THE LAW. Psalms 119:18, "Open thou mine eyes, that I may behold wondrous things out of thy law." There is great reason to think that what David chiefly intends by "wondrous things," are the wondrous works of God written in the book of the Law, and that this is a prayer that he may rightly apprehend those wondrous works. The Psalmist observes, Psalms 106:7, "Our fathers understood not thy wonders in Egypt," etc. Here he prays that it may not be so with him; and Moses observes, Deuteronomy 29:2-4, "Ye have seen all that the Lord did before your eyes in the land of Egypt unto Pharaoh, and unto all his servants, unto all his land; the great temptations which thine eyes have seen, the signs, and those great miracles: yet the Lord hath not given you an heart to perceive, and ears to hear, and eyes to see, unto this day." Here the Psalmist prays that it may not be so with him, but that his eyes may be opened, that he may have eyes to see, that he may understand God's wonders.

The word translated "wonders" is Niphlaoth. I find this word, for the most part, signifies "wonderful works" through out the Old Testament; and it is generally so translated in this book of Psalms, as Psalms 9:1, Psalms 26:7, and Psalms 40:5, and Psalms 71:17, and Psalms 72:18, and Psalms 75:1, and Psalms 86:10, and Psalms 96:3, and Psalms 98:1 and Psalms 107:8, Psalms 107:15, Psalms 107:21, Psalms 107:24, Psalms 107:31. Yea, it very commonly is used to signify the wonderful work written in the Pentateuch, Psalms 78:4, Psalms 78:11, Psalms 78:32; and Psalms 105:2, Psalms 105:5; and Psalms 106:7, Psalms 106:22; and Psalms 136:4, and Psalms 145:5 and Psalms 119:27. The place last mentioned much confirms this interpretation of this 78th verse of the 119th Psalm, and also confirms the thing I would prove by it, viz. that the history of the facts was in what they called the Law. In that 27th verse of the same Psalm, the Psalmist says, "Make me to understand the way of thy precepts: so shall I talk of thy wondrous works"; that is, "Open my eyes that I may rightly understand thy way revealed in thy Law, and then I shall be suitably affected with thy wondrous works: and so shall I discourse of," or "meditate on" parenthetical, "thy wonderful work written in that Law."

When the Psalmist says in the 129th verse of the same psalm, "Thy testimonies are wonderful: therefore my soul keepeth them," 'tis reasonable to suppose that what he calls God's testimonies, which he says are wonderful, are not only the precepts of the Law but the revelations of it, including history and prophecy of the wonderful works of God.

The whole can't be counterfeit: none will pretend that there was no such nation as the Jews carried into Babylon, etc. And 'tis hard counterfeiting the root and stock of a tree for the real history, as branches to be set on, so as to make the whole like a natural tree, so but that a narrow search would soon discern the disagreement and ill connection.

Ezra was a very likely person to be acquainted with the ancient writings and records of Israel, being the son of Seraiah the high priest. Compare Ezra 2, at the beginning, with 1 Chronicles 6:14 with the preceding verses. There was no family he could have been born in, no circumstances he could have been brought up in, wherein he could have had such advantages for thorough acquaintance with these things. And his brother Jozadak, being high priest in Babylon in the time of the captivity, Haggai 1:1, he must have the keeping of all such records as were preserved there, particularly of the sacred writings. The religious Jews in the captivity would repair to the priests for instruction in these things, and especially to the high priest and such as were next to him; and his brother Ezra, giving his mind greatly to these things, he must have all possible advantage for knowledge.

CONTINUED HISTORY NOT FORGED BY ANY MAN OR COMBINATION OF MEN AFTER THE CAPTIVITY. The seeming contradictions, the differences of numbers, etc., are a proof of this.

PRESERVATION OF THEIR RECORDS THROUGH THE CAPTIVITY. 'Tis evident they preserved most of their books of record of their genealogies, by Ezra 2:62. How much more would they be likely to take care to preserve their Law, and the grand and public records containing the accounts of the most important affairs of their nation, through the times of their genealogies. 'Tis evident they had the sacred books in the captivity, by Daniel 9, and, among others, the Law of Moses, v. Daniel 9:13.

Concerning Moses' divine authority, see "Miscellanies," no. 1300.45

CONTINUED HISTORY. The double history of the kings, that in Kings and that in Chronicles, confirms the truth of the facts.

The GENEALOGY of Zephaniah, Zephaniah 1:1.

Zephaniah 1:1 confirms the history of the kings of Judah.

PRIESTS TAUGHT THE LAW. Jeremiah 18:18.

There was the MEMORY of the wonders in Egypt and in other places, wrought in Moses' time, still remaining in Jeremiah's time, among the nations round about, Jeremiah 32:20.

LORD'S DAY.46 "Ezekiel 43:27, 'it shall be, that upon the eighth day, and so forward, the priests shall make your burnt offerings on the altar, and your thank offerings; and I will accept you, saith the Lord God.' The words clearly predict, that the seventh day's solemn service should cease; and that on the eighth day, i. e. on the day after the seventh, the solemn worship of God should be attended, and spiritual sacrifices offered on the gospel altar." Dr. Increase Mather, Meditations on the Lord's Day, p. 10.47

LORD'S DAY. Psalms 111:4, "He hath made his wonderful work to be remembered." Doubtless, he hath made his most wonderful work of all to be remembered.

LORD'S DAY. When God appeared in any place, that place was holy. So Christ sanctified the first day of the week, by appearing from time to time on that day.

HISTORY TO THE DISCREDIT OF THE NATION. Jeremiah 32:23, Jeremiah 32:30, Jermiah 32:32-35, Isaiah 30:9, etc.

Ezekiel, ch. 2, ch. 16, ch. 20, chs. 22 and 23.

'Tis a great argument that the Jews did not FORGE SACRED WRITINGS AFTER THE CAPTIVITY, that after Malachi's death never any pretended to be a prophet, or palm any writing of theirs on the people as of divine inspiration; whereas if there had been prevailing among them such a humor of forgery after the captivity, it would have rather been likely to increase as the people manifestly degenerated, grew more proud, self-righteous, and got more and more into a humor of exalting their nation above all other nations, till this came to an extreme height in Christ's time.

Insist on this argument of the impossibility of forging sacred writings among the Jews after the CAPTIVITY, viz. that whether the Jews had SYNAGOGUES before the captivity or no, yet 'tis beyond dispute that after the captivity, long before Christ's time, they had synagogues everywhere, all over the land, and all over the world, and everywhere it was the established custom to read all their sacred writings, the Law and the Prophets. It could not be extensively and universally, and with so full agreement established and settled, as it was in Christ's and the apostles' times, if something like it had not very long been a custom, at least in the land of Canaan.

That the Jews that remained in other countries, in all parts of the world, AFTER THE CAPTIVITY, held so much of one mind and in one way as to religion, is an argument that they kept a number of the SACRED BOOKS among 'em, even from the time of the captivity, and that they were not new writing or books sent among 'em afterwards from Judea, writing that they were unacquainted with before.

CAPTIVITY. 'Tis evident the prophet Daniel had the Law of God and the writings of the Prophets in his hand. 'Tis manifest the book of the prophet Daniel was not forged after the captivity.

'Tis not likely they would forge that prophecy of the Messiah in the 9th of Daniel, and that destruction to their country that should follow, as Stapferus observes.48 There never was any controversy about the authenticity of this book, though Daniel 12:2 is very contrary to the sect of the Sadducees.

If it had been forged after the fulfillment of most of the events--as after the death of Antiochus Epiphanes--the defects in the Hebrew and the exceeding difference of the time, the temper, manners, etc., would have occasioned many things to be in the book that would have discovered the fallacy, it being about 400 years. There would have been many disagreements in chronology, and many inconsistencies with true history and other records of the Jews, that by an accurate examination might have been plainly discovered.

And how exceeding unlikely that, in those late times, this book could have been added to the canon and accepted by the Jews, who were exact and careful, even to superstition, about their canon. And then the sect of the Sadducees subsisted, who would never have received such a book as it is, with ch. 12:2.

Besides, the death of Antiochus Epiphanes is not like enough to account for the exact fulfillment of the prophecies: for nothing is more exactly fulfilled than the prophecy of the Roman Empire.

METHOD. 1. To show that the children of Israel, before the captivity into Babylon, did dwell in the land in that division of tribes, under those laws, and in general in that state in which they are represented to have dwelt there, in the books of the Old Testament in general.

The state, as to a great part of it, would be remembered by many then alive. How, show what parts.

2. That that history of their nation that we have after Moses' time, was a history they had in esteem among them before the captivity as their true history, and was in general a true history; and those Psalms, Proverbs and prophecies,49 etc., were no counterfeit thing, as pretended to be of a different date and different original from what they truly were; and the Pentateuch in general was what they called the Law of Moses, and was handed down as such from ancient time.

METHOD. The first point to be proved: that the Jews had those writings before the captivity that are pretended to be written before the captivity.

Wherein50 the revelation made by Nehemiah differed from that by Christ. It was not so happy a revelation.

It was by no means so great a revelation, considering the state the world was in before, especially if we measure51 the greatness by the degree of good and positive effect; nor in any respect, if we consider the degenerate state the Christian world was in before Mahomet.

Infinitely more wonderful revelation if we consider the means.

Mahometanism might be by nature. .. the religion is more agreeable to nature.

EZRA DID NOT FORGE THE JEWISH SCRIPTURES AFTER THE CAPTIVITY. About Ezra's forging all the Jewish scriptures, pretended to be written before the captivity: how vast a work would it have been; how impossible in those days, when writing was difficult and slow in the character, and with their materials, in comparison of what it now is; how great a thing, to invent all these histories and put 'em all together, in such connection, with Psalms, songs, Proverbs and so many prophecies relating to innumerable things in the history, infinite ways connected with it: how could it be done without betraying the fraud in ways innumerable, by multitudes of inconsistencies, things spoken disagreeing with the time, etc., etc.? And the style is apparently different. There must have been vast labor in each part, to make it appear so much like real history: to mention the names of men and names of places, the particular, minute circumstances, to imitate nature so much, represent the working of their tempers, to invent speeches and deeds so worthy of God, so agreeable to the divine nature and majesty.

To cover all with a semblance of so much simplicity and sincerity, in representing52 the faults of the people, and the faults of the best and most eminent and renowned of their prophets, princes, etc. To make all agree with their genealogies, to invent the genealogies in Genesis and other places. To make all parts of the geography agree. To invent the chronology.

To connect all with that part of the history that was within memory.

To invent all the knowledge and wisdom discovered in Psalms, Job, Proverbs, Ecclesiastes and in the Prophets, and the poetical and prophetic, exalted style.

What should move him to invent such a book as Solomon's Song?

Why did he invent such short prophecies as Obadiah?

Why such seeming differences as between Chronicles and Kings?

Why such a book as Ruth invented?

How diverse the style of different boks: Job, Psalms, Proverbs, Ecclesiastes, Isaiah, Hosea and other short Prophets, Amos.

Writing so many verses beginning with the different letters of the alphabet, as in the 119th Psalm and Lamentations.

The impossibility of forging the books of Jeremiah and Ezekiel, because men's memories must confute them.

And if they were not forged, there would be the difficulty of connecting all the preceding history with them, which they allude to in almost all parts of it.

To have invented one such book as the book of Job, if it could be done at all, would have taken up great part of the life of such a man in that age, when the means of education to prepare men for it, were such as they were.

And so of the book of Psalms and Proverbs.

To have composed and woven and connected such a frame as the Pentateuch, with the various histories, revelations, doctrine, laws, etc., would have been a vast work indeed, filling up an age.

To make all the prophecies in the books of Moses to agree well with facts.

Would have made the prophecies more plain, to agree with facts.

Would not have put in any prophecies beyond his own time, as those of Balaam respecting ships from Chittim.

And the blessing of Judah respecting Shiloh's coming.

A vast work, to have invented the frame of the tabernacle so minutely, and the work of the Levites in carrying it.

That prophetical song, Deuteronomy 32.

If Ezra had not been of good character, 'tis not likely he would have been approved by the king as a man of good character, one liked and approved as such among his own people, and them that knew him.

And if he had been so grand a rogue, it is not likely he would have obtained so good a character, and be so well spoken of by Nehemiah, and so much trusted by the king and the people, who were a serious, honest generation.

How different a style is the book of Ecclesiastes from most other books, and how diverse a genius does it manifest. How unlikely a thing that a book of that great wisdom and depth of philosophy, should only be the fiction of a vile, rascally fellow; and so the book of Proverbs.

Who can suppose all these natural strains of elevated devotion, with so vast a variety, suiting such different occasions, different circumstances and different frames, and different times and persons as the book of Psalms, should be, with all the rest, a fiction of a deceitful imposter brought up in Babylon?

The plain difference of style between the books of Chronicles and the other Historical books before Ezra and Nehemiah, show that Ezra did not write both that and others.

If the books of the Prophets had been forged after the captivity by one of the Jews, they would have been written in a very different manner; and particularly, the prophecies there found of the restoration from captivity would have been quite otherwise composed than they are. There would have been more of a literal, plain agreement. The prophecies would have been more in the language of history, and like the history of their return, mentioning the particulars which Ezra saw, describing them after the manner of men, and such things concerning the Messiah and his kingdom: would not have been so blinded with the restoration from Babylon, as if they had been one event, and all to be accomplished at the same time; as the prophecies in the latter part of Isaiah from the 40th chapter, and many of the prophecies of Jeremiah, and the latter part of the Ezekiel from the 34th chapter to the end, with the description of such a temple, etc., so very diverse from that which Ezra saw built, and such a division of the land, etc.

It is sometimes spoken of as a great work of God, to deliver Abraham and Israel from idolatry, Joshua 22; and so when they returned from the captivity.

It is spoken of as a strange, unheard of thing, that a nation should change their gods.

It is manifest that the Jews in Jerusalem had the Law of Moses among them long before Ezra came up to Jerusalem, and probably before Ezra was born, by Ezra 3:2, Ezra 3:4 and Ezra 6:18, and Haggai 2:11-13, Zechariah 3:7.

And it appears that the Jews in Jerusalem had the book of the Prophets among them long before Ezra came thither, by Zechariah 1:4-6 and Zechariah 7:7.

And about eleven years after Ezra went up to Jerusalem, Nehemiah, the king's cupbearer at Shushan, had the Law of Moses there as a book he was well acquainted with; see Nehemiah 1:7-9.
