NOTES ON CHRISTIANITY

One11 of the texts against atheism. Job 12:7-9, "But ask now the Beasts," etc. "Who knoweth not in all these that the hand of the Lord hath wrought this?"

Doc. When we consider the various creatures that we behold in the world, it is exceeding plain that it is God that hath made them. He that cometh to God must believe that he is.

See more texts in Mastrict, "de Existentia et Cognitiene dei," and see also Turretin.12

____________________________________

RATIONALNESS AND CONGRUITY OF CHRISTIANITY. 'Tis rational to suppose that there should be some way for the Creator and First Being to manifest the wonderfulness and boundlessness of his grace, as well as his power and wisdom. But no13 way can possibly be thought of whereby this should be done so much, in so high a degree, as that which the gospel reveals.

____________________________________

BOOKS OF THE OLD TESTAMENT NOT FORGED. The Jews never would, in books that they forged, record themselves such a perverse, wicked people as they are recorded in those books. Vid. Is. 30:8-9, 19, with my note upon it;14 and Deut. 32:20, Is. 1:4, Is. 65:2-4, etc.

____________________________________

APOSTLES SPAKE BY INSPIRATION. This is given as an infallible mark by the apostle John, whereby the primitive church might distinguish between those that maintained truth and those that maintained error were to be tried, and whereby even the spirits themselves that persons in these times were inspired with were to be tried: viz. their hearing the apostles, or their hearkening to and complying with these things that they taught. Which was making of their doctrine a rule immensely too much, if they in their doctrine were not under the infallible guidance of the Spirit of God, and if the Christian church in general had not clear evidence that they were so.

See texts in Common-Place Book to the Scriptures, in the chapter of the Scriptures.15

SUCCESS OF THE GOSPEL. "Tertullian tells the Roman Senate, that though the Christians were but of yesterday, yet they had filled all places and offices, hesterni sumus et omnia implevimus, that they were strong enough to master the Roman Empire; nay, that so great were their numbers, that if they should but agree to retire out of it, the world would wonder at its own solitude. And in his book against the Jews, he says, 'The Christian religion had enlarged its conquests beyond those of the Roman Empire, and had subdued those places which were inaccesible to their armies.' Pliny take notice in his letter to the Emperor concerning the Christians, that there were such multitudes of them that the 'temples and sacrifices of the gods were almost forsaken.' Tacitus speaks of ingens multitudo, an immense number, even in Nero's time, the very first age of Christianity. In short, the prevalency of the Chritian religion was such, that it gave occasion to the heathens to call it η χρατουσα Δοξα, the powerful and prevailing sect." Bennet's Inspiration of the Scriptures, p. 276.19

_____________________________________

The epistles of the apostles were written for general use, with a design that the church of God in general should be benefited by them, and so were received in the apostles' days. The apostle Peter speaks of the epistles of the apostle Paul as things that were generally known and in use in the church of God, in his Second Epistle, third chapter, [verses] 15-16, though none of the epistles of Paul are supposed to have been written more than about seven years before that epistle of Peter.

_____________________________________

It may be argued from John 17:20 that the word of the apostles was to be standing rule for the church of Christ through all ages: "Neither pray I for those alone, but for them that shall believe on me through their word."

The apostles did pretend to speak by inspiration. See I Cor. 2:1, 13; I Tim. 6:3.

______________________________________

The apostles' doctrine and precepts were looked upon as infallible and as having the force of a rule by them and the church in their days . This doctrine was thus spoken under the names of "the traditions that had been delivered" to them, the "doctrine that had been delivered," etc. I Cor 11:2, "Now I praise you, brethren, that you remember me in all things, and keep the ordinances" (or "traditions," as it is in the margin) "as I delivered them unto you." II Thess. 2:15, "Therefore, brethren, stand fast, and hold the traditions which ye have been taught, whether by word or our epistle." Chap. 3:6, "Now we commend you, brethren, in the name of our Lord Jesus Christ, that ye withdraw yourselves from every brother that walketh disorderly, and not after the tradition which he received of us." Rev. 3:3, "Remember therefore how thou hast received and heard, and hold fast, and repent." II Pet. 2:21, "For it had been better for them not to have known the way of righteousness, than, after they have known it, to turn from the holy commandment delivered unto them." Jude 3, "It was needful for me to write unto you, and exhort you that ye should earnestly contend for the faith which was once delivered to the saints." See also I John 4:6, "He that knoweth God heareth us; he that is not of God heareth not us. Hereby know we the spirit of truth, and the spirit of error." Gal. 1:8-9, "Though we, or an angel from heaven, preach any other gospel," etc. See vv. 11-12, and I Cor. 15:1, 3; Gal. 1:15 to the end, and chap. 2:1-15.
