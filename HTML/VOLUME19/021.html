THE EVERLASTING LOVE OF GOD

HIS "appearance, his countenance, words and whole demeanor," Samuel Hopkins recalls in The Life and Character of the Late Rev. Mr. Jonathan Edwards, "was attended with a seriousness, gravity and solemnity." Preaching from a manuscript, Edwards spoke with "clearness and precision," without gestures, "without much noise and external emotion,"1 What reading, then, did he give to the statement, "But I shall insist upon but one thing here as an evidence of your being a saint, and so of your being the object of the everlasting love of God, viz. love to god"? That sentence occurs in his discourse on the thirty-first of Jeremiah after the opening paragraph of the second head of the Application, the use of self-examination, and in manuscript (L. 13r.) appears as it does here, except for Edwards' standard abbreviations for evidence and, in the first instance, God; the closing phrase, spaced and much larger—and hence more legible—than the accompanying text, stands apart on a line of its own, centered. A somewhat rare occurrence in sermon manuscripts, enlarged letters usually signal major divisions, as they do here with "first" (L. 2v.) and "secondly" (L. 6r.), the first and second propositions of the Doctrine; rarely do they occur elsewhere in a text: "repentance" appears in the manuscript of Justification by Faith Alone (LL. 52v. and 53r.) but not in the published version in that form. Obviously, Edwards meant to emphasize love to God in order to distinguish it from love of God, the phrase immediately preceding it; the sermon is, after all, about the reciprocal nature of divine love and its duration, from everlasting to everlasting, in his formulation. Left unanswered, of course, is how he delivered the isolated words, with what noise, what emotion. For the rest of it, logic prevailed, that and the covenant of redemption.

As Edwards explains it, the love of God to his saints had its being from eternity, "before the world was," and would continue "immutably the same" after their creation as before, their names, like those of the tribes of Israel on Aaron's breastplate, inscribed upon the heart of Christ "from all eternity." God's love, self-moved and from his sovereign good pleasure,

-- 474 --

depends on nothing temporal, nothing in the saints, neither their good works nor their "lovely qualifications." All God's arts are acts of love to them, both before time and in it, for he created the world out of love for them, especially the "highest heaven," and entered into a covenant with his Son for their redemption, promising eternal life to "such and such persons." As well, the love of God to his saints is to eternity. His love is unaffected by circumstance or condition, unlike theirs to him, and is typified by Christ's constant love of his "cowardly and unsteady" disciples in the time of his trials; and the fruits of God's love to them will abide to eternity, their blessedness everlasting. That the elect are assured of this rests in the unchangeable nature of God, in his truth and in his will, in his timelessness—that which is from eternity must be to eternity—and in his Son. Inasmuch as Jesus is the "same in substance" with God, it is impossible that the Father should not love his Son; furthermore, that Christ stands for the saints, even in his "terrible and amazing" suffering and death, secures God's love of them, else the Father denies and disappoints the Son and frustrates him "in his end." Therefore, the saints may delight in the "eternal transaction" that accorded them redemption, "a small number" out of multitudes, and in God's "absolutely" free love, an act out of time and nature; and they may know the evidence of it either intuitively, in seasons of "strong and lively" affections, or, more likely, in reflection upon the nature and properties of divine love, notably, obedience to God's commandments. And, finally, they may find great comfort in it and, because they are the objects of God's everlasting and immutable love, "unspeakable joy."

* * *

Above the text on the left of the first of sixteen duodecimo leaves is "RD," Edwards' History of Redemption symbol, and on the right, the date, "March 1736." The manuscript divides into two preaching units, the first ending after two-thirds of one page (L. 10v.) and the second beginning with the untitled Application, after the text (in part) and the Doctrine, on the page following (L. 11r.). There are two other abbreviated pages in the manuscript (LL. 14r. and 14v.), but in contrast to many others of the period, the writing covers, if sparingly, the whole of the last leaf of this one (L. 16v.) and closes with a deletion in the middle of things, implying perhaps another (unfound) leaf. Typical in all other respects, the manuscript may lay claim to an uncertain place for novel orthography: Edwards renders all forms of assure, unmistakably, ashure.

THE EVERLASTING LOVE OF GOD
Yea, I have loved thee with an everlasting love.2 Jeremiah 31:3

There is a prophecy in the foregoing chapter of the restoration of Israel from their captivity and dispersion, which is continued here in this chapter. And here in this context two things are set forth, viz.

1. God's former kindness and love to Israel in days of old; as in the first clause of this verse, "The Lord hath appeared of old unto me." God has in this a special respect to his love, manifested to them in first separating them from other people, and calling them to be his people; as in the last clause of this verse, "with lovingkindness have I drawn thee"; i.e. "I manifested my love to thee in bringing of thee home to myself to be my people at first."

And God has respect particularly to his great mercy shown the people in bringing of them out of Egypt, and carrying them through the wilderness to Canaan; as appears by the preceding verse, "Thus saith the Lord, The people which were left of the sword found grace in the wilderness."

2. Here is set forth God's future lovingkindness to Israel in the mercy that he would show them in times to come; as in the verses following the text: "Again I will build thee, and thou shalt be built, O virgin of Israel: thou shalt again be adorned with thy tabrets, and shalt go forth in the dances of them that make merry. Thou shalt yet plant vines upon the mountains of Samaria: the planters shall plant, and shall eat them as common things"; and so throughout the chapter. And,

3. The reason of both is given in the words of the text, "Yea, I have loved thee with an everlasting love," or with an eternal love, with a love that is

-- 476 --

without beginning and without end. This is the foundation of God's former kindness to them. This was the reason why God had drawn them to him to be his people, because he loved them before they were drawn. Their cleaving to God, and choosing him to be their God, and worshipping him, was not the cause that God loved them; but God loved them before, and that was the cause of their cleaving to him and choosing him. He loved them, and so drew them to him to be his people, and to acknowledge and serve him as their God. If God had not loved them first, they never would have followed him nor served him; for in themselves they were very much inclined to the worship of other gods, and to depart from the true God. So in Egypt they served other gods; as Joshua 24:14, "Put away your gods which your fathers served on the other side of the flood, and in Egypt." And Ezekiel 20:5–8,

And say unto them, Thus saith the Lord God; In the day when I chose Israel, and lifted up mine hand unto the seed of the house of Jacob, and made myself known unto them in the land of Egypt, when I lifted up mine hand unto them, saying, I am the Lord your God; in the day that I lifted up mine hand unto them, to bring them forth of the land of Egypt into a land that I had espied for them, flowing with milk and honey, which is the glory of all lands: Then said I unto them, Cast ye away every man the abominations of his eyes, and defile not yourselves with the idols of Egypt; I am the Lord your God. But they rebelled against me, and would not hearken unto me: they did not every man cast away the abominations of their eyes, neither did they forsake the idols of Egypt: then I said, I will pour out my fury upon them, to accomplish my anger against them in the midst of the land of Egypt.

This, viz. that God had loved them with an everlasting love, was the reason why he showed them such mercy so long ago, why he spoke to them of old. It was because his love to them was older, viz. from everlasting.

And that God loved them with an everlasting love, was the reason why God would show them such mercy hereafter; notwithstanding all their provocations wherewith they had provoked him to anger, and though God would execute such terrible judgments on them. Though the trouble and distress was dreadful that God would bring upon them, so that there was none like it; yet because his love to them was an everlasting love, they should be delivered out of it; as in [the] Jeremiah 30:7, "Alas! For that day is great, for there is none like it: it is even the time of Jacob's trouble; but he shall be saved out of it." Though their wound as to all creature help was incurable, as [the] Jeremiah 30:12–14

-- 477 --

Jeremiah 30:12–14, yet God would restore health unto them, as [the] Jeremiah 30:17.

Because God loved them with an everlasting love, therefore God would never utterly destroy Israel; as in [the] Jeremiah 30:11, "For I am with thee, saith the Lord, to save thee: though I make a full end of all nations whither I have scattered thee, yet will I not make a full end of thee."

DOCTRINE.

The love wherewith God has loved his saints, is an everlasting love.

That is,3 it is from eternity to eternity, as I have shown is the meaning of the text, and would now particularly show,4

I. That God's love to his saints is from eternity. And here I would show, first, that it is from eternity in its being; second, that it is from eternity in its foundation; and, third, that God's acts from eternity have been acts of love to his saints.

First. God's love to his saints has had being from all eternity. God often in his Word is setting [forth] how great his love is to his saints, how dear they are to him. But this love of his to them, he had before ever they had any being.

There it was in the heart of God of old. In former ages, thousands of years ago, in the ages before the flood, and when God created the world, there was that love; yea, and before the foundation of the world. And if we look back never so far, there is no beginning of it.

This love that God has to his saints that is thus everlasting, is not only general, as God did from all eternity love saints, i.e. loved their5 character; the qualifications of saints were what he naturally delighted in. But his love to the particular persons is from eternity. He did as it were know them by name, and set his love in such particular saints. And therefore God is said to have foreknown his saints; i.e. he from all eternity saw them particularly, and knew them as his own. Romans 8:29, "For whom he did foreknow, he also did Predestinate"; 1 Peter 1:2, "Elect according to the foreknowledge of God

-- 478 --

the Father"; 2 Timothy 2:19, "Nevertheless the foundation of God standeth sure, having this seal, The Lord knoweth them that are his." This foreknowledge is particular: he knows them as it were by name. Exodus 33:12, "I know thee by name, and thou hast also found grace in my sight"; and Jeremiah 1:5, "Before I formed thee in the belly I knew thee." And therefore their names are spoken of as being "written in the book of life" (Revelation 13:8). God wrote their names in that book from all eternity. There was the same great love to the saints before the world was, as there is after they have a being, and they are brought home. There was not only some love, but the love was the same, and to the same degree. It is not altered. The love of God to the saints is from all eternity immutably the same.

There was the same love in God the Father, and the same in Jesus Christ. The high priests of old had the names of the tribes of Israel written on their breastplate, which was to typify that Christ has the names of the saints, who are the spiritual Israel, written on his heart; and those names were written there from all eternity. God the Father and the Son, both from all eternity, beheld them and loved them.

Second. The love of God to his saints is not only from eternity in its being, but in its foundation; i.e. the love of God to his saints has not its foundation in anything temporal. There are some that will allow that the love of God to the saints in its being is from eternity, but not in its foundation; but hold that it has its foundation in God's foresight of that which is temporal, as particularly in the foresight of their holiness and good works. They suppose that the saints' faith, and repentance, and obedience is the foundation of God's love to them, and that God loves the saints from eternity no otherwise than as he foresees that they will in time believe, and repent, and live holy lives. So that though they allow the love itself in its being to be eternal, yet assert that 'tis the foresight of something temporal that is the foundation of it. But the love of God to his saints has not its foundation in anything temporal. The ground of the love is eternal as the love itself. He don't love them from eternity, because he foresees that they will believe, and repent, and the like. The ground of his eternal love is not to be sought for in the saints, but in God's own heart; that God loves the saints is from himself, and not from them. His love is a free and sovereign love, and is from his own sovereign good pleasure, as we are taught. Ephesians 1:4–5, "According as he hath chosen us in him before the foundation of the world, that we should be holy and without blame before him in love: having predestinated us unto the adoption of children [by Jesus Christ] to himself, according to the good pleasure of his will." The love of God towards his people is self-moved. Deuteronomy 7:7–8, "The Lord did not set his love

-- 479 --

upon you, nor choose you, because you were more in number than any people; for ye were the fewest of all people: but because the Lord loved you."

The faith and holiness of the saints is so far from being the foundation of the eternal love of God that 'tis the fruit of it. God has loved them from all eternity, and that is the reason that he has given them faith and holiness, and has brought them home to himself truly to believe in, and love, and fear, and serve God; as in the verse of the text, "I have loved thee with an everlasting love: therefore with lovingkindness have I drawn thee."

Third. God's acts which God hath done even from eternity, have been acts of love. The things that God hath done before ever they had a being, have been acts of love; even the acts that have been done in time, and in those acts that are in their own nature eternal. And,

1. I would show how that God's acts that have been done since the beginning of time, [have] been acts of love to his saints. God's providential dealings with them before they were converted were so; and not only so, but God's acts that were done before they were born, yea, in former ages of the world. How many things might be mentioned, but I shall now only particularize in two things, viz.

(1) Christ coming into the world and laying down his life. This though it was done many ages since, yet it was done from love to the saints that are now living. It was from Christ's great love to the saints that are now living, and to the saints that are yet to be born, as well as to those that then were or had been before. It was from love to them in God the Father, that he sent his Son into the world to die. And it was from the great love of Christ to them, that he came down from heaven, and was made flesh, and dwelt here in a mean and low condition, and gave himself a sacrifice to divine justice. Every saint may say, as the apostle Paul does in Galatians 2:20, 6 "He loved me, and gave himself for me."

(2) God made heaven for them in the beginning of the world. God in the creation of the world had a respect to his elect. He made the whole world—sun, moon, and stars, heaven and earth—out of love to them; as is evident by Psalms 136:6–9, "To him that stretched out the earth above the waters: for his mercy endureth forever. To him that made great lights: for his mercy endureth forever. The sun to rule by day: for his mercy endureth forever. The moon and stars to rule by night: [for his mercy endureth forever]." By this it appears that God's works of creation were works of mercy to his people.

-- 480 --

But especially God's creating the highest heaven, that most excellent and glorious part of the creation, was from love to his elect. When God created heaven, he made it, and prepared it, and gloriously adapted it on purpose to be a blissful habitation and an eternal possession for them. And therefore Christ at the end of the world will invite his saints to come and possess the kingdom prepared for them from the beginning of the world. Matthew 25:34, "Come, ye blessed of my Father, inherit the kingdom prepared for you from the foundation of the world." Thus God has exercised love to his saints in the things that he has done from the beginning of the world.

2. God hath exercised love to his saints in what was done from eternity, particularly in the covenant of redemption that was made between the Father and the Son. That was a covenant that the Father and the Son from all eternity entered into with each other. God the Father, from love to them, did from all eternity appoint Jesus Christ his Son to be their security. 1 Peter 1:20, "Who verily was ordained before the foundation of the world, but was manifest in these last times for you." And Christ from all eternity, from his great love to them, undertook to stand for their security, and to die for them. Christ became engaged to the Father to become incarnate, to go through such great labors and extreme sufferings in these conditions; that such and such particular persons might be redeemed, might have all their sins pardoned, and might have eternal life, who were the objects of his eternal love. And God the Father did in that covenant of redemption, give such and such persons by name to Jesus Christ from his eternal love to them.

And 'tis this that Christ has reference to when he speaks of those that God the Father had given him. John 17:9, "I pray not for the world, but for them which thou hast given me; for they are thine." And God did from all eternity promise to his Son that he would give eternal life to such and such persons; and therefore the blessings that believers receive are said to be given them in Christ before the foundation of the world. 2 Timothy 1:9, "Who hath saved us, and called us with an holy calling, not according to our works, but according to his own purpose and grace, which was given us in Christ before the world began." And God gives Christ power over all mankind, that he bestow that eternal life to those that were given him in that covenant of redemption. John 17:2, "As thou hast given him power over all flesh, that he should give eternal life to as many as thou hast given him." Thus the love that [God] has to his saints is everlasting, as 'tis from eternity. I proceed now,7

-- 481 --

II. To show that 'tis also everlasting as 'tis to eternity. Here I would, first, show that the love of God to his saints is everlasting in this respect; and, second, what assurance the saints have of it.

First. I would show how the love of God to his saints will be to eternity. 'Tis so in two respects, viz. in the being of it, and in the fruits of it.

1. The love of God to his saints is to all eternity in the being; that love towards them, which hath been in God's heart from all eternity, will remain there to all eternity. The saints in themselves are poor changeable creatures: their state and circumstances in this world is very changeable. Men's love towards their earthly friends oftentimes changes with the circumstances of those that are beloved by them: while they are in prosperity, their love holds to them, and they continue to be their friends; but when they come to be in adversity, their love fails. Proverbs 14:20, "The poor is hated of his own neighbor: but the rich hath many friends." And [the] Proverbs 19:7, "All the brethren of the poor do hate him: how much more do his friends go far from him? he pursueth them with words, yet they are wanting to him."

But such changes won't change or alter the love of God to his saints. They are liable to feel changes: they may pass through great and sore affliction, may be brought very low, so as to be generally hated and despised by men. They may come down from earthly prosperity to embrace dunghills, and may become the object of the contempt of men, hated and persecuted even to death; as was the case of Job, "But now they that are younger than I have me in derision, whose fathers I would have disdained to have set with the dogs of my flock" (Job 30:1); and Heman, "Lover and friend hast thou put far from me, and mine acquaintance into darkness" (Psalms 88:18).8 But yet God's love to them won't fail. Romans 8:35, "Who shall separate us from the love of Christ? shall tribulation, or distress, or persecution, or famine, or nakedness, or peril, or sword?"; and [the] Romans 8:38–39, "For I am persuaded, that neither death, nor life, nor angels, nor principalities, nor powers, nor things present, nor things to come, nor height, nor depth, nor any other creature, shall be able to separate us from the love of God, which is in Christ Jesus our Lord."

The saints are not only very changeable in their circumstances, but also in their persons and in their hearts. Man is a changeable creature, soon changes his mind, changes his judgment, and changes his disposition. The saints if left to themselves, they would be very changeable in their love

-- 482 --

to God. They would soon lose it all and turn enemies, as Adam our first father did. And though God won't suffer their love to him utterly to fail, yet they show themselves to be exceeding changeable in their respect to God, as to the frames they are in: atone time warm, and another time cold: at one time zealous and considerably engaged; at another [time] dull and dead as a stone: at one time taken up with thoughts and meditations about God and Christ, and desires and affections towards them; at another time full of worldliness and carnality: at one time in the exercise of the sweet and lovely Christian graces of humility, and love, and submission; at another time there rises up all manner of corruption, pride and covetousness, and envy and willfulness, and revenge.

And even the saints, when God leaves them, are apt to be swayed by little temptations, and little things will draw 'em away, and cause them to act strangely towards God. Thus changeable are the hearts of the saints towards God; but God's heart towards them is unchangeable. His love always remains and never decays, nor is subject to any mutation at all.

This was exemplified in Christ and his disciples, when Christ was on earth. How weak, and feeble, and easily shaken were they—one while they believed; at another time they were full of doubts—so that Christ has often occasion to cry out of them, "O ye of little faith." They were good friends to Christ, but yet how dull were they sometimes; and that even when Christ was going to die for them, and was even in his agony, and did but desire that they would watch with them; yet they were dull, and fell asleep. And when Christ waked them, and more earnestly desired them to watch with him but one hour, when his soul was sorrowful unto death; yet they soon fell asleep again. And how cowardly and unsteady did they show themselves, when Christ was apprehended. They all forsook him, and fled, and durst not appear in his cause or on his behalf. Then they forsook him in adversity. And Peter, when he sees Christ bound and in the hands of his enemies, durst not own him, but denied him, even with oaths and curses. But yet all this while Christ's love remained constant towards the disciples. There was no unsteadiness there. John 13:1, "Now before the feast of the passover, when Jesus knew that his hour was come that he should depart out of this world, having loved his men which were in the world, he loved them to the end." Thus God's love to his saints is9 to eternity in the being of it.

2. It is also to eternity in the fruits of it. The first visible fruits of it are those that are given in conversion: the saving benefits that are then bestowed,

-- 483 --

and those that always endure. There is then that given to them, that shall never be taken away from them. Luke 10:42, "Mary hath chosen the better part, which shall not be taken away from her." Then is given a spirit of holiness, which shall everlastingly remain. That spirit shall be in them a fountain of living water, "springing up into everlasting life" (John 4:14).

That spiritual life that is then infused, is what shall never be extinct. John 6:50–51, "This is the bread that cometh down from heaven, that a man may eat there of, and not die. I am the living bread which came down from heaven: if any man eat of this bread, he shall live forever."

That future blessedness, which is the fruit of this love of God to them, is what has no end: it never will cease. As God's love will always remain, so shall the manifestations of it: they shall always continue in the enjoyment of that love. Thus I have briefly shown how the love of God to his saints is to eternity. I proceed now, in the

Second place, to show what assurances the saints have of this, or what grounds they have to be assured that God's love to them will be to eternity.

1. The immutability of God is what they may be assured from, that God's love to them will be to eternity. It is declared and abundantly manifested of God that they are the objects of his love: God hath manifested a great love to them. As God is unchangeable, therefore so his love will be unchangeable.

It was the especial occasion of God's at first declaring his name to be Jehovah, or "I am that I am," to signify the immutability of his love to his people; as Exodus 3:14 and Exodus 6:3, to encourage the children of Israel in Egypt that he would not forsake them, seeing he had once set his love on them, and designed and promised to their forefathers to make them his people.

2. The faithfulness of God. God has by many great, and precious, and positive promises engaged never to forsake his people; and he has confirmed it from time to time by his oath; as we may see in the Genesis 22:16–18; and so Psalms 89:3–4, and Psalms 89:35; and Psalms 110:4.

This the Apostle takes notice of. Hebrews 6:17–18, "Wherein God, willing more abundantly to show unto the heirs of promise the immutability of his counsel, confirmed it by an oath: that by two immutable things, where in it was impossible for God to lie, we might have a strong consolation, who have fled for refuge to lay hold upon the hope set before us." God has engaged this to his saints in covenant, which covenant is "an everlasting covenant, and ordered in all things, and sure" (2 Samuel 23:5).

Thus the faithfulness of God is engaged, that his love shall last forever.

-- 484 --

Psalms 89:33, "Nevertheless my lovingkindness will I not utterly take from him, nor suffer my faithfulness to fail."

God is a God of truth. Every word of God is pure: 'tis impossible one jot or tittle of his word should fail. God is the truthful God: 'tis impossible for God to lie.

3. The saints need not fear that God's love towards them will be mutable, because the foundation of it is not a mutable thing. If the foundation of God's love to them was their goodness, their being true to him, and continuing to do as they should do, or to be as they should be; it would stand on a very mutable, uncertain foundation, and the saints might well be afraid. But it is not so. God's love don't depend on their constancy: it is not built on that foundation. It don't stand on anything in them.

The foundation of God's love to his saints is something higher than that: it is something above the reach of changes. God's love to them is not founded upon anything here in this mutable world; but 'tis something infinitely above all these things, something unchangeable, viz. the eternal sovereign will and good pleasure of God; so that the saints need not fear it will fail.

4. The saints may be assured that God's love to them will be everlasting, because it hath already been from eternity. That love that is but of late beginning, may soon come to an end; but that which has been from eternity, will and must be to eternity. The love of men one towards another oftentimes grows up suddenly; and when men are soon hot, they are commonly soon cold.

But the love of God to his saints is no sudden, upstart thing; but it has been in the heart unchanged from all eternity. This shows the constancy and immutability of it, and may put it out of doubt with those that are the objects of it, that it never will fail.

This the Apostle uses as an argument of the faithfulness of God's promises. Titus 1:2, "In hope of eternal life, which God, that cannot lie, promised before the world began."

5. Another thing that may give strong assurance to the saints of this, is that they have one to stand for them that God doth necessarily love, even Jesus Christ. Jesus Christ is God's own Son, the same in substance with the Father; and God necessarily loves him. 'Tis impossible but that God should love his Son. 'Tis as impossible as that God should cease to love himself; for he is the same in essence with himself. Therefore this may assure the saints that God's love to them will be everlasting, for

(1) They may be assured that Christ will forever stand for them, because he has died for them. If Christ don't stand for his saints, he will disappoint

-- 485 --

himself, and frustrate his own end in dying; and we may he sure he won't frustrate himself in that which cost him so dear.

(2) If Christ stands for them, God will never cast them off; because if he should so do, he would disappoint his Son of his end in dying. God sent him into the world to die; and seeing he has died, and undergone such terrible and amazing suffering, God, who infinitely loves him, won't go to disappoint him and frustrate him in his end.

This was the end that he had in mind in dying, to make those that should believe in him everlastingly happy in God's love. This was Christ's dependence. He undertook to die, and did actually undergo death in this dependence, viz. that those that he died for should {be everlastingly happy}. The Son of God, who presently knows the Father, thought the promise of the Father sure ground enough for him so far to depend upon and trust to, as to venture to undergo these terrible sufferings in a dependence on it.

And seeing he has undergone it, God won't frustrate his Son of his end, that he should fail of that which was the joy that was set before him, but he shall surely obtain it. He will give his Son the success that he desired and depended on. And he will give this honor in his mediatorial office, as that he shall actually bring those that come to him to eternal, never-ending glory in the enjoyment of his love.1

[APPLICATION.]

Use I, Of Instr.

First. Hence the saints may learn how greatly God has distinguished them from others, whom he never set his heart upon. When God from eternity mentioned your name, their name was not mentioned.2 In the covenant that {was made between the} Father and Son {from all eternity}, they were left out, no notice [was] taken of them in that eternal transaction. Their names were not written in [the] book of life. [They were] passed by: many great men, {many} rich men; many princes and nobles; many men of great abilities and learning, and that excelled in the endowments of nature, and have had a most genteel education; [many] gallant persons. And if the world were to choose, those would be the chosen men on the earth. But God passed by those {great men}. Their names were

-- 486 --

never written on Christ's heart. When God made the world, heaven was not made for them; and when God sent Christ into the world, {he did not die for them}.

Passing by multitudes of those, the infinite and eternal mercy and love of God found you out as it were in a corner. Your name was mentioned {in the covenant that was made between the Father and Son}. When God made heaven, he had respect to you {and your salvation}. When Christ came into the world, it was to lay down his life and spill his precious blood to save your soul, a poor miserable creature. That it may be, [you] are but little taken notice of by men, make no great show or figure in the world, are vastly below many that are passed by in natural understanding, in knowledge and learning, and in earthly honor and dignity, and have but little of the world.

Many have been passed by that have excelled you in moral endowments, lived more exactly, {and have} never so much guilt; those were not mentioned. But yet your name was mentioned, though a poor sinful {creature, little taken notice of by men}.

God set none of his love upon them; but they were reprobated to eternal misery and destruction. Romans 9:13, "Jacob have I loved, but Esau have I hated."

Many have been passed by, [the] far greater part of the world; and there are but few {chosen}. 'Tis but a small number on whom God did thus from eternity set his love.

Second. Hence we may learn the freeness of the love of God to his saints. This doctrine does most clearly teach and show that the love of God is absolutely free and sovereign, and not for anything in the saints, any loveliness of theirs; not for any of their works, or anything that they have done; that no price of theirs has purchased it, because God loved them before even they had done anything, or had any being. And that it could not be, not only not for the sake of anything in them, but neither for the sake of anything that others have done. It could not [be] for the sake of their parents or ancestors, because God also loved them before ever they had a being. God's love to them was before their being, or the being of their ancestors, or any creature both in the order of time and also in the order of nature; for it was from everlasting. God set his love upon them before the foundation of the world. The love of God to them was from everlasting, as has been shown both in the being and also in the foundation of it; so that the foundation of God's love could not be any of their works, or any of their lovely qualifications. If we look for the foundation of the love of God in anything short of eternity, we shall fail of finding of it.

-- 487 --

Let the saints consider this freeness of the love of God, and seek earnestly and diligently that they may love, and bless, and praise God accordingly.

Use II may be of Self-Exam.3 If it be so, that may put all upon strictly examining themselves whether they be saints or not, that so they may know whether they have been the objects of this eternal love of God. We can't open book of God's eternal decrees, but only as God himself opens it and reveals what he hath purposed from eternity, by the event and fruit of his purposes. Men can't tell whether they are the objects of God's eternal love or no, till they are either converted or damned; but either of these will make it manifest. He that is converted and is become a saint, if he knows that, may know himself to be the object of God's eternal love. Therefore make diligent inquiry with respect to this matter. There are many of you that hope that you are saints. If you be so indeed, the doctrine shows that you are happy and blessed persons beyond all expression and conception. But the thing is to be satisfied upon good and sure grounds, that you are indeed a saint.

But I shall insist upon but one thing here as an evidence of your being a saint, and so of your being the object of the everlasting love of God, viz. love to god.

It is declared in Proverbs 8:17 by the Son of God, "I love them that love me." God don't love the saints, because they love him; but on the contrary, they love him, because he first loved them; as 1 John 4:19. The love of God to them is the reason why God has given them love to him.

But yet the saints' love to God gives the evidence of his eternal love to them. They can't know that God has eternally loved them, till they come to love him. But then great care must be taken to distinguish the true love of God from the counterfeits of it. Hypocrites sometimes have affections that they call love to God that are hypocritical and vain. The true love of God makes it evident that persons are the children of God two ways.

First. Sometimes the strong and lively exercises of love to God, do give a kind of immediate and intuitive evidence of our relation to God. Divine love is the bond by which the soul of the saint is united to God; and sometimes when this divine love is strong and lively, this bond of union can be seen as it were intuitively. The saint sees that he is united to God, and so is God's; for he sees and feels the union between God and his soul. He sees clearly and certainly that divine love that does, evidently beyond all contradictions

-- 488 --

or exception, unite his soul to God. He knows there is an union; for he sees and feels it so strong that he can't question it or doubt of it. 1 John 4:18, "There is no fear in love; for perfect love casts out fear." How can the saint doubt but that he stands in a childlike relation to God, when he plainly sees a childlike union between God and his soul?

He that has such strong exercise of a divine and holy love to God, he knows at the same time that this is not from himself. This that he feels so strong in his own heart, brings its own evidence with it that it is from God. It is a childlike union of his heart to God that God himself gives. And therefore in seeing and feeling this union, he sees that God has taken his heart, and has united it as a child to him; so that he does as it were see that he is a child of God. This seems to be that "bearing witness with our spirit, that we are the children of God" (Romans 8:16); which in Scripture is called "the Spirit of God." The Spirit of God gives these holy motions, and exercises childlike love to God, that naturally inclines the heart to look on God as his Father. 'Tis called the "spirit of adoption, whereby we cry, Abba, Father," [in the] foregoing verse. God in giving this spirit does manifest himself to be our Father; for the soul does as it were feel itself to be God's child, as it feels the strong and clear exercises of childlike dispositions and affections, and so as it were feels a childlike union, and sees a childlike relation to God.

This is called in Scripture "the earnest of the Spirit." This is that white stone and new name, which no man knows but he that receives it, that we read of (Revelation 2:17).

But love to God does not give this intuitive evidence of their being the children of God in all the saints, and, it may be, in none of the saints at all times. 'Tis only at some special seasons of the strong and lively exercises of love to God that God sometimes grants to some of his people. And therefore,

Second. Another way by which love to God is in evidence of our good estate, is by reflection on it, and examining the nature and properties of it, and the way of its working. And there are several properties that I shall mention by which the true love of God is distinguished from counterfeits.

1. He that truly loves God loves him for the excellency and glory of his nature.

2. Those things in God that are chiefly offensive to natural men, are what those that do truly love God do delight in, and love God for.

3. None but they that do truly love God do love him as their chief good.

4. He that truly loves God, he renounces himself for God's sake.

5. They that do truly love God do keep his commandments. True love

-- 489 --

is not merely loving in word and in tongue, but in deed and in truth. The Scripture way of examining the tree is by the fruit. We are there told that men are to be known by their fruits; that "every good tree bringeth forth men good fruit, but a corrupt tree bringeth forth evil fruit"; that "men don't gather grapes of thorns, or figs of thistles." We are there told that he [that] "receives seed into good ground is he that hears the word, and understands it," and brings forth the fruit of it. The tares were not distinguished from the wheat, till the fruit was brought forth; but we are told, Matthew 13:26 that when the blade was sprung up and brought forth fruit, there appeared the tares also. Christ in the John 15, tells us of two sorts of branches in him: one that bears fruit, which shall be purged or pruned, that it may bring forth more fruit; and another that bears no fruit, that shall be cut off and taken away; and tells us in the John 15:8, if we bear much fruit, that we shall be his disciples. The Scriptures teach that it is not hearing the Word, but doing of it that is the thing will be looked [for]. And we are told that they that hear and do not, build [their] house on sand; but they that hear and do them, build their house on rock; and that "a good man out of the good treasure of his heart bringeth forth good things: and an evil man out of the evil treasure bringeth forth evil things"; and compares visible Christians to various sorts of ground: that "which drinks in the rain that comes oft upon it, and brings forth herbs meet for them by whom it is dressed, receives blessing from God. But that which bears thorns and briers is rejected, and is nigh unto cursing; whose end is to be burned."

And Christ forewarns us that not they that say, "Lord, Lord, have we not prophesied in thy name?" and that they that shall say, "Lord, Lord open to us," are found workers of iniquity. They shall be bid to depart from him, as those that he don't know. And as to the love of God, Christ has very expressly and fully warned us not to take anything for true love but that which appears by keeping his commandments. John 14:21, "He that hath my commandments, and keepeth them, he it is that loveth me"; John 14:23, "If any man love me, he will keep my words"; John 14:24, "He that loveth me not keepeth not my sayings"; John 14:15, "If ye love me, keep my commandments"; and John 15:14, "Ye are my friends, if ye do whatsoever I command you."

Use III may be of Consolation to the godly. If you are one who, by what has been laid down in the preceding use, appears to be a true saint, and one that loves God in sincerity, this doctrine affords matter of unspeakable joy and comfort for you; and that on these accounts:

First. That you are the objects of the love of so great and glorious a being. 'Tis a great thing. They may well be pronounced happy that he hath

-- 490 --

set his love upon. That is the eternal being, the first and the last, the great creator, mighty possessor of heaven and earth that sits king forever and rules over all, king of angels and men; that blessed and only potentate before whom all nations are as nothing; who humbles himself to behold the things that are in heaven; who is the eternal object of the praises and hallelujahs of angels in heaven. Surely they are happy indeed that are beloved by such an one.

Second. How great a thing is it that God should set his love upon you from eternity.

Third. 'Tis [a] matter of great consolation to you, that you have so sure a foundation for your eternal happiness as the everlasting love of God; [an] unchangeable love of which you have this evidence, that it has remained unaltered already from eternity; [that it is] from eternity in its foundation; [that it] don't stand on a temporal foundation, such a poor, unstable foundation as your moral qualifications.
