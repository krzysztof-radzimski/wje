FALSE LIGHT AND TRUE

After a paragraph of explication, a doctrine "in much the same words" as his text from II Corinthians, and a brief argument for it, Edwards devotes the remaining two-thirds of this two-part sermon not only to Satan's transformation into an angel of light, the subject at hand, but to an extended definition of spiritual light, a subject he preached upon in Northampton almost a year earlier and reworked for publication in Boston nine months later as A Divine and Supernatural Light. There, he contrasts spiritual light—"supernatural light" appears only in the title—with natural light, the inferior light of the unregenerate that fosters convictions of sin and misery, impressions upon the imagination, "new truths" from inspiration, and shallow affections for religious things. Here, he contrasts true light with false, the "counterfeit" light of Satan that mimics not only the saving light of God but also the natural light of man, particularly the workings of the imagination and the words of inspiration. Impressions falsify the experience of spiritual reality, Edwards contends, because images of outward things as perceived by "bodily senses" are superficial and transient, seated as they are in the imagination, not the understanding and the heart; and if "sometimes the imagination may help the heart," nothing can "alter" it but spiritual discovery, the "sweet, and glorious, and delightful" sense of the heart. New truths falsify the experience of Scripture, since the canon is complete, and the words of inspiration, such as the prophets and apostles spoke, are already transcribed; and though spiritual light opens God's written word to the understanding, it cannot reveal what is not there. But false light is at an even farther remove from spiritual light than natural light, for it discovers "nothing to the soul" nor convinces it of "the truth and reality of divine things." Lost in the fall from grace, it cannot feign holiness or loveliness since both are contrary to the devil's evil nature. It shuns humility and charity for puffery and selfishness, and fails of habit and permanence, a light more like "a comet or blazing star" than the abiding, if at times clouded, sun. And it breeds hypocrisy, a monster "all head and no hands or feet, or else all hands and feet and no head." If Satan had appeared to Eve as the cruel and malicious spirit he is, the wild beast ranging

-- 121 --

"our streets" instead of the wily serpent of Eden, "mankind might have stood in the favor of God to this very day."

"God's ape," some call him, for like God he requires worship and erects temples exacts sacrifices and invites conversions, inspires prophets and indites scriptures. He commonly poses as the "perfectly innocent and holy" seraph he once was, putting on "a saint-like show" in perverse imitation of the divine teacher, counselor, and comforter, "an angel of darkness." As a false teacher, he plies doctrines of salvation "under color of divine truth," convincing Muslims that Mohammed is a "true prophet," Catholics that they serve the interests and kingdom of Christ, and Quakers that their inner light is indeed the Holy Spirit, and cites (and abuses) Scripture in confirmation. As a false counselor, he appears at once holy and kind, persuading sinners to corrupt their duties through cunning biblical glosses and misleading inferences, as well as persuading them to neglect their duties to secret prayer and communion, for instance, since others who have been converted have done so. As a false comforter, he flatters men with counterfeit grace, with counterfeit faith and counterfeit love, with counterfeit repentance, charity, humility, and thankfulness, "imitating the discoveries of God's spirit" and the work of Jesus, the true angel of light, "'the light of the world.'"

Truth and knowledge are often compared to light in the Scriptures, Edwards reminds us, ignorance and error to darkness. Though he transforms himself into an angel of light, Satan labors "to blind men," to lead them into darkness.

* * *

Alexander B. Grosart copied out this duodecimo manuscript of twenty leaves on the same day, March 31, 1854, as he had another, but unlike sermon no. 314, preached almost half a year earlier, False Light and True shows his hand. Although neither manuscript offers substantial difficulties in reading, Grosart's emendations crowd these pages, as he alters words, deletes them, inserts them, or simply overwrites or strikes them. For all that, this sermon, like the other, was never published in the nineteenth century and, given the lack of repreaching symbols in this manuscript, like the other, was probably never preached from again. So once more on a summer sabbath in Northampton—"July 1734" appears in the upper right-hand corner of the first leaf—the congregation heard Edwards on the divine supernatural light. This time, amid the celebration of the gift of grace, he warned them repeatedly of deceptions lurking even there.

FALSE LIGHT AND TRUE
For Satan himself is transformed into an angel of light. 2 Corinthians 11:14

One principal occasion of the Apostle's writing this epistle, was what he had been informed of concerning some false teachers that were come among them, that endeavored what they could to draw them off from the doctrine the Apostle had taught them, and to that end did to their utmost to vilify his person, and set up themselves as apostles of Christ in opposition to Paul and other true apostles. The Apostle speaks of these in the verse next before the text: "For such are false apostles, deceitful workers, transforming themselves into the apostles of Christ." "And no marvel," says the Apostle in the text and 2 Corinthians 11:14; "for Satan himself is transformed into an angel of light. Therefore it is no great thing if his ministers also be transformed as the ministers of righteousness; whose end shall be according to their works." In like manner, as these false teachers pretended to be the true apostles of Christ, and pretended to be sent by him, and to act as Christ's servants to reveal his truth and promote his interest; so the devil himself, as bad and as much of an enemy to God as he is, yet puts on the disguise of an holy angel, who are pure and spotless, and are not enemies to God, but his servants and prime ministers of his kingdom. The doctrine that I would observe is in much the same words.

DOCTRINE.

Satan oftentimes transforms himself into an angel of light.

The devil to gain his purposes poses under many transformations, and puts on many disguises. He manages all by deceit. If he acted openly and without disguise, and appeared what he is indeed, viz. a wicked, malicious, cruel spirit, he would frustrate his own ends, which are to oppose God,

-- 123 --

and to seek the ruin of men. If he appeared in his true shape, others would be aware of him, and would be upon their guard against him, and would not so readily fall in with his motions, and be influenced and led by him. When the devil first began his work in this world in tempting our first parent, it was under a transformation. He transformed himself into the shape of a serpent, or else actually entered into one of those animals, that he looked upon to be the best disguise for his purpose at that time. So he frequently transforms himself into many other forms, as he thinks will best suit his occasion; and commonly puts on a saint-like show, and appears as a good and holy angel; for the devil is a great hypocrite and father of hypocrites. Satan once was an angel of light: he was perfectly innocent and holy, a bright and glorious seraph. And though now he be fallen, and is become an angel of darkness, yet he often finds it for his purpose to put on the appearance of what he once was.

I would take notice of several instances wherein he doth thus.

I. He doth so in doing the part of a teacher in the many false doctrines that he teaches in the world. Satan takes upon him to be a teacher, and the far greater part of the world subject themselves to his instructions. He is exceeding busy in the world to teach men one false doctrine or other.

Thus the whole antichristian kingdom, they have their doctrines from the devil. The vast Mahometan empire has no other teacher than Satan; the Jews, they have the doctrines that they receive from him; and so the many sorts of heretics that are in the world.

Satan in teaching these false doctrines transforms himself into an angel of light in the following respects.

First. He teaches those false and wicked doctrines under color of divine truth. God improves1 the angels as his ministers some way or other to guide and instruct men, and therefore ministers of the gospel are called angels (Revelation 1:20). The seven golden candlesticks are seven churches; and the seven stars are the angels of the seven churches, that is, the ministers or teachers of the churches. They are called angels, because they are teachers of divine truth.

Satan transforms himself into an angel of light in that he pretends to diffuse abroad the light of truth, to give men the light of the knowledge of the truth. Truth and knowledge are often in Scripture compared to light, as ignorance and error are compared to darkness. All Satan's real design is to blind men, and lead 'em into error; but he doth it under pretense of teaching them the truth.

-- 124 --

He pretends that the doctrines that he teaches are from God, and that they are the doctrines which God hath revealed to the world. He don't tell them that the doctrines that he teaches are from hell, as indeed they are but pretends that they are from heaven, are holy doctrines; and that all [are] bound to receive them with the greatest veneration as sacred truth; and that it is a great crime, and will be highly provoking to God, for any to call them in question.

Thus he makes the Mahometans believe that what he teaches them about Mahomet is from God; that the Alcoran is the book of God; that the whole of it was revealed to him by God; and that Mahomet was a true prophet of God. And he makes them think that they, in believing as they do,2 are accepted of God of all people in the world. So the doctrine that he teaches the papists, 'Tis under color of being from the true God and his infallible spirit, [under] pretense of promoting [the] interests of Christ and his kingdom. So what he teaches the Quakers from the Holy Spirit of God, which he makes them believe dwells in them. And the innumerable heresies there are, he makes them believe that they are all divine doctrines. And how many false and hellish doctrines are there that he teaches men under pretense of its being taught in the Scriptures! He comes as a preacher of the word of God, with his places of Scripture to confirm what he teaches.

Satan makes abundance of use of the Scriptures to establish and confirm his doctrines, though he greatly abuses it, and wrests it from its true meaning. This was a practice that he made use of so long ago as the apostles' times, "which they that are unlearned and unstable wrest" (2 Peter 3:16).3

Second. He teaches those doctrines under pretense of seeking their salvation. He teaches those doctrines that have the most direct tendency to their everlasting ruin; but he hides that from them, and makes them believe that they are the only doctrines by which they can be saved; that none but they that believe them shall go to heaven; and that those that believe otherwise shall be damned.

Herein he transforms himself into an angel of light, or makes an appearance of acting as a good angel. The good angels, they seek men's salvation: they are sent forth to be ministers to them that shall be the "heirs of salvation" (Hebrews 1:14). He makes as though he would guide men in the ways of salvation, when indeed he is leading of them down to the pit of everlasting misery.

-- 125 --

II. When he loth the part of a counsellor, Satan not only takes upon him to be a teacher to instruct men what to believe, but of a counsellor to direct them in their practice.

And in this also he transforms himself into an angel of light; and that two ways: viz. first as he puts on a show of holiness; and also, second, a show of goodness or benevolence in those wicked counsels that he gives men. The good angels are angels of light, [and] differ from the evil angels in these two things; viz. that they are holy spirits, in that they love God, and have respect to his commands, where the evil angels are wicked spirits; and also in this, in that they are good, benevolent spirits, full of kindness to men, seeking the welfare of men; the devils, they are malicious, cruel spirits.

But Satan in the counsels that he gives men, transforms himself into angel of light in both these respects: he puts on a disguise of holiness, and also of kindness to men.

First. He, in the counsels he gives men, puts on a disguise of holiness; and that two ways.

1. In that he counsels men to sin under pretense of duty. When Satan would tempt men to sin, this is a stratagem he oftentimes makes use, viz. to persuade men that 'Tis their duty. Thus oftentimes when he counsels men to a violent pursuit after the world, he suggests to them that 'Tis more than their duty. They ought to take care to provide for themselves and for their families. He calls their anxiousness and eagerness after the world a prudent care.

So he often dissuades men from parting with some of their estates for the benefit of their neighbor in want, or for some public service, under pretense of duty. He tells them that 'Tis their duty to be frugal and saving of what they have. He calls that bountifulness and liberality, which is indeed their duty by the names of profuseness and prodigality, and so represents it as sinful. So sometimes he persuades men to set up their own wills in any affair, in opposition to others, under pretense of conscience. So sometimes he counsels men to be contentious and quarrelsome, and makes them the means of fomenting a great deal of strife, under pretense of holy zeal. And many other like ways has Satan of tempting men to sin under pretense of their obeying God's commands.

2. He oftentimes puts on a disguise of holiness in his temptation, in that makes use of Scripture in his temptation. Thus the devil makes use of Scripture in his tempting Christ. Matthew 4:6, "And he saith unto him, If thou be the Son of God, cast thyself down: for it is written, He shall give his angels charge, and in their hands they shall bear thee up, lest at any time thou dash thy bot against a stone."

-- 126 --

So he often abuses the Holy Scriptures to tempt men by putting his false and deceitful glosses upon them, or drawing false inferences from them.

Thus the devil oftentimes makes use of Scripture to tempt men to discouragement and despair. He, by some wrong interpretation or application of some awful texts of Scripture, makes men think that there is no hope for them, and that it is not worth the while for them to seek salvation, and so thence tempts them to leave off seeking.

So the devil oftentimes makes use of Scripture to flatter men, and to harden them in their sins. He makes use of the declarations of infinite mercy that there are in the word of God, to tempt men to presumption and make 'em the more bold in sin. To the same purpose, he oftentimes makes use of the instances of the falls of the saints that we have an account of in Scripture, to harden them in their sins.

Second. Satan transforms himself into an angel of light in counselling men, in that he puts on a disguise and pretense of kindness to them. He often will lead men into by paths with that, that this is the likeliest way to their spiritual good. Thus when persons are under convictions and exercise of mind about the state of their souls, Satan is ordinarily very busy with them with his counsel and advice. He is always ready at hand upon every difficulty to whisper them in the ear, and to direct them to this or that as the most prudent course, and what will be most for their good. None [is] so forward to give counsel in such cases.

Thus Satan often tempts them to neglect such and such duties with that, that if they do them, they shall only provoke God the more and increase their own guilt. Thus he sometimes counsels persons to neglect the duty of secret prayer, and to absent themselves from the Lord's Supper. So sometimes Satan advises persons when under difficulties to keep their difficulties to themselves, by no means to make them known.

And sometimes it is to be feared that persons under conviction are tempted to allow of wicked thoughts, blasphemous risings of heart against God, and very envious and malicious thoughts against their neighbors that are converted; for the devil tells them they will be in the more likely way to be converted for it, because others that have been converted have been so. They tell of many wicked and blasphemous thoughts they have found within themselves, a desperate, quarrelling spirit; and therefore they look upon it that this is the way, that when persons are so, then they are in a likely way to be converted. They look upon it [as] necessary in order to see their own hearts. And hence Satan tempts them to humor such a disposition, when they feel it rising within 'em, that so they might be in a good way to be converted.

-- 127 --

And many other ways does Satan lead persons into snares, and in ways that tend to their own ruin, under pretense of leading of them in the likeliest way to their own good. Thus Satan transforms himself into an angel of light when he acts the part of a counsellor.

III. Satan transforms himself {into an angel of light} in doing [the] part of a comforter by flattering men in a false light, instead of spiritual and saving light. There are counterfeit graces and a counterfeit light that the devil oftentimes flatters men in, and influences them to depend upon, instead of true grace and the true saving light of God's spirit. Every grace has its counterfeit. As there is a counterfeit faith and a counterfeit love, a counterfeit repentance, a counterfeit charity, {a counterfeit} humility, {a counterfeit} thankfulness; so also there are counterfeit discoveries in imitation of those saving discoveries that are made of God, and Christ, and spiritual things in the work of conversion, that Satan himself may be the author of; or if he [is] not the author of them, may make use of them to deceive and blind the subjects of them, and in thus imitating the discoveries of God's spirit, transforms himself into an angel of light.

Herein indeed he puts on the disguise of him, who in a superlative manner, may be said to be an angel of light, viz. Jesus Christ, the Angel of the Covenant, who is "the light of the world." For it is not any created angel that can give true spiritual light to the soul; but this is the work of Jesus Christ by his Holy Spirit.

The devil by some is called God's ape.4 He endeavors what he can to ape God in his ways and works. Thus God requires worship of his creatures; so the devil, he hath set up himself for God in many parts of the world, and requires men to worship [him]. God under the Old Testament hath a temple, and oracles, and the ark of the covenant. God had sacrifices. God has given Christ, but there arose false Christs.5 God hath sent his Son, the Great Prophet, into the world. The devil, to ape God in this, has raised up a false prophet Mahomet, and drawn away a great part of the world to trust in him.

God hath given the world the Holy Scriptures to be their rule. The devil,

-- 128 --

he apes God in this, and has forged false scriptures in imitation of it, such as the Alcoran of Mahomet.

Of old, when there were men that were inspired by the Spirit of God, the devil, he imitated that inspiration. There were false apostles and false prophets, and raised up to rival the true; so that some could not distinguish between a true inspiration by the Spirit of God and a false inspiration by evil spirits, that was common in those days. And therefore God gave them rules by which they might try the spirit whether they were of God. 1 John 4:1, "Beloved, believe not every spirit, but try the spirits whether they be of God: because many false prophets are gone out into the world."

And as the devil did mimic the extraordinary operations of the Spirit in inspiration in these days, so he doth still counterfeit the ordinary operations of God's Spirit in conversion. And there are many things that he deceives men with, and persuades them to look upon them as spiritual light and saving discoveries that are not so.

APPLICATION.

[The Application] is in an

Use of Exh. To all to beware of Satan that they be not deceived by him. If Satan always appeared as he is, everyone would beware of him; for he is a most malicious, cruel spirit. The most cruel of all beings, he delights in others' misery: and no misery that he can bring them into, can be great enough to glut his cruelty; nor can he ever be satisfied with the numbers that he makes miserable. It don't content Satan to bring any temporal calamity upon men, let it be never so great; but he seeks to bring the misery of hell upon them, which is far greater than 'tis possible for men to endure in this life, and never will have any end. What a kind of horror doth it raise in our minds to hear of the torments that have been effected upon others by some cruel men; but what are those to those miseries that Satan endeavors to bring upon men's souls? What is the utmost torment that men can inflict on the bodies of men in this world to eternal damnation in hell? We read of history of some that have put multitudes to death by their cruelty; but what is this to the execution that Satan has made among men, the millions and thousands of millions that Satan has undone and drawn into the pit of eternal damnation?

Now I say, if the devil always appeared to men as he was such a cruel spirit, everyone would beware of him. If men knew what Satan's real design was in his temptation, if they knew that he designed nothing but their undoing, they would beware of him.

-- 129 --

If the devil had appeared as he was to Eve without a disguise, and she had known the design that he was upon, she never would have hearkened to his temptation, and mankind might have stood in the favor of God to this very day. But our calamity is that Satan is a subtil spirit as well as a cruel one. He hides himself: he disguises his wickedness and cruelty: he conceals his real designs from men, and hereby deceives them with a false show and appearance; whereby men are emboldened to hearken to him and follow. And they are taken by him "as the bird that hasteth to the snare, and knoweth not that 'tis for his life," but thinks it is to feed pleasantly upon that which [is] nothing but bait to entice him withal.

The devil is "a roaring lion, going about, seeking whom he may devour" (1 Peter 5:8). But men would beware of him, if he appeared in such a shape. Men would not be so fearless of him, when he ranges about in our streets, and enters into their houses. If there should come a lion, or bear, or some such wild beast into town, how would all the town be raised, and be up in arms against him! But the devil is a wolf in sheep's clothing. He is a roaring lion, but looks as harmless to men as a lamb. Hence men are bold with him, have a great deal to do with him, and think no harm, are no way upon their watch or guard against him.

Yea, he comes to men not only in a harmless but a saint-like and friendly appearance, that men don't know it to be the devil. He looks to them more like an angel of light. Yea, he oftentimes puts on such a disguise that men mistake him for Jesus Christ himself, the Angel of the Covenant, who is an infinitely holy person, and the greatest friend that ever mankind had. Now, this shows us what danger we are in, and what need we have to beware of Satan, and labor that we may not be ignorant of his devices, but able to distinguish him and know him under all his disguises; able to discern him and see his design, even when he comes under the appearance of an angel of light, and to stand upon our watch and guard continually. Satan is under vastly the greater advantages to ruin men upon this account, of his thus transforming himself.

Be exhorted therefore earnestly to take heed, lest you be led away and taken captive by Satan, when he transforms himself into an angel of light; for Satan now is under the advantage to do you so much mischief as when he is under such a disguise.6

[I would] particularly insist on this exhortation under two heads.

First. Particularly beware that you don't follow his counsel under spiritual exercises and difficulties, how much so ever he appears under the disguise

-- 130 --

of a friend; as though he would direct you to what is most for your good, as he commonly does in such cases. Take heed that you don't hearken to him: take heed that you don't mistake the influences and suggestions of the devil for the motions and strivings of the Spirit of [God].

You may in such cases distinguish between God's counsel and the devil's counsel. Let Satan endeavor to hide and disguise himself as much as he will, you may distinguish them.

[I would] direct [you] to make much use of two things, [the] word [and] prayer.

1. If you would not be deceived by the counsels of Satan, resolve with yourself that you will in all cases follow these counsels and directions that you find written in the Word of God, and then you will be safe. You may distinguish, to the law and to the testimony, whoever it is that counsels you, if he speaks not according to this word; though he may appear as an angel of light, yet there is no light in him. If anything be suggested to you under spiritual difficulties as best for you, [or] most for the interest of your soul, inquire with yourself, "Is this agreeable to what is written in the Word, to the commandments and counsels of God there, or is it not?" If this method were taken and kept to, many would escape that now are ensnared and ruined.

Thus for instance, when Satan tempts persons to leave off praying and other duties, under that notion that by attending those duties they shall get no good, but only make God more angry, and so make their case worst; if they would but consider with themselves, "What is it that the word of God directs me to? Does God in his word command and counsel me to pray, or does he not?" By this means, persons might discern that this counsel is from the devil, though he appears as an angel of light. So again, if persons under conviction are moved to give way to blasphemous thoughts, and an envious, spiteful spirit against the godly, under the notion of being in a way to see {their} own heart {in a} more hopeful way to be converted; if they would but turn a thought and inquire, "Does God in his word counsel me to give way to such a spirit? Is there any such counsel written in the Word of God, or whether or no God command and counsel the contrary?" If you would make such an inquiry, you might soon determine whence this counsel comes.

Satan though he appears as an angel of light, yet can't bear the light of God's word, because it shows what he is. Satan can transform himself into an angel of light, but he can do it best in the dark. If we come to see him in the light of God's word, we may see through the disguise, and so Satan loses his ends. And though Satan sometimes makes use of Scripture in his

-- 131 --

temptation, yet if we come to examine these temptations by the scriptures, comparing one scripture with another, the deceit will soon be discovered. Thus Satan made use of Scripture to tempt Christ; but yet Christ repelled the temptation by the scriptures. Says he, "Cast thyself down: for it is written, He shall give his angels charge concerning thee." Christ makes answer, "But it is written again, [Thou shalt not tempt the Lord thy God]" (Matthew 4:6–7).

The word of God is called "the sword of the spirit," by which the Christian should arm himself against his enemies (Ephesians 6:17).

2. Make much use of prayer, seeing you have such a subtil and cruel enemy, that so indefatigably seeks your ruin, and that so artfully disguises himself to deceive you. Keep close to God. Forsake [him] not. Be continually with him, near to him in the duty of prayer, that he would be your guard and your counsellor; that he would defend you from the wiles of the devil; that he would instruct you, and undertake to be your counsellor by the conduct of his own Holy Spirit; that he would enable [you] to discern the subtil devices of Satan, that you may escape his snares that he spreads for you. If you are one of them that dwells under the shadow of the Almighty, he will "deliver [thee] from the snare of the fowler," as Psalms 91:3. That Satan has such arts to deceive and blind men, shows the need we stand in of a keeper. We are no more safe without God's protection than a parcel of lambs are safe in the midst of a company of wolves. We are poor, weak, fallible creatures, are easily deceived and lead astray. If we han't God to be our keeper and guide, Satan will find very easy work with us to deceive us and lead us astray. His schemes are too deep laid for us. He is too subtil an adversary for us to manage of our own skill without God's counsel and direction. This is one thing that shows the great and continual need we have of prayer. Therefore the Apostle in the forementioned place in the Ephesians 6, after directing us to take "the sword of the spirit," which is the word of God, to fight against our spiritual adversaries with, immediately subjoins in [the] Ephesians 6:18, "Praying always with all prayer and supplication, and watching thereunto with all perseverance."7

[Second.] The second thing that I would particularly insist upon under this exhortation, is that you would take heed that Satan don't deceive you with some false light, instead of spiritual and saving light.

You have heard in the doctrinal part that Satan deceives many in this respect; that he transforms himself into an angel of light, in that he often

-- 132 --

hides himself under a disguise or counterfeit of spiritual light. And here I would, first, offer some consideration to you to stir you up to this duty; and, second, lay down some things to be observed for your own safety in this particular.

First. [I would] offer some motives to stir you up to care and watchfulness; in this particular,

1. Consider there may be in some things a great resemblance between false discoveries and true. They may greatly affect the mind as well as true discoveries: they may put the mind into a very great motion: they may greatly affect the mind with joy: they may blow a person up with zeal: they may incline and dispose a person to talk much about the things of religion. We can't tell what impressions the devil may make upon the mind. He is doubtless an artist at his work of mimicking God. Thus of old, when God gave his Spirit to men in inspiration, the devil raised up false prophets and inspired them; so that there was difficulty and need of strict inquiry to discern the false from the true. And if he could imitate inspiration so, why may he not imitate spiritual discoveries? We have good reason to conclude that the devil has much to do with Quakers and other such like enthusiastical people; that he influences their minds, and makes strong impressions upon them, in imitation of divine discoveries made to them, and they have a great show and semblance of sanctity and zeal. Their minds seem to be put into an extraordinary motion by that false light that they have. And why may not Satan as well influence the minds of others to deceive 'em?

And besides that false light that Satan himself is the author of, he may deceive others with the common illuminations that the Spirit of God is the author of. Those common illuminations may be from the Spirit of God, and may be good things and of great advantage, if a good improvement be made of them. But Satan oftentimes flatters persons in them, and deceives them by them, and these have in some things a resemblance to spiritual discoveries. But,

2. There is yet indeed a vast difference between true and false discoveries. There is the great resemblance to stir you up to be diligent and strict in your care and examination. And that there is a vast difference may also stir up, as it may encourage you that your care and labor won't be in vain. If there was such a likeness between false discoveries and true, that there was really but little difference, this would be discouraging to any person that goes about the inquiry as being to no purpose. But as there is a great resemblance so as that distinguishing is a matter of difficulty, and requires care and diligence; so there is a very great difference that the things in

-- 133 --

themselves are very distinguishable. These two things are consistent, that there may be a great resemblance and yet a vast difference. There may for instance be a great resemblance between a picture or image and a man; so that a person that sees 'em at a distance, or beholds 'em without opportunity for narrow inspection, may not be able to distinguish an image of a man and a man. Yet what a vast difference is there between a real, living man and an image made of brass, or stone, or wax, or the like, as will appear to anyone that particularly examines [them]. There may be a great resemblance between false discoveries and true in superficial appearance, but not to a thorough scrutiny.

Satan can't give any spiritual discovery. He can't let in divine light into the soul, nor anything indeed like it. He can't give a discovery of the spiritual excellency of Christ and glory of God, and the glory of the way of salvation.

The giving of such a discovery is the greatest and most excellent gift that God himself imparts to the minds of men; and therefore, without doubt, is far beyond Satan to give. Satan can't give the spiritual knowledge of God, for he han't it himself; and surely he can't give what he has not.

So that the difference is great enough for them to be distinguished one from the other. The difference is indeed immensely great.

3. Consider that if the light that be in you be darkness, how great will be your darkness; agreeable to Matthew 6:23. If you flatter yourself in a false light and false discovery, instead of true saving light, your light will be but darkness. It will be nothing but mere delusion, which, instead of giving of you more light than others, will but confirm the darkness of your mind. It will shut your eyes the closer, and render your blindness the more incurable. Other men are in darkness, but there is more probability of their obtaining light sometime or other; for they don't think they have obtained [it] already, and therefore are more liable to be made sensible of their own misery and necessity, and so to be stirred up to seek after divine light. But as for you, if you are deceived with a false light, and depend on false discovery, you suppose you have already obtained what they have to seek. You have got that which you have taken up, contented with, and so ben't sensible that you have anything to seek.

You have built your house; you have settled upon your foundation. They have their house yet to build, and therefore there is a greater probability of their building upon a right foundation. I therefore now would proceed, in the

Second place to lay down some things that may be of direction to you in this affair, that you mayn't be deceived with a false light instead of the true.

-- 134 --

1. Let it be remarked that that is not spiritual light that discovers nothing to the soul. Some persons tell of their seeing a light at such or such a time, when all that they mean is only some impression they have had upon their imagination. They can give no account of anything discovered to them by that light that they saw. If a person has impressed upon their minds of a light or brightness, however great the light might seem to be and however strong the impression of it may seem to be upon the mind; yet if that light be only an idea of an outward light, such as is seen by the bodily eyes, and discovers nothing of God, nor Christ, nor of the gospel it is nothing; neither is the subject anything the better, but is in danger of being the worse. 'Tis to be feared Satan sometimes deceives ignorant persons with such things as these.

They, it may be, will say that it was a great light that they saw, above the brightness of the sun. But let it be as great as it will, and as bright as it will, they are nothing the better for it, if nothing was discovered or made known to them by it.

Spiritual light always discovers something to the mind, something of God, or Christ, or some divine thing. There never shines any spiritual light into the mind but that there is some knowledge got by it; some understanding is gained by it. Spiritual light reveals something. It either discovers something of the excellency of the things of religion: the excellency of God or Christ; the excellency of the holiness of God, or truth of God, or the justice of God, or the mercy of God; or the wonderfulness of the grace of God; or [the] dying love of Christ; or the excellency of the way of salvation, or the like.

Or it reveals something of the truth of the things. It gives some discoveries of the evidence of their truth: the truth of the gospel, or the truth and certainty of some particular promise, or the reality of some other divine thing.

Or [it] lets one into a better understanding of the nature and design of divine things, of [the] way of salvation, and other ways and works of God's wisdom. That light that a man sees that he gets no knowledge or understanding by, that discovers nothing at all to him, and that he is none the wiser for, he had as good be without it as with it.

2. Spiritual light don't only always discover something to the soul, but it always discovers some spiritual thing. If we think we see a light, if it only discovers outward things to us, it is of no service or advantage to us.

For instance, if persons have in their minds an idea of God or Christ strongly impressed upon the mind in a visible form or shape, appearing with only an outward beauty, or an outward glory or brightness, [or] light

-- 135 --

shining about them; herein though something indeed seems to be discovered yet 'tis no spiritual thing; 'tis altogether outward.

Such kind of light as this is quite a different thing from spiritual light, Such things are nothing but impressions upon the imagination.

But here I would a little particularly explain what I mean by impressions made upon the imagination.

The imagination is the power of the mind whereby a man is capable of having the images or ideas of an outward thing in his mind. And when a person has some strong and lively idea or image of some outward thing impressed upon his mind, that is what is called an impression upon the imagination. Thus for instance, if a person has a lively impression of the countenance of his absent friend, that is an impression on his imagination; for the form of countenance is an outward [thing]. So if a person seems to have an impression or strong notion in his mind of a voice, or of some bright visible light, or of the pleasantness or nauseousness of the taste of anything, these things are impressions on the imagination; because all these are all images of outward things that we perceive with our bodily senses.

So any image or idea of any outward thing whatsoever, is an impression upon the imagination. But spiritual light don't consist in any impression upon the imagination, but is an exceeding different thing from it.

If persons therefore have an impression made upon their minds as though they saw God or saw Christ in an outward, visible shape—suppose the shape of a man with a great beauty of countenance, and with an extraordinary light shining round about them (we'll suppose it above the brightness of the sun)—if there be this and this only, it is an impression upon the imagination; because a shape, and form of countenance, and a shining brightness are all outward things. And if there be this and this only, if there be no discovery of the spiritual excellency of God or Christ with it, here is no spiritual light. So if a person seems to hear a voice as though one spoke to him, or to hear music, [or] seems to hear music in heaven, such things ben't spiritual light; because voices and sounds ben't spiritual things but outward things.

Such impressions on the imagination differ exceedingly from spiritual light, especially in two respects.

(1) It differs as to the object. The object discovered by spiritual light is some spiritual object; but the object discovered by this is only an outward object.

(2) It differs in the seat of it. The seat of spiritual light is in the understanding and in the heart; but the seat of this is in the imagination.

-- 136 --

Spiritual light discovers the spiritual excellency and glory of God and Christ, and not merely an outward excellency. There is a great deal of difference between spiritual excellency and outward excellency. If we see or conceive of great beauty of the countenance, that is outward excellency. When we see the brightness and glory of the sun, that is an outward glory. So if we conceive of a being or countenance shining like the sun, why still it is but an outward glory.

Spiritual excellency consists in the excellency of holiness, and truth, and justice, and grace, and such like. Therefore if a man seems to see God or Christ with great beauty of countenance and great outward glory, if that be all—if there be no sense of the spiritual excellency of God accompanying of it—that is nothing. If the body of man were never so beautiful, and shone brighter than the sun, that would be no spiritual excellency.

When the disciples see Christ when he was transfigured, "and his face did shine as the sun, and his raiment was white as the light," that was not seeing the spiritual glory of Christ; though 'tis probably a great discovery of his spiritual glory [that] might accompany it. The light that Paul saw on his way to Damascus, that was "above the brightness of the sun," was not spiritual light. It was not spiritual light, because it was what he saw with his bodily eyes: men can't see spiritual light with their bodily eyes. It was a miraculous outward light. There was a spiritual discovery of the glory of Christ that either accompanied or followed that spiritual light, but it was not spiritual light. And if we should now see heaven opened, and see all the glory that the glorified body of Christ is in there, that would not be a spiritual discovery. That is no spiritual discovery that wicked men are capable of seeing as well as godly men. But wicked men are capable of seeing the outward majesty, and beauty, and glory of Christ that is in heaven, and will see it at the day of judgment; for Christ shall come in the glory of the Father, "and every eye shall see him, and they also which pierced him" (Revelation 1:7). They will see him then with a brightness above the brightness of the sun, but will see no spiritual light.

Spiritual discoveries are discoveries of some spiritual glory. The spiritual glory of God consists in the glory of his attributes and perfections; and men may have many imaginations of an outward glory, and see nothing of the glory of God, or the excellency of his attributes. Spiritual light consists in a sense of the glory of God and Christ on the account of their spiritual excellency, a sense of the excellency of God's holiness and faith, fullness and grace, and the light.

I desire one thing to be observed before I leave this head, that what I have said mayn't be misunderstood, viz. that though spiritual light don't

-- 137 --

consist in impressions in the imagination, yet such impressions do very often accompany spiritual discoveries. Persons, while they are under the power of a great sense of the divine spiritual excellency of God or Christ, of their holiness, and grace, and other attributes, it may break upon their imagination, and commonly does do so; so that they often conceive in their imaginations of a pleasant, beautiful, and glorious form of countenance in Christ; and sometimes have a lively conception of his sufferings on the cross: how the blood runs from his veins, and of Christ standing with his arms open, or of his speaking or calling to them, or the like. And sometimes the imagination may help the heart, as the prophet Isaiah's seeing the outward glory of God enabled him to have a greater sense of his spiritual glory; as in the beginning of the Isaiah 6.

But this is what I say, that when persons' discoveries consist only in a sense of what is outward, if there be nothing but impressions of an outward beauty, or light and voices, and such like things, it is nothing; and therefore are carefully to distinguish between what is outward and what spiritual, lest they be deceived, and to see to it. Imaginations of outward things ben't all. Who can tell but that Satan, who transforms himself into an angel of light, may impress such things on the imagination; that he may cause there to be in men's minds an image of a beautiful countenance, and a bright shining accompanying of it, when they are thinking of Christ, and other such like things. Satan can't give a discovery of the spiritual excellency of God or Christ; but he may, for aught we know, raise in men's minds an idea of an outward excellency and glory. And 'tis to be feared that many persons are deceived by such things. Weak and ignorant persons especially are liable to it, and all had need to beware of him who transforms himself into an angel of light on this account. Such impressions on the imagination, though they may affect men, yet they no way alter their hearts, work no kind of change in their natures. Such sort of discoveries are very transient and superficial in their effects; excepting very often there is this effect of 'em that is permanent and lasting, viz. that deceit and false hope that they [are] betrayed into of it.

3. Let it be remarked what spiritual light reveals to men is nothing distinct from what is contained in the word of God. Spiritual light don't reveal any new truths not contained in the word of God, but only gives men a right understanding of these things that are contained in the word of [God]. For the Spirit of God to reveal new truths not already written, is inspiration such as the Prophets and Apostles had. But there is a great difference between inspiration and spiritual light; nor are we to expect inspiration

-- 138 --

as prophets [had] now in these days, since the canon of the Scriptures is completed.

There are some that pretend to such influences of the Spirit of God as these, to have new truths revealed to them immediately, as it was to the Prophets of old, and pretend that they have no need of the Scriptures. So it is with the Quakers, and they pretend that spiritual light consists in this. And so sometimes others are apt to fall into the same delusion.

Sometimes persons are apt to imagine that they have things revealed to them, new things that they can't find written in the Word, when it is there. 'Tis time for persons to begin to suspect whether or no some evil spirit has not a considerable hand with them to delude and deceive them.

Spiritual light reveals no new thing to men, but only gives a due understanding of them, and shows the excellency and the truth. For this the Scripture is plain. Thus the Psalmist prayed that God would open his eyes that he might behold wondrous things out of God's law (Psalms 119:18). Spiritual light only opens our eyes to behold that wondrous things are contained in God's law, and not anywhere else. So it is evident that this spiritual light only reveals no other things that are contained in the word of God, because 'tis called the light of the glorious gospel. 2 Corinthians 4:4, "In whom the god of this world hath blinded the minds of them which believe not, lest the light of the glorious gospel of Christ, who is the image of God, should shine unto them." Now, that can't be said to be the light of the glorious gospel that reveals things that are not contained in the gospel.

What spiritual light discovers to us is as in a glass. 2 Corinthians 3:18, "But we all, with open face beholding as in a glass the glory of the Lord." But that glass is the glass of the word.

Therefore when persons depart from the word, and give heed to those impressions that they find made on their minds that have no foundation in the word, they are very unsafe. We are never safe any further than we go with the word to the law and to the testament. If the spirit that moves us speaks anything not contained in this word, it is because there is no light in {those impressions}.

Good men are liable to be led astray, and sometimes have been deceived in this respect. They have given way to impressions, which8 they have found have been made upon their minds; and have supposed themselves to [be] under the influence of the Spirit of God; and have never inquired whether they had the word for their foundation. All, both converted

-- 139 --

and unconverted, therefore had need to beware, lest they be led aside in this respect by him who transforms himself {into an angel of light}.

4. Spiritual discoveries are of a convincing nature; that is, they have a tendency to convince the soul of the truth and reality of divine things. He that has a true discovery of the glory and excellency of the scheme of the gospel, and the way of salvation by Christ, it will have a tendency to convince him of the truth of the gospel, that these doctrines are the doctrines of God. He that truly and spiritually understands them sees such divine excellency in them as tends to convince him that they are of God, and not of man. He sees that in them that is above man, above the wisdom of man, and evidently holding forth a majesty, and holiness, and divine excellency that is far above man, and above all creatures.

But though the false light of [the] Liar has not such a tendency,9 it may affect a man greatly: it may put his mind into a great emotion and perturbation. The affections may be strong, but his discoveries that he has have not that convincing power. They don't discover to him the reality of the things of the gospel, don't cause him to rest in them as real and certain truth. They han't a tendency to convince him and satisfy him that Jesus Christ is the son of God and Savior of the world, and that the way of salvation revealed in the gospel is the very way of life. He that truly sees Christ, he will believe on Christ (John 6:40).

5. Spiritual things appear excellent and glorious things upon the account of the holiness of them to one that has spiritual light. God appears a glorious God to him upon the account, because he is so holy a God, and not only because he is a merciful God, and because he's been so good and merciful as to pity, and help, and save them. And so Christ is a lovely and glorious Savior in his eyes, because he is the Holy One of God; and the way of salvation appears an excellent way, a desirable, lovely way to him, because it is so holy a way.

An hypocrite may be in some sort affected with some other of the attributes of God, as for instance with his goodness. An hypocrite may be affected with it, that God should be so good as to send Christ into the world to undergo so much for sinners. An hypocrite may have a kind of affection to God for his goodness to him, for his being so kind. He may suppose that God greatly loves him, that he is very dear to God, and may have an affection to God upon that account; that is, that natural gratitude in men that may be wrought upon, and work men up to a high degree of affection

-- 140 --

to God and to Jesus Christ. They may be affected with the thoughts of the dying love of Christ.

But an hypocrite can't see the glory and excellency of the holiness of God and Christ, and of the gospel. He don't love God for his holiness. They don't delight and rejoice in the way of salvation by Christ, because 'tis an holy way. Holiness in the eyes of one that is spiritually enlightened is the chief beauty of all divine things: God and Christ [are] ravishingly beautiful upon the account of their infinite holiness. Holiness is the chief beauty of the way of salvation: holiness is the beauty of the Bible: holiness is the beauty of God's commandment: holiness is the beauty of heaven that makes it seem a pleasant and happy place in the eyes of one that sees by a spiritual light.

Satan can no more easily imitate anything else in divine light than this, and that for these two reasons: first, because holiness is that which is most contrary to his nature; and second, then he can find no natural principle in men to work upon here; for holiness is the very thing that the corrupt nature of man is at direct enmity against. There is nothing so opposite to Satan's nature as holiness, and that is nothing so opposite to the corrupt nature of man. There is a principle of nature to work upon to affect a man with the good, viz. a natural principle of gratitude; but there is no natural principle to draw his heart towards the holiness of God.

6. Spiritual light han't its seat only in the head, but mainly in the heart. It primarily consists in a sense of the excellency of divine things in the heart. There is a twofold knowledge of good that God has made the mind of man capable of, viz.

(1) That which is merely speculative, whereby men have only a notion of divine things in their heads. Thus a natural man may have a notion that God is just, and that he is good, and that he [is] holy, and that Christ is one of wonderful love. But,

(2) The other is that which consists in the sense of the heart, where by men have in their hearts a sense of the excellency of these things, and they seem sweet, and glorious, and delightful to him. He has not only a notion in his head of the attributes of God; but he has a sense of the excellency of them in his heart that delights his heart, and draws his heart, and changes his heart. 'Tis accompanied with a relish of these things in the heart.

Spiritual light can't consist in anything that the devil himself has. The devil has no spiritual knowledge of God, because spiritual knowledge is a holy knowledge. But there is no such holy light in that prince of darkness.

We must consider what sort of knowledge of God it was that the devil

-- 141 --

lost when he fell. Before he fell, he had the spiritual knowledge of God; but when he fell, he lost it. But he did not lose his notional knowledge of him. He has a speculative knowledge still that he is a powerful God, and a great God, and a wise God, and a holy God; but the thing that he lost was his sense of the excellency and loveliness of those attributes. This is that therefore wherein the spiritual knowledge of God consists; and this is that which Satan can't give, let him do his utmost at transforming himself into an angel of light.

'Tis a sign that persons have no spiritual light, when their love is not [in] accordance to their light. So much the more light a person has of the right sort, so much the more love will he certainly have. The clearer his discovery, the greater his love, the stronger possession will a spirit of charity have of his soul. He'll be so much the more of a loving disposition to love God and to love men, and especially those that are the people of God.

7. This spiritual knowledge is not the knowledge that "puffeth up." 'Tis not the knowledge that we read of in the 1 Corinthians 8:1, that is set in opposition to charity.

It is not a sort of knowledge that is ostentatious and delights to show itself to be seen of men. 'Tis not the knowledge that inclines men to boast and to vaunt themselves. Therefore 'tis not that knowledge that disposes men to be censorious, apt to judge others, and to say to others, "Stand by thyself, come not near to me; for I am holier than thou." The false light of hypocrisy has such a tendency as this. But this spiritual light rather disposes men to be much [more] humble, lowly in their carriage before God and men. It is the foundation of that charity that we read in the 1 Corinthians 13:4–5, that it "vaunteth not itself, is not puffed up, doth not behave itself unseemly."

8. Spiritual light is an habitual light. 'Tis not a transient light; but where it is, there is a habit of it, that is, an abiding principle of it in the soul. Though the exercises of it ben't always existing, yet the principle is abiding. 'Tis a light that never goes out, though it be sometimes clouded like the sun. It is a light that never goes out, though it be often hid with clouds, and sometimes for a long time together. But false lights are more like a comet or blazing star that may make a great show, and set people awondering for a little while, but then goes out, and comes no more. Though the exercises of spiritual knowledge don't always remain, yet a man may distinguish that light that is transient in, and that which the soul has a habit of.

9. Those that have truly had spiritual discoveries, their religion won't consist all in discoveries. There are some that tell much of what they have

-- 142 --

seen, and what has been discovered to them; but that is all the religion they have to boast. They show little or none in their lives and conversation. They that have had true discoveries, their discoveries have changed their natures. For true discoveries are of a transforming nature (2 Corinthians 3:18); and they will dispose a man to a holy will and conversation; to obey all God's commands, not only to talk like Christians, but to live like Christians; to be of a Christian spirit, and to behave in a Christian manner, both towards God and towards men.

Hypocrites are monsters. They are all head and no hands or feet, or else all hands and feet and no head. They seem to be great in some parts of religion. They are sometimes great in religious talk, and great in pretended experiences; but they have no life or conversation to show that is answerable. He has great zeal, but no charity. But a true Christian has all the parts of a Christian, every grace of the spirit he receives of Christ's fullness, and "grace for grace" (John 1:16). He puts on "the new man" (Ephesians 4:24). He don't only put on a new head, and new eyes, and a new tongue, but new heads and new feet, and all the members are new. He is made after the image of Christ, "the image of Christ [that created him]" (Colossians 3:10). Now, the image is not monstrous, having only some parts without others, but hath all the parts in due proportion. A true Christian's religion consists in doing as well as receiving. It is not all in receiving discoveries from God; but there is a proportionable serving and glorifying God.
