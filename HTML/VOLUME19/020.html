GOD AMONGST HIS PEOPLE

EARLY in the year's traditional November thanksgiving sermon, Edwards reads the Isaiah 12:1–6—"a song of the church," he calls it—the last of them the text for his discourse, "Cry out and shout, thou inhabitant of Zion: for great is the Holy One of Israel in the midst of thee." Though it is an uncommon practice in much of his preaching during the period, he harvests Scripture in broad swaths now, quoting the first five verses from the forty-third of Isaiah in the Doctrine and then, one upon another, three verses from the Isaiah 55, two from the Isaiah 29, three from the Isaiah 35, and three from the Isaiah 49 in the Application. Sometimes he reduces a lengthy text to one of more practical size, paring the middle verses of Psalms 111:3–9, for instance, while still preserving the integrity of its narrative; at other times, he all but creates a longer text by reaping a succession of shorter ones, as he does with Psalms 97:12, Psalms 22:23, Psalms 30:4; Psalms 132:9, and Psalms 145:10–12 to explain the "work of praise" expected of saints. He transcribes in whole or in part, in particular or in paraphrase, roughly seven scriptural texts per manuscript page, nearly 150 in all, attributing only half of them.1 Citations from seventeen books of the Old Testament, including two from Habakkuk, crowd out those from the New Testament by more than six to one, the nearly sixty excerpts from Psalms, twice those from Isaiah, forming a rich mosaic of intricate figures and tropes. His biblicism, always remarkable, if seldom on display like this, is nothing short of stunning here.

Late in the discourse, Edwards ventures, "It may be said to this town this day, 'Cry out and shout, O Northampton, for great is the Holy One of Israel in the midst of thee.'" That he had earlier judged the city of Zion to be one of the "principal types" of the church, and its king, David, "the most remarkable type" of Christ enables him to historicize his text, as he would at some length another verse from Isaiah several years later, and to extend its reach locally. He recalls the events "in this town, within this twelvemonth past," recounting how God appeared "evidently and wonderfully"; how in a short time a "very great number" of all sorts converted,

-- 452 --

even "little children"; how more was done in a week, at times in a particular day, than was done in a year; how townsfolk were in "remarkable health" through winter and spring, "when the work of God did most remarkably prevail"; how young people in private societies cast off "vain and lewd" songs for anthems of praise; and how at the same time that Northampton knew God's presence, awakenings swept Windsor, Connecticut, and York, Maine, "without being influenced at all one by another." All is owing to God's sovereign and arbitrary will, not to "any particular conjuncture of circumstances in a town," not to piety, training, or prayers, but to the divine presence dwelling with, in the midst of, and among its people, a litany intoned throughout the sermon. "No, it is not for anything in us," Edwards reminds them, intimating at one point that their greater guilt may have warranted their "greater privilege." To those in a Christless condition, he offers the hope of grace yet abounding, though he concedes, "there be not that progress made now in this work that there was"; to those under the saving effects of grace, he summons the duty of praise: "Every Christian should be a priest," sacrificing "the calves of their lips" (Hosea 14:2) and rejoicing in psalms of thanksgiving. Of the "miserable, distressed souls" of March and early June, there is scarcely mention.

* * *

The notation atop the first leaf of the twenty-one duodecimo leaves that make up the manuscript indicates that God Amongst His People was preached "November 1735, Thanksgiving" and, in shorthand to the left, a "second time," without time or place. The manuscript shows occasional signs of reconsideration or haste: five pairs of brackets in different ink (LL. 5r., 5v., 7r.–7v., 8r., and 8v.–9r.); citations and expanded texts between existing lines (LL. 4r., 6v., 7v., 10r., and 11r.); several lines of omission (LL. 1r., 4r., 6v., 16r., 20r., and 21r.); transposed passages (LL. 17r.–18v.); a text altered to temper it (L. 11v.); one blank page (L. 13v.) and another, but for a citation and a partial text, almost so (L. 21r.). Still, the manuscript as a whole seems relatively free of the usual array of minor deletions and additions.

GOD AMONGST HIS PEOPLE
Cry out and shout, thou inhabitant of Zion: for great is the Holy One of Israel in the midst of thee. Isaiah 12:6

IN the foregoing chapter is a prophecy of the glorious kingdom of the Messiah: how that "there should come a rod out of the stem of Jesse, and a branch shall grow out of his roots"; and how that "the wolf should dwell with the lamb, and the leopard lie down with the kid, and the calf and the young lion and the fatling together"; and how that the Gentiles should seek to that root of Jesse, and after due time, the Jews also should be called, and they and the Gentiles should be joined together in the worship of the true God, and in a trust on the same Savior.

They are very glorious times of the church that are there prophesied of, by reason of an extraordinary flourishing of religion, and the glorious kingdom of Jesus Christ.

This chapter is a song of the church on this joyful occasion, and suited to such a glorious dispensation as is here prophesied of; and there being but six verses of it, I will read the whole of it to you. "And in that day thou shalt say, O Lord, I will praise thee: though thou wast angry with me, thine anger is turned away, and thou comfortest me. Behold, God is my salvation; I will trust, and not be afraid: for the Lord Jehovah is my strength and my song; he also is become my salvation. Therefore with joy shall ye draw water out of the wells of salvation. And in that day shall ye say, Praise the Lord, call upon his name, declare his doings among the people, make mention that his name is exalted. Sing unto the Lord; for he hath done excellent things: this is known in all the earth. Cry and shout, thou inhabitant of Zion, for great is the Holy One of Israel in the midst of thee." In this last verse, which I have taken for my text,

1. Who is here called upon, and that is the "inhabitant of Zion"; by which is intended the church. She is here called the inhabitant of Zion,

-- 454 --

and is often in Scripture called the "daughter of Zion."2 Zion was a particular part of the city of Jerusalem that was strong by nature, being built upon a steep rocky mountain, which mountain was called Mount Zion. The same was called the city of David, because David took it from the Jebusites, and dwelt there. 2 Samuel 5:7, "Nevertheless David took the stronghold of Zion: the same is the city of David."

This city of Zion, or this part of the city of Jerusalem called Zion, was one of the principal types of the church of Christ, and was fitly so made use of upon several accounts. It was part of that city that God had chosen to place his name there, part of the Holy City, as it was called. It was as it were a city within a city. And then it was built exceeding strong upon a high rock or mountain; and therefore suitably represents the church of Christ, that is built upon the everlasting rock; and is like Mt. Zion, that "cannot be removed, but abideth forever" [Psalms 125:1]. And then it was the city of David, that king that was the most remarkable type of Christ. It was a city that David took out of the hands of his enemies by his own valor, as Christ redeemed the church out of the hands of his enemies. There was David's palace, as Christ hath as it were his house and dwelling place in the church. Therefore that [that] is here called the inhabitant of Zion, is the church, the spouse of the King of Zion.

2. What she is called upon to do, viz. to cry out and shout, as a company or host are wont to do on any occasion of extraordinary joy; as the people "shouted with a great shout," when they brought the ark into the camp, "so that the earth rang again" (1 Samuel 4:5); and as the people shouted at the proclamation of the king (1 Samuel 10:24). The inhabitant of Zion is here called upon to shout with the voice of joy and praise.

3. The reason why she should shout, viz. that the God of Israel was in the midst of her. He was in the midst of her as her king, as Balaam observes of Israel in his parable. Numbers 23:21, "The Lord his God is with him, and the shout of a king is among them."

4. Why this should be such cause of shouting that the God of Israel was [in] the midst of her, viz. because he was so great and holy a God: "Great is the Holy One of Israel, for he was in the midst of thee."

DOCTRINE.

When God is in the midst of a people, 'tis just cause of exceeding joy and praise in them that so great and holy an one is amongst them.

First, briefly show, what is intended by God's being in the midst {of a people}; second, when God is amongst {them}.

I. What is intended by God's [being in the midst of a people].

First. 'Tis not intended merely that God is with them by his omnipresence; for so he fills heaven and earth, and is present everywhere [Jeremiah 23:24]. "In him [we] live, and move, and have our being" [Acts 17:28]. [He is] present in all places, and with all people, and all persons equally. Nor,

Second. 'Tis not merely that he appears to be there present by very great and extraordinary providences; for so he may be said to be present with a people in a way of judgment. He may visit a people with awful manifestations of his power and wrath, and so may be said to be in the midst of them: so he was said to come into Egypt. Isaiah 19:1, "Behold, the Lord rideth upon a swift cloud, and shall come into Egypt: and the idols of Egypt shall be moved at his presence, and the heart of Egypt shall melt in the midst of it." But,

Third. God is said in to be amongst a people in the sense of the doctrine, when he is with them in the exercises of his grace and mercy towards them as their God. In this sense most commonly it is that God is said in the Scriptures to be with a people, and in the midst of a people, and to dwell amongst them, and the like.

We observe that when persons are great friends one to another, they delight to be together, to be with one another: they choose to dwell together. Thus parents and children want to dwell together, whereas enemies are wont to stand at a distance. And therefore God's manifesting himself in works of mercy amongst a people as their God, he is said to be with them.

'Tis sometimes so with respect to a people that God may be said to be their God. Deuteronomy 10:21, "He is thy praise; he is thy God that hath done for thee these great things, which thine eyes have seen"; and Leviticus 26:12, "I will be your God, and ye shall be my people." So God becomes the covenant God of a people: he is their king. Isaiah 33:22, "The Lord is our judge, the Lord is our lawgiver, [the Lord] is our king"; Deuteronomy 32:6, "He is thy father that hath bought thee."

And when he manifests himself to be such by the exercises of his mercy

-- 456 --

and grace amongst them, then is God said to be with a people. Thus 'tis said, Isaiah 41:10, "Fear not, for I am with thee: be not dismayed; for I am thy God." This great privilege is promised to God's people in gospel times. Ezekiel 37:26–27, "Moreover I will make a covenant of peace with them; it shall be an everlasting covenant with them: and I will place them, and multiply [them], and set my sanctuary in the midst of them forevermore. My tabernacle also shall be with them: yea, I will be their God, and they shall be my people."

II. When God is thus in the midst (of a people), 'tis just cause of {joy and shouts}, that so great {and holy an one is amongst them}. 'Tis the greatest cause that a people can have to rejoice and to praise. Though they may be low in the world, they have greater cause of joy and thankfulness than if they abounded never so much in the wealth and good things of the world; more than if the earth should bring forth by handfuls [Genesis 41:47], or the abundance and wealth of the seas should be gathered together together them [Numbers 11:22], and the heavens should shower down silver and gold and pearls on them, and the rock should pour out rivers of oil to them [Job 29:6]. They have greater cause to rejoice and praise than they that find great spoils.

First. Because of his being so great and holy a God, he is worthy to be exceedingly rejoiced in and praised. The sum of the divine glory consists in his greatness, and in his holiness, and his goodness; and each of these are expressed as signified in the text. His greatness and holiness are expressed, and his goodness and grace are signified, by his condescending to be in the midst of poor, sinful, fallen men.

And hereby he is a most glorious being, infinitely the most excellent object that can entertain our understandings, affections, and praises. "His name alone is excellent" in all the earth, and "his glory is above the heaven" [Psalms 148:13]. Among the gods there is none like unto him [Exodus 15:11]. There is none in heaven can be compared to him [Psalms 89:6].

So great is he that the nations before [him] are as the "drop of a bucket" [Isaiah 40:15]. He is worthy to be exceedingly praised for his greatness. Psalms 95:1–3, "O come, let us sing unto the Lord: let us make a joyful noise to the rock of our salvation. Let us come before his presence with thanksgiving, and make a joyful noise unto him with psalms. For the Lord is a great God, and a great king above all gods." And he is "glorious in holiness, fearful in praises, doing wonders" [Exodus 15:11]. "There is none holy as the Lord" [1 Samuel 2:2]. "Who is so great a God as our God is?" (Psalms 77:13). God is not only omniscient, so that he knows everything; and almighty, whereby he can do everything, [and] so is an infinitely great

-- 457 --

God; but he is also infinitely holy, which renders his great power and knowledge infinitely beautiful and lovely. God's people are called upon to praise him upon this account. Psalms 99:2–3, "The Lord is great in Zion; and he is high above all people. Let them praise thy great and terrible name; for it is holy"; and Psalms 99:5, "Exalt ye the Lord our God, and worship at his footstool; for he is holy"; again, [the] Psalms 99:9, "Exalt ye the Lord our God, and worship at his holy hill; for the Lord our God is holy." With what rapture of joy do the angels of heaven, praise God on the account of his holiness. Revelation 4:8, "And they cease not day and night, but cry, Holy, holy, holy, Lord God Almighty."

And God is also most worthy to be rejoiced in and praised that he is so infinitely good; that though he be so great and holy, yet he will be pleased to be in the midst of such poor, unworthy creatures, as we are. 1 Kings 8:27, "Will God indeed dwell on the earth? behold, the heaven and heaven of heavens cannot contain thee."

There is glory sufficient in these things exceedingly to delight our souls, and raise them to the highest possible pitch of joy and praise.

Second. By God's being amongst a people, they have opportunity to behold this exceeding glory and excellency of God. If God were far off from them, then they would not behold him; he would be out of sight to them. But by his being in the midst of them, they have opportunity to entertain the eye of their souls with the views of his glory; for this is one of those exercises of his grace and mercy, whereby he is said to be amongst them as their God. They that God thus dwells with, he will manifest himself to (John 14:21).

If God dwells with a people, they thereby have opportunity of acquaintance with him, and [of] seeing his greatness and his beauty. They have opportunity to see his glory in those great works of mercy, in which he manifests himself amongst them.

God dwells amongst them, and as it were walks amongst them; and they have opportunity as it were to see his footsteps. Psalms 68:24, "They have seen thy goings, O God; even the goings of my God, my King, in the sanctuary."

God reveals himself to such as he dwells with by his Spirit: such a people know God. God reveals himself to them, though not unto the world. They are like the children of Israel in Egypt, who when all the rest of the land was covered with thick darkness, they had light in their dwellings [Exodus 10:22–23]. God reveals his greatness and majesty: he discovers his beauty. 2 Corinthians 3:18, "We all with open face beholding as in a glass the glory of the Lord."

If God were never so glorious a being, yet if his glory were not seen,

-- 458 --

there would not be that cause for joy and praise. But a people with whom God dwells, they have thereby opportunity to see his glory.

Third. When so great and holy an one is in the midst of a people, they have cause of exceeding joy and praise, for God's kindness to them, and that because,

1. Hereby they have a most sufficient and sure defense from evil. Such as have God with them, have cause exceedingly to rejoice in their safety. They are not exposed as others are; for they dwell on high, and their place of defense is "the munitions of rocks" [Isaiah 33:16]. For so great is God that none can reach him, none can overcome him, or control him. They that have him for their king, they are safe: they need not fear their enemies He is "the Lord strong and mighty, the Lord mighty in battle" [Psalms 24:8]. He is above all the enemies of his people. They are all before him, as the "chaff before the wind" [Psalms 35:5]. They that set themselves against them, "the moth shall eat them up" [Isaiah 50:9]: they shall consume and vanish away. If at any time they are afraid, they may safely put their trust on him; if at any time they are assaulted, they may resort to him; and he is a strong rock and high tower. "The name of the Lord is a strong tower: the righteous runneth into it, and is safe" (Proverbs 18:10). God is one that is able to help. "There is none like the God of Jeshurun, who rideth on the heaven in their help, and in his excellency on the sky. The eternal God is their refuge, and underneath are not mortal, but everlasting, arms" [Deuteronomy 33:26–27].

How is it possible that anyone should be more safe than "he that dwells in the secret place of the Most High, and abides under the shadow of the Almighty." Truly, they need "not be afraid of the terror by night, nor of the arrow that flieth by day, of the pestilence that walks [in darkness]" [Psalms 91:1, Psalms 91:5–6].

No society are so defended as the churPsalms 48:11–14, "Let Mount Zion rejoice, and the daughters of Judah be glad, because of thy judgments. Walk about Zion, and go round…her bulwarks, and consider her palaces; that ye may tell it to the generation following. For this God is our God forever and ever: he will be our guide even unto death."

The reason why Zion is so strong and safe, is because God is known in her palaces for a refuge. There is his tabernacle and dwelling place, and there he baffles the designs of the enemies of his people. "There he breaks the arrows of the bow, the shield, and the sword, and the battle" [Psalms 76:3]. "As the mountains are round about Jerusalem, so the Lord is round about his people" [Psalms 125:2]. There, therefore, God's people need not be afraid of ten thousand of their enemies that set themselves

-- 459 --

against them round about, "for no weapon that is formed against them shall prosper" [Isaiah 54:17].

This holy God has firmly promised his people that he will help them and protect them. Isaiah 43, first five verses:

But now thus saith the Lord that created thee, O Jacob, and he that formed thee, O Israel, Fear not: for I have redeemed thee, I have called thee by thy name; thou art mine. When thou passest through the waters, I will be with thee; and through the rivers, they shall not overflow thee: when thou walkest through the fire, thou shalt not be burned; neither shall the flame kindle upon thee. For I am the Lord thy God, the Holy One of Israel, thy Savior: I gave Egypt for thy ransom, Ethiopia and Seba for thee. Since thou wast precious in my sight, thou hast been honorable, and I have loved thee: therefore will I give men for thee, and people for thy life. Fear not: for I am with thee: I will bring thy seed from the east, and gather thee from the west.

Hence that people that have so great and holy an one dwelling with them, they dwell in a peaceable habitation, and in sure dwellings, and in quiet resting places. They may therefore justly glory in God and say, "The Lord is our defense; and the Holy One of Israel is our king" [Psalms 89:18]. "God is our refuge and strength. Therefore will we not fear, though the earth be removed, and though the mountains be carried into the midst of the sea; though the waters thereof roar and be troubled. The Lord of Hosts is with us; and the God of Jacob is our refuge"; as in Psalms 46.3 Such a people have reason to shout with the voice of triumph, as triumphing over their enemies. Psalms 47:1, "Shout with the voice of triumph."

2. 'Tis the greatest glory and honor of a people to have so great and holy a God in the midst of them. God hereby puts the greatest possible honor upon a people. How can a people be more highly dignified than by having so glorious a being dwelling with them? 'Tis spoken of as the greatest honor. Deuteronomy 4:7, "What nation is there so great, who hath God so nigh unto them, as the Lord our God is in all things that they call upon him for?" This renders a people excellent above all other things. That people that have this great and holy one [in the] midst of them, they partake of his glory. His glory is in a measure reflected from them: they shine with somewhat of the glory of God upon them. And therefore 'tis said to the church, in the Isaiah 60:1, "Arise, shine."

When God comes to dwell amongst a people, he makes of those that

-- 460 --

were a company of poor, naked, destitute, polluted, helpless creatures an eternal excellency, a holy and blessed people. He clothes them "with change of raiment" [Zechariah 3:4], and adorns them with the most "excellent ornaments" [Ezekiel 16:7]. He in infinite grace says to them, as Isaiah 52:1, "Awake, awake; put on thy strength, O Zion; put on thy beautiful garments, O Jerusalem, the holy city"; and as in the Isaiah 54:11–12, "Oh thou afflicted, tossed with tempest, and not comforted, behold, I will lay thy stones with fair colors, and lay thy foundations with sapphires. And I will make thy windows of agates, and thy gates of carbuncles, and all thy borders of pleasant stones."

3. A people that have such an one dwelling in the midst of them have the best comforter. He is the "God that comforts those that are cast down" [2 Corinthians 7:6]; yea, he is the God of all consolation [Romans 15:5]. He is a fountain of peace and comfort to such a people.

He affords the best relief to poor, sad, and burdened souls. In him they may find rest [Matthew 11:29]. Those that have wandered, and have been tossed to and fro, and have found no rest, in him they have rest. They that have been afraid and terrified, in him they may have quietness and assurance forever. He is to a people that he dwells with, "as an hiding place from the wind, and a covert from the tempest" [Isaiah 32:2]. The comforts he bestows are not sensual and fleshly,4 but spiritual and holy: "all joy and peace in believing" [Romans 15:13]. He gives peace, "not as the world giveth" [John 14:27]. That comfort and quietness that he bestows on his people, is vastly more valuable than all that they can have from their worldly enjoyments. He puts joy and gladness into the heart better than when corn and wine and oil are increased. God is a tender father to his people, ready to pity them under all their affliction, and takes care of them. "In all their affliction he is afflicted, and the angel of his presence saves them: in his love and in his pity he redeems them; he bears them, and carries them" continually [Isaiah 63:9]. He has a most constant love to them and care for them than has a tender mother to her sucking child. Isaiah 49:15–16, "Can a woman forget her sucking child, that she should not have compassion on the son of her womb? yea, she may forget, yet will not I forget thee. Behold, I have graven thee upon the palms of my hands; thy walls are continually before me." God's comforting his people is compared to a mother's tenderly comforting a child that is hurt. Isaiah 66:13, "As one whom his mother comforteth, so will I comfort you." Christ comes to such a people on that errand "to preach good tidings unto the meek, and

-- 461 --

to bind up the brokenhearted, to proclaim liberty to the captives, and the opening of the prison to them that are bound; to comfort all that mourn" [Isaiah 61:1–2]; [and] to appoint to them that mourn amongst them, "beauty for ashes, the oil of joy for mourning, the garment of praise for the spirit of heaviness," that so everlasting joy may be to them; so that they may greatly rejoice in the Lord, and their soul be joyful in their God, who clothes them with "the garments of salvation," and covers them with "the robe of righteousness" [Isaiah 61:3, Isaiah 61:10]. Whatever afflictions and sorrows such a people might labor under before, yet if God be amongst them, they shall no more be termed forsaken. God will be amongst them as their shepherd, who will "gather his lambs with his arm, and gently lead those that are with young" [Isaiah 40:11].

Such a people are an happy people: they partake of the sweetest joys. God deals bountifully with them: he leads them as in green pastures by the side of still waters [Psalms 23:2]. They partake of that river, "the streams whereof make glad the city of God" [Psalms 46:4].

4. A people that has so great and holy a God in the midst of them, are sure of a supply of all their wants. In that he is so great, he is able to supply them. And in that he is so holy and dwelling in the midst of them, they may be sure that he will supply them; for he dwells amongst them as their covenant God: he has bound himself to supply them. And in that he is so holy, 'tis impossible that he should fail of his promise: God has often promised that he will take care for the supply of his people. Isaiah 33:15–16, "He that walketh righteously, and speaketh uprightly,…bread shall be given him; his waters shall be sure"; and Psalms 34:10, "The young lions do lack and suffer hunger; but they that seek the Lord shall not want any good thing."

The river that supplies the city of God, is a full and never-failing stream: there is enough, and it never is dry. And they that trust in God, and whose hope the Lord is, they shall be like trees "planted by the waters that spreadeth forth her roots, and shall not see when heat comes, but her leaf shall be green; and shall not be careful in the year of drought, nor shall cease from yielding fruit" [Jeremiah 17:8]. God has promised that he will never fail them, nor forsake them.

The people that has God amongst them, they have the fountain of all good in the midst of them. There is a full fountain, indeed an inexhaustible and infinite fountain, enough for the supply of everyone, under all their circumstances and necessities: whatever anyone wants, he may go to this fountain, and there he may have a supply. Such a people that have this God amongst them, may glory in him, and say, "The Lord is our shepherd;

-- 462 --

we shall not want" [Psalms 23:1]; and may say, as in Habakkuk 3:17–18, "Although the fig tree shall not blossom, neither shall fruit be in the vines; the labor of the olive shall fail, and the fields shall yield no meat; the flock shall be cut off from the fold, and there shall be no herd in the stalls: yet will we rejoice in the Lord, and joy in the God of our salvation."

They shall have a supply of all good for the body that they need, and they shall have in God all that they need for their souls. Psalms 84:11, "For the Lord God is a sun and shield: the Lord will give grace and glory: and no good thing will he withhold from them that walk uprightly."

5. They that [have] God [as] such an one dwelling with them, are not only sure of supplies for the present, but to all eternity. They not only have such mercies as other people have, temporal blessings and favors; but they have everlasting good bestowed upon them; for God will forever be with his people as their God. Psalms 48:14, "For this God is our God forever and ever." When all those earthly things fail, yea, when their bodies fail, God won't fail. Psalms 73:26, "My flesh and my heart faileth: but God is the strength of my heart, and my portion forever." God will save and defend them from evil forever. Isaiah 45:17, "But Israel shall be saved in the Lord with an everlasting salvation: ye shall not be ashamed nor confounded world without end." Christ has promised that his sheep shall never perish. John 10:28, "And I give unto them eternal life, and they shall never perish," and none is able to "pluck them out of my hand." And God will not only defend them from evil forever, but they shall be supplied with everlasting good. He will "feed them, and lift them up forever" [Psalms 28:9]. They have an everlasting inheritance that they shall be eternally rich in the possession of. 1 Peter 1:3–4, "Blessed be the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, which according to his abundant mercy hath begotten us again unto a lively hope by the resurrection of Jesus Christ from the dead, to an inheritance incorruptible, and undefiled, that fadeth not away."

And they shall have everlasting comforts and joys. He "hath given us everlasting consolation" (2 Thessalonians 2:16). They shall dwell in God's presence, where there is "fullness of joy [and] pleasures for evermore" [Psalms 16:11]. That people amongst whom God dwells, there he "commands the blessing, even life for evermore" [Psalms 133:3].

This renders such a people happy above all. It adds an infiniteness to their happiness, and gives them cause of joy and praise unspeakable.

6. When so great and holy a God dwells among a people, he not only able to supply them with all good, but he himself is the sum of all good. God is theirs, and therefore they are happy, if they have nothing else. If all other things fail, the enjoyment of God is sufficient to make them completely

-- 463 --

happy. They that have God for their portion, they have a sufficient portion. Such a people may well glory in their portion, and make their "boast in the Lord" [Psalms 34:2], and say, "The Lord is the portion of our inheritance and our cup: thou maintainest our lot. The lines are fallen unto us in pleasant places" [Psalms 16:5–6]. "Whom have we in heaven but thee? and there is none upon earth that we desire beside thee" [Psalms 73:25].

7. And lastly, when so glorious a God is in their midst, this renders temporal mercies to be mercies indeed to them. This is the blessing of blessings, a blessing that makes all enjoyments to be blessings.

If God ben't amongst a people, 'tis in vain to them, though they have never so many other things. If they have never so great outward prosperity their prosperity is in vain to them: it can't profit them. But if God be with a people, this will render outward good things profitable to them. They have much more to rejoice in, and praise God for, the fruits of the earth: the earth yields her increase to such a people in mercy, and in fulfillment of God's covenant promises. All things are then covenant blessings. Psalms 67:6, "Then shall the earth yield her increase; and God, even our own God, shall bless us." This renders public mercies to be blessings to such a people. They have the more reason to rejoice in, and praise God for, all their enjoyments whether public or private; for they may be assured that all is in mercy unto them [Psalms 86:5]. For all things shall work together for their good.

And this don't only render other mercies profitable; but when God is with a people, it sweetens all other enjoyments. It adds a sweetness and relish to all that they have; for they may take it as the fruit of God's love, and partake of [it] in peace and joy in the Lord [Galatians 5:22]. Thus it was with the primitive Christians, when God was so remarkably amongst them. Acts 2:46, "And they, continuing daily with one accord in the temple, and breaking bread from house to house, did eat their meat with gladness and singleness of heart."

APPLICATION.

Use I. Hence we in this town have cause exceedingly to rejoice in God, and Praise his name at this time. We have that cause to rejoice and praise that is spoken of in the doctrine; for God has most evidently and wonderfully manifested himself amongst us of late. He hath appeared amongst us in his glory: he hath manifested himself in his greatness: he hath made bare "his glorious arm," and proceeded to do mighty works, and works of wonder amongst us [Isaiah 63:12].

-- 464 --

He hath appeared amongst [us], as one that is far above all the enemies of our souls, as stronger than Satan and all the powers of darkness. He hath conquered "principalities and powers, and made a show of them openly" [Colossians 2:15]. It hath appeared that stronger is he that has been with us than he that is in the world. God has in the midst of us, punish "leviathan that piercing serpent" [Isaiah 27:1]. There has been a great battle between Christ and his angels and the dragon and his angels; "and the dragon has been in a great measure5 cast out, that old serpent, who is the devil, and Satan" [Revelation 12:7–9]. And God has rid6 in triumph amongst [us] in "the chariots of salvation" [Habakkuk 3:8].

He hath appeared amongst [us] in the exercises of infinite and sovereign grace, in the abundant bestowment of the greatest blessing that 'tis possible should be bestowed on any people. The manner in which God has appeared, has been very extraordinary, and calls for extraordinary notice from us. We should take heed to ourselves that we never forget these signs and great wonders that we have seen. When we consider how great the work, how extraordinarily it was carried on, and what a great, and what an happy, change and alteration has been made in this town within this twelvemonth past; I say, when we consider this, we have reason to say, as Balaam prophesied, "It should be said of Jacob and of Israel, What hath God wrought!" [Numbers 23:23]. "How great are God's works!" [Psalms 92:5]: there are no works like his. God's all-sufficient grace and mighty power has appeared in many respects in a very extraordinary manner, in that the awakening influences of his Spirit were so general and almost universal, and in that there has been so much of the saving influences of his Spirit. For though none can tell the exact number, and none can certainly distinguish the particular persons that have experienced [them]; yet 'tis beyond all question that there have been a very great number that have been savingly converted to God. None can doubt of it, that have had opportunity to observe, what has been to be observed.

And also in that so great things have been done in so little time, in some measure agreeable to the prophecy. Isaiah 66:8, "Who hath heard such a thing? who hath seen such things? Shall the earth be made to bring forth in one day? Shall a nation be born at once? for as soon as Zion travailed, she brought forth her children."

And the sovereignty and all-sufficiency of divine power and grace, has gloriously appeared in being extended so to so many of all sorts: young

-- 465 --

and old, great and small, old men and little children. We have multitudes of influences of this nature, any one of which before would have been accounted very extraordinary, and worthy to be greatly noted.

And in the extraordinary degrees of light and comfort, which God has afforded to many persons. But I need not insist on a description of the work of God amongst us; for God hath not wrought those things long only7 in the days of your fathers, so that you need to be told of them. But your eyes have seen God's greatness, and "his mighty hand, and his stretched out arm" [Deuteronomy 4:34].

And though there be not that progress made now in this work that there was and there be a great alteration as to that, yet God is still in the midst of us There are so many amongst [us] that God has made his own people that he will never leave nor forsake, but always dwell with; that though the work of conversion should wholly cease, yet God would be in the midst [of] us: there would be very much of the presence of God amongst [us]. God is surely here in this place and amongst this people. We have therefore reason this day of exceeding joy and praise.

We are a people blessed of the Lord of his free, and sovereign, and undeserved grace. He hath made us an happy people, by granting his own presence amongst us. It may well be said to this town this day, "Cry out and shout, O Northampton, for great is the Holy One of Israel in the midst of thee. Praise the Lord, call on his name, declare his doings among the people, make mention that his name is exalted. Sing unto the Lord; for he hath done excellent things" [Isaiah 12:6, Isaiah 12:4–5].

How many poor, sinful creatures, poor, miserable, distressed souls amongst us, have of late been brought together to Mount Zion, to a glorious feast prepared there, to sing and rejoice in God. God hath sent "a plentiful rain amongst us, whereby he has refreshed his inheritance, when it was weary" [Psalms 68:9]. We have seen the goings, "even the goings of God, our King, in his sanctuary" Psalms 68:24], here. "Mercy and truth have here met together; righteousness and peace have kissed each other. Truth has sprung out of the earth; and righteousness has looked down from heaven" [Psalms 85:10–11]. "God's work is honorable and glorious, and his righteousness endureth forever. He hath made his wonderful works to be remembered, because he is gracious and full of compassion. He hath given meat to them that fear him.…He hath showed his people the power of his works.…The works of his hands are verity and judgment.…They stand fast forever and ever, and are done in truth and uprightness. He

-- 466 --

hath sent redemption to his people: he hath commanded his covenant forever: holy and reverend is his name" [Psalms 111:3–9].

Mot. 1. Let it be considered that this is evidently God's work, and not man's. None of us can attribute any of it to ourselves, or to any of our own strength. There has been most evidently the finger of God to be seen, an immediate, invisible, living hand that he wrought on the minds of multitudes amongst us. Things most apparently beyond the powers of nature, have been wrought. Many of the subjects of this work have, in the time of it, known that it was not owing to them, nor to any man. Their own sense has been witness enough that they were under the influence of a superior power. And as to the means that have been used, how long have the same means been used in vain? All has been dead. Why should there be such a difference that there should be as much done in a week, yea, probably in some particular days, as used at some times before to be done in a year?

This work has appeared to be only of God, in that it has been, in so many instances, wrought on those that we should have thought to be most unlikely to be the subjects of such a work.

And this particularly manifested it to be of God, and not from any natural cause or human endeavors; that though it has been of late times before now so very rare, that any remarkable work of this nature has appeared in any towns at all in the country; that yet now such a work began in several other places of the country about the same time that it did here, without being influenced at all one by another: for it was begun in them before they had opportunity to hear one of another; so it began here, and at Windsor, and at York, at the eastward part of the country.

If it were owing [to] any particular conjuncture of circumstances in a town, 'tis strange that there should happen such a conjuncture at several distant places that have no communication one with another at the same time, when otherwise it is so rare that there ever happens to be such a conjuncture of circumstances8 in any place.

This shows that 'tis unreasonable to ascribe it to any other than an immediate divine power that works arbitrarily where and when God will. We have reason therefore to give all the praise to God, and say, as Psalms 72:18–19, "Blessed be the Lord God, the God of Israel, who only doth wondrous things. And blessed be his glorious name forever and ever."

Mot. 2. What reason have we to acknowledge that this is not owing to any worthiness in us, but to free and sovereign grace alone that we have such a privilege bestowed upon us. Let none say it was because we were

-- 467 --

better than other people. For if it were so that then there were more godly people in the town before the beginning of this {outpouring of God's Spirit}, yet that is part of the mercy that I speak of that was owing to free and sovereign grace. We are by nature no better than others. Let none say that it was because this town have been better trained up and instructed from their beginning than other people. For if it has been so, who must we thank for that that is wholly owing to God, if in that respect this place has been made to differ from others? And if we have had greater privileges in this respect, our guilt has been greater than other people, that we were so sinful and unprofitable under such privilege.

Let none say that it was because there was more of a spirit of prayer in this place than other towns. For if there was more of a spirit of prayer, God did not bestow this mercy on us, because we had a spirit of prayer for it; but, on the contrary, he stirred up a spirit of prayer, because he had a design of bestowing this mercy. The spirit of prayer is the fruit of this grace of God to us, and not the cause.

No, it is not for anything in us. We have been exceeding undeserving of such a mercy. What dullness and deadness was there amongst [us]. How much unbelief. How was the glorious Redeemer treated amongst [us]. How did he knock and call in vain [Revelation 3:20]. How was he made light of. And how few was there that seemed to be so much as seriously concerned for the good of their souls.

There is this to show that 'tis only sovereign grace that all this is to be attributed to; that many of those that have had the experience of the saving grace of God, were our most vain, light, irreligious persons. So that 'tis sovereign grace has made us to differ from any other people. If anyone ask, how comes this people to have so much more of the presence of God than some other people; the reason to be given is that this was according to the good pleasure of God's will. If it be asked, why he should show such favor to this people; the right answer is, he loved us because he loved us. And to God we ought to say, "Not to us, not to us, O Lord, but thy name be the glory" [Psalms 115:1].

[Mot.] 3. Consider that this mercy that God has bestowed upon us, is spoken of in Scripture as the greatest cause of joy and praise to a people. So it is in Isaiah 54:1, "Sing, O barren, and thou that didst not bear; break forth into singing, and cry aloud, thou that didst not travail with child"; and v. [Isaiah 54:5], "Thy Maker is thine husband; the Lord of hosts is his name; and thy Redeemer the Holy One of Israel; the God of the whole earth shall he be called."

So in the fifty-fifth of Isaiah, [the] eleventh, twelfth, [and] thirteenth

-- 468 --

verses: "So shall my word be that goeth forth out of my mouth: it shall not return unto me void, but it shall accomplish that which I please, and it shall prosper in the thing whereto I sent it. For ye shall go forth with joy, and be led forth with peace: the mountains and the hills shall break forth before you into singing, and all the trees of the field shall clap their hands. Instead of the thorn shall come up the fir tree, and instead of brier shall come up the myrtle tree: and it shall be to the Lord of hosts for a name, for an everlasting sign that shall not be cut off." And Isaiah 29:18–19, "And in that day shall the deaf hear the words of this book, and the eyes of the blind shall see out of obscurity, and out of darkness. The meek also shall increase their joy in the Lord, and the poor among men shall rejoice in the Holy One of Israel."

We have had such an occasion of joy and praise amongst us as that described in the Isaiah 35: "The wilderness and the solitary place shall be glad for them; and the desert shall rejoice and blossom as the rose. And the parched ground shall become a pool, and the thirsty land springs of water: in the habitation of dragons, where each lay, shall be grass with reeds and rushes. And the ransomed of the Lord shall return, and come to Zion with songs and everlasting joy upon their heads: they shall obtain joy and gladness, and sorrow and sighing shall flee away" Isaiah 35:1, Isaiah 35:7, Isaiah 35:10]. That which God the Father says to Christ in the Isaiah 49:8–9, is applicable in our case: "Thus saith the Lord, In an acceptable time have I heard thee, in a day of salvation have I helped thee: and I will preserve thee, and give thee for a covenant of the people, to establish the earth, to cause to inherit the desolate heritages; that thou mayst say to the prisoners, Go forth; to them that are in darkness, Show yourselves"; which is spoken of in the Isaiah 49:13 as occasion of joy and praise to heaven and earth: "Sing, O heavens; and be joyful, O earth; and break forth into singing, O mountains: for the Lord hath comforted his people."

[Mot.] 4. We have not only the bestowment of so great mercy on ourselves to praise God for, but also on other people. Herein the dispensation of God is very extraordinary, as well as on other accounts, that this blessing should be so extensive; that so many towns should be partakers of it at once, not only those near, but many far off. This renders the work more glorious, so that we should rejoice and praise as we love our fellow creatures, and have a concern for the kingdom of our God. Such a thing is new to us, and unknown in our day; and, in being so extraordinary, gives us cause to hope for yet more glorious things, concerning the city of our God.

[Mot.] 5. Our obligation to praise is added to, in that God has not only

-- 469 --

bestowed this great mercy, but even given many temporal blessings with it. It has been a time of remarkable health amongst us, especially the last winter and spring, in the time when the work of God did most remarkably prevail: there has not been such a time of remarkable health for many years. And God has also made the earth to yield her increase to us: he has bestowed a plenty of outward good things. God's "paths have dropped fatness" to us [Psalms 65:11]. And he has continued peace to us, when we have been afraid of war [Leviticus 26:6].

Let us therefore this day, and from day to day, with the greatest ardency, and in yet the most exalted manner we are capable of, rejoice in God's goodness, and praise his holy name.

(1) We ought to praise God singly and separately.

(2) And let every family praise God, There is now great cause for the voice of joy and praise to be heard in our houses. Salvation has this year past, as we have reason to hope, come to most of the houses of this town, and to many in many of our houses.

There never was so much cause why our houses should be filled with holy rejoicings and praises as now. How many parents have reason to rejoice and praise God with their children, and children with their parents, and brothers and sisters together.

How many parents can say, "This my son, this my daughter, was dead and is alive, was lost and is found" [Luke 15:24]? How many can come joyfully to God and say, "Here am I, and the children which thou hast graciously given" [Genesis 33:5]?

(3) Let all private societies be filled with praises. Whereas private companies used in time past to be filled up much with unprofitable discourse, now they should be filled with praises. Let God be praised in your private meetings for religion, and let your tongues praise the Lord in your occasional meeting with your companions and neighbors. Societies of young people that were wont to be filled with vain talk and lewd songs, should now be filled with praise.

(4) Let us with the greatest joy and exaltation of soul, praise God in our public solemn assemblies. We never had so much cause to rejoice and praise God in public; for when we meet together, God is now more remarkably amongst [us], and great is the Holy One of Israel in the midst of us. There never was a time wherein all sorts—young men and maids, old men and little children—had so much cause to praise the name of the Lord together; for there never was a time in the town, wherein there were so many of each of these sorts that had a new song put into their mouths [Psalms 40:3].

-- 470 --

How beautiful and becoming is it for a congregation, a multitude, to join together in sincerely praising and magnifying the Most High God. Psalms 111:1, "Praise ye the Lord. I will praise the Lord with my whole heart, in the assembly of the upright, and in the congregation"; and Psalms 149:1, "Praise ye the Lord. Sing unto the Lord a new song, and his praise in the congregation of saints."

Obj. 1. Some may object and say they han't partook of the benefit of this great mercy, and why should they praise God.

Ans. You are not excused from the duty of praising God for so glorious an opportunity, because you han't made a good improvement of it. 'Tis true, ['tis] so much the more cause to lament your own sin and folly.

'Tis because you would not. You had the offer of the same blessings {of God}, but no relish for them, no real desire of them.

Your duty is to bless God, and humble and abase yourself.

If you are not thankful, it shows a continuance in the same obstinacy.

Obj. 2. "I have met with great and sore affliction. God has dealt awfully with me."

Ans. You ben't excused from praising God for undeserved mercy, because you have had deserved affliction. All afflictions are deserved, {not} less than deserved; {all} mercies, not the less undeserved. The mercies spoken of are in themselves nevertheless great, and meriting your praises.

Praise God for mercies, and humble yourself under affliction at the same time.

There is such a thing as praising God in a humble, brokenhearted manner, and yet in a very joyful manner at the same time.

This shows the blamableness of that which, I understand, is too much a custom here; that after some great affliction, they won't join their voices in that part of divine worship, saying praise to God. It would be more becoming to show themselves more of Job's spirit, who, when under exceeding great affliction, yet said, "Blessed be the name of the Lord" [Job 1:21].

Use II is to stir up those that remain yet in a Christless condition, that they may be partakers of the benefit of this great mercy of having God in the midst of us.

This may well make you sensible of your misery, who are an alien from the common; {who} have not God dwelling with you, though he dwells in the town; [who] are a poor, destitute, miserable creature, having no God for your covenant God to help you, and bless you. Your case is exceeding doleful, but yet this blessedness is what you also may obtain. Its being granted so abundantly all around you, should stir you up with utmost and

-- 471 --

indefatigable earnestness, and to seek the same. Earnestly and frequently make that prayer in the One Hundred and Psalms 6:4–5, "Remember me, O Lord, with the favor that thou bearest to thy people: O visit me with thy salvation; that I may see the good of thy chosen, that I may rejoice in the gladness of thy nation, that I may glory with thine inheritance."

Use III is to exhort those amongst us especially to praise God, that have good grounds to think that they have experienced the saving effects of God's presence. Others have reason to praise God, but these above all others. One such has received more mercy than all the natural men in the world. Consider you are no more worthy than any other. 'Tis God has made you to differ.

Praise is a thing especially becoming of the saints. Psalms 33:1, "Rejoice in the Lord, O ye righteous: for praise is comely for the upright."

The work of Christians should be praise. They are called converted and made new creatures, that they might be to the praise of God's glory. Every Christian should be a priest. God has called them, that they might be "a holy priesthood, to offer up spiritual sacrifices" (1 Peter 2:5).9

And the sacrifices that they should offer, should be "the calves of their lips" [Hosea 14:2]. The saints are especially called to the work of praise in the Holy Scriptures; as Psalms 97:12, "Rejoice in the Lord, ye righteous; give thanks at the remembrance of his holiness"; Psalms 22:23, "Ye that fear the Lord, praise him"; Psalms 30:4, "Sing unto the Lord, O ye saints of his"; and Psalms 132:9, "Let thy saints shout for joy"; and Psalms 145:10–12, "All thy works shall praise thee, O Lord; and thy saints shall bless thee. They shall speak of the glory of thy kingdom, and talk of thy power; to make known to the sons of men his mighty acts, and the glorious majesty of his kingdom."

Remember therefore the great things that God has done for you. "Though you have" as it were "lien1 among the pots," yet God has made you "as the wings of a dove covered with silver, and her feathers with yellow gold" [Psalms 68:13]. When you was poor, and in a forlorn case, and as it were "beggars on the dunghill," God hath exalted you to glorious dignity; he has "set you among princes, and made you to inherit the throne of his glory" [1 Samuel 2:8].

Remember the misery of your case. Remember the sorrow you was in, your fears, and darkness, and misgivings of heart, and how God delivered you. [You] have reason to say, as in the Psalms 18, at the beginning,

-- 472 --

"I will love thee, O Lord, my strength. The Lord is my rock, and my fortress, and my deliverer; my God, my strength, in whom I will trust; my buckler, and the horn of my salvation, and my high tower."2

Remember how helpless you was, and how God helped you; for 'tis God that gives strength and power to his people.

Remember what temptations you had, and how much too strong your enemies were for you. If it had not been the Lord that was on your3 side, then had your enemies swallowed you up quick [Psalms 118:6].

Remember how you cried to the Lord, and he unsnared and delivered you. You have cried to God with your mouth, and let him be extolled with your tongue; "for verily God hath heard you, and attended to the voice of your prayer" [Psalms 66:17, Psalms 66:19]. Let that therefore be the language of your heart. Psalms 116, at the beginning, "I love the Lord, because he hath heard the voice of my supplications. Because he hath inclined his ear unto me, therefore will I call upon him as long as I live." God hath brought you up out of "an horrible pit, and out of the miry clay, and set your feet upon a rock." Let a new song therefore be ever in your mouth, "even praise unto your God" [Psalms 40:2–3].

Consider that all the tribute that you can render for those great things is praise. You can make no other return for Christ laying down his life; [you] can't be profitable [to him]. God expects no other tribute. And how meet and suitable is it that you should render this with all your heart and soul.

You have reason to be glad and rejoice for yourselves and one for another. This is the disposition of the saints, and 'tis beautiful in them. Psalms 142:7, "The righteous shall compass me about; for thou shalt deal bountifully with me"; and Psalms 34, at [the] beginning, "I will bless the Lord at all times: his praise shall continually be in my mouth. O magnify the Lord with me, and let us exalt his name together."
