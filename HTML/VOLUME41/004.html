"Some Things Written Concerning My Very Dear and and Honored Father, Mr. Richard Edwards" (Short Version) RELife edited Timothy Edwards

Some Things Written Concerning my <very> Dear and

and Honoured Father Mr. Richard Edwards

Late of Hartford, Deceased, who Departed This

Life in <the comfortable> hopes of a Glorious Resurrection to Life

again April 20th, on a Sabbath day about Sing-

ing Time in the Forenoon, Aged (according to his

own Account within about a fortnight or three

weeks <of> Seventy one. Or within a very Little of it at

Least.

Of his Person, &c-

He was of A middle stature, rather Taller then

Shorter then Men of an Ordinary Pitch. Of a strait

well shaped Body, Not Corpulent but Rather spare &

slender with a Good proportion and Simetry in its parts.

he had especially in his younger years a well set and ve

ry Good head of hair with a Comely Countenance, since

I can Remember he was at Least in my eyes as plea-

sant a Man to Look upon As most I have seen. And

he had a smile that made him (upon Occation) Appear

with a still more Gratefull and Amiable Aspect, for it

had a pleasancy in it beyond what I have seen in Many <yea most> o

thers. He had a handsome Gate, and was of a strong healthy

Constitution, and Bore his Age in all Respects Extraordina

ry well, he had a Lively aspect and a Conly Countenance e-

ven to his Last Sickness.

Some things Concerning his Natural

Mental, and Moral qualifications Miscellaneously Considered

He was full of Life and spirits and as hath been already

Intimated of a very Lively Active Stirring Temper, yea I

Think that I have <hardly> even seen a man stir with that brashness marginal

Life and Vigour in his Business that he did. And herein he

Went beyond Many (if not Most) Young Men even when

He was above severty years of Age. He was foar from

being a slothfull person, for he was of a very diligent dispo-

sition and An early Riser being one that sav’d and Im-

prov’d his Time in a way of honest Industry & diligmarginal

marginal

In his Business to the Last.

He had a Strong Clear Voice and a very Good utterance

and Could upon Occation Accent his words very well, and

speak beyond most with an emphasis. he had a masculine

forcible way of expressing himslef. his memory, Invention, fancy

Apprehension and Judgment were all Good. he had a quick fan-

cy a pleasant Ready wit, with a very Good solid Judgment; which

[ ] things meet but in few as they did in him. He was a very Ra-

tional Clear headed wise understanding man: he could argue in

A Matter and Reason <in> a Case that was Laid down

To him, and in a Case very well; he had a very Good Thought

With him, Could quickly take and Reach a Case that was Laid down

To him and in a few words speak hims <and> to ye purpose, and strike as

I may say ye pith and heart of a Matter. he was a man of Con-

siderable Reading both in Law History & Divinity. He was well fur

nish’t for society and very pleasant in Conversation. A Covetous nar

row private selfish spirit was that which he abhorred and was him

self a Man of Courage and Resolution: in an honest and Just

Cause he favoured not ye face of man. he consider’d and well weighed

what he undertook and engaged in. & when he was well satisfied yt

his way was Right in ye sight of God, he went on in it with a Resolute

Bold undaunted spirit and neither ye flatterie frowns of men Could

Turn him out of it.

He had a very Good Spirit of Government; he so carried himself in

his place in his family that his Children and servants both Loved and

feared him. and as he knew how to Govern Others so also he had wis-

dom to Govern himself: he had a Good Command of his spirit & knew

how to keep under his own affections and passions as well as most

men which is a Great Gift of God; and a high point of practical wis

dom

He was a very observing seeing and Discerning man: he had a very Good

Insight into persons and things; he was (beyond most men) a man of busi-

ness and understood it in many respects very well, had a very good forecast

in it, and faculty for the management of it and was Capable of almost any

business he was not A stiff tenacious selfwill’d man, but of a flexible per-

suadable yieldable spirit Ready to hearken to Reason & Reasons light

when it was held forth to him though by one much his Inferior he was a

person of sobriety and Temperance one that according to my observa

tion had a goodname on that account even from his youth. As he

was far from being of a poor Mean Servile spirit, so he was as far

from a proud haughty scornfull spirit being Courteous affable and obli-

ging even to ye poorest & meanest & kind & winning in his speech & be

havoiur even to Children. and <that> According to my Remembrance about

what I have usually (if ever) observed. He was of a very Candid Charitable

Disposition of an Ingeneous forgiving spirit very Ready upon any suitable

acknowledgement of them to forget and forgive Injuries and abuses.

He was a Neighbourly kind Loving spirited Man and of a Mercifull &

Compassionate Nature, Much Inclin’d to pity & Relieve such as were in

Distress whether man or Beast he was of an affectionate melting Tem

per apt above most to be affected upon proper Occations even to Tears.

he was not a Rash unadvised but a very Thinking & Considerate

man. He was as able as most, yea I think I may beyond most

marginal

To give Counsel and advice in matters of Difficulty and

in Differences between Neighbours.

He had an Excellent Gift and faculty in dealing with men

in a way of Reproof and Counsel about their faults and miscar-

riages he would therein Expostulate and reason with them very Convincingly & Persuasively and sometimes in a very solemn

and affecting manner. he was Certainly a wise Reprover; he

wanted Neither Courage wisdom nor utterances Zeal or affection

in so Difficult a work as that in which, the truth is, I hardly ever

knew ye Like of him

He hat’d flattery & Loved and practiced plain dealing he

was a Man of truth & Greatly abhored all deceit guile & fals

hood. He was a faithfull friend and might be safely trusted

with ye Greatest secrets. he was a just and upright man, fair

and honest in his Dealings, moderate in his prices, A good pay

master A man of his word. He hath in his Time Dealt I suppose

with some Gentlemen in this Country for Thousands and as far

as ever I could perceive his Credit was allwayes Good with them.

I have known him from his Young years to ye age of Just

About seventy One, In all which time I never observed any

Thing that savoured of a dishonest spirit in him, and as as far as

I know, from his Youth he had ye Reputaton of an honest man

and Lived & Died Generally well esteemed both at home and

Abroad On that account, and that though he was one way or other

for many years a man of business and of Dealings and Concerns

with Man above most.

He was very Ready to Plead ye Cause of the Fatherless and ye

Widow and to stand for ye Right of the Injured and Oppressed &

To help them that Could not help themsleves. And how much

hath he spent in the service of some that have needed his advice

and help for Nothing & of others for a small Matter.

He was a man that had much of a Greatfull spirit in him. Greatly ab-

horring that Sordid Ingratitude and Disingenuity which is too much ye Temper

of some Persons: He was apt to take notice of and was easily affected with

any Kindness or Respect showed him even by such as were much his Inferior

yet he was far from slighting such a thing in the poorest and meanest of men

he was <as> well beloved in his Native Town as most men: he had such an

Interest in the esteem and affection of his Neighbours that he Lived Desired

and Died Lamented by them. And indeed Considering the many businesses in

which he was Concern’d with men and how he had occation to deal with

men of all sorts & how many God in his providence has cald him to oppose

Thwart & Cross & that in their Interest & sometimes in ye face of ye Country

(as the Kings Attorney) to Bear Testimony against their miscarriages, I

say Considering these things with that [ ] Close plain dealing spirit yt

he was of It is much that he should be so well belov’d & Respected and

that in ye Town where he for so many years dwelt as he was.

And as he was so well belov’d and Respected at home so he was also abroad

He was of so winning a Deportment and Behaviour towards those with whom

He Conversed that I am apt to think But few have been treated with more

marginal

Kindness Respect & Civility (of his Rank & quality I mean) than he

hath been by strangers in One place and another when his Occation

have Call’d him from home and that from his youth. Where ever he

went (as far as I have been able to Learn) he met with uncommon

expressions of Respect and Kindness, at Least It hath been very commonly

so.

He was Good (yea amiable in his Conversation and behaviour) in ye

Several Relations wherein he stood. To his wife he was a very Tender

affectionate Kind & Loving husband. To his Children he was not only a Good

Father, but he was so in many Respects so sensible of their Griefs &

troubles when any of them was under affliction, so tenderly & affectionate-

ly Concern’d for their Good & wellfare so ready to pray for them so able to

advise and Counsel them, and so much in it unwearied in Doing for

them, & so pleasant in Converstion ith them &c- that I cant but

think It may truly be said of him that he was one of the best of Fathers.

he was Good as a friend being one of the truest most cordial and faith

full friends that Could be found among men. he was Good as a

Neighbour yea kind and Good beyond and many to his poor Neighbours

free and familiar with them Ready to help and serve them to my

knowledge as well as others. He was Good to his servants and al-

wayes Respected and Belov’d by them. He was Good to strangers, be

ing very ready & much desposed to show all due Respect to such of

Them as were persons of quality, and in a Courteous affable kind & O-

bliging manner to entertain and treat them all. he was Good yea very

Good to the poor Dumb Bruit Creatures yea to all sorts of them that

belong’d to him & that both without doors & within yea <he> was of such

Tender <and> Mercifull spirit & of so Kind & Good a Disposition

towards every Creature that in that respect I cant know that ever I

knew the Like of him.

He was Noted for a Man of sobriety and Temperance (used that De-

servedly from his youth. Though he was of a pleasant Cheerfull natu-

ral Temper as has been already Intimated yet he was moderate in

his Mirth Neither Ordinarily nor Over Merry or Given to much

Laughter.

In his Dealings he was not only fair Just and honest as is above expressed

But also Gentle and easy with his Debtors being very Kind and Com-

passionate to, patient & forebearing with those of them that were poor

or Complained that they Could not pay him.

There is one thing more that may truly come into ye score of his virtues

& be added to those many amiable things that were in him and that

is Though he was <of> no mean but as hath been already Observed of a very

manly spirit yea he was naturally of a modest Temper & disposition

which he expressed in a Respectfull Treating of strangers & persons of

Quality, and in not ambitiously Aspiring after high seats and a place

in Publique Assemblies above his Rank as the manner of some is but

rather Choosing one below it <this also may be [ ] added to his character viz that was very far of and Remote from any thing that savoured of an affectatious and fantastical spirit [ ]+>

<[ii] +Though he abhored whatsoever was Rude and Clownish, &

Loved what was handsome both in speech and behaviour yet I cant

Remember that ever in my Life I saw or observed any thing in him

that was in the Least fantasticall or affectatious: he was very Remote

in all his Managements, and in his whole Conversation from any

thing of that nature.>

That it plased the most High to Endow and Adorn my

Dear Departed Father with many virtues which Rendered <him> very

Lovely and Desirable in his Life and much Lamented in his Death

especially by those that best knew him and that very Deservedly. --

And ye Truth is (If I may be allowed to say it who was so nearly

Related to him) such were his Natural & moral endowments & quali-

fications that he was in My esteem a Man of a Great Many but

though what I have already observed be much yea through Grace it

is not all I know some things more to add (to my Great Comfort con-

cerning him.

marginal

In ye next place I shall Consider him

and Give some account of him as a Christian.

For about or Near forty years ago as Near as I can Ga-

ther, when I was but a Child of about Eleven or twelve

years of age when I was under Great spiritual Impressions

my self I Observed so much of a Religious spirit appearing

in ye Discourse and Conversation of my Father, that I well re

member I took him to be then a Godly man one that truly

feared God, and was want from time to time in a particu-

lar man<ner> in my secret prayers to bless God for it, Looking upon

it as a great mercy and favour of God to me that he had Given

me so Good a Father this <was> matter of rejoicing and thankfull-

ness to me in those dayes. but possibly I may something

misreckon as to ye Distance of time It might not be quite

so Long ago. It was as I Remember some Considerable time

before I Left Going to school and so before I was past my

Childhood, and therefore not very much short of ye Time I

have mentioned But after this for several years It pleased God

To Exercise him with a very Great and sore try-

al which was of Long Continuance the Like whereunto all things

Consider’d Doth seldom happen in the Course of Divine providence ye

Latter end whereof proved An hour of Great and Overbearing Temp

tation to him, ye Effect whereof Its needless now particularly to

mention, I shall only say this of it that it was matter of Great

hearts Grief and of much sorrow and bitterness of soul to him and

for which I have Reason to hope he did unfeignedly and heartily

mourn even with true Repentance and Godly sorrow. In what

follows I shall principally and almost wholly have Respect to ye

Time that hath passed since what I have but now hinted at which

took up or Contained ye Last seven and Twenty years of his Life or

thereabouts which began at or near his Joining in full Communion

with the Church, which I hve Reason to think was the best part

of his Life. If in ye following account of that Religious

Christian spirit that I observed in My Father I sometimes

express my self possitively I intend but this by such expressions

viz that so it appear’d to me and that so by ye acquaintance

I had with him, I may in ye Judgment of Christian Charity think

& say of him.

I shall now Briefly mention (having done it more

Largely Elsewhere) several Things Concerning him viz

my Dear Departed Father as A Christian in ye fol-

lowing particulars. viz,

He was very full and Clear in his Belief and apprehensi

on of that Grand fundamental Article of our holy Religion

ye Existence or Being of God, which he seem’d to make not only

ye object of his faith but also of his Choice, and to Delight in &

Receive [Rejoice?] in the Love of it.

He Also Believed ye <great &> Misterious Doctrines of ye Trinity

& of ye Incarnation of ye Eternal son of God & therein ye Divine

Nature or Godhood of the Lord Jesus Christ our Blessed Redeemer

marginal

And that the holy Scriptures are of Divine Original and

Appointed of God to be the Great Rule and Directory both of our

Faith & Lives. And as he Believed that God is so he seemed

to have the fear of God before his eyes. Though he might suddenly

by ye strength of Temptation together with ye weakness of Natural In

firmity be Overtaken in (or with) a fault yet I have Good Reason

to Believe that Nothing would have [?] him to do that which he Realy

thought would offend God: and that he would not deliberately sin

against his Conscience for any thing in the world. and that he

did both Dread and abhore to do any wicked thing, and that he

had a Gracious Respect to God therein.

He took Care to Maintain the worship of God in his Family

The holy scripture and other Good Books ere in the season thereof

Much Read in his house. His Children were by his Example taught

That Christian Practice of Reading Excellent sermon Books and

Treatises in Divinity: This Indeed was his way and Manner ever

since I can Remember. And I am apt to think there was as much

of that Religious and pious practice (if not more) in his house than

In most houses even of them that fear God in the Land. He was a

serious savoury spirited man very often both in his Letters to his

Children and In his Discourse Dropping something that savoured

of a Religious and serious and gracious spirit Tending to His Ma-

kers Glory, and their Edification and spiritual Good.

He seemed to Lay Great weight upon God Threatnings expressed

In his word against vicious and wicked and upon his promises there

in made to pious and virtuous persons as verily expecting and

Looking for an Accomplishment of both. he was one that much

observed and eyed ye providence of God he Regarded the works of ye

Lord and Considered ye Operation of his hands and that according to

my Observation more than most Christians. he Thankfully ac-

knowledged the Great Goodness of God both to himself and his [family] &

seemed Graciously disposed yes much disposed to Give him ye

praise and Glory thereof especially in Mercies of a more Remark

able nature. And this gratefull sense of Divine Goodness he ex

pressed both in prosperity and adversity both in health & sickness

both living & Dying as I shall more particularly observe after

wards

He also Observed the hand of God in afflictive dispensations of pro

vidence. In ye Day of Adversity he Consider’d he took Great notice

of <such> awfull passages of providence as hapn’d in The Town where

he dwelt, or in the Land and seemed to be above many If not

above most Christians affected with them. And when It was

(as for a Great part of his Life it was) a Time of affliction

and trouble with himself he Considered it as the Correcting &

Chastizing hand of God upon him, and was very apt at such a

Time to make humbling Reflections upon himself.

He hated vice and wickedness whereever he saw it and ab-

hored to make Light of it or Justifie it in Any though never so

nearly Related to him: he would with a warm Zeal Tes

tify Against miscarriages and ill behaviours in them that

were his own flesh and Blood as in any in the world. If

his Children did amiss they must expect no favour from him

He was in such (as well as in other) Cases of a very upright and

Impartial Spirit, so that I cant but think according to my ac

quaintance with men that in Deut 33. 9 may be apply

marginal

ed to him more than to many true Christians neither

Did he acknowledge his Brethren nor know his own Chil-

dren.

In the Duty of Prayer he seemed to Draw nigh to God in a

very solemn and Reverent manner such were his high appre-

hensions that he had of God that it seemed to be his delight

to exalt and magnify his name and to see others do it by ap-

proaching and addressing his Glorious Divine majesty in that

Duty with all possible humility solemnity fear and Reve-

rence: I dont Remember that I have ever Observed a Christian

act more Christian like or express more of a serious solemn de

vout and Religious spirit that he was want to do in this thing

He seemed to have an admiring sense of the power wisdome

Greatness majesty and Glorious excellency of God in the work of

Creation and providence and also of his wonderfull mercy and

Grace to mankind in the work of Redemption by Jesus Christ

and I have Reason to hope that God had given him some uncom-

mon discoveries of his Glory in both these.

By what he hath said to me Concerning the Excellent Di-

vinity Books that I have sometimes Lent him I have reason

to hope that he was graciously affected and edified in Reading

them and that he Received ye truths Contained in them with Faith

and in the Love of them.

Another thing savouring of a Religious Christian Spirit in him

was his Dealings with his neighbours as there might be Occati-

on in a way of friendly and Christian admonition which he

would do seriously moderately Lovingly & wisely and some

times (the Case calling for it) in a very Close [ me] Convinc-

ing affecting and solemn manner. and Indeed as there were

but few that Could do this Difficult Duty so well so there

were not many that did so much at it or did so much Good by

it as I have Reason to believe as he did. The Truth is for Reprov-

ing of sin Dealing with proud Rugged Rough sturdy high spi-

rited men or with any in a prudent serious solemn Christiann

manner & for Giving Good Counsel he was one of a Great

Many, If not one of a Thousand. & Considering his Capacity

for disposition to this Great & difficult Christian duty and ye

Good he hath Done in it I could but think his Death is upon this

as well as upon Many other Accounts Much to be Lamented

And as me had the wisdom suitably to Give so he had ye

Grace with a Christian spirit to Take a Reproof and that

patient <humble> meek and upright spirit <in him> upon such Occations

hath not a Little affected me since his Departure

He was in the things of Religion far from being a man of

A Drie Insensible spirit: In hearing of ye word and In discourse

of spiritual Things especially of the Death and sufferings of

The Lord Jesus Christ he was apt to be affected and hath often

been so as I have Reason to Conclude both by what I have heard

and seen even to shedding of Tears and so also in the duty of

prayer. he was of a more Tender sensible affectionate spirit

than many that are Truly Gracious I have known but few

marginal

like him in this Respect; how often hath an affect

ing sense of things of a spiritual nature made such

an Impression on his Tender heart that the Tears

have stood in his eyes

He was much in Giving Good Counsel to his Children

especially when some special Occation Call’d for it, and on

ye Sabbath day after ye afternoons exercise Improving ye

sermons that way which they had that day heard & the good

Books that might then be read in his family besides what

frequently dropt from him of that nature at other Times

neither was he wanting <herein> towards others that dwelt under

his Roof. I have heard that one of his maids should say

who was esteemed a serious Religious Person that in that

Respect she never knew ye Like of him. And I think I may

truly say so my self. his children were in this Respect pri-

viledg’d above many of the Children of Godly Parents, as by

what I have Observed I have Good Reason to think.

He was a very Comfortable Christian Friend in affliction

he was able and Disposed beyond most to speak Comfortably

and sutably to such as were in Trouble There were but few

at Least among private Christians that Could apply them

selves so pertinently pithily & properly savourily and

suitably and in so Comprehensive a manner and that marginal

both for their Direction and Support to the Case of such

as were in affliction as he Could
