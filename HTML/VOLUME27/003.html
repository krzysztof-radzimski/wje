Part III Controversies 3 (ed.) Jonathan Edwards

REGENERATION, BEING NEW-CREATURES, RAISED FROM DEATH IN SIN.

By these things in the New Testament, is not meant merely persons being brought into the state and privileges of professing Christians, according to Taylor. See miscellaneous papers stitched together against Arminianism, pp. 36-38.1

When Christ says unto Nicodemus, John 3:3, "Verily, verily, I say unto thee, Except a man be born again, he cannot see the kingdom of God," He don't mean merely, "Except a man be brought to a participation of the new state and privileges of the Christian church, he cannot enter on the possession and privileges of the Christian church": for that would be nonsense, and only to say, "Unless a man be born again, he cannot be born again", or, "Unless a man enter into the new state of things as created by the Messiah, he cannot enter in the new state of things as created by the Messiah." Nor can he mean that, "Unless a man be a professing Christian, he cannot see the future and eternal privileges of the kingdom of heaven"; for he supposes many heathen will see the kingdom of God in that sense.

And how unreasonable would it be to suppose that Christ would teach this doctrine of the necessity of being instated in his new-modeled church, as such a great, important and main doctrine of his.

Taylor, to make out his scheme, is forced to suppose that by "being born of God" is meant two things in the New Testament (p. 127 of his Key, near the top;2 see on Original Sin, pp. 144c, etc.); so he is forced to suppose that by "the kingdom of God" is meant two things parenthetical; and so two senses of our being of the truth, our being of or in God, and knowing God parenthetical. He is forced to suppose that many of the expressions signifying antecedent blessings, are to be taken in a double sense parenthetical.3

See how evidently being born of God, signifies something else than a being brought into the state of professing Christians. 1 John 2:29, "If ye know that he is righteous, ye know that every one that doth righteousness is born of him"; ch. 1 John 3:9, "Whosoever is born of God doth not commit sin; for his seed remains in him: and he cannot sin, because he is born of God"; ch. 1 John 4:7,4 "every one that loveth is born of God, and knoweth God"; ch. 1 John 5:4, "whatsoever is born of God overcometh the world"; v. 18, "We know that whosoever is born of God sinneth not; but he that is begotten of God keepeth himself, and that wicked one toucheth him not."

So 'tis exceeding apparent that by knowing God, and being of God, and in God, having this hope in him, mean something,5 besides our Christian profession and principles and privileges. 1 John 2:3, etc., "hereby do we know that we know him, if we keep his commandments. . . . whoso keepeth his word, in him verily is the love of God perfected: hereby we know that we are in him"; ch. 1 John 3:3, "every man that hath this hope in him purifieth himself, even as he is pure"; ch. 1 John 3:14, "We know that we have passed from death to life, because we love the brethren"; ch. 1 John 4:12, "If we love one another, God dwelleth in us."

Mr. Taylor supposes that this same Apostle, by being born of God, means being received to the privileges of professing Christians. John 1:12 parenthetical, 1 John 5:1 and v. 18 parenthetical, 1 John 3:1 parenthetical.6

THE MEANING OF THE TERMS REGENERATION, CALLING, REDEMPTION, NEW CREATION, REGENERATED, SANCTIFIED, ELECTED, RAISED UP, ETC., IN SCRIPTURE.7

EXPRESSIONS which Mr. TAYLOR supposes are used in the New Testament to mean8 the state and privileges of professing Christians:9

Elect, chosen, chosen from the beginning, before the foundation of the world, being a chosen generation parenthetical; being saved, bought, purchased, redeemed, delivered from this present evil world, delivered from the power of darkness and translated into the kingdom of his dear Son, delivered from the wrath to come, purchased with his own blood, bought with a price, redeemed to God by his blood out of every tongue, kindred, people and nation, redeemed from all iniquity, redeemed from their vain conversation, redeemed from death and from the curse of the law parenthetical; being called, called of Jesus Christ, called saints, called with a holy calling parenthetical; created in Christ Jesus unto good works, created in righteousness and true holiness, putting on the new man, being God's workmanship, created in Christ Jesus unto good works parenthetical; being quickened after dead in trespasses and sins, quickened together with Christ, being alive from the dead parenthetical; being begotten of his own will by the word of truth, being regenerated or begotten again to a lively hope, being born or begotten again not of corruptible but of incorruptible by the word of God, being saved by the washing of regeneration and renewing of the Holy Ghost, being so new creatures that old things are passed away and all things become new parenthetical; being the children, sons and daughters of God, which were born to God, heirs of God and joint-heirs with Christ, being those that have received Christ, to whom gave he power to become the sons of God-"Behold, what manner of love the Father hath bestowed upon us, that we should be called the sons of God," 1 John 3:1—being the children of God through faith in Jesus Christ, being new-born babes parenthetical; being of the household and family of God, being the brethren of Christ, being the house of God, being of the family that is in heaven and earth parenthetical; having an interest in the heavenly inheritance or having that inheritance belonging to them, being made heirs according to the hope of eternal life, heirs of the kingdom which Christ has promised to them that love him parenthetical; having their names written in the book of life parenthetical; being no more strangers and foreigners but fellow citizens with the saints and of the household of God, which in time past were not a people but being now a people, being now at peace with God, being reconciled to God parenthetical; being espoused to God, having Christ for their husband parenthetical; having the Jerusalem which is above to be their mother parenthetical; being washed and sanctified and justified in the name of the Lord Jesus, and by the Spirit of God parenthetical; being saved by the washing of regeneration and renewing of the Holy Ghost parenthetical; being saints, holy brethren, a holy nation, partakers of the heavenly calling parenthetical; being the temple of God, the holy temple of God, the temple in which God dwells and walks, having the Spirit of God dwelling in them as his temple, being lively stones, built up a spiritual house, being a holy temple to the Lord, an habitation of God through the Spirit parenthetical; being made kings and priests, being made to sit on thrones to judge and reign over others, being a holy priesthood, a royal priesthood, kings and priests unto God, reigning on earth, having it granted to them to sit with Christ on his throne as he overcame and is set down with the Father on his throne, having power over the nations to rule them with a rod of iron, reigning with Christ, judging the world, judging angels parenthetical.

Being justified by faith, having peace with God parenthetical; being in Christ Jesus parenthetical; being called into the FELLOWSHIP of Jesus Christ, being Christ's, being unleavened, being by one Spirit all baptized into one body, being the body of Christ, and members in particular parenthetical; being those that had put on Christ, being Christ's and therefore Abraham's seed and heirs according to the promise, having received the adoption of sons, having the spirit of the Son sent forth into their hearts, that they know God, or rather were known of God parenthetical; being faithful in Christ Jesus, being blessed with all spiritual blessings in heavenly places in Christ, being PREDESTINATED to the adoption of children by Jesus Christ, having redemption through Christ's blood, the FORGIVENESS of sins, having obtained an inheritance IN CHRIST, being PREDESTINATED according to the purpose of him who worketh all things according to the counsel of his own will, being sealed with the Holy Spirit of promise, being quickened together with Christ after dead in sin, being raised up together and made to sit together in heavenly places in Christ, being RECONCILED to God, being an habitation of God through the Spirit, being now LIGHT in the land, though they had sometimes been darkness parenthetical; having the hope of the gospel laid up for them in heaven; being made meet or counted worthy of the inheritance of the saints in light; being delivered from the power of darkness and translated into the kingdom of God's dear Son, being RECONCILED in the body of Christ's flesh through death, Christ's being in or among them the hope of glory, receiving Jesus Christ the Lord, being circumcised with the circumcision made without hands in putting off the body of sins of the flesh, risen with Christ through the faith of the operation of God, being quickened and FORGIVEN all trespasses, having their life hid with Christ in God, putting OFF THE OLD MAN with his deeds and PUTTING ON THE NEW MAN, which is renewed in knowledge after the image of him that created him parenthetical, being all the CHILDREN OF THE LIGHT and of the day, not being of the night nor of darkness parenthetical; being come to Mt. Zion, the city of the living God, etc., as in Hebrews 12, receiving a kingdom that could not be moved parenthetical; having their hearts purified in obeying the truth through the Spirit unto unfeigned love of the brethren, called out of darkness into MARVELOUS LIGHT, having now returned to the Shepherd and Bishop of their souls, being heirs together of the grace of life, standing in the true grace of God, obtaining the like precious faith with the apostles, the divine power had given them all things pertaining to life and godliness through the knowledge of him that had called them to glory and virtue, whereby were given to them exceeding great and precious promises parenthetical; they have their sins forgiven for Christ's name's sake, they know him that is from the beginning, they have overcome the wicked one, they have known the Father, the Word of God abides in them, they have an unction from the Holy One and they know all things, they are of God and have overcome God's enemies, because greater is he that is IN THEM than he that is in the world, God has given them eternal life, they BELIEVE ON THE NAME OF THE SON OF GOD, they may know that they have eternal life, the Son of God has given understanding, that they may know him that is true, and they are IN HIM that is true parenthetical

Why does the Apostle say concerning apostates, "THEY WERE NOT OF US; if they had been of us, they would no doubt have continued with us: but they went, that they might be made manifest that they were not all of us," if it be, as Taylor supposes, that professing Christians are indeed of the society of Christians, to all intents and purposes have all their privileges; are truly the children of God, members of Christ, of the household of God, saints, believers that have obtained like precious faith; are all one body; have one spirit, one faith, one inheritance; have their hearts purified, sanctified; are all the children of light; are all of the household of God, fellow citizens with the saints; have all fellowship with Christ, etc.?

'Tis true, the nation of the Jews are in the Old Testament said to be ELECTED, CALLED, CREATED, MADE, FORMED, REDEEMED, DELIVERED, SAVED, BOUGHT, PURCHASED, BEGOTTEN. But particular Jews are nowhere so spoken of, at least with reference to the same things, viz. their national redemption, when they were brought out of Egypt, etc.

David, in the book of Psalms, though he is so abundant there in giving thanks to God for his mercies, and also so often in praising God for God's redeeming his people out of Egypt, and the salvation God wrought for the nation and church of Israel at that time, yet never once blesses God parenthetical, that God had chosen him and redeemed him, bought him, regenerated him; never parenthetical speaks in the language of the Apostle, "he loved me, and gave himself for me" parenthetical—though he often speaks of the blessedness of the man God had chosen and caused to come nigh unto him, agreeable to the language of the New Testament, and often blesses God for redeeming and saving him in particular—but never, in any of these things, has respect to those national privileges, nor any other of the penmen of the Psalms: which is indeed very strange, if the privilege of being bought, made, created, etc., as applied to the nation of the Jews, be that10 which the Apostle in the New Testament applies to himself in particular, and which this and the other apostles apply to many other particular persons.

The case was quite otherwise in the Christian church, with regard to election, redemption, creation, etc., from what it was with the Jews. With the Jews, election, their redemption out of Egypt, their creation, was a national thing; it began with 'em as a nation, and descended as it were from the nation to particular persons. Particular persons were first of the nation and church of the Jews, and so by that means had an interest in this election, redemption, etc., that God wrought of old. Being of the nation and church of Israel, was the ground of a participation in these privileges. But 'tis evident 'tis contrariwise in Christians: with regard to them, the election, redemption, creation, regeneration, etc., are personal things. They begin with particular persons and ascend to public societies; men are first redeemed, bought, created, regenerated, and by that means become members of the Christian church, and this is11 is the ground of that membership. Paul's regeneration, and Christ's loving him and giving himself for him, was the foundation of his being of the Christian church, that holy nation, peculiar people, etc.; whereas David's being one of the nation of Israel, is the proper ground of his participation in Israel's redemption out of Egypt, and of that birth and formation of the people that was at that time.

'Tis apparent that the case is thus. It cannot be otherwise. 'Tis evident that the new creation, regeneration, calling and justification, are personal things, because they are by personal influences—influences of God's Spirit on particular persons and by personal qualifications.

Their regeneration was a personal thing, and therefore 'tis not called only an entering into the new creation, or obtaining a part in the new world or new Jerusalem, etc., but a putting off the old man and putting on the new man. They first are raised from the dead, and by that means come to belong to the church of Christ. They are first lively or living stones, and by that means come to belong to the spiritual house, the holy temple; by being lively stones, they come to be fit to be parts of the living temple, and capable of it, so that their being alive is prior to their belonging to the Christian church. The Christian calling is represented as being the ground of their belonging. They are called into the church, called into the fellowship of Jesus Christ. Their spiritual baptism, or washing, is prior to their being in the church. They are by one Spirit baptized into one body. They put on Christ, and so become interested in Christ, and sharers with those that had a part in him. By such a personal work of the Spirit of God, they were first made meet to be partakers with the saints in light, before they were partakers.

That professing Christians are said to be sanctified, washed, etc., does not argue that all professing Christians are so indeed: for Taylor himself says,12 it should be carefully observed, that "'tis very common, in the sacred writings, to express, not only our Christian privileges, but also the duty to which they oblige, in the present, or preterperfect tense: or, to speak of that as done, which only ought to be done, and which, in fact, may possibly never be done." Matthew 5:13, "Ye are the salt of the earth," i.e. "ye ought to be"; Romans 2:4, "the goodness of God leadeth thee to repentance," i.e. "ought to lead thee"; ch. Romans 6:2, 11, ch. Romans 8:9, Colossians 3:3, 1 Peter 1:6, "Wherein ye greatly rejoice," i.e. "ought to rejoice"; 2 Corinthians 3:18, "we all, with open face" parenthetical "beholding as in a glass the glory of the Lord, are" parenthetical "changed into the same image from glory to glory"; 1 Corinthians 5:7, "ye are unleavened," i.e. "obliged by the Christian profession to be"; Hebrews 13:14, "we seek" parenthetical "a city to come"; 1 John 2:12-15, 1 John 3:9, vv. 4, 18, and in other places. See Taylor's Key, p. 139, no. 244, and p. 141, no. 246.13 This overthrows all his supposed proofs, that those which he calls "antecedent blessings," do really belong to all professing Christians.

It will follow14 that SIMON THE SORCERER had an interest in all the antecedent blessings, yet the Apostle tells him, he was at that time "in the gall of bitterness, and bond of iniquity" parenthetical. If he was really justified, washed, cleansed, sanctified, how was he in the bond of iniquity? Justification, forgiveness, etc., is a release from the bond of iniquity; if the heart be purified by faith, it don't remain in the gall of bitterness.

UNIVERSAL REDEMPTION

Visible Christians that live under means of grace, by reason of the offers they have of salvation, happy opportunities, and great means of it, are spoken of as in possession of it.

So they are said to have their names blotted out parenthetical; to sell their birthright parenthetical, as Esau, who had his birthright parenthetical, though those never properly have it; so persons are spoken of as being thrust out of the kingdom of God parenthetical.

So Christ is sometimes spoken of as having died for them, for he did die to give them an opportunity to obtain. They are spoken of as being redeemed and purchased sometimes hereby parenthetical.

God is represented as giving it to them conditionally, as though he, upon their profession and promises, trusted them that do, and deals with men as we do one with another: for in his judicial proceeding with visible saints, God does thus treat with them as trusting them on their profession and engagements, and finding out afterwards whether they are sinners or no by trial. And he at last, in judging of them, will proceed according to such evidences, though he by his omniscience stands in no need of such evidences. Vid. "Miscellanies," no. 689.17

The objection18 will be that God may do evil, that good may come of it. Answer: I don't argue that God may commit evil that good may come of it, but that he may will that evil should come to pass, that good may come of it; and permits that it may come to pass, that good may come of it. 'Tis in itself absolutely evil for any being to commit evil, that good may come of it; but it would be no evil, but good, even in a creature, to will that evil should come to pass, if he had wisdom sufficient to see certainly19 that good would come of it, and that more good would come to pass in that way than another. And the only reason why it would not be lawful for a creature to permit evil to come to pass, and that it would not be wise and good and virtuous in him so to do, is that he has not perfect wisdom or sufficiency, so as that it is fit such an affair should be trusted with him20—he goes beyond his line, he goes out of his province, he meddles with things too high for him. It is everyone's duty to do things fit for them in their sphere, and commensurate to their power. God never betrusted this providence into the hands of creatures of finite understandings, nor is it proper that he should.

If a prince were of perfect and comprehensive wisdom and foresight, and should see that an act of treason would be for the great advancement of the welfare of his kingdom, it might be well and virtuous in him, to will that that act of treason should come to pass; yet it would be very ill in him if he did not, and would be a21 prudent and wise thing in him to permit it to come to pass, to let the traitor alone to go on in his own way, and not by power to restrain him; and yet he22 might hate the treason at the same time, and he23 might properly also give forth laws at the same time forbidding it24 upon pain of death, and might hold these laws in force against this traitor.

The only difficulty in this, they25 themselves allow, though God permits sin, and that if he permit it, it will come to pass, so that the only difficulty about the act of the will that is in it, is26 that God should will evil to be, that good may come of it: but it is demonstrably true that if God sees that good will come of it, and more good than otherwise, so that, when the whole series of events is viewed by God, and all things balanced, the sum total of good with the evil is more than without—all being subtracted that need to be subtracted, and added that is to be added—I say, if the sum total of good, thus considered, be greatest, greater than the sum without it, then it will follow that God, if he be a wise and holy Being, must will it.

For if this sum total that has the evil in it, when what the evil subtracts is subtracted, has yet the greatest good, then 'tis the best sum total, better than the sum total that has no evil in it. But if, all things considered, it be really the best, how can it be otherwise, than that it should be chosen by an infinitely wise and good Being, whose holiness and goodness consist in always choosing what is best? Which does it argue most—wisdom or folly, a good disposition or an evil one—when two things are set before a being, the one better and the other worse, to choose the worse and refuse the better?

There is no inconsistency or contrariety between the declarative27 and perceptive will of God. It is very consistent to suppose that God may hate the thing itself, and yet will that it should come to pass; yea, I do not fear to assert that the thing itself may be contrary to God's will, and yet that it may be agreeable to his will that it should come to pass, because his will in the one case has not the same object as his will in the other case. To suppose God to have contrary wills towards the same object is a contradiction, but not to suppose him to have contrary wills about different objects. The thing itself, and that the thing should come to pass, are different; as is evident, because 'tis possible that one may be good, and the other may be evil. The thing itself may be evil, and yet it may be a good thing that it should come to pass. It may be a good thing that an evil thing should come to pass, and oftentimes it most certainly and undeniably is so, and proves so.

UNIVERSAL REDEMPTION. God himself, in some respects, treats persons according to their outward appearance, till their behavior proves 'em to be otherwise than they profess to be. He does as it were enter into covenant with them upon their profession, and in that covenant gives 'em Christ's benefits, his redemption and eternal life, as it were trusting to it, that they are sincere; and therefore he represents himself as under expectation of their behaving thus and that, and being disappointed and deceived, as a deceitful bow, having failed God's expectation, and so taking it from them. This may be called a visible covenant. Those that are in this covenant, are of the VISIBLE CHURCH. These are sometimes called saints in Scripture, and visible saints; and these are called righteous men in Ezekiel, where he speaks of righteous men turning away from their righteousness parenthetical, which takes off the argument from thence for FALLING from GRACE. So such as these are called God's people, and elect and redeemed. And hence God is said to prove persons, that he may know what was in their hearts, to know whether they are sincere, as though he only trusts to their profession and had a mind to know whether their profession was sincere. Vid. "Miscellanies," no. 689, and especially §6, touching those texts, 2 Peter 2:1 and Hebrews 10:29.28

UNIVERSAL REDEMPTION. When in Scripture phrase it is said that Christ died for all parenthetical, that don't seem to have so much of the appearance of universality, as if he had said that he died for every one; and yet if he had, it would not have implied an universality as to persons in Scripture expression. As is evident by Luke29 16:16, "since that time the kingdom of God is preached, and every man presseth into it"; i.e. every sort, the Pharisees, the Sadducees, the publicans and soldiers, agreeable to . . ..30

And therefore, when it is said Christ "tasted death for every man," Hebrews 2:9, it don't follow that an universality is intended.

Vid. notes on 1 Timothy 2:4.31

Dr. Stebbing himself, a noted advocate for Arminianism, insists upon it, when he thinks it serves his turn, that to argue that things asserted in Scripture are to be understood universally from such general terms, is unreasonable; speaking of that prophecy of Joel 2:28-29, in his Treatise Concerning the Operations of the Holy Spirit, 2nd ed., p. 84,32 he says as follows:

It is said indeed that this promise must be general, because it is by the Prophet extended to all flesh, i.e. to all persons; and because the Apostle, speaking of the same promise to the Jews, says, it is "to you, and to your children, and to all that are afar off," Acts 2:39. But will these men always argue from general expressions after the same manner? How easily then might they prove from Scripture, against common sense and experience, that the power of warning miracles belongs to every Christian? For thus says our Savior, Mark 16:17, "these signs shall follow them that believe; In my name they shall cast out devils; they shall speak with new tongues," etc. Will our enthusiasts dare to pretend to such gifts as these? No, they will not. And yet, they are promised to them that believe; which phrase as much comprehends every Christian, as the phrase all flesh comprehends every person. From this therefore, and numberless instances that might quickly be produced, it is evident that general terms are not always to be taken in their full latitude.

PARTICULAR REDEMPTION OF THOSE WHO WENT TO HEAVEN BEFORE CHRIST'S DEATH.] They33 insist upon it, that Christ so died for all, that he died with a design that all should have the benefit of his death. Let us consider how it was with respect to those that were dead and gone into eternity before his death. 'Tis evident that he died for those of them that went to heaven, with an actual aim at their benefit. But how unreasonable would it be to say this of those which were already gone to hell? And the reason why it is unreasonable, is because the case of those who were already gone to hell was so decided, that there could be no hope of any benefit to them by his death. He knew certainly they would have none; the matter was already made as certain as it was possible.

UNIVERSAL AND PARTICULAR REDEMPTION. It should be, and 'tis very unreasonable to suppose, that Christ aimed at that benefit by his death, which he knew with utmost possible certainty never would be obtained. But the case is just the same as to God's view with regard to them that should go to hell after Christ's death, as it was with those had gone to hell before: the damnation of both was alike certain to him, alike clearly and fully seen, alike present with him; and therefore it was equally impossible in himself, for him properly and in strictness of speech, to aim at one more than the other. The whole reason why I can't aim at bringing to pass some benefit to a person that lived many ages ago, as well to some person now living—as, for instance, at relieving Joseph when persecuted by his brethren, as well as helping one of my neighbors in distress—is that I can have no expectation of the former, and may have some hope of the latter. If any say, "There is something further in the case; I not only know the former never will be, but I know it cannot be, I know it to be impossible": I answer, that the only reason why I can't aim at a thing I look upon impossible, is that I cannot have any expectation or hope that it will be, for this reason, because it is impossible; and 'tis impossible I should do anything from a hope of an event that I have no hope of. The impossibility has influence to prevent my aiming at it, no otherwise than it cuts off hope of obtaining it; so that if I am equally without hope of an event some other way, or on some other account, besides the impossibility of it, 'tis all one as to the possibility of my aiming at it, or making it my end in what I do, or acting with a view to it or from a hope of it—which are the same thing.

Christ's death was of virtue and influence for salvation among different sorts of persons, just after the same manner in David's time as it is now; and it had respect to various sorts of persons then, in the same manner as now: for though Christ's death was future, it was present with God, and he knew for whom he would die, and what love and respect he would die with, and be influenced by in dying. Supposing that Christ's death had been deferred longer still—supposing it had been deferred to the end of the world, till all that ever should be saved were gathered in—but his death were very clearly revealed now, with the scheme of redemption: would it be proper, in treatises of Christ's redemption, to say it was universal in the manner that Arminians do; or, when speaking of his future death, to say it would be for all, aiming at the salvation of all mankind?

The most universal expressions of any that are found in the Scripture, are sometimes used there in a limited sense, as Colossians 1:23, "the gospel . . . which was preached to every creature which is under heaven"; and to the like purpose is that, John 1:9, "This is the true Light, which lighteneth every man that cometh into the world." In these places, the expressions are as if what was meant, was most universal with respect to individuals, when yet what is really intended is only universal with respect to kinds, and without limitation by any rule, or being limited or restrained from any one individual person by any rule given.

As to that place, 1 Corinthians 15:22, "as in Adam all die, so in Christ shall all be made alive," it is in effect explained by the Apostle, in the same chapter, to mean all that are in or of these two Adams, vv. 45-49, especially v. 48: that as all that are of the earthly parenthetical, are dusty like him, and die and turn to dust, so are all such as are heavenly, and of the heavenly Adam; they are heavenly like him, and immortal.

See concerning God's willing all men to be saved, under the head of Predestination, pp. 000-00.34

If ever I should publish anything concerning universal and particular redemption, remember to read Fraser's Treatise on Justifying Faith, from p. 84 to p. 270.35

See little long papers, pp. 9-10, 21-23.36

Christ's incarnation, his labors and sufferings, his resurrection, etc., were for the salvation of such as are not elected, in Scripture language, in the same sense as the means of grace are for their salvation; in the same sense as the instruction, counsels, warnings and invitations that are given them, are for their salvation.

SAVING GRACE DIFFERS FROM COMMON GRACE IN NATURE AND KIND

See bundle of papers on this subject.43

To suppose only a gradual difference, would not only be to suppose that some in a state of damnation are, within an infinitely little, as good as some that are in a state of salvation parenthetical; but this, taken with the Arminian notion of men's falling from grace, will naturally lead us to determine that many that are once in a state of salvation, may be in such a state, and out of it, scores of times in a very short space: for though he is in a state of salvation, he may be but just in, and may be infinitely near the limits, between a state of salvation and damnation. And the habits of grace being only contracted and raised by consideration and exercise, and the exertion of the strength of the mind, and lost when a man falls from grace by the intermission or cessation of these, and by the contrary acts and exercises of man,44 so that45 the habits and principles of virtue are raised and sunk, brought into being and abolished by these things, and both the degree of them and the being of them wholly depend on them, the consequence will naturally be, that46 when a man is first raised to that degree of a virtuous disposition, as to be in a state of salvation, and the degree of virtue is almost infinitely near the dividing line, it will naturally be liable to be a little raised or sunk every hour, according as the thoughts and exercises of the mind are; as the mercury in the barometer or thermometer is never perfectly at rest, but is always rising or subsiding, according to the weight of the atmosphere or the degree of heat.

See little long papers, pp. 32, etc.47

FOREKNOWLEDGE OF GOD

That God foreknows the sins of men, Isaiah 33:1,48 "when thou shalt cease to . . . deal treacherously, they shall deal treacherously with thee." So Christ foreknew that Judas would betray him, and that Peter would deny. He foresaw that it would certainly be; he says, "Verily I say unto you." So he foreknew the persecutions of the church, how that brother should deliver brother, and children rise up against their parents, to cause 'em to be put to death, etc. parenthetical. How often does Christ foretell his disciples of the persecutions they should meet with; and how much persecution, with the aggravated wickedness of it, is foretold in the Revelation. And what wickedness of Antichrist, that should share in the antichristian apostasy, is foretold by the apostles in their Epistles. And what wickedness of the scoffers of the last days, is foretold by the apostle Peter.
