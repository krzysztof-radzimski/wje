Living Unconverted Under an Eminent Means of Grace

Solomon Stoddard died on February 11, 1729, upon which Edwards became Northampton's sole pastor. While Edwards was privately questioning his grandfather's views on various aspects of conversion in the "Miscellanies" and in notebooks such as "Faith" and "Notes on Conversion From Various Authors," he was more diplomatic in public. He encouraged a shift in congregational loyalty to himself by deferring to the legacy of Stoddard in sermons, chief among them Living Unconverted Under an Eminent Means of Grace, delivered very shortly after his grandfather's death and possibly for a town fast day.1

In The Torments of Hell Are Exceeding Great, Edwards had likened the people of Northampton to "stocks and stones unmovable, that are talked to and preached to, to make them bestir themselves, in vain." Stoddard had been a powerful revival preacher, yet Edwards found himself confronted with a congregation who, having heard the truths of religion delivered in the most convincing of ways by that "eminent means of grace," Stoddard himself, were now deaf. They have become, he flatly states here, "sermon proof." The problem of his congregation's lackadaisical attitude would prompt Edwards in the years to come to search out the meaning of language and the sensationalist nature of words; in this sermon, he adopts the short-term strategy of paying homage to Stoddard's memory and of using the lesson of his "removal" to affect his listeners. Edwards flatters the memory of his grandfather by imitating his style.

The text, Jeremiah 6:29–30, provides the controlling metaphor for the sermon. Stoddard himself is the "bellows" that are burnt out; all that is left now is the "reprobate silver" that is "rejected." A people who have long been privileged with such an "eminent means of conversion" and

-- 358 --

yet have not been converted are in danger of being "left of God." Like Jerusalem of old, the chosen city of God, "they have a great deal more to answer for than others that have lived under less advantages." Their sins are more "aggravated," and they themselves more "obstinate" and ungrateful, because they have gone on in their heedless ways under the clear light of the gospel and with the knowledge of God's laws. Being accustomed to hearing the preaching of a powerful minister, they have become "effectually hardened," so that inferior preaching has no hope of reaching them. "They that have been used to the roaring of cannons," Edwards says in a self-effacing moment, "won't be likely to be much moved with the noise of small arms." Those who have "stood it out" may even be guilty of resisting the Holy Ghost.

Edwards' only use is "of Awakening to the unconverted of this town," where he makes his most explicit references to Stoddard and his parishioners' guilt in not being more religious. "You have stood it out until the bellows are burnt," but "the founder melted in vain as to you. He did not cease blowing till the bellows were worn out… trying if he could not extract some true silver from amongst the lead." Stoddard's death may mean that there are no more "elect vessels" in Northampton for God to gather in. "God sent Mr. Stoddard to this place because he had an elect people here… and it looks darkly upon you that are left." Like the children of Israel who witnessed all of God's wonderful works in the wilderness and still did not believe, the people of Northampton will be refused entrance into the land of promise; God will now take away his Spirit and leave those left behind to increase in sin. "Woe to them that go to hell out of Northampton and that lived under Mr. Stoddard's ministry!" Edwards warns. When they are in hell, the sermons they heard "won't be forgotten, though you have so little regarded them…. Though you was so quiet under 'em when you heard 'em, yet when you hear 'em again, you will not be so quiet; they'll be thunder to you." The craft of this sermon demonstrates Edwards' mastery of Stoddard's style and his readiness to move on and make his own mark on awakening preaching.

Living Unconverted Under an Eminent Means of Grace Jeremiah 6:29–30.2 The bellows are burnt, the lead is consumed of the fire; the founder melteth in vain: for the wicked are not plucked away. Reprobate silver shall men call them, because the Lord rejected them.

Jeremiah was one of the last prophets that was sent to warn Judah and Jerusalem before their destruction. He lived to see their destruction and captivity into Babylon. He speaks abundantly in this prophecy of that destruction, warning the people of it and pressing it upon them, that they would reform their evil ways, that they might escape it.

He is upon this subject in this chapter, setting forth [the] greatness of the sin and obstinacy of the people and foretelling their destruction. They had had many prophets to warn them and declare the word of God to them, but they had turned a deaf ear to them all; and they filled up the measure of their sin and obstinacy by their treatment of the prophet Jeremiah.

God tells him that he had set him as a tower and fortress amongst the people, to try their ways, in the Jeremiah 6:27; and God compares his trying of them by the threatenings and warnings of his word to the trials of metals in the fire. He had said in [the] verse foregoing the text that they were brass and iron. Jeremiah 6:28, "They are grievous revolters, walking with slanders: they are brass and iron; they are all corrupters"; that is, they are a base metal, they are not true silver and gold, as God's true and faithful people are, but they are like the baser sort of metals. To the same purpose is that, Ezekiel 22:18, "Son of man, the house of Israel is to [me] become dross: all they are brass, and tin, and iron, and lead, in the midst of the furnace; they are even the dross of silver."

-- 360 --

The way of trying of gold and silver was in furnaces of fire, as we have often mention of it in God's Word. 'Tis this is alluded to in our text, where the case is represented thus: that God had been by his prophets trying of these metals in the fire, to see if he could not extract some gold and silver out of it, for metals, when they are tried in furnace, that that is base metal will be consumed of the fire, and what remains will be only pure metal. The prophets had been blowing the fire so long that the bellows were burnt out, and yet they could extract no good silver; it all proved to be lead.

"The lead is consumed of the fire." As I said before, when they tried metals, the baser part would be consumed and the purer part would remain; but it was all consumed, it all proved lead, and so consumed of the fire. So that the founder melted in vain: he gets nothing by it; he put something into the fire, but he gets nothing out: all that he put in is consumed of the fire.

"The wicked are not plucked away." That is, if they were not wicked, they would bear the fire, and so the founder would have something to take out. But they are all refuse metal that burns up in the fire, and so they ben't plucked out of the fire no more.

"Reprobate silver shall men call them"; that is, refuse silver. The meaning of the word "reprobate" is "rejected," or "disapproved." By reprobate or refuse silver means hypocrites, that have a gloss like silver, but when they come to be tried in the fire prove but lead and dross; and therefore God will not accept of them, but they shall be consumed of the fire.

Thus it was with Judah and Jerusalem. God had been a long time trying if he could not make something of them. He had sent his former prophets to try them, to see if they could not, by blowing, extract some silver and gold from amongst them, but they had tried as long as they lived. The prophets, they did not live forever, and God had tried till he was weary; but now his patience was worn out, and his former prophets were dead, and they sought Jeremiah's life, so that the bellows were as it were burnt out.

Doctrine.
It argues great danger of being finally left of God, when sinners have lived long unconverted under eminent means of conversion.

And that upon these three accounts:

I. Their sins are abundantly more aggravated than other men's.

-- 361 --

II. Their hearts are more hardened against means of grace than other men's.

III. They have resisted the Holy Ghost more than others, and so will be more likely to be finally left by him.

[Reason] I. Their sins are much more aggravated than other men's. All those that live under the gospel, that enjoy the means of grace, let them [be] more or less eminent: if they continue impenitent, their sins are much greater than the sins of other men, much greater than the sins of the heathen that worship the sun, moon and stars and bow down to all the host of heaven, and to images, and worship the devils; that are in brutish ignorance and have a brutish, sottish way of worship. God gives us to understand in his Word that he is nothing near so much provoked with their sins as he is with the sins of them that live under means of grace. Amos 3:2, "You only have I known of all the families that are in the earth: therefore will I punish you for all your iniquities"; that is, "You only have I acknowledged for my people, and have brought you near to myself."

The heathen nations that were round about Canaan, they lived in idolatry and witchcraft, in lust and cruelty and all ways of vice; they wholly rejected the true God and his religion. But yet God was not so angry with them as he was with Jerusalem, nor did he punish them so severely. Daniel 9:12, "And he hath confirmed his words, which he spake against us, and against our judges that judged us, by bringing upon us a great evil: for under the whole heaven hath not been done as hath been done upon Jerusalem."

God had rather that men were quite cold than lukewarm. That is, he had rather that they should be heathen than wicked professors of Christianity. God cannot endure such wicked men; they are exceeding loathsome to him; he will spew them out of his mouth (Revelation 3:15–16).3 And the Apostle tells us, 2 Peter 2:21, that we had better not have known the way of righteousness, than, after we have known, "to turn from the holy commandment delivered unto them."

And for the same reason, those that have lived in sin under the best and most eminent means, their sins are abundantly greater and more heinous and provoking unto God than the sins of those that have lesser advantages.

We have instances in Scripture of it. Chorazin and Bethsaida and Capernaum were cities that enjoyed extraordinary and very eminent

-- 362 --

means of grace. Other cities of the land also lived under means of grace: they had Moses and the prophets; yea, sometimes Christ and his disciples were in other cities. But in these cities, Christ was wont more commonly to converse, to preach and work miracles, and therefore Christ denounces a special woe against them. Matthew 11, beginning with the twentieth verse:

Then began he to upbraid the cities wherein most of his mighty works were done, because they repented not: Woe unto thee, Chorazin! woe unto thee, Bethsaida! for if the mighty works, which were done in you, had been done in Tyre and Sidon, they would have repented long ago in sackcloth and ashes. But I say unto you, It shall be more tolerable for Tyre and Sidon at the day of judgment, than for you. And thou, Capernaum, which are exalted unto heaven, shalt be brought down to hell: for if the mighty works, which have been done in thee, had been done in Sodom, it would have remained until this day. But I say unto you, That it shall be more tolerable for the land of Sodom in the day of judgment, than for thee.

Not that there were none converted in these cities by Christ's preaching and miracles. He had commonly a great many hearers that had a seeming delight to hear him. Multitudes used to flock after him, and throng and crowd him sometimes, so that he could not find time to eat his bread. And there were some that did sincerely believe on him. Christ was not altogether without success among them in his preaching, but the effect was nothing to the means, nothing such as God expected; and God was therefore exceeding angry with them, to destroy them with such a dreadful destruction as he threatened.

So it was with Jerusalem. They enjoyed eminent means of grace. Christ was often there: he would often have gathered her children as a hen gathers her chickens under her wings. And there were some there also converted, but the bulk of them would not, and rejected Jesus Christ and despised his gospel; and therefore God was so provoked, that a little after he destroyed them in such a manner as gave the most remarkable tokens of God's vengeance that ever was seen in any temporal destruction. They had such tribulation as Christ foretold that never was, nor ever should be, on any nation (Matthew 24:21).

So it was with Jerusalem before its first destruction. They enjoyed distinguishing means of grace. Samaria and the Ten Tribes that were carried away captive by Salmanesser had great means of grace; they

-- 363 --

had many prophets sent to them, to warn them before their destruction. But Jerusalem had greater because it was the city that God had chosen, and there was the temple where God dwelt. And therefore God tells Jerusalem that Samaria had not committed half her sins. Ezekiel 16:51, "Neither hath Samaria committed half of thy sins; but thou hast multiplied thine abominations more than they." How was it that Jerusalem's sins were so much greater than Samaria's? Samaria left the true worship of God in Jeroboam's time and never returned to it anymore till the captivity by Salmanasser. They worshipped the calves at Bethel and Dan and had not one reformation. Of all the kings they had, they had not so much as one good one, whereas Jerusalem had several good kings, several reformations, and they worshipped God in the temple, in the place that he chose. But here was the difference: Jerusalem had greater means than Samaria had, and therefore God reckons Samaria's sin not one half of Jerusalem's sins.

God, in Ezekiel 16:46–47, tells them that what Sodom and Samaria had done was a little thing in comparison of what Jerusalem had done. "And thine elder sister is Samaria, she and her daughters that dwell at thy left hand: and thy younger sister, that dwelleth at thy right hand, is Sodom and her daughters. Yet hast thou not walked after her ways, nor done after their abominations: but, as if that were a very little thing, thou wast corrupted more than they in all thy ways."

And so it surely is nowadays. They that live impenitent under the most powerful and eminent means of grace, they have a great deal more to answer for than others that have lived under less advantages. Those that have lived under eminent gospel means, they have greater advantages than even Jerusalem itself. If Sodom's sins and Samaria's sins were not half Jerusalem's sins, Jerusalem's sins were not half so great as the sins of some that live under great gospel light.

The sins of them that live under eminent [means] are much greater than others', principally upon these three accounts: they sin with more presumption, with more obstinacy, and more ingratitude.

First. Their sin is attended with greater presumption. They sin against clearer knowledge. They that sin, and distinctly know that they do sin, they sin with horrid presumptions and must be more especially provoking. He that knows his Lord's will, and don't do it, "shall be beaten with many stripes" [Luke 12:47].

They that have lived long under eminent means, they know better than others what sort of God that is they sin against and what authority they contemn, and what threatenings they despise and run the venture

-- 364 --

of, and what reason there is that they should not sin; and therefore they sin with a high hand. When men sin through ignorance, God is willing to make allowances and more easily forgives; but [not] when they sin presumptuously. Numbers 15:27–28, Numbers 15:30–31, "If any soul sins through ignorance, then he shall bring a she-goat of the first year for a sin offering. And the priest shall make an atonement" for him. "But the soul that doeth ought presumptuously, whether he be born in the land, or a stranger, the same reproacheth the Lord; and that soul shall be cut off from among his people. Because he hath despised the word of the Lord, and hath broken his commandment, that soul shall utterly be cut off; his iniquity shall be upon him."

Paul tells us how great a sinner he was, how he persecuted the church of God. But, says he, "I obtained mercy, because I did it in ignorance and unbelief" (1 Timothy 1:13), intimating that sins that are committed against knowledge are not so easily forgiven. So Christ prays the Father to forgive his crucifiers because they knew not what they did. But men that have lived in sin under eminent means of grace, they know what they did.

Sinning against such light is as it were rebelling against God to his face. Let us consider how provoking would it be to a master or a prince to have a servant or subject despise his authority and disobey his commands openly to his very face, especially if his positive command were joined to warnings and threatenings.

Those that have lived in sin under eminent means, their sin has broken through many greater restraints than other men's. Their sin has been more victorious. He has had more powerful restraints than other men; he has more warnings and more conviction set before him. Sin that is so victorious over restraints must needs appear greater and more heinous.

Second. They [that] have lived long unconverted under eminent means, they have sinned with very great obstinacy. If a man sins once willfully against light and knowledge, it is very provoking; but abundantly more so when they [show] obstinacy under repeated commands, and repeated reproofs and threatenings, and repeated endeavors to bring him to obedience. And there is nothing is a greater sign of men's approaching ruin than obstinacy under powerful means and endeavors to bring them to repentance. Proverbs 29:1, "He, that being often reproved hardeneth his neck, shall suddenly be destroyed, and that without remedy."

And we have abundancy of instances of it in Scripture: God did not

-- 365 --

destroy the old world till he had often reproved them by Noah; nor Sodom till he had sent them Lot, a preacher of righteousness; nor Pharaoh and the Egyptians till they had been often reproved by Moses and in God's providence; nor Saul till he had often reproved him by Samuel and otherwise; nor Samaria, nor Jerusalem, nor Chorazin and Bethsaida, etc.

Third. They that continue unconverted under such eminent means, their sin is greater because it is aggravated by a greater ingratitude. Those to whom God gives extraordinary means, they are highly favored and privileged by the distinguishing mercy of God above others. They are exalted up to heaven, as Christ speaks of Capernaum [Matthew 11:23]. God is so gracious to them as to give the best means, and privileges them above their neighbors. And therefore when they continue impenitent, they abuse [the] distinguishing mercy of God: and what is more provoking than to have kindness abused and trampled on? Seeing therefore that those who have long continued impenitent under eminent means of grace, their sin is so much greater and more provoking than the sin of others, they are in greater danger of eternal ruin; their sin is seldomer pardoned.

Reason II. Another reason why such are in greater danger of being finally ruined, is that they are more hardened against means than others.

'Tis always the effect of God's word, either to harden or to soften. It opens the eyes of some, and turns them from darkness unto light. The eyes4 of others are closed by it. Isaiah 6:10, "Make the heart of their people fat, and make their ears heavy, and shut their eyes; lest they should see with their eyes, and hear with their ears, and understand with their heart, and convert, and be healed." Isaiah was commanded to do this no other way, but only going and preaching the word of God to them.

The light of the gospel enlightens some, and others it strikes blind. It brings scales over their eyes, as the light which Paul saw did upon his eyes. And the better and more powerful the means are, the more effectually do they harden if they don't soften. There can be no more effectual way to harden a man than for him to go and live in sin under some eminent minister. They that have been used to powerful preaching, and are used to hear and stand it out, they grow sermon proof.

If a man has been used to more ordinary means, and that don't

-- 366 --

convert him, there is some hope that he may be profited by extraordinary means; but they that have stood it out under eminently powerful means, they are more effectually hardened against that that is inferior. They that have been used to encounters with lions, and to conquer them, won't be afraid of weaker animals. They that have been used the roaring of cannons, won't be likely to be much moved with the noise of small arms.

They that have been used to eminently powerful means, and have stood it out to the last as long as those means lasted, they have got the victory over means of grace. They have been used to the battle, and they have been used to conquer; and they that have been used to conquer, they grow bold in sin. Their sin will not be very easily conquered, and especially when they have been used to conquer champions.

Reason III. [The third] and last reason of the doctrine is that such as have lived long unconverted under eminent means, they commonly have been more used to resist the Holy Ghost than others, and therefore are in more danger of being finally left by the Holy Ghost. God very commonly gives strivings of his Spirit together with eminent outward means of grace. Those that live long under a powerful ministry do experience more or less of the awakening motions and strivings of God's Spirit, however stupid they may come to be at last.

And those that, notwithstanding these strivings, continue impenitent, 'tis because they have resisted the Holy Ghost: and that is the way to provoke the Holy Ghost finally to leave them. Thus did the Jews in Christ's and the apostles' times provoke God finally to leave and forsake them. Acts 7:51, "Ye stiff-necked and uncircumcised in heart and ears, ye do always resist the Holy Ghost." Their long resisting God's Spirit made him so dreadfully to destroy them.

Application.

[The] Use [is] of Awakening to the unconverted of this town. There have [been] few places that have enjoyed such eminent powerful means of grace as you of this place have enjoyed. You have lived all your days under a most clear, convincing dispensation of God's word. The whole land is full of gospel light, but this place has been distinguishingly blessed of God with excellent means for a long time under your now deceased minister.

And it argues a dismal degree of obduracy and blindness, that persons could stand it out under such a ministry. In what a clear and

-- 367 --

awakening manner have you hundreds of times had your danger and misery in a natural condition set before you! How clearly have you had the way of salvation shown to you, and how movingly have you had the encouragements of the gospel offered to you!

Such as can live all their days under such means of awakening and of conversion, and have stood it out and have been proof against such preaching, are undoubtedly of exceeding hard hearts. They that are still unawakened, doubtless their hearts are much harder than if they had not lived under such great advantages. Powerful preaching, if it don't awaken, it hardens more than other preaching.

Those means are now gone; you'll have them no more. You have stood it out until the bellows are burnt. You had the preaching, the calls and warnings of your eminent deceased minister till he was worn out in calling and warning and exhorting of you. God was so gracious, and so loathe that you should perish, that he continued his ability of preaching to wonderment. But the founder melted in vain as to you. He did not cease blowing till the bellows were worn out, as it were burnt out, in vain, trying if he could not extract some true silver from amongst the lead. He was very loathe to give you over till he had persuaded, and God seemed loathe to give you over by continuing of him so long to call upon you and warn you. But how many wicked are there that are not yet plucked away? And it truly looks with a dark aspect upon them. They have reason to be afraid whether ever they shall be plucked away, and that for these reasons:

First. They have missed a precious opportunity which God put into their hands. It was a great price5 that God put into your hands, by ordering it to be your lot to live under such a ministry. God was nigh to you; you lived under a burning and shining light. This opportunity has been continued beyond what you could expect, but yet you have let it slip. You have not improved the price that God put into your hands.

Solomon tells us that to everything there is a season and a time [Ecclesiastes 3:1–2]. God gives men some special opportunity for every purpose and every business, wherein, if they set themselves about it in good earnest, they may much more easily succeed in it than at another time. So there is a time to be converted in, when God is near and when men are under more special advantages for that work. But, as Solomon observes, the misery of man is great upon him, because he knows not his time. Ecclesiastes 8:6–7, "Because to every purpose there is time

-- 368 --

and judgment, therefore the misery of man is great upon him. For he knoweth not that which shall be: and who can tell him when it shall be?" Your misery therefore is great upon you, that you did not improve that time which you don't know but was your only time. You promised yourself good opportunity hereafter, but you know [not] that which shall be, and who can tell you when it shall be. And Ecclesiastes 9:12, "For man also knoweth not his time: as the fishes that are taken in an evil net, and as the birds that are caught in the snare; so are the sons of men snared in an evil time, when it falleth suddenly upon them." Men that don't know their time and improve their opportunity, they promise themselves future opportunity; but their6 destruction overtakes them at once when they think nothing of it, as a bird that is carelessly feeding is caught by the snare when they suspected no evil, and as the fishes are encompassed with the net while they are briskly playing in the water. It may therefore justly be a terrifying consideration, that you have missed such an opportunity as you have had.

Second. This will appear if we consider the end why God sends eminent ministers to a place and continues them there. He does it that his elect people that are there may be gathered in. Sometimes God sends some eminent messengers to a place only because he has one elect vessel there. Thus Christ must needs go to Samaria, a place where he was not wont to go, only to convert the woman of Samaria. So when he went to the coast of Tyre and Sidon, it seems to be for nothing else but to show mercy to the woman of Canaan. And when he went over into the country of the Gadarenes, it seems to be on purpose to deliver and convert him that was possessed with a legion of devils.

So when God has many elect in a place, God usually sends some eminent minister to be there to gather them in. Thus Paul continued a year and six months at Corinth by God's direction, because God told him he had much people in that city (Acts 18:10). Thus when Paul assayed to go into Bythinia, the Spirit suffered him not (Acts 16:7). It was because God had not any work for him to do there, any people to gather in; but he was commanded to go and preach at Macedonia, for God had many elect to be gathered in there, as appeared by the effect of his preaching among them. Acts 16:9, "And a vision appeared to Paul in the night; there stood a man of Macedonia, and prayed him, saying, Come over into Macedonia and help us."

And God sent Mr. Stoddard to this place because he had an elect

-- 369 --

people here, and he continued him till he had done the work his Master designed him for. Many have been gathered in by his ministry, and it looks darkly upon you that are left, especially you that have lived unconverted a long while under his ministry.

If you hope that God will bless those means that may be used for time to come, if God does do so, it would be very just if he should resolve that none of you who have stood out against the calls and warnings of your former minister should have any benefit of it, but that his blessing of the means of grace for time to come should be upon your little ones, the rising generation that have not been guilty of despising and being unprofitable under those eminent means that you have despised.

The children of Israel in the wilderness enjoyed eminent means of grace. They see all God's wonders in Egypt and how he had brought them out with an outstretched arm, and they had seen God's miracles at the Red Sea and had heard God speaking from Mount Sinai, but they yet continued disobedient and stiff-necked. Therefore God swears in his wrath that none of them but Joshua and Caleb should enter into Canaan; but their little ones, that had not been guilty of contemning those eminent means, they should enter in. So it would be most just if God should deal with you that have despised and been obstinate under these powerful means you have enjoyed.

Third. God sometimes takes away his Spirit from a people when he takes away eminent means of grace. Thus it was in Christ's time: while Christ lived among them, the Jews enjoyed eminent means of grace, and God's Spirit strove with them; but when Christ died, the Spirit of God left them and their day of grace was past. Christ, when he was going up to Jerusalem to be crucified, he laments over the city, and says, "If thou hadst known, even thou, at least in this thy day, the things which belong unto thy peace! but now they are hid from thine eyes" [Luke 19:42]. These eminent means of grace were at an end, and so their day of grace was at an end. There were eminent means afterwards by the preaching of the apostles and their miracles, but yet God put an end to their day of grace because they had outstood those eminent [means]. So that whatever endeavors may be used with sinners for time to come, yet those that have long continued obstinate under so powerful a ministry are in great danger of being left by the Spirit of God.

We have another instance in Israel after Joshua's time. Judges 2:7, "The people served the Lord all the days of Joshua, and of the elders

-- 370 --

that outlived Joshua, who had seen all the great works of the Lord, that he did for Israel." But when they were dead, they soon departed from God, and God departed from them.

Sinners therefore that have long continued in this town so have great reason to be startled by the consideration of what they have enjoyed and what condition they are now in. Consider how dreadful your punishment will be if you continue impenitent to the last. Woe to them that go to hell out of Northampton and that lived under Mr. Stoddard's ministry! We are ready to wonder at the wickedness we hear there is in some parts of the world, in the West Indies and in other places; but they han't one half of the sins to answer as obstinate sinners will have that go from this place.

And how dreadful is the case of those vicious persons that yet continue unreformed. How doleful will the punishment of drunkards be that have been unreformed by all means they have enjoyed and that yet continue unreformed.

The many powerful awakening sermons that you have heard won't be forgotten, though you have so little regarded them. They'll all be remembered, and you must hear 'em rehearsed again. Though you was so quiet under 'em when you heard 'em, yet when you hear 'em again, you will not be so quiet; they'll be thunder to you. Every word will pierce your heart through and through with torments.
