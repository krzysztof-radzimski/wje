Glorying in the Savior

In this sacrament sermon, Edwards shows how believers are brought together to constitute the invisible church, of which Christ is the head. Formerly, Edwards notes in the explication, only Abraham's seed was the subject of God's favor. But Israel was a "typical nation" and represented "the true people of Jesus Christ in all ages." It is through Christ that Abraham's spiritual seed is justified.

After distinguishing the people of Christ from unbelievers in the Doctrine, Edwards dwells on what it means for the blessed to glory in Christ, and why they should do so. Glorying in Christ means having a sense of the great privileges bestowed by God and declaring that sense. Believers should boast of Christ's excellencies and glory in their dependence on him and in his honor because he is the most righteous, just, and gracious Lord. As they are redeemed sinners, believers should praise God (for instance, with "divine song") and not glory in their own pride or think they can be saved through their own self-righteousness.

From the beginning of the sermon, Edwards takes aim at what he perceives to be the superficial religion of many of his parishioners. Though many may "profess a better religion," yet they "improve" it so little that they should be ashamed. He criticizes hypocrites whose "tongues and outside" belong to Christ, but not their hearts. The doctrine, he states, "reproves" those who "value and esteem themselves" because of any "natural qualification" they may have. Challenging his listeners' assumptions about their status as saints, Edwards goes on to denounce any who "glory" in their "party among Christians" and in their "profession," which has gained them admittance to the sacraments.

We can surmise that Glorying in the Savior was preached for the sacrament service, most likely held on November 2, 1729, that was announced at the conclusion of the sermon on 1 Peter 3:4 (133). In the first head of the first proposition, Edwards refers to attending on the ordinances of Christ, and in the Application he exhorts his listeners, "Particularly

-- 459 --

endeavor that you may attend on this ordinance of the Lord's Supper with the spirit of true glory."

∗ ∗ ∗

The manuscript consists of eight duodecimo leaves. Edwards repreached the Sermon in February 1754. He made some minor additions and deletions in a later ink, but gave no indication of whether he repreached the whole or part of the sermon. Portions of the sermon are outlined, particularly the latter part of the second and the whole of the third propositions, as well as the final exhortation of the Application.

-- 460 --

Glorying in the Savior Isaiah 45:25.1 In the Lord shall all the seed of Israel be justified, and shall glory.

In this latter part of the chapter, we have a prophecy of the calling of the Gentiles, or of their becoming of God's people, beginning with the twentieth verse: "Assemble yourselves and come; draw near together, ye that are escaped of the nations: they have no knowledge that set up the wood of their graven images, and pray unto a god that cannot save." In the twenty-second verse, Christ invites the Gentile nations to come to him and be saved: "Look unto me, and be ye saved, all the ends of the earth: for I am God, and there is none else." And in the twenty-third verse, he declares his immutable will of making the whole Gentile world acknowledge him by an oath: "I have sworn by myself, the word is gone out of my mouth in righteousness, and shall not return, that unto me every knee shall bow, every tongue shall swear." In the twenty-fourth verse, 'tis foretold how he shall be acknowledged and trusted in: "Surely, shall one say, in the Lord have I righteousness and strength: even to him shall men come; and all that are incensed against him shall be ashamed." And then we have an account, first, of the confusion that shall be brought upon his enemies; and second, of the great advantages and happiness which shall anoint those that are his people: the first of these in the latter part of the twenty-fourth verse, "All that are incensed against him shall be ashamed"; the latter in our text, "In the Lord shall all the seed of Israel be justified, and shall glory."

In the words we may take notice,

1. Of the subject spoken of, the seed of Israel. In the foregoing verses, it is prophesied that the Gentiles should look to Christ and [be]2

-- 461 --

saved, and that unto him their knee should bow and their tongues swear, and that in him they should say, "I have righteousness and strength." So that being justified and glorying is not here appropriated to the seed of Israel according to the flesh, but 'tis the spiritual Israel that is spoken of; not those that are of the loins, but those that are of the faith of Abraham. If they are Christ's, then they are Abraham's and Israel's seed. The nation of Israel was a typical nation; they were a type of the invisible church, the true people of Jesus Christ in all ages.

2. Two things spoken of them. The first, the act of God relating to them: "They shall be justified"; the last, an act of theirs: they "shall glory." Which is the opposite to being ashamed, spoken of in the last words of the foregoing verse: they "that are incensed against him shall be ashamed." But these, on the contrary, shall glory. 'Tis the last of these things predicated of the seed of Israel we shall insist on at this time.

3. Who is the foundation of this justification and glorying of the seed {of Abraham and Israel}. "In the Lord3 shall all the seed of Israel be justified, and shall glory." By which Jesus Christ is meant: for 'tis he that is spoken [of] in the preceding verses, in whom one shall say, he has righteousness {and strength}, and every knee [should] bow (Philippians 2:10–11).

Doctrine.
The people of Jesus Christ have great cause to glory in their Savior.

I. Who are meant by the people of Christ.

II. What is implied in glorying in Christ.

III. What cause they have to glory in him.

I. Who are meant by the people of Christ.

First. Not all those that are called Christians, or are his professed and visible people. They are not all Israel that are of Israel; there are multitudes of those that have Christ living that will [not] be owned by him.

There are great multitudes of those that call themselves "Christians." The part of the world that is called "the Christian world" is very large, comprising many whole nations. But Christ's flock is but small in comparison of [these]: they are but a handful; they are but thinly sown here and there in the Christian world.

-- 462 --

That which is commonly called "the Christian [world]"4 in distinction to the heathens, Jews and Mahometans is a mixed multitude of every kind, good and bad, drunkards, lascivious, covetous…. But the people of Christ, they are of one kind: the prevailing dispositions of their hearts are the [same],5 and their main work is the same.6

'Tis not all those that make a more particular profession of godliness. Many of these have no cause at all to glory; though indeed they profess a better religion, and enjoy greater light and have higher privileges, yet they so improve [them], that they have so much the more reason to be ashamed.

But,

Second, those whose hearts are truly united unto Christ, and who love and follow him. Those are truly Christ's people whose hearts—and not they whose tongues and outside—are his. The whole Christian world have a7 seeming union with Christ; they [have] a seeming subjection to him.8 They separate from the heathens and Mahometans and Jews, those open enemies, and profess to be on Christ's side; they have those outward and visible appearances of union with him, in that they go with God's people [and] attend on the ordinances9 of Christ; they seem to be his disciples, in that they outwardly hear his instructions, and seem to be his subjects, in that they hear his commands.

But God looks at the heart, and they only are indeed the people of Jesus Christ and of his flock that have given their hearts to him and serve him: such as have received him by faith, that really have been so convinced of the reality and sufficiency and excellency of Christ as a savior, and have entirely inclined and drawn the heart to him, to choose and acquiesce in him;1 such as love in truth, that have seen his

-- 463 --

excellency and desirableness; such as have subjected themselves to him and chosen him for their king; such as have indeed given themselves to him; such as have admitted Christ into the heart and placed him upon the throne there; such as have been begotten by the Spirit of Christ, been born of God, and been made partakers of Christ's nature and temper.

And such whose hearts have left all other things for his sake, and such as do forsake other things and cleave to him in their practice; such as are not of the world, but leave the world for Christ: such as these are the true Israel; these are the peculiar people of God. Israel of old was separated from all other nations; they were not reckoned among the nations. So these are not of the world: their hearts have left it for Christ, and they follow him and not the vanities of the world.

II. What is implied in the people of Christ glorying in Christ.

To "glory" is for a person to express his high esteem of his own advantages of excellency, honor or happiness above others. The word in the text in the original signifies to show forth one's own praise, or to exalt oneself with one's own praises. Therefore boasting or glorying is generally taken in an ill sense, as an expression of a haughty disposition.2 Thus when the Apostle, when he is setting forth the exceeding great wickedness of the heathen, he mentions this, amongst other things, that they were "boasters" (Romans 1:30). Self-praise is spoken of, as by the wise man, as a foolish thing. Proverbs 27:2, "Let another praise thee, and not thine own mouth; a stranger, and not thine own lips."

But there is one sort of glorying that is no manifestation of pride, but the contrary, and is most consistent with prudence and but this one sort.3 1 Corinthians 1:31, "Let him that glorieth, glory in the Lord."

Glorying in Christ contains these:

First. A humble and joyful sense of mind of the great privilege and happiness they have above others in the interest they have in Christ. Those that are so highly favored and privileged of God, God expects that they should be sensible of it: insensibility of it argues stupidity and ingratitude. 'Tis but a thing becoming one so favored, that he should be sensible how greatly he is distinguished from others. It becomes them to have a very high esteem of their own privilege in being in

-- 464 --

Christ Jesus; they should esteem themselves more happy in having an interest in Christ, than if they had the whole world in possession.

It becomes them to admire their own happiness, and their sense of it should excite exultation of heart and joy of soul. Christian joy is a principal part of the disposition exercised in glorying in Christ.4

Second. In declaring before others that joyful sense and the grounds of it. As it becomes a Christian that his heart be full of a joyful esteem of its own privilege and happiness, so it is becoming that [that] sense should be manifested and declared.

This joyful sense ought to be manifested in the behavior. A cheerful obeying of Christ's commands and adherence to him through all, even the greatest difficulties—enduring the cross and despising the shame, neglecting with contempt all other pleasures and profits and honors that are set before him to allure him—is the best way of manifesting that he esteems the happiness.

This is very proper to be done in divine songs. We find the Psalmist was very much in his divine songs enjoyed in glorying in his God; so was Moses, Deborah, and Hannah and the blessed Virgin.

And particularly at the Lord's Supper. 'Twas designed partly for this end, that we might, by celebrating this ordinance, give testimony of our sense of our own happiness.

And upon occasion, it is very proper for Christians to declare their sense of their superior happiness, particularly to those of other religions, as the children of Israel were wont to glory in their God. "Their rock is not as our Rock," say they [Deuteronomy 32:31]. They should likewise express it to one another.

Its being in Christ implies three things:

1. To glory of him. To boast of his excellencies and beauty, to speak of or otherwise to declare in a joyful, triumphant manner of his transcendent beauty and glory, as the spouse doth. Canticles 5:16, after describing him, she says: "This is my beloved, this is my friend, O daughters of Jerusalem." To boast of the acts of Christ, of his works, especially towards them in saving and redeeming of them, his wonderful love in doing and suffering so much for them.

To boast of his relation to them, as the spouse does. Canticles 2:16, "My beloved is mine, and I am his."

To glory [in]5 the promises of Christ.

-- 465 --

2. To glory in a dependence on Christ.6

3. To glory in his name and for his honor, and of their own honor, only as they are in him. 'Tis not so much a love to their own honor that disposes them thus to glory, as the glory of Christ. Psalms 115:1, "Not unto us, O Lord, not unto us, but unto thy name give glory, for thy mercy, and for thy truth's sake." The believer aims therein at the glory of Christ; he boasts and glories in Christ's name, because he would have all to see and own the glorious excellencies of Christ.

And he aims at his own honor only secondarily, as he is a member of Jesus Christ or a partaker with him in his glory. As the parts of the body partake with the head, there should be such an union of the believer's heart with Jesus Christ, that whatsoever honor Christ has, he ought to feel affected with joy as other men do when they themselves are honored, and they are to account it their own honor. But he should seek his own honor, considered as in this glorying in Christ, considered as separate from him.7

III. We are come now to inquire what cause they have thus to glory in Christ.

First. They have a more glorious Lord. Christ is the most glorious king: there are none of the sons of the mighty can be compared unto him. He is KINGS OF KINGS [Revelation 19:16], which phrase not only signifies that he rules over other kings, but that he is the most excellent king, as "Song of Songs" signifies the most excellent song.

He is a king of higher descent. Others are sons of nobles, but he is the Son of the Most High God; others have royal blood, but he has the divine nature.

His kingdom is of greater extent, who rules over heaven and earth; whose dominion is over the angels of heaven as well as over the potentates of the earth; who sits in heaven as his throne; whose kingdom is of greater duration, being an everlasting kingdom, and his dominion without end. He is a king of greater wisdom and strength.

He is a "King of righteousness" (Hebrews 7:2) who is perfectly just in his government. He is the "Prince of Peace" [Isaiah 9:6], a king of the most unparalleled clemency and grace. Never was any kingdom8 ruled by a government so mild and gentle and gracious.

He is exceeding gracious in the manner of his ruling his people by sweetly and powerfully influencing their hearts by his grace: not governing

-- 466 --

them against their wills, but by powerfully inclining their wills. Psalms 110:3, "Thy people shall be willing in the day of thy power." The laws of his kingdom, they are most excellent; his commandments are pure and righteous, altogether exceeding perfective of our nature, and such as above the laws of all other governments tend to the peace, the flourishing, the beautiful order, the excellency and the happiness of his people.

The invisible church of God is by far the most happy kingdom in the world. He that is their king is their father, their brother and husband, one that has a love stronger than death to his people, has infinite wisdom to guide him in governing of [them and] infinite strength to defend them. The citizens of Zion have great cause to glory in their king, agreeable to Balaam's observation of them, Numbers 23:21, "The shout of a king is among them."

How much a more glorious Lord have they over them than wicked men, who are under the prince of the power of the air, whose hearts are under the dominion of Satan, who have their reason and all their other faculties lorded over by impure lusts and vile affections.

How much a more glorious God have they than the heathen. How much [a] more glorious head of their religion have they than Mahometans. Psalms 149:2, "Let Israel rejoice in him that made him: let the children of Zion be joyful in their King."

Second. They have a far better savior. Indeed, he is the only true Savior. But there are many other things that men are trusting in to be saviors to them, but Jesus Christ is far the best. All other confidences will fail: they will prove but broken staves, [a] false foundation; they will not be able to accomplish salvation for those that trust in them. But Christ fully and completely finishes that work of salvation for all that trust in him.

He has more worthiness than other saviors. He is infinitely worthy, being a divine person and infinitely near and dear to God. He has that advantage that no other savior has, that he is in the bosom of the Father. He is so worthy and excellent in the sight of God, that God is always well-pleased in him and cannot fail of accepting all that are in him for his sake. The worthiness of other saviors, if it comes to be inquired into, will be found to be nothing; their excellency will prove deformity and loathsomeness.

Christ has a better righteousness. Christ performed a glorious righteousness for his people, perfectly fulfilled the law, being obedient unto death, and by his sufferings has fully satisfied divine justice and answered the law. 'Tis an infinitely more glorious righteousness than

-- 467 --

that that many other men trust in, even their own righteousness, which is as filthy rags; 'tis a righteousness that will justify 'em. They shall be justified, and therefore shall they glory.

More strength. All dangers and enemies. Help in all difficulties. Others trust in an arm of flesh. But these rejoice or glory in Christ Jesus, and have no confidence {in an arm of flesh}. More grace.

Third. They have a far better portion. Christ is above all others excellent, fairer than the sons of men, altogether lovely. Rose of Sharon [Canticles 2:1].9 How highly privileged are they that are united to so excellent a person, that have such an one for their best friend, and to be enjoyed by them forever.

They that have Christ, they have a soul-satisfying portion. They have the truest pleasures and comforts. Here is to be found the proper happiness of the soul. Least liable to accidents and change… Here is the best employment for the understanding…. Such as have Christ, they have better and greater riches than others…. Better honor… Far better pleasures than sensual men. The joys are more exquisitely delighting than ever was enjoyed by the greatest epicure. [There are] no pleasures like those that are by the enlightenings of the Spirit of Christ, the discoveries of the beauty of Christ and the manifestations of his love.

Their portion will be of longer duration. Others' portions will fail, but…1

They have upon these accounts great reason to glory:

1. Before the devil, their grand enemy. Satan to his utmost seeks the misery of mankind, and to his utmost endeavors to afflict God's people. But God will give them to truimph over him: glorying is included in triumphing. They shall tread upon the lion and adder, the young lion and dragon shall they trample under foot [Psalms 91:13].

After Satan has done his utmost, they are still out of his reach because they are in Christ. They are happy, and will be so to all eternity, and he most miserable.

2. Before wicked men, such as have their portion in this life, that think themselves much happier in the enjoyment of their worldly and sinful enjoyments than those that deny [them], that are proud of their wealth and honor and prefer themselves before [God].

Hypocrites that trust in their own righteousness and have dependence on what is outward.

{They have great reason to glory}, particularly,

-- 468 --

3. Before their persecutors. They may triumph over them. They can't hurt them, because they are in Christ Jesus. With all the mischief they can do them, after they have exerted themselves to the utmost to render them miserable and to make themselves happy, yet they are happier than they. By reviling.

A martyr in the flames may triumph over their persecutors, as many of 'em have done.

Though their persecutors may be their judges, and may have 'em in their power, may lord it over 'em, yet they are above 'em.

Application.

[Use] I. What reason have we to praise God, who has given us so much cause to glory in Christ Jesus, that we that deserve so much shame should have so much cause to glory. We were in a forlorn condition: we were depressed to the lowest depths of misery and wretchedness; we were filthy and abominable, had made ourselves viler than the earth, and deserved nothing but shame and everlasting contempt; we had nothing to glory in, but all the circumstances of our case were such as administered to us just cause of shame and confusion of face.

But God has been pleased to provide one for us to take away our guilt and disgrace, and to be the glory in the midst of us; to put great honor upon us, to be as a covering to hide our nakedness, and not only so, but to adorn us and make us glorious; to be to us wisdom, to bring us from our shameful ignorance and darkness; to be our righteousness for the removal of our guilt and to procure acceptance with God for us; to be our sanctification, to change [us] from sinful and loathsome to holy and amiable; to be our redemption, to deliver us from all trouble and danger, and make us happy and blessed forever; to bestow upon us gold tried in the fire, that of poor we might become rich, and that he might exalt us from the dunghill and set us among princes. That God should take us, who were under bondage to sin and Satan, and give us such a glorious victory over our adversaries, and cause us thus to triumph over those that had us captives and were so much stronger than we; that God gives [us] so much greater privileges than others, that we should have such a king, {is reason to praise God}.

Use II is of Reproof to all such as glory in anything else but in the Lord. Such as glory in themselves, that highly value and esteem themselves upon the account of anything of theirs, any natural qualification, acquired advantages or any of their own actions.

-- 469 --

It reproves all sorts of pride. Such as are lifted up with their own natural abilities, are proud because they have better sense [and] more wit than their neighbor. Such as are proud of their riches, and glory in their being in high and honorable stations or any outward advantages. Such as trust to these things. Jeremiah 9:23–24, "Thus saith the Lord, Let not the wise man glory in his wisdom, neither let the mighty man glory in his might, let not the rich man glory in his riches: But let him that glorieth glory in this, that he understandeth and knoweth me, that I am the Lord which exercise lovingkindness, judgment, and righteousness, in the earth: for in these things I delight, saith the Lord."

It especially reproves such as trust in their own righteousness. This is most directly contrary to glorying in Christ Jesus, that is, to set up ourselves as saviors to glory in instead of Christ.

And then the doctrine reproves such as glory in their being of such and such a party among Christians, that highly value themselves in a profession and outward advantages. Such as these have confidence in the flesh.

Exh. [I.] Counting all other things as loss and dung, strive to win Christ. Consider on the one hand, how worthless all those things are which men are wont to set their hearts upon, and on the other hand, what great cause of rejoicing and glorying.

You have nothing to glory in while you are out of Christ. Forsake all, sell all, that you may have this pearl of great price. Choose the better part that shall not be taken away from you.

Exh. II. Let the people of Jesus Christ be exhorted to glory in Christ Jesus. 'Tis a most becoming thing in the blessed people of Christ—for his people are a blessed people—for them to rejoice and glory in their King, their Savior and their portion. 'Tis a becoming thing that the shout of a king should be heard in that glorious city of Zion. 'Tis a most becoming sight to see that company of the redeemed of the Lord to be humbly triumphing over the world. Endeavor to obtain this spirit, which will be obtained only by obtaining much of the Spirit of Christ.

Particularly endeavor that you may attend on this ordinance of the Lord's Supper with the spirit of true glory.

In order to this, three things must be done:

First. Strive to see more of the excellency of Christ and of the happiness of having an interest in him. Consider of the glories of Christ's person. [The] glories of his benefits.

Second. Strive to get assurance of your interest in Christ. By having grace strong and lively.

-- 470 --

Third. To be more sensible of your absolute dependence on Christ. 'Tis a humble sense of this that is in the first place necessary in order to a right glorying in Christ Jesus, or else you'll glory in yourself, and in you own name and for your own honor. To be of a spirit of humility and dependence is the way to have the Spirit of God and of glory rest upon you.
