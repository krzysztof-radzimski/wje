Seasons of Ingathering
And I looked, and behold a white cloud, and upon the cloud one sat like unto the Son of man, having on his head a golden crown, and in his hand a sharp sickle. And another angel came out of the temple, crying with a loud voice to him that sat on the cloud, Thrust in thy sickle, and reap: for the time is come for thee to reap; for the harvest of the earth is ripe. And he that sat on the cloud thrust in his sickle on the earth; and the earth was reaped. And another angel came out of the temple which is in heaven, he also having a sharp sickle. And another angel came out from the altar, which had power over fire; and cried with a loud cry to him that had the sharp sickle, saying, Thrust in thy sharp sickle, and gather the clusters of the vine of the earth; for her grapes are fully ripe. And the angel thrust in his sickle into the earth, and gathered the vine of the earth, and cast it into the great winepress of the wrath of God. And the winepress was trodden without the city, and blood came out of the winepress, even unto the horse bridles, by the space of a thousand and six hundred furlongs. Revelation 14:14–20.

In the foregoing chapter we have a particular description of Antichrist and the dismal consequences of his presence in the world. In this chapter we have represented four things with relation to Antichrist, from the first verse to {the twelfth}. And in this last part of the chapter, in the words that have been read, now to be insisted on, we have represented the great and opposite dispensations of God towards the elect and reprobates at the time of the downfall of Antichrist, concerning which we may observe:

1. What the elect and reprobates are represented [as] in the words. The elect are represented as God's wheat or the good grain of his field, the wicked as the grapes of the vine. That 'tis God's elect is meant by the harvest, and reprobates by the vintage, is manifest by other scriptures. [The] elect [are described as] Gods wheat, Matthew 3:12, “Whose fan is in his hand, and he will thoroughly purge his floor, and gather his wheat into the garner; but he will burn up the chaff with unquenchable fire.” And so in the

-- 480 --

parable of the wheat and tares (Matthew 13:24-30). John 12:24, “Verily, verily, I say unto you, Except a corn of wheat fall into the ground and die, it abideth alone: but if it die, it bringeth forth much fruit”; [and John 4:35] “fields white to the harvest.” So Mark 4:26-29, “So is the kingdom of God, as if a man should cast seed into the ground; and should sleep, and rise night and day, and the seed should spring and grow up, he knoweth not how. For the earth bringeth forth fruit of herself; first the blade, then the ear, after that the full corn in the ear. But when the fruit is brought forth immediately he putteth in the sickle, because the harvest is come.”

Reprobates are here represented by {grapes of the vine}. 'Tis manifest that 'tis the wicked that are intended by what is here said. They are elsewhere represented by the grapes. Joel 3:13, [“Put ye in the sickle, for the harvest is ripe: come, get you down; for the press is full, the fats overflow for their wickedness is great.”] Isaiah 63:3, [“I have trodden the winepress alone; and of the people there was none with me: for I will tread them in mine anger, and trample them in my fury; and their blood shall be sprinkled upon my garments, and I will stain all my raiment.”]

2. We may observe what dispensation of God towards those different kinds of persons is here represented, viz. God's bringing them both to their appointed end for the glory of God's justice and mercy, represented by their ingathering, especially that which shall be at the time of the destruction of Antichrist and the introduction of the glorious day of the Christian church.

[Doctrine.]

There are several observations I would raise from this passage of holy writ for our improvement:

Obs. I. The righteous and the wicked are both the Lord's, as the produce of the husbandman's fields and vineyard, or as the different sort of vessels in a man's house. 2 Timothy 2:20, “But in a great house there are not only vessels of gold and of silver, but also of wood and of earth; and some to honor, and some to dishonor.” God has made both for himself as the husbandman [has made his] plants. Proverbs 16:4, “The Lord hath made all men for himself and even the wicked for the day of evil.” Both are in his hands and at his disposal, as the husbandman has the management and ordering {of his fields}. Thus the matter is represented here.

God has an use for both. As the husbandman has various uses for distinct [plants], and as an householder has various uses for different vessels; so here, when the harvest of the grass is ripe, God has one use for that, and when the cluster of the vine [is ripe, God has another use for that].

-- 481 --

They are both suffered to live to answer his end— just so long as to answer his end, and no longer— as the husbandman suffers [his plants].

Obs. II. There are certain appointed and remarkable seasons for the ingathering of both elect and reprobates. Such a season is spoken in the passage of Scripture we are upon.

First. What is meant by the ingathering of each of these.

Second. There are certain remarkable appointed seasons for both.

First. What is meant by the ingathering of elect and reprobates. By the ingathering of God's elect is meant their being gathered into God's kingdom; and by the ingathering of reprobates is intended their being brought into a state of suffering divine vengeance.

1. By the ingathering of the elect is meant their being gathered into God's kingdom. [They] shall be gathered together in one (Ephesians 1:10). God's people are called his church for that reason, because they are a company gathered together. What they are gathered into is God's kingdom, represented as his garner, his barn (Matthew 13:30), his house in the parable of the man that made a great supper (Luke 14:23).

Therefore there is a twofold ingathering of the elect spoken of in Scripture, both compared to an harvest:

(1) The gathering them into God's kingdom of grace in their conversion. This is represented as the gathering in of God's harvest. John 4:35-38, “Lift up your eyes, and look on the fields; for they are white already to harvest. And he that reapeth receiveth wages, and gathereth fruit unto life eternal: that both he that soweth and he that reapeth may rejoice together. And herein is that saying true, One soweth, and another reapeth. I sent you to reap that whereon ye bestowed no labor: other men labored, and ye are entered into their labors.”

(2) The gathering them into the kingdom of glory. This also is [represented in] Matthew 13:30, “Let both grow together till the harvest.”

2. The ingathering of reprobates is God's bringing them into a state of suffering the vengeance of God. That is evidently intended in the passage of Scripture we are upon. The vengeance of God is executed on wicked men in a threefold destruction: spiritual, in giving them over to judicial blindness; temporal, in taking away their lives; [and] eternal. And accordingly the wicked, that are the clusters of the vine of the earth, may be said to be gathered when they are brought under either of these judgments of God: when God forsakes 'em and gives 'em over finally into the hands of Satan; when their lives are taken away by some awful judgment of God on the wicked world; and when they are cast into hell.

Having thus shown what is meant by the ingathering of elect and reprobates, I now proceed,

-- 482 --

Second. To show that there are certain appointed remarkable seasons of the ingathering [of] both. God is gathering in both at all times. But there are some special seasons appointed for the accomplishing this in a more remarkable manner.

1. There are certain remarkable seasons for gathering in the elect into the kingdom of grace. These are often spoken of in the Prophets. Such a season was [in the apostles' day]. Besides those that are general, there are {special seasons} in particular places.

And though God be in all ages continually gathering in his elect unto the kingdom of glory, yet there is one certain day that is more especially appointed for that purpose. Both seem to be signified by that [verse, Matthew 24:31], “He shall send forth his angels with the great sound of a trumpet to gather together his elect.” So,

2. There are also certain appointed remarkable seasons for the gathering in the wicked and bringing them into a state of wrath, remarkable seasons of bringing them under God's spiritual vengeance: in the wilderness; in Israel before the captivity; after Christ and the apostles had preached the gospel to the Jews. So [there] will be after that great light has been offered to the wicked, that shall shine forth to introduce remarkable seasons of bringing wicked men to death and hell, to temporal and eternal destruction. I mention them together because they always go together: the flood; the destruction of Sodom and other cities of the plain; the destruction of the wicked inhabitants of Canaan; the destruction of Judah and Jerusalem. Ezekiel 22:19-22, “[Therefore thus saith the Lord God; Because ye are all become dross, behold, therefore] I will gather you into the midst of Jerusalem. As they gather silver, and brass, and iron, and lead, and tin, into the midst of the furnace, to blow the fire upon it, to melt it; so will I gather you in mine anger and in my fury, and I will leave you there, and melt you. Yea, I will gather you, and blow upon you in the fire of my wrath, and ye shall be melted in the midst thereof. As silver is melted in the midst of the furnace, so shall ye be melted in the midst thereof; and ye shall know that I the Lord have poured out my fury upon you.” [So] the second destruction of Jerusalem. They were gathered from [the vine of the earth].4 [So] the vast destruction that will be of wicked men's lives at the time of the downfall of Antichrist, represented in the latter part of the nineteenth chapter of Revelation.

But above all, the day of judgment will, above all others, be the time of the vintage or the ingathering of the cluster {of the vine}.

-- 483 --

Obs. III. The more remarkable seasons of the ingathering of the elect into the kingdom of God, and the gathering of the wicked into a state of vengeance and wrath, usually go together or follow quick one upon another. So the matter is represented in this portion of Scripture.

In those parts of the world, the vintage followed the harvest {of the cluster of the vine}. There seems to be an allusion in our text to that.

That those ingatherings usually accompany one another is represented also in other passages of Scripture. Isaiah 61:2, “[To proclaim the acceptable] year [of the Lord, and the day] of vengeance [of our God].” So Isaiah 66:5-08 “Hear the word of the Lord, ye that tremble at his word; Your brethren that hated you, that cast you out for my name's sake, said, Let the Lord be glorified: but he shall appear to your joy, and they shall be ashamed. A voice of noise from the city, a voice from the temple, a voice of the Lord that rendereth recompense to his enemies. Before she travailed, she brought forth; before her pain came, she was delivered of a man child. Who hath heard such a thing?” So vv. Isaiah 66:14-16, “And when ye see this, your heart shall rejoice, and your bones shall flourish like an herb: and the hand of the Lord shall be known toward his servants, and his indignation toward his enemies. For, behold, the Lord will come with fire, and with his chariots like a whirlwind, to render his anger with fury, and his rebuke with flames of fire. For by fire and by his sword will the Lord plead with all flesh: and the slain of the Lord shall be many.” So vv. Isaiah 66:20-24,

And they shall bring all your brethren for an offering unto the Lord out of all nations upon horses, and in chariots, and in litters, and upon mules, and upon swift beasts, to my holy mountain Jerusalem, saith the Lord, as the children of Israel bring an offering in a clean vessel into the house of the Lord. And I will also take of them for priests and for Levites, saith the Lord. For as the new heavens and the new earth, which I will make, shall remain before me, saith the Lord, so shall your seed and your name remain. And it shall come to pass, that from one new moon to another, and from one sabbath to another, shall all flesh come to worship before me, saith the Lord. And they shall go forth, and look upon the carcasses of the men that have transgressed against me: for their worm shall not die, neither shall their fire be quenched; and they shall be an abhorring unto all flesh.

The records and prophecies of Scripture confirm this. The vintage presently follows the harvest. So [it will be] at the day of judgment.

Obs. IV. The ingathering of both elect and reprobates is when they are ripe. Thus the matter is represented in the text.

-- 484 --

This is true, in a sense, of the ingathering of the saints into the kingdom of grace. The fields are first white to the harvest before a great ingathering of souls. So it was in Samaria. The fields ben't white to the harvest till the grain is ripe. The meaning of it [is] that the state of things in providence is come to a ripeness for such an event, other things accomplished, and their minds brought to some preparatory disposition; and this seems to be especially intended in the text.

And their ingathering into the kingdom of glory is not till they are ripe in another sense, i.e. not till the work of grace has been carried on so far that they are fitted for that place in glory which God has designed them for. So the stones of the temple. Thus all are ripened. To this end are they continued here in this world after their conversion. To this end are all God's providences, afflictions, mercies, changes, temptations, means of grace, opportunities, all the influences of God's Spirit upon them. As soon as ever they are ripe, they shall be gathered in.

So likewise the wicked are gathered into a state of wrath when they are ripe. The grapes are not gathered and cast. 'Tis not God's manner for God to give men over to {destruction} till {the harvest is ripe}. There are some sins especially by which men ripen for this judgment. Again, God is not wont to cast the wicked into hell till they are ripe. Every wicked man has a certain measure of sin, to which he shall be permitted to come, to fit him for a certain measure of punishment. And wicked men, many men, are suffered to live for no other end but to fill up the measure of their sin and grow ripe for destruction. God sometimes bears long for that end. Romans 9:22, “[What if God, willing to show his wrath, and to make his power known], endured with much longsuffering [the vessels of wrath fitted to destruction].” [So the] old world. [So the] Amorites. Genesis 15:13-16, “And he said unto Abram, Know of a surety that thy seed shall be a stranger in a land that is not their's, and shall serve them; and they shall afflict them four hundred years; And also that nation, whom they shall serve, will I judge: and afterward shall they come out with great substance. And thou shalt go to thy fathers in peace; thou shalt be buried in a good old age. But in the fourth generation they shall come hither again: for the iniquity of the Amorites is not yet full.” [So with the] church of Israel, forty years in wilderness; with Jerusalem before its first destruction; Jerusalem before its second [destruction], about forty years after Christ had denounced such great tribulation.

Obs. V. The attributes of God call aloud both for the salvation of the elect and the destruction of unbelievers. Thus the angels “cry aloud.” They do so as the angels of God, as sent by him and delivering his message,

-- 485 --

which intimates to us that the attributes of God do greatly require it. Yea these angels seem to be visionary representations of certain attributes of God. For we can't suppose a created angel, a ministering spirit, would so speak to the Son of God.

The mercy of God calls aloud for the salvation of the elect. The angel {who came out of the temple} probably represents God's mercy [v. Genesis 15:15]. Not only his mercy, but also his truth and justice. These attributes cry aloud, because the blood of Christ cries aloud. Abel's blood cried. Especially will they cry for present salvation when once they are ripe.

So God's attributes cry aloud for the destruction of impenitent sinners: God's majesty, his holiness, truth and justice. The angel [who came out from the altar] seems to represent God's justice [v. Genesis 15:18]. The honor of God's attributes would greatly suffer if {impenitent sinners went unpunished}, which shall not be. And when wicked men are ripe for destruction, then they will cry for immediate {destruction}, that they may not pass one moment beyond the time. Thus when the sin of Sodom was ripe. Genesis 18:20, “And the Lord said, Because the cry of Sodom and Gomorrah is great, and because their sin is very grievous.”

Obs. VI. 'Tis easy with God both to convert the elect and to destroy the reprobate. Both these are represented. 'Tis easy with him to convert a soul. 'Tis but for him to speak the word. When God has a design of mercy, nothing shall stand in the way of it to hinder it: whatever disadvantages persons are under; whatever blocks seem to be in the way; however blind [they are]; {however} hard [they are]; what strong possession soever Satan has {of them}, yea, though there be a legion [of] devils.

He can easily bring in multitudes, a great harvest. He has spoken of a time when he will. It looks exceeding difficult, but when his time comes, it will be only for him to give forth the word.

And so he can easily destroy his enemies, however strong. All attempts to save themselves [are] in vain, though they are kings [or] great multitudes. This is represented in the text. {God will destroy} the wicked of the whole earth, though hand join in hand. There are now exceeding strong kingdoms that Satan has set up.

Obs. VII. Both elect and reprobates, when they are gathered in, are as it were cut off with a sharp sickle. When the elect are gathered in and brought savingly home to Christ, they are as it were cut off from their former happiness and dependence by the word of God, that is said in Scripture to be “sharper than any two-edged sword” [Hebrews 4:12], as it were kills [the wicked]. And as the wheat, when it is reaped with a sickle, is cut off from two things— from the ground and from its own stock and root— so

-- 486 --

the wicked, when they are gathered into the great winepress {of the wrath of God}, are cut off by the sharp sickle of God's wrath, cut off from all good, {cut off from} all their portion, all their hopes and expectations.

Though they won't be persuaded to quit their worldly happiness, yet they must be pressed. Though they won't forsake their self-dependence and hope, yet it shall be overthrown.

Obs. VIII. The destruction of wicked men will be by the wrath of God [the] winepress of [God's] wrath. Not as the calamities of the godly are in this world. All that they shall suffer shall be from God's wrath. Their punishment of loss [shall be from God's wrath]. Some of their sufferings shall be more immediately from the devils {tormenting them} and {other condemned souls}, yet they will be primarily from the wrath of God. The torments of their bodies will be from God's wrath. What they will suffer from themselves will be from wrath. And this will be a thing that will be the sting of all these sufferings. But their main suffering will be from an immediate sense of the wrath and hatred of God in the soul.

Obs. IX. The wicked, when they are cut off from the earth, shall be cast into a place that is prepared on purpose for the executions of God's wrath on all the objects of his wrath, [the] winepress of his wrath, prepared on purpose. The use of it is to glorify his wrath. “What if God, willing to show his wrath, [and to make his power known, endured with much longsuffering the vessels of wrath fitted to destruction” (Romans 9:22)].

[Hell is] a place every way fitted for the purpose. Everything in it [is] contrived and fitted for that, as all the parts of a winepress are prepared for the making of wine from the grape.

'Tis the grand receptacle of all the vessels of wrath. God has prepared but one place of torment for all in the whole universe that {remain impenitent}. There the devils [will torment them].

Obs. X. In that place of misery will be to be seen, by the wrath of God, a stupendous and prodigious destruction, signified by two things: a great winepress, and the vast quantity of blood. Which represent a mighty destruction, two ways: immense as to the number of sufferers, and immense as to the degree of destruction in particulars.

The force of a great winepress is greater than of a small one.

Obs. XI. Wrath shall be suffered by wicked men as it were with the weight of omnipotence. [God shall] trample [them underfoot], make his power known.

Obs. XII. With perfect hatred and without mercy, without the pity, without mercy, as trampled on [as grapes in a winepress].

Obs. XIII. Destruction will be perfect: blackness of darkness; grind to

-- 487 --

powder [a] fire [in which to be] consumed; death; despair. Those expressions [are] exceeding awful, in Isaiah 63.

Obs. XIV. God will have no other use to put them to but to bear misery.

Application.

Use I may be of Self-Examination, to put all upon examining themselves whether or no they are of God's harvest that have been gathered into the kingdom of grace.

First. Inquire whether you have been cut off from the earth and from your own stock and root. Have you {been cut off} from the earth? [Is your heart] truly weaned [from this world]? [Do you] choose another portion? [Is it your habit] truly to renounce [the things of this world]? Does it prove so when you have a trial? [Are you] cut off from your own stock?

Second. Have you been brought home to Christ?

Third. Have you been gathered with others in one [harvest]?

Fourth. Are you going to heaven?

Fifth. Are you improving your time and means to ripen for heaven?

Use II may [be] of Awakening to those that are yet in a natural condition. How awful that which has been said represents your case to be. Hitherto you have been some of the grapes that have, all your lifetime, been ripening for the winepress. Consider how certain what you have heard represents your destruction to be, if you continue Christless. [You] have heard how that clusters are {destroyed} in God's hand. 'Tis most easy with God. You won't remain always in the vineyard. You must be gathered in. God's attributes call aloud, {as} the angel that comes out of the altar. As soon as ever you are ripe, then [you are] cut off with the sharp sickle, then cast into the winepress, [and] there bear the wrath of God. [It is] a place prepared by God on purpose [for your destruction]. [Consider God's] omnipotence; hatred and without mercy; contempt; misery immense; destruction perfect. [God's winepress has] no other use. Consider how you will bear [it].

Consider how long some of you have been ripening.

I am afraid some of you will not hearken now, as you have not hearkened heretofore. I am afraid some of you live only to fill up the measure of your sins.5

Use III, of Exh.

-- 488 --

First. To all to improve special seasons of ingathering persons into the kingdom of grace. [You have] heard how there are some seasons that God has appointed. [You] have heard how that commonly a vintage presently follows an harvest, [a vintage] gathered into a state of wrath in spiritual respects by being hardened. Take heed. Commonly a remarkable time of gathering wicked men to hell presently follows.

Here, particularly improve the present season. Now the words of the text are in a measure fulfilled. The angel has called to Christ to thrust in his sickle. Consider,

1. How happy those are that are of God's harvest. [Consider] who gathers them: the Son of man. Though he cuts 'em off, 'tis not in wrath. 'Tis as a loving Savior. [Consider] what they are gathered for: to sit with him in glory, to wear a golden crown as he does, to be with him, to see the glory, to partake.

2. In a time of great harvest or remarkable ingathering of souls, sometimes most of [the] adult generations are brought in that are ever to be brought in. So amongst the Jews in Christ's time. And providence seems to confirm it, that it was so here. Others are hardened and so, in some sense, are gathered into the winepress.

3. Consider how remarkably God, in this ingathering of souls that now is, seems to frown on those that long neglected and abused means of grace.

4. This is probably [a] forerunner of that very harvest spoken of in the text, and therefore, we may the more expect it will be conformed to it.

It may be the opportunity you now have, will be all the share you ever will have in that great harvest.

5. Consider that impenitent, negligent souls at such a season ripen apace for the winepress. As the same sun [shines on the harvest, so it shines on the vintage].6 Don't your hearts sensibly harden? How vastly greater is your guilt now than in the beginning. Take heed you don't ripen for that judgment. [Take heed] of being given over.

6. God now seems to be withdrawing from many parts of the land. If God should withdraw from us, great calamity will surely come upon the souls of those that are left behind. I warned you before, in the last time of {awakening}, and my words are verified. You that are now left behind, have now special reason to think with yourselves how you will bear.

And now, consider whether you will renounce the world. You must be cut off. If you ben't cut off by the sharp sickle that is in Christ's hand, you

-- 489 --

must [be cut off by God's wrath].7 If you are cut off by the former, it will be all that you shall suffer. But [if by the latter, you shall suffer eternally].

Second. To the godly, to labor to grow ripe for an ingathering into the kingdom of glory. Till fruits are ripe, there is a mixture of something noxious. But as they grow more ripe, they are purer, sweeter and more wholesome. Labor after purer and riper experiences. They are not always the ripest and best experiences that {taste the strongest}. They are best that [taste the purest and sweetest].

And seek that your fruits may be ripe. [Seek] to have your corruptions more subdued, [seek to be] more weaned from the world, more spiritual, more heavenly, humble, meek, more charitable, more unspotted from the world. [Seek to have] more and more of the spirit and practice that Christ was of.

[Seek to be] more andmore in your spirit and practice as they are in heaven. They are ripest for heaven that are most like those that are in heaven.
