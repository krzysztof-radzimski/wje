Praying for the Spirit
How much more shall your heavenly Father give the Holy Spirit to them that ask him? Luke 11:13.

This discourse of our blessed Savior, [of] which the text is a part, is occasioned by one of his disciples asking him to teach 'em to pray. The disciples were as it were Christ's family, and he used to pray with them. Christ had just now been to prayer with this his family and, when he had done, one of his disciples said to him, “Lord, teach us to pray as John taught his disciples” [Luke 11:1]. Accordingly Christ grants their request and gives 'em for a pattern that prayer commonly called the Lord's Prayer.

But then he teaches them that merely to say the words of a prayer is not sufficient, but that they must be earnest and importunate. He represents this by the comparison of one of them going to a friend at midnight, {and saying unto him, Friend, lend me three loaves; for a friend of mine in his journey is come to me, and I have nothing to set before him. And he from within shall answer and say, Trouble me not: the door is now shut, and my children are with me in bed; I cannot rise and give thee. And Jesus says unto them, Though he will not rise and give him, because he is his friend, yet because of his importunity he will rise and give him as many as he needeth} [Luke 11:5-08]. Then Christ, to encourage 'em thus to pray for the blessings they need, assures 'em of success if they do so. Verses Luke 11:9-10, “Ask, and it shall be given you; seek, and ye shall find; knock, and it shall be opened unto you. For every one that asketh receiveth; and he that seeketh findeth; and to him that knocketh it shall be opened.” And to confirm what he had said, he compares their asking what they need of God to a child's asking food of its parents: “If a son shall ask bread of any of you that is a father, will he give him a stone? or if he ask a fish, will he for a fish give him a serpent? Or if he shall ask an egg, will he offer him a scorpion?” [vv. Luke 11:11-12]; and concludes with this verse, wherein is the text: “if ye then, being evil, know how to give good gifts unto your children: how much

-- 214 --

more shall your heavenly Father give the Holy Spirit to them that ask him?” [v. Luke 11:13].

Three things we may take notice of in the words of the text:

1. The blessing asked for, viz. the Holy Spirit, which is the greatest blessing that can be asked. 'Tis that by which we are sanctified and quicked, by which we have spiritual life, by which we are conformed to God and have his image, and have the redemption of Christ applied to us and so are possessed of all the blessings of his purchase. In short, 'tis that by which we are actually possessed of true holiness and happiness.

2. We may observe who this blessing is asked of, viz. a heavenly Father.

3. His readiness to bestow it when it is asked: God is4 much more ready than earthly parents {to give good gifts unto his children}.

And there is a twofold reason implied in the manner of expression why God is more ready to bestow this blessing than earthly parents are to bestow bread on their children when they ask it. The reason is intimated by signifying:

(1) What manner of father he is of whom this gift is asked. 'Tis a heavenly Father. Not an earthly father such as children come to and ask bread of, but an heavenly Father who, as he exceeds earthly fathers in glory and excellency, so he does in goodness and love, as much as the heaven is high above the earth. “If you then, being evil,” Christ says, “know how [to give good gifts unto your children: how much more shall your heavenly Father give the Holy Spirit to them that ask him?]” As an heavenly Father is infinitely high above an earthly father, so heavenly love and kindness is infinitely above all earthly love. Isaiah 55:8-09, for my ways are not your ways, “saith the Lord. For as the heavens are higher than the earth, so are my ways higher than your ways, and my thoughts than your thoughts.”

(2) What manner of blessing is it that is asked, viz. the Holy Spirit. The manner of expression seems plainly to carry this in it: that God will be peculiarly ready to bestow the Holy Spirit on them that ask him. If you, being evil, know how to bestow earthly good things, earthly food, on your children, how much more shall your heavenly Father be ready to bestow the Holy Spirit, that blessing which is so much better than what is earthly, that his children stand so much more in need of than they do of earthly food, and that will do 'em so much more good?

We see that earthly parents, the more need their children stand in of anything and the more beneficial and profitable it would be to 'em, the more ready they are to bestow. Surely therefore our heavenly Father will

-- 215 --

be very ready to bestow the Holy Spirit on his children, a blessing that will be so infinitely beneficial and profitable to 'em. The blessing is as much above those earthly blessings, that earthly food which children ask for of their parents, as the heavenly Father that bestows it is above those earthly fathers. 'Tis an heavenly blessing that is bestowed as well as an heavenly Father that bestows it, and this is one reason why God will be more ready to bestow it.

If we, when we stand in need, ask earthly bread of God, he will be much more ready to bestow this upon us than earthly parents are, but much more when we ask [for] the Holy Spirit.

Doctrine.
Of the more excellent nature any blessing is that we stand in need of, the more ready God is to bestow it in answer to prayer.

With men it is otherwise. They are much more ready to bestow things of small nature.

Prop. I. God is a prayer-hearing God. There is scarce anything that is more frequently asserted of God in Scripture than this, that he stands ready to hear prayer. Psalms 65:2, “O thou that hearest prayer, to thee shall all flesh come.” 'Tis often said, if we seek him he will be found of us. 'Tis asserted, Psalms 34:10, that “they that seek the Lord shall not want any good thing.” [In] Psalms 50:15, we are directed to call upon God in a day of trouble and, 'tis added, he will {deliver thee, and thou shalt glorify him}. [In] Psalms 86:4-05, God is said to be plenteous in mercy to all that call upon him; and [in] Psalms 145:18-19 the Lord is said to be nigh to all that call: “The Lord is nigh unto all them that call upon him, to all that call upon him in truth. He will fulfill the desire of them that fear him: he also will hear their cry, and will save them.”5

[Prop.] II. Some blessings that we need and should pray for are of a more excellent nature than others.

[Prop.] III. God is most ready to bestow the most excellent blessings we need in answer to prayer.

First. 'Tis more agreeable to the6 infinite goodness of God's nature.

Second. 'Tis more agreeable to the design of God the Father in the great contrivance and work of God in redemption, more to the glory of his grace. Romans 9:23, “that he might make known the riches of his glory on

-- 216 --

the vessels of mercy.” Ephesians 1:5-07, “Having predestinated us unto the adoption of children by Jesus Christ to himself, according to the good pleasure of his will, to the praise of the glory of his grace, wherein he hath made us accepted in the beloved. In whom we have redemption through his blood, the forgiveness of sins, according to the riches of his grace.” And ch. Ephesians 2:4-07, “But God, who is rich in mercy, for his great love wherewith he loved us, even when we were dead in sins, hath quickened us together with Christ (by grace ye are saved); and hath raised us up together, and made us sit together in heavenly places in Christ Jesus: that in the ages to come he might show the exceeding riches of his grace in his kindness toward us through Christ Jesus.” To glorify his Son, [God is ready to bestow the most excellent blessings].

What God has done in the means of procuring good for us shows [the riches of his grace]. The bestowment of the greatest mercies is more agreeable to what God has done.

Third. The more Christ has success, [the more God is ready to bestow the most excellent blessings]. Christ died principally to purchase blessings of the most excellent kind, and therefore he doubtless is most worthy to bestow [them]. God the Father is most forward to do that which will most gratify and answer his Son's intercession.

Fourth. Those desires that are expressed in prayers for the most excellent blessings are the most excellent desires, and therefore God will be most ready to gratify 'em.

Fifth. 'Tis especially agreeable to his covenant. There are absolute promises of these blessings here, but not of any particular temporal blessings, but only under certain conditions, provided they are best for us. These are always best. These therefore are most absolutely promised.7

Application.

Use I. Of Instr.

First. If God be so liberal to us, we should not be straight-handed.

Second. Hence how much are those to blame that seek those things that are mean and worthless more than those blessings that are spiritual and divine. If God be most ready to bestow those blessings that are most excellent, then surely we should be most ready to seek 'em.

The blessings that we need are of two sorts: temporal and spiritual. Our heavenly Father knows that we have need of these things. We need food

-- 217 --

and raiment and habitations to shelter us from the weather, and we need the help and society of friends. And some have these things in much greater plenty than others; there are different degrees of temporal enjoyments. Some han't only food and raiment, but are fed with the richest dainties, and are clothed in robes and adorned with gold and pearls, and dwell in stately palaces and possess vast treasures and reign over kingdoms.

But there is another sort of blessing that the children of men are capable of and that they stand in need of, which are through the influences of the Holy Spirit: such as the sanctification of our natures, our being made partakers of the divine nature, having the love of God shed abroad in our hearts, being united to the Son of God, being the children of God, having the presence of God, the light of his countenance and communion and fellowship with him, having divine peace and joy and living a divine life, and being hereafter possessed of eternal life and glory— which are more excellent things than the other, as high above all the temporal enjoyments of princes as heaven is high above the earth. They are infinitely the most necessary, the most profitable, the most honorable, the most pleasant and delightful and satisfying, and the most durable blessing.

And yet men, even nominal Christians, do commonly seek that other kind of enjoyments, those mere worthless things, with much the greatest earnestness. When they are in want of these outward things, they are much more concerned about their want than about their want of those divine blessings, and are far more assiduous in seeking of them. They don't need any preaching to stir them up to take thorough care to obtain those outward things. Whenever they begin to be a little in want of those things, they begin to [be] pinched for want of bread to eat, and they are hungry; and if they ben't come to that but only foresee the danger of it, how will they bestir themselves. And if they begin to suffer for want of those things, how much do they make of their sufferings. They think themselves sorrowfully out, if at any time they ben't well provided for with food and clothing, or wood to burn, or fodder for their cattle.

But when their souls pine and languish for want of spiritual blessings, when they go without spiritual food day after day and it be a time of great scarcity and even famine on that account, how little are they affected with this. They seem to be quiet with it. How little do we hear of any cry of their necessitous, distressing circumstances.

And if there be anything said about it, how it is only a few faint, ineffectual speeches. And at the same time, [they] show by their practice that they ben't very uneasy with it; they ben't awakened with their necessitous

-- 218 --

circumstance, as when they begin to pinch for want of outward things. They can suffer month after month, if not year after year, without making any great stir about it.

And what is said about [it], as if it were a lamentable thing, is only words of course. And when God is prayed to to bestow those blessings, 'tis more as a matter of form than anything else. If rain be withheld and there be a drought, everybody is concerned. It is spoken of and lamented how the grass withers, and how the corn dies, and what a poor crop there is like to be; and there is, it may be, a great deal of praying for rain. But there may be a spiritual drought year after year, and not only in their crop, but no harvest at all in spiritual respects. Souls may be generally withering and drying up. And God is not very earnestly sought to, not because God is not as ready to bestow these blessings as the other— for he is, as has been shown, more ready to bestow them. If God were much more backward to bestow them than he is temporal blessings, yet if there were any possibility of obtaining them it would concern [us] to seek them with vastly greater earnestness and diligence than we do temporal things, they being infinitely more necessary for us and will be so much more profitable to us. But when that is not the case, but God is as ready, and more ready, {to bestow spiritual blessings}8 as well— the things9 more worthy to be sought on their own account— it shows the absurdity of such a negligence with respect to seeking of them, when we are so engaged in seeking the world.

If there had been such a withholding of temporal blessings in this town as there has been of spiritual blessings for some years past, yea, if it had not been one half so long, we should not have borne it so patiently as we have borne a withholding of spiritual blessings. The town would long ago [have] been alarmed, all souls, high and low, young and old, children crying to their parents and parents crying over their children, all would have been terrified. And there would doubtless have been many meetings to contest what we should do for supplies; and if supplies could not have been had without, persons would have gone far for them. Yea, if necessary provision had not been to have been had1 nearer, we should have sent beyond the seas for provision.

But here we have lain in groaning circumstances in our souls, in pinching want of spiritual supplies year after year, and but little appearance of

-- 219 --

a general concern about it. [We] ben't much engaged about to get supplies, though they have been ready at hand. And God has been ready to bestow if we had had an heart to seek it of him. Romans 10:6-08, “Say not in thine heart, Who shall ascend into heaven (that is, to bring Christ down from above)? Or, Who shall descend into the deep (that is, to bring up Christ again from the dead)? But what saith it? The word is nigh thee, even in thy mouth, and in thy heart: that is, the word of faith, which we preach.” We have been bestirring ourselves about other things and making a great bustle and noise. We have bestirred ourselves about a market for our cattle, and we have bestirred ourselves about the inner commons, and have very notably bestirred ourselves in some other matters;2 but we have not stirred up ourselves to lay hold on God, or to turn from our sins and earnestly to seek the returns of God's Holy Spirit.

Let us seriously consider this day how much God has been neglected among us of late by young and old. What have we been spending our time about this four years past? Have we acted as a people that have been sensible of the wants of their precious souls? Have we behaved ourselves as a people that are sensible that they are strangers and sojourners on earth, and have no continuing city here, and that look for a city that has foundations, {whose builder and maker is God} [Hebrews 11:10]; that han't their treasure or hearts in this world but in heaven? Have we behaved ourselves as becomes the children of an heavenly Father that knows they must live upon bread of his providing?

Great part of you pretend to have been converted a few years ago, and if it was so, what was you converted for? What did [God] call you to? Had he no work for you to do? Did he call you to be idle? Did he convert you and make you his children for nothing but to pursue the dust of the earth, or did he call you into his family to bite and devour one another and fill his house with broils and contentions? And had God nothing better to bestow upon you, when he had made you his children, than a little money or land, that you seem so much to behave yourselves as if you thought this was your chief good?

'Tis time for you seriously to bethink yourselves. You have been wandering long enough. You have sojourned long enough in Meshech [Psalms 120:5]. 'Tis time now to return to your God and to come with weeping and lamentation, laying your hands on your mouths and laying in the dust before God, and to abhor yourself in dust and ashes, and to cry to God

-- 220 --

that he would turn you from your backslidings and wash you from all those pollutions with which you have been polluting yourselves.

Use II may be to exhort God's people in this town earnestly to cry to God for the renewed pouring out of his Holy Spirit upon us. You see by what been said under this doctrine what great encouragement there is for us to seek this mercy of God, for this is the most excellent of all blessings that a people stand in need of; and therefore, as you have heard, God is more ready to bestow it in answer to prayer than any other blessing. And therefore there is no reason in the least to scruple but that we may obtain this blessing if we set our hearts and souls to seek it. Doubtless we may have it if we will. Christ says of it in the context, v. Luke 11:9, ask and receive.

We have seen in some instances how ready God is to hear prayer for such a blessing as rain. There have been often remarkable answers of prayer given for that blessing in time of drought. But God is much more ready to bestow spiritual showers; he is more ready to shower down of his Holy Spirit than he is rain.

And God don't only give us peculiar encouragement in his Word to ask it of him but he, at this time, is in his providence making an offer of it to us. Christ evidently, at this very time, is come, stands at the door with this blessing in his hand ready to bestow it upon us, waiting to see whether we will have it or no. Now is the time for us to cry to God for this mercy; now God is near; now is the time for us to call upon him.

Here Christ stands with this great and precious blessing in his hands. You may hear his voice. Evidently he stands at the door and knocks. Will ye have this blessing of him or no? Will you lay aside your sins, your parties, your contentions; will you leave all other things and set yourselves, with all your hearts and souls, to seek this mercy? Christ now offers that, if you will open your mouths wide, he will fill them. Don't refuse to do it, though3 your mouth is full already with the food of your lusts that you will not spit out but hide as sweet morsels under your tongues and keep still within your mouth.

Don't now begin one and another to make excuse about your having bought oxen that you want to feed and to sell. Don't say we have bought land there, we must needs go and see it, or we needs come to a division of it; we have worldly affairs that we must needs settle. Don't refuse that glorious feast that Christ now is calling you to for the sake of your farms or your cattle or any other concerns whatsoever.

I am bold to say that God is now offering the blessing of his Holy Spirit

-- 221 --

to this town, and I am bold to say we may have it only for asking. Indeed, if we ask in such a manner that, at the same time that we ask, we show that we have no sense of the value of the blessing we ask, and that we don't much desire it— if we ask in such a manner as implicitly to ask and deny4 at the same time— then we have no reason to think we shall be heard.

At the same time that we pretend to ask this mercy we may, in effect, ask a denial, by two things:

First. By a cold, indifferent asking without any engagedness of spirit, only asking in a formal manner. Such an asking is an asking of the mouth but not of the heart. 'Tis no prayer in the sight of God. It don't at all argue want of readiness in God to bestow this blessing, that he insists on such an asking as is in some measure answerable to the value of the mercy asked for. Needful to prepare us {for this blessing is an understanding of its value. It is} best for us {to pray with engagedness of spirit}. There is more mercy shown in such a way of bestowing {this blessing than if God did not insist on an asking that is answerable to the value of the mercy asked}.

When Christ speaks in the text of God's readiness to bestow the Holy Spirit on them that ask it, and when he says in the context, ask and receive, {seek and ye shall find, knock and it shall be opened unto you}, he don't mean that God will bestow it without importunate prayer, as appears by his preceding words: “I say unto you, Though he will not rise and give him, because he is his friend, yet because of his importunity he will rise and give him as many as he needeth” (v. Luke 11:8). This explains what Christ means when he says ask, {and it shall be given you}.

Second. Asking in words but, at the same time, resisting the bestowment in practice. This is another way of begging a denial at the same time that we ask— asking for God's Spirit to deliver us from our sins and, at the same time, manifesting by our practice that we are not willing to be delivered, but are resolved to hold on; asking for the Spirit5 to awaken us and make us deeply concerned about {the things of religion} and, at the same time, going into vain company and taking a course to shout out or drown all concerns; asking for a spirit of love and peace and, at the same time, showing in practice a design to continue in contention, or take a cause that directly tends to foment division. Prayers won't be heard unless they are without wrath (1 Timothy 2:8). If any this day hold old scores, let 'em not imagine [that God will hear their prayer].

This is not the way that you must seek this blessing that Christ now is offering

-- 222 --

us. For this is indeed not to seek it [at] all, but to resist it, to seek the contrary, to ask that we may go without it. But if one really seeks this blessing, we may have it. The reason why we han't had it bestowed already is [that we have not really sought it].

I am bold to say, that if you will seek this blessing and take no more pain for it than most of you take every year for a little worldly profit, you shall have it. God will bestow it upon [you]. It is a great deal more certain that you will have success in this.

And how much more worth is it than all those temporal things you labor for from year to year? We have had experience of the worth of such a blessing, and we shall be above all inexcusable if we don't earnestly seek it.

Therefore let all souls now stir up themselves. Aged people, let them cry earnestly for such a mercy, that they may have the advantage of one more such blessed season to prepare for death in before they go out of the world, and that they may say with good old Simeon, “now lettest thou thy servant depart in peace, according to thy word. For mine eyes have seen thy salvation” [Luke 2:29-30].6

Let heads of families earnestly cry to God for this blessing. Let concern for yourselves and compassion for your poor children, that you have been the instruments of bringing into the world, stir you up. If God should not any more remarkably pour out his Spirit upon us, in all likelihood most of your poor children, the bigger part of the rising generation, will burn in hell to all eternity. Consider [that] you have been the instruments of bringing children into the world in a miserable state and condition, under wrath, and will you not earnestly seek a blessing by which they may be saved?

Let young people now earnestly cry to God. Let sinners cry to God. Let saints cry to God. Let all cry for the pouring out of the Spirit on their own souls and on others. Let all that are admitted near to God, to communion with God, improve that opportunity.

Let us put away all our abominations, cast away our idols. Let young people put away all vanity, all those diversions that have a tendency [quench the Spirit].

And let elder people have done with whatever tends to hinder, let what will become of one affair and another. Let this be your resolution: that you will not do anything that shall tend to quench the Spirit of God; that you will rather cast yourself on providence and wait upon God with respect to the issue of difficult affairs.

-- 223 --

And seeing there is some hopeful beginning of a work of God {in this town}, let us be tender of it, deal gently with it as a newborn child, take heed that we don't quench the fire that is newly kindled by any means whatsoever. We may soon extinguish it to our shame and sorrow.

And if there be anyone here that won't do his part in seeking this blessing but, on the contrary, will, for his own worldly interest, or out of spite against any of his neighbors, or for the sake of any less does permit that which tends to quench the beginnings of a work of God, and so will not enter into the kingdom of God himself, nor suffer those that are entering to go in: how justly might it be with him as it was with the unlooking lord in Samaria, who would not hearken to what Elisha, his minister, said to him about the plenty in Samaria, who had this curse denounced upon him, that he should see it with his eyes but should not eat thereof [2 Kings 7:2]. How justly might such an one go forever without any benefit of a pouring out of the Spirit and without any of his saving influences on his heart. Let none, therefore, by any means do anything to hinder such a blessing, lest it should be thus with him.
