The Blowing of the Great Trumpet
And it shall come to pass in that day, that the great trumpet shall be blown, and they shall come which were ready to perish in the land of Assyria, and the outcasts in the land of Egypt, and shall worship the Lord in the holy mount at Jerusalem. Isaiah 27:13.

In this and the three preceding chapters are foretold great things that should be accomplished at a certain future day; and therefore we have repeatedly in these chapters, this expression, “in that day shall” this and that “come to pass.” And we learn what day it is, by the things that are spoken to be accomplished in that day. Chapter Isaiah 24:21-23, “And it shall come to pass in that day, that the Lord shall punish the host of the high ones that are on high, and the kings of the earth upon the earth. And they shall be gathered together, as prisoners are gathered in the pit, and shall be shut up in the prison, and after many days shall they be visited. Then the moon shall be confounded, and the sun ashamed, when the Lord of hosts shall reign in mount Zion, and in Jerusalem, and before his ancients gloriously.” Twenty-fifth chapter, ninth verse, “And it shall be said in that day, Lo, this is our God; we have waited for him, and he will save us: this is the Lord; we have waited for him, we will be glad and rejoice in his salvation.” Twenty-sixth chapter, first [and] second verses, “In that day shall this song be sung in the land of Judah; We have a strong city; salvation will God appoint for walls and bulwarks. Open ye the gates, that the righteous nation which keepeth the truth may enter in.” Twenty-seventh chapter, first, second [and] third verses, “In that day the Lord with his sore and great and strong sword shall punish leviathan the piercing serpent, even leviathan that crooked serpent; and he shall slay the dragon that is in the sea. In that day sing ye unto her, A vineyard of red wine. I the Lord do keep it; I will water it every moment: lest any hurt it, I will keep it night and day.” Twelfth and thirteenth verses, “And it shall come to pass in that day, that the Lord shall beat off from the channel of the river unto the stream of Egypt, and

-- 439 --

ye shall be gathered one by one, O ye children of Israel. And it shall come to pass in that day, that the great trumpet shall be blown, and they shall come which were ready to perish in the land of Assyria, and the outcasts in the land of Egypt, and shall worship the Lord in the holy mount of Jerusalem.” The things spoken, show the day to be the glorious gospel day, the day when God shall set up the kingdom of his Son on the earth.

In the text we may observe,

1. The salvation of God's people foretold, concerning which two things are here represented:

(1) The miserable state they are saved from, which is a perishing state of captivity and banishment, here represented by the state that the poor captives and outcasts of the children of Israel were in in Assyria and Egypt, where they were removed far off from the land of Canaan, the good land, the land of the inheritance and rest of God's Israel, and where they were in the hands of their enemies. The Egyptians were their old taskmasters that had been so cruel to them before their redemption out of Egypt, and always seemed to have retained an enmity against them. And the Assyrians were a people that were, in Isaiah's time, at war with the Jews, when they came up under Rabshakeh to destroy Jerusalem [Isaiah 36].

In those distant countries of their enemies, the poor, captivated, outcast Jews were ready to perish by the hard usage they met with under the hand of their enemies. To this is compared the misery that men are naturally in in bondage to sin and Satan, and in a state of banishment and estrangement from God.

And the salvation of God's spiritual Israel from spiritual captivity and banishment is represented by the salvation of the literal Israel from these countries.

(2) Here is represented the happy state they are brought to in this salvation, which is a state of holiness and nearness to God. They shall be brought to “worship the Lord in the holy mount at Jerusalem.”

Which is a lively representation of the happiness that persons are brought to by Christ's salvation, which is a state of spiritual exaltation. They that are God's, dwell as it were in the mount; they live above the world; they dwell on high, as it is said, Isaiah 33:16, “He shall dwell on high: his place of defense shall be the munitions of rocks: bread shall be given him; his waters shall be sure.” They dwell on a mountain, and it is God's holy mountain. The happiness and honor of the saints consists in their holiness and nearness to God.

And their happiness don't consist in a state of idleness. They are not by the salvation of Christ brought to God's holy mountain, there to be inactive

-- 440 --

and slothful, but to serve God. They shall come and “worship the Lord in the holy mount at Jerusalem.”

2. We may observe the means by which they shall be brought into this state of salvation, viz. by the preaching of the word of God, here called the blowing of the “great trumpet.” “And it shall come to pass in that day, that the great trumpet shall be blown, and they shall come which were ready to perish.” They shall come at the sound of that trumpet. The blowing of trumpets of old, in the time of the typical dispensation, was one main type of the preaching of the word of God. And therefore it was appointed by God in the Law that trumpets on all occasions were to be blown by priests. So the trumpets were to be blown by them in the feast of trumpets, in the first day of the seventh month, and so in the beginning of the jubilee; and in the new moons and in all their feast days, and to call solemn assemblies, and over their sacrifices; and even the trumpets that were blown in war in their armies were, by God's order, by the priests, hereby representing that the preaching of the word is by the sacred order of the ministers of the gospel.

[Doctrine.]

What I design from these words, is to show how the preaching of the Word may fitly be compared to the blowing of a trumpet, and especially as that was used among the Israelites of old; and then make some application.

I. There was a loud and awful trumpet blown by angels at the giving of the Law at Mt. Sinai. Exodus 19:16, “And it came to pass on the third day in the morning, that there were thunders and lightnings, and a thick cloud upon the mount, and the voice of the trumpet exceeding loud; so that all the people that was in the camp trembled.” Verse Exodus 19:19, “And when the voice of the trumpet sounded long, and waxed louder and louder, Moses spake, and God answered him by a voice.” [Chapter] Exodus 20:18, “And all the people saw the thunderings, and the lightnings, and the noise of the trumpet, and the mountain smoking: and when the people saw it, they removed, and stood afar off.”

II. Trumpets were wont to [be] blown to give the people warning of danger by an approaching enemy, to which there is often allusion in the Prophets. Jeremiah 4:5, “Declare ye in Judah, and publish in Jerusalem; and say, Blow ye the trumpet in the land: cry, gather together, and say, Assemble yourselves, and let us go into the defensed cities.” And ch. Jeremiah 6:1, “Ye children of Benjamin, gather yourselves to flee out of the midst of Jerusalem,

-- 441 --

and blow the trumpet in Tekoa, and set up a sign of fire in Beth-haccerem: for evil appeareth out of the north, and great destruction.” Hosea 5:8-09, “Blow ye the cornet in Gibeah, and the trumpet in Ramah: cry aloud at Beth-aven, after thee, O Benjamin. Ephraim shall be desolate in the day of rebuke: among the tribes of Israel have I made known that which shall surely be.” Amos 3:6, “Shall a trumpet be blown in the city, and the people not be afraid? shall there be evil in a city, and the Lord hath not done it?” Ministers giving people warning of their danger is compared to this very thing in Ezekiel [ch. Ezekiel 33:4-05].

III. The priests were wont to blow their silver trumpets over the sacrifices. Numbers 10:10, “Also in the day of your gladness, and in your solemn days, and in the beginnings of your months, ye shall blow with the trumpets over your burnt offerings, and over the sacrifices of your peace offerings; that they may be to you for a memorial before your God: I am the Lord your God.” 2 Chronicles 7:5-06, “And king Solomon offered a sacrifice of twenty and two thousand oxen, and an hundred and twenty thousand sheep: so the king and all the people dedicated the house of God. And the priests waited on their offices: the Levites also with instruments of music of the Lord, which David the king had made to praise the Lord, because his mercy endureth for ever, when David praised by their ministry; and the priests sounded trumpets before them, and all Israel stood.”

IV. The silver trumpets were sounded on Mt. Zion to call the solemn feasts. Numbers 10:2, “That thou mayest use them for the calling of the assembly.” Tenth verse, “And in the day of your gladness, and in your solemn days, and in the beginnings of your months, ye shall blow the trumpets over your burnt offerings, and over the sacrifices of your peace offerings; that they may be to you for a memorial before your God: I am the Lord your God.” They saw the mountain at a distance; when they came near they heard the joyful sound.

At three joyful seasons, especially: one monthly, another yearly, another once in fifty years.

First. Monthly, at new moons. Numbers 10:10, “Also in the day of your gladness, and in your solemn days, and in the beginnings of your months, ye shall blow with the trumpets over your burnt offerings, and over the sacrifices of your peace offerings; that they may be to you for a memorial before your God.”

Second. Yearly, at the feast of trumpets, at the beginning of the seventh month. Leviticus 23:24, “Speak unto the children of Israel, saying, In the seventh month, in the first day of the month, shall ye have a sabbath, a memorial of blowing of trumpets, an holy convocation.” Numbers 29:1, “And in the

-- 442 --

seventh month, on the first day of the month, ye shall have an holy convocation; ye shall do no servile work: it is a day of blowing the trumpets unto you.” The world is supposed to have been created in that month. The tabernacle in the wilderness was supposed to be built in that month. The temple of Solomon was dedicated in that month. Christ [was] born [in that month].

It was a kind of holy month: every seventh day, (every) seven years, and seven times seven years.

Every one of these sabbaths represented the gospel day.

Third. At the jubilee. Leviticus 25:8-10, “And thou shalt number seven sabbaths of years unto thee, seven times seven years; and the space of the seven sabbaths of years shall be unto thee forty and nine years. Then shalt thou cause the trumpet of the jubilee to sound on the tenth day of the seventh month, in the day of atonement shall ye make the trumpet sound throughout all your land. And ye shall hallow the fiftieth year, and proclaim liberty throughout all the land unto all the inhabitants thereof: it shall be a jubilee unto you; and ye shall return every man unto his possession, and ye shall return every man unto his family.”

This especially represents the preaching of the gospel at the beginning of the jubilee of the world.

V. At the sound of the trumpet, the people were to set forward on their journey towards Canaan. Numbers 10:1-02, “And the Lord spake unto Moses, saying, Make thee two trumpets of silver; of a whole piece shalt thou make them: that thou mayest use them for the calling of the assembly, and for the journeying of the camps.” Natural men should seek {to set forward on their journey towards Canaan}. Godly men {should seek the heavenly Canaan}.

VI. At this sound they were to engage in battle with their enemies. Numbers 10:9, “And if ye go to war in your land against the enemy that oppressed! you, then ye shall blow an alarm with the trumpets; and ye shall be remembered before the Lord your God, and ye shall be saved from your enemies.” Numbers 31:6, “And Moses sent them to the war, a thousand of every tribe, them and Phinehas the son of Eleazar the priest, to the war, with the holy instruments, and the trumpets to blow in his hand.” Jos 6:4, “And seven priests shall bear before the ark seven trumpets of rams' horns: and the seventh day ye shall compass the city seven times, and the priests shall blow with the trumpets.”

VII. With this they proclaimed their kings. 2 Kings 9:13, “Then they hasted, and took every man his garment, and put it under him on the top of the stairs, and blew with trumpets, saying, Jehu is king.” And 2 Kings 11:14,

-- 443 --

“And when she looked, behold, the king stood by a pillar, as the manner was, and the princes and the trumpeters by the king, and all the people of the land rejoiced, and blew with trumpets.”

VIII. With this they brought up the ark of God to Zion. 2 Samuel 6:15, “So David and all the house of Israel brought up the ark of God with shouting and the sound of the trumpet.” Psalms 47:5, “God is gone up [with a shout, the Lord with the sound of a trumpet].”

Application.

The Use that I would make of what has been said, is of Exhortation to all to hear the sound of this trumpet.

First. To Christless sinners. Here let several things be considered:

1. We, all of us, are favored with that important and joyful sound. We sit within the hearing. We are some of those highly favored people, spoken of, Psalms 89:15, “Blessed are the people that know the joyful sound.” They have blessed opportunities and advantages, if they do but know it, and they are indeed blessed that hearken to and obey that sound.

We are peculiarly favored in this beyond many millions of mankind, that dwell out of the hearing of this sound. It never once reached their ears. They are in great danger, but they have no trumpet to give them warning of their danger, and so destruction comes upon them unexpected, and they have no opportunity to escape.

They are perishing, and they have no joyful trumpet to give 'em notice of a Savior and to call 'em to this Savior, as we have.

But we have the warning trumpet very plainly sounding in our ears, fully informing us of the strength and approaches of the enemy.

We hear the joyful inviting sound of the gospel, [the] sound of the silver trumpet over the great sacrifice. {We hear the joyful sound} calling us to the glorious gospel feast, {calling us to the} jubilee, proclaiming Christ the King that God has set on his holy hill of Zion.

2. This is a day wherein this trumpet is sounding in an extraordinary manner. God has raised up some that he has fitted with an extraordinary zeal and fervency of spirit to warn and invite sinners, so that the trumpet has been sounded of late more frequently and loudly than ordinary. And you yourselves, amongst others, have heard that one has in this land, and in many other lands, very loudly and with great effect sounded this trumpet.3 Which is attended with more than commonly loud warnings and calls in providence, in the extraordinary works of God that he appears in

-- 444 --

on the souls of men. We know not but that this may be the beginning of the sound of that trumpet of the great jubilee of the world, or a forerunner of it.

3. Consider that this is the trump of God that you hear sounded, and therefore all should hear. Isaiah 18:3, “All ye inhabitants of the world, and dwellers on the earth, see ye, when he lifteth up an ensign on the mountains; and when he bloweth the trumpet, hear ye.” That word that is preached is the word of God, and when it is preached according to his institution, you ought to look upon [it] as the sounding of the trump of God to give men warning and to call them to himself. The gospel is the glorious gospel of Jesus Christ. Christ stands and proclaims the joyful tidings of salvation to all, and that with a loud voice; as you may see, John 7:37, and it came to pass, [“In the last day, that great day of the feast, Jesus stood and cried, saying, If any man thirst, let him come unto me, and drink.”] This was at the great feast of tabernacles, which was one of those joyful feasts on which the silver trumpet used to be blown.

So that you should look on that word that you hear from time to time as the voice of the great God. How solemn and awful therefore is it, and how much requiring your attention?

And now especially is God himself sounding the great trumpet and loudly warning sinners to fly from the wrath to come, and loudly calling the poor outcasts that are ready to perish to come to him, to his holy mountain in Jerusalem.

Now is he setting up an ensign in the mountains in a most conspicuous manner. These great, remarkable and unusual things which he is now bringing to pass in various parts of the land are the ensign that he sets up as it were in the mountains, in the most open and public view, to give all warning and to be a notification to all of the approaching wrath and of the offered mercy. And therefore, let all observe; let every creature hearken when Jehovah speaks and when the Son of [God] sounds the trumpet. When the great God speaks, it becomes all heaven and earth most devoutly and solemnly to attend. Isaiah 1:2, “Hear, O heavens, and give ear, O earth: for the Lord hath spoken.” Who is he that is so stupid and so impious as to be regardless at such a day as this, when the Almighty sets up his ensign and blows the trumpet amongst us?

Let him know that, unless he soon awakes out of sleep and shows a greater regard to the voice of God in the trumpet of the gospel, there will soon be another sound heard: and that is the sound of his thunder, when God shall thunder upon him with his great power and in his awful wrath.

Hearken, you poor captive souls: you that are ready to perish in Assyria,

-- 445 --

and the poor outcasts in the land of Egypt; you that have [been] led away to the utmost ends of the earth, into a miserable state and condition, and are there still lost and undone. The great trumpet is now blown! Don't you hear the sound of it this day, hear it sounded so long, and do you yet remain regardless? Hearken, look up, and behold the ensign that God has set upon the mountains. Here is an awful warning of your day {of judgment}, and here is a glorious loud proclamation of liberty and salvation. There is a great captain of salvation appears, that is come into the world to deliver poor lost captives and exiles. Therefore, now arise, shake yourselves from the dust, look up out of the dark dungeon, out of the horrible pit. Come, return to Zion, and gather yourselves to the mountain of the house of the Lord; come and worship at his holy mountain Jerusalem.

Hear the awful trumpet from Mt. Sinai that warns of wrath and eternal damnation, that made all the people in the camp to tremble, and will make your heart to tremble, unless it be harder than a rock. And hear also the joyful trumpet at Mt. Zion that offers mercy, and calls and invites you to come. Go thither to God's holy temple, to the great sacrifice that has there been offered for sin, and to the sweet incense offered by the great high priest, and to a glorious gospel feast that God has provided for poor famishing souls.

This trumpet on Mt. Zion is the voice of Christ. You are going from Mt. Zion, you are wandering away from it further and further; but Christ is sounding a trumpet now to call you back. See Revelation 1:10, “And I heard behind me a great voice, as of a trumpet.” You may see whose voice this is by the Revelation 1:12-17:

And I turned to see the voice that spake with me. And being turned, I saw seven golden candlesticks; and in the midst of the seven candlesticks one like unto the Son of man, clothed with a garment down to the foot, and girt about the paps with a golden girdle. His head and his hairs were white like wool, as white as snow; and his eyes were as a flame of fire; and his feet like unto fine brass, as if they burned in a furnace; and his voice as the sound of many waters. And he had in his right hand seven stars: and out of his mouth went a sharp two-edged sword: and his countenance was as the sun shineth in his strength. And when I saw him, I fell at his feet as dead. And he laid his right hand upon me, saying unto me, Fear not; I am the first and the last.

And you may see what it is. This great voice of Christ, that is like a trumpet, says, v. Revelation 1:11, “I am Alpha and Omega, the first and the last”; and v. Revelation 1:18,

-- 446 --

“I am he that liveth, and was dead; and, behold, I am alive for evermore Amen; and have the keys of hell and of death.” This is a joyful voice {calling us to the glorious gospel feast}. Therefore hearken to this voice, and come to this glorious Person that was dead and is alive, that died for sinners and {is now the King that God has set on his holy hill}. Christ, who thus proclaims with a great voice, is on Mt. Zion. Revelation 14:1, “And I looked and lo, a Lamb stood on Mt. Zion, and with him an hundred and forty and four thousand, having his Father's name written in their foreheads.” And there it is that he sounds the silver trumpet; 'tis to call you to him, to Zion.

This Zion is the heavenly Zion. Christ, with a great voice as of a trumpet, is calling you poor, undone, miserable creatures up to heaven. Revelation 4:1, “After this I looked, and, behold, a door was opened in heaven: and the first voice which I heard was as it were of a trumpet talking with me; which said, Come up hither.” Thither it is that this joyful sound invites you. As of old the trumpet was sounded on Mt. Zion to call the people up to the top of the mountain of the house of the Lord, to their solemn feast, so the voice of Christ, sounding in the silver trumpet of the gospel, is calling from the borders of hell to come up to heaven and there celebrate an eternal feast. Hearken, therefore, all you poor guilty souls that go about concerned and distressed under a vast load of guilt. Hearken, you that complain of your hard and senseless hearts.

Hearken, you poor discouraged souls, you that are ready to say that you are cast out of God's sight: yet let the joyful sound of this trumpet awaken your attention and encourage your heart to look towards God's holy temple. Look up, see the door that is opened in heaven, the door of mercy and of hope. See the pleasant light that shines forth from that door. 'Tis the light of the new Jerusalem; 'tis the light of the glory of God and the Lamb. Thither you are invited to enter; the way is open. The root and the offspring of David, the bright {and morning star}, says, Come; and the Spirit and the Sender says, Come; and whosoever will, let him come [Revelation 22:16-17].

And let stupid senseless sinners, that are not at [present] much awakened or greatly engaged for their salvation at such a time as this, hearken to the sound of the great trumpet which they hear this day.

I am afraid there are some of you have heard it so often, that you are grown deaf with it; you have lost your hearing. And the louder the trumpet sounds, and the longer you hear it, the more and more deaf you grow.

Here consider two things.

1. Consider what the prophet Ezekiel says of those that hear the sound of the trumpet and won't take warning. Ezekiel 33:4, “Then whosoever

-- 447 --

heareth the sound of the trumpet, and taketh not warning; if the sword come, and take him away, his blood shall be upon his own head.”

2. If you won't be awakened by the sound of the trumpet that you now hear, I'll tell you of one trumpet that you must hear, that will awaken you: that we read of, [1 Corinthians 15:52, “For the trumpet shall sound, and the dead shall be raised incorruptible, and we shall be changed.”]

Second. Let those that think themselves godly hearken to the sound of the great trumpet. 'Tis directed to you as well as others. 'Tis directed [to] all the inhabitants of the world (Isaiah 18:3).

You are warned by it.

You are invited by it.

You are called by it at this day, in an especial manner, to come and help bring up the ark of God.

You are now called to come and honor the King which God is setting on his holy hill of Zion.

You are called at this day especially to engage in battle with your enemies.

You are called at this day especially to go forward in your journey towards the heavenly Canaan.

In some respects, the sound of the gospel trumpet is especially to the people of Christ, for they are his people. They are the spiritual Israel that belong to Zion, that are to come thither to worship God in his holy mountain.

They are his armies, that have Christ for their captain; and, as when a general commands the trumpet to sound, 'tis for the notification especially of his own soldiers.

Therefore, let none such remain slumbering and regardless at this day, when their King and the captain of their salvation is in so remarkable a manner causing his great trumpet to be blown.

3. I.e. George Whitefield. ↩
