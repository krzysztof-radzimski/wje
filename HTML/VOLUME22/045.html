Mary's Remarkable Act
And being in Bethany in the house of Simon the leper, as he sat at meat, there came a woman having an alabaster box of ointment of spikenard very precious; and she brake the box, and poured it on his head. Mark 14:3

Bethany was a small town not far from Jerusalem where Christ often resorted when he came up from Galilee to Jerusalem, where he was well acquainted and where were a number of true disciples to whom Christ was very dear, among whom was the woman we have an account of in the text. We have here an account of a remarkable act of this woman. I call it a remarkable act because 'tis recorded in sacred history as such and is taken notice and spoken of by Christ as such.

[Doctrine.]

In speaking from these words I shall take the following method: I. I shall briefly take notice of the nature and circumstances of the external act of this woman.

II. Observe how it was resented3 by Jesus Christ, on whom the act was done.

III. Give the reason why it was so resented.

And then make application for our own practice.

I. I would briefly take notice of the nature and circumstances of the act of this woman towards Christ.

First. She anointed the Lord Jesus Christ, which according to the customs of those times was a token of extraordinary respect. Olive trees abounded in that country, and oil, which they had from the olive tree, was a thing that was in great use amongst them. With it they were wont to anoint themselves when they would most adorn themselves, as they were

-- 382 --

wont to do in times of great rejoicing. The people of Israel are therefore condemned for that, that they would so adorn themselves and use such a token of joy in a time of great public calamity, in Amos 6:6, “They drink wine in bowls, they anoint themselves with the chief ointments, and are not grieved for the afflictions of Joseph.” And so they were wont to anoint their friends, upon whom they would put peculiar respect when they received a visit from a friend peculiarly dear to 'em or especially honorable. They were wont to anoint them. And therefore Christ, speaking to Simon the Pharisee who found fault with the woman that anointed his feet, [said], Luke 7:44-46, “Seest thou this woman? I entered into thine house, thou gavest me no water for my feet: but she hath washed my feet with tears, and wiped them with the hairs of her head. Thou gavest me no kiss: but this woman since the time I came in hath not ceased to kiss my feet. My head with oil thou didst not anoint: but this woman hath anointed my feet with ointment.”

Anointing was used in token of the highest honor that could be put upon any, and therefore men were wont to be designed to the highest offices by anointing. So persons were anointed to the office of high priest, as Aaron was. So they were also anointed to the office of prophet, as Elisha. So in like manner they were designed to the office of a king, as Saul and David were anointed.

Second. She anointed the Lord Jesus Christ with very precious ointment. It is said it was ointment of spikenard, very precious. It was excellently perfumed, so that it gave an excellent odor. In those days they were wont [to employ] a great deal of art in perfuming of ointment, with mixtures of very dear and costly spices. It seems that this ointment, which this woman poured upon Christ, was perfumed with the choicest spices. Spikenard, as here mentioned, was4 one of the most excellent spices, and doubtless it was mixed with great art, so that it was great cost that she expended. That ointment that she had in that box was a very great treasure. And therefore we are told that there were some that were there present that “had indignation within themselves, and said, Why was this waste of the ointment made? For it might have been sold for more than three hundred pence, and given to the poor” [vv. Mark 14:4-05]. But such was the esteem that this woman had for Jesus Christ that she thought nothing too costly to be expended to put respect upon him. She willingly and joyfully did it, that she might honor her beloved Lord and Savior.

-- 383 --

Third. The ointment was poured forth out of a box of alabaster, of which two things may be noted:

1. That alabaster was a precious substance. It is a kind of precious stone.

2. That it is [a] thing noted for its extreme, pure, shining whiteness. No substance is found in the natural world that is of a more pure white, as is observed by those that treat of natural bodies and write natural history.

It is exceeding white and of a pure glistening color, without the least stain or obscurity. And therefore 'tis common to a proverb, “as white as alabaster,” or “as pure as alabaster.”

Fourth. We may observe the way in which the precious ointment was poured forth out of this alabaster box, viz. by breaking the box. The box was not opened to let out the ointment any other way than by breaking of it, which rendered the expense the woman was at to show her respect to Christ still the greater. She did [not] only spend the precious ointment but also the costly box, that contrived to show her respect to Christ.

The woman here spoken seems to have been Mary, the sister of Martha and Lazarus, who lived in Bethany, that was a dear love of Jesus Christ. All three of them, Lazarus and his two sisters, were great lovers of Christ, but among these Mary seems to have exceeded. Jesus loved Martha and her sister and Lazarus, and they all loved him, but Mary is commended above Martha. When she was cumbering herself with much serving, Mary sat at his feet and heard his word and, as Christ testifies, chose the better part (Luke 10:38-42).5

That Mary is the woman spoken of in the text seems manifest: not only that she lived in Bethany, but the evangelist John does particularly inform that it was she, in the beginning of the twelfth chapter of John: “Then took Mary a pound of ointment of spikenard…” [v. John 12:3]. This6 must be the same story; {it is set in the} same place, {it occurs at the} same time, a little before the Passover. “Then Jesus six days before the Passover came to Bethany” [v. 1]. And that what followed shows it to be the same: “Then saith one of his disciples, Judas Iscariot, Simon's son, which should betray him, Why was not this ointment sold for three hundred pence, and given to the poor?” [vv. John 12:4-05].

-- 384 --

Fifth. She anointed both Christ's head and his feet with the precious ointment.7

Sixth. The house was filled with the odor of the ointment.

Seventh. This was as Jesus sat at meat.

Having thus briefly taken notice what it was that this woman did, I now proceed, in the

II. [Second] place, to observe how Christ resented it. He graciously accepted it, and highly commended and wonderfully rewarded it.

First, He graciously accepted it. There were some there present that resented it very ill. They had indignation within themselves and said, “Why was this waste of the ointment?” They pretended that their resentment was out of regard to the poor from a charitable spirit, because they were unwilling that should be wasted that might be laid out for their benefit.

But Christ himself did not resent it at all amiss. He said not one word against it, but reproved those that murmured against her. He did not at all accuse her of folly and wastefulness. Whatever the pretense of those that murmured against her was, it was at bottom only from corrupt principles in them that they objected: partly from covetousness {over the price of the ointment}, partly from envy {for the privilege she enjoyed}. But Christ, who knew her heart, disapproves of nothing that she did. Yea,

Second. He highly commends her, and that openly. He says, “she hath wrought a good work on me. She hath done what she could. She is come aforehand to anoint my body to the burying” [vv. Mark 14:6, Mark 14:8]. He accepts her in what she did, though indeed her act was no ways profitable to him. It did him no real service, but yet he commends her that she had done what she could. She put honor upon him by it and showed that, if it had been in her power, she would have done more. She showed her sincerity, for she stuck at no cost to put honor on her dear Redeemer.

Third. He wonderfully rewards [her]. He promises and declares, “Verily I say unto you, Wheresoever this gospel shall be preached throughout the whole world, this also that she hath done shall be spoken of for a

-- 385 --

memorial of her.” Seeing she had so laid out herself to put honor upon Christ, Christ declares that he would put honor upon her. I come now, in the

III. [Third] place, to show why such great notice was taken of it by Jesus Christ. The act in itself, though it was expensive, yet was no way profitable to Christ. It not only was not profitable to Christ's divine nature, which needs nothing and is beyond the reach of our goodness; but it was not really profitable to his human nature, which was capable of being profited and which indeed was in such a low and needy state that it depended on the charity of his followers. If she had fed him when he was hungry, or if she had given him drink when he was thirsty, or entertainment and quiet lodging when he was weary, that would have been some real benefit to his human nature. But to pour ointment on his head was no real profit to him.

But yet very great notice is taken of it, that she should not only be commended very much, but that it should be spoken of as an act worthy to be published through the world, and to have a public record kept of it for a memorial of her throughout all generations, that it might be known by all the church of God to the end of the world. That such an act should be made so much of, is worthy to be inquired into. And there are these two reasons may be given of it:

First. Because it was a testimony of sincere gracious respect she had to Christ in her heart. Christ takes great notice of a little true religion. If there be but so much as a cup of cold water given in love to Christ, it shall in [no] case lose its reward.

This act of the woman's was not worthy in itself to be made so much of, but in the eye of the grace of Christ it was a great thing: for he saw the love that she8 did it from. And the graces of the saints are exceeding precious in his eyes, for his heart is ravished with [them]. Canticles 4:9, “Thou hast ravished my heart, my sister, my spouse; thou hast ravished my heart with one of thine eyes, with one chain of thy neck.”

Second. Another reason that may be given of it is this: that without doubt there is some spiritual thing signified and represented by this act of the woman. I don't mean that she intended to represent any spiritual thing by it, or that she understood any mystical signification in her own act. But without doubt, as this act of her's was ordered in divine providence, and as she was moved to it by the Spirit of God, and as it is recorded in sacred history, there is some mystery concealed in the story. It is remarkable that

-- 386 --

Christ should in a special manner as it were order this act to [be] published through the world and recorded in sacred history. There was doubtless many other acts that his disciples did that, in themselves, were more beneficial and done from sincere love to him that there is no such care taken to record and publish. But the reason, doubtless, is there is some special spiritual instruction contained in the relation that is very profitable for the church of Christ. 'Tis also remarkable that several acts of this nature are recorded in sacred history and so much notice taken of 'em there. One is in the seventh [chapter] of Luke 7, [vv.] Luke 7:36-38, “And one of the Pharisees desired him that he would eat with him. And he went into the Pharisee's house, and sat down to meat. And, behold, a woman in the city, which was a sinner, when she knew that Jesus sat at meat in the Pharisee's house, brought an alabaster box of ointment, and stood at his feet behind him weeping, and began to wash his feet with tears, and did wipe them with the hairs of her head, and kissed his feet, and anointed them with the ointment.” This is not the same story that is in the text, for it was in a different place. That which we have an account of in the text was in the town of Bethany near Jerusalem; this, in Luke, is in the city of Capernaum in Galilee. But yet the act is very much like it. There, as here, a woman comes to show her respect to Christ by anointing him; and there, the ointment is out of [an] alabaster box, as here. That woman anointed Christ's feet and wiped them with the hair of her head, as Mary does in the text.

It is manifest that many, yea, most of those external acts of Christ, and concerning him, were mystical or symbolical and represented something spiritual. So did his miracles, as I have often observed; so his washing the feet {of his disciples}. So doubtless did his disciples' washing his feet, and anointing them and his head with precious ointment, as this woman did in the text. And doubtless 'tis much for the sake of the spiritual instruction contained in them that those things are so particularly recorded.

Having observed thus, that there is something spiritual intended by this story as recorded in Scripture, now you will doubtless be ready to

Inquire what is it, What spiritual things are signified by these acts of this dear friend of Jesus in thus anointing him?

And in answer to this inquiry, I would observe in general that there seems to be no better key to open the spiritual signification of this act of Mary's, than to consider what spiritual things in her this outward act of her's was an expression of. This act of hers was no insignificant act. It was an expression of something that she felt within. There was some spiritual

-- 387 --

thing within her that she intended this outward act as an expression and sign of. And if we examine and consider what that was, that will open to us the whole mystery, and show us what was signified or represented by these outward actions of hers.

If we look into the matter, we shall find that which she then was in the exercise of in her soul, that she expressed and intended to testify by this action, was what the action very naturally represented. And here, to be more particular:

1. At the same time that she poured forth the precious ointment out of her box, she as it were poured forth grace and divine love out of her heart upon Jesus Christ. She was a woman that had a great love to Christ, and was at that instant in an high exercise of grace and love to Christ, and did as it were pour forth her love upon [him] as she poured forth her sweet ointment out of her box. And this was what she intended to signify by it: she poured out this sweet ointment on Christ as a testimony and token of her love to him. And therefore this I suppose to be the spiritual thing represented and signified by it.

The sweet ointment poured forth out of Mary's box on the head of Christ, was a type of true grace and divine love flowing forth out of the heart of the true believer towards Jesus Christ. Grace in Scripture is often compared to oil or ointment. So it is in the parable of the ten virgins: the wise virgins had oil in their vessels; their oil signifies grace and their vessels that contained their oil signifies the same with the box, the vessel that contained Mary's oil, viz. the heart. They had oil in their vessels, that is, they had grace in their hearts.

So grace is compared to ointment with which the soul is anointed of God, in 1 John 2:20, “But ye have an unction from the Holy One, and ye know all things”; and v. 1 John 2:27, “But the anointing which ye have received of him abideth in you.” So 2 Corinthians 1:21, “Now he which stablisheth us with you in Christ, and hath anointed us, is God.”

And grace or divine love in the soul is naturally represented by a sweet perfumed ointment. For as that is sweet and has an excellent savor, so has the grace of God, and it brings an excellent savor and sweetness in the soul.

The Spirit of God, or the author of grace in the soul, was of old typified by a perfumed ointment, as by the holy anointing oil that was used in the tabernacle and temple. Exodus 37:29, “And he made the holy anointing oil, and the pure incense of sweet spices, according to the work of the apothecary.” This was a type of the Spirit of God as given in his gracious influences

-- 388 --

on the heart. So Christ compares the graces of his spouse to sweet perfumed ointments. Cant.9 Canticles 4:10, “How fair is thy love, my sister, my spouse! how much better is thy love than wine! and the smell of thine ointments than all spices!” As Mary's ointment was very precious and costly, so is that grace that is in the heart of a saint. It is a most precious thing and very costly. It cost a great price. All the silver and gold in the world would not have purchased it. It cost Christ's precious blood. Mary poured her sweet ointment upon Christ; so does the grace that is in the heart of a saint, when in exercise, flow out to the Lord Jesus Christ. Christ is anointed by the church as Jacob anointed the pillar, and as all Israel anointed David.

2. Mary, at the same time that she poured forth precious ointment out of an alabaster box, poured forth divine love towards Christ out of a pure heart. I have observed before that alabaster was a substance remarkable, even to a proverb, for its extreme whiteness and purity. So the saints are spoken of in Scripture as those that are pure in heart. Mary was such an one; she was a true saint, an Israelite indeed in whom was no guile, and was an eminent saint. All the saints, with respect to the guilt of sin, are already perfectly pure; they are perfectly washed from all guilt in the blood of Christ. And as to sincerity, they have pure hearts. In conversion God creates in men a clean heart, and renews in them a right spirit. And when the work of sanctification that is begun in them is finished, then their hearts shall be as clean and pure as alabaster, perfectly free from all stain of sin, and shall be presented to Christ without spot or {blemish}. Thus it is out of a pure heart that the saints do pour forth the exercises of divine love towards Jesus Christ.

3. Mary poured forth this precious ointment on Christ out of a broken box. So true grace flows out of a broken [heart].

Believers don't only pour forth the exercises of grace out of a pure heart. True Christian grace and divine love always flows out of a broken heart, an heart broken with a sense {of sin}. And the heart of a saint never sends forth such a sweet savor as when it is most broken and contrite, as is evidenced by Psalms 34:18, “The Lord is nigh unto them that are of a broken heart; and saveth such as be of a contrite spirit”; and Psalms 51:17, “The sacrifices of God are a broken spirit: a broken and a contrite heart, O God, thou wilt not despise.” Isaiah 57:15, “For thus saith the high and lofty One that inhabiteth eternity, whose name is Holy; I dwell in the high and holy place, with him also that is of a contrite and humble spirit, to revive the spirit of the humble, and to revive the heart of the contrite ones”; and

-- 389 --

Isaiah 66:1-02, “Thus saith the Lord, The heaven is my throne, and the earth is my footstool: where is the house that ye build unto me? and where is the place of my rest? For all those things hath mine hand made, and all those things have been, saith the Lord: but to this man will I look, even to him that is poor and of a contrite spirit, and trembleth at my word.” And grace never gives as sweet a savor to the believer himself.

This admirably represents the excellent frame that a believer is in {when of a broken heart}. In this also the external act of the woman in the text was a representation of what was then in her heart. By what the evangelist John informs us, we learn that the deed not only {touched} his head but {also} his feet. “Then took Mary a pound of ointment of spikenard, very costly, and anointed the feet of Jesus, and wiped them with her hair: and the house was filled with the odor of the ointment” [John 12:3]. One thing showed by this was her brokenness of heart. And that other parallel instance that we have in Luke does more remarkably illustrate this. We are told in [Luke 7, ] v. Luke 7:38, [she] “stood at his feet behind him weeping, and began to wash his feet with tears.” What a broken-hearted frame was she in! Though this be not the same instance, yet it probably was like it. Thus is livelily represented to us what the love and what the joys of a true Christian soul are.

4. The breaking of the box seems also to represent the believer's devoting and making a sacrifice of his whole heart to Christ. As 'tis said in Psalms 51:17, “The sacrifices of God are a broken spirit: a broken and a contrite heart, O God, thou wilt not despise.” Mary did not only devote that precious ointment to Christ and spend that upon him, but she devoted the box, that was also precious. As sacrifices that were offered to God of old were slain and cut in pieces, so she broke her precious box.

5. As she did not only anoint Christ's head with this ointment but also his feet, so the exercises of divine love that flow out of pure and broken hearts don't only flow out to Christ but his members— but the meanest of them. This precious sweet oil of the woman that flowed out of her alabaster {box is a type of that sweet love that flows out to the meanest of the members of Christ's body}.

6. It is said, John 12:3, “the house was filled with the odor of the ointment,” whereby [John] represents two things:

(1) It shows how the soul is filled {with the odor of true grace and divine love}. So it doubtless was then with her. And it may be observed that the more pure the heart is, the more it's like an alabaster box; and the more it is broken, the more is the soul filled.

(2) That the church of God is filled. [The] church [is] often represented

-- 390 --

under the representation of an house. When a person is much in such excellent frames, it tends to diffuse the savor and benefit of it to all around. [When] one in a particular church {is much in such excellent frames}, it tends to fill that whole church {with the savor and benefit of it}. And when grace [is] in its lively and humble exercise in one part of the church, the savor of it is diffused in other parts of the church, as we have seen heretofore and see at this day.

7. Mary thus poured {the ointment} on Christ while he sat at meat, which represents to us:

(1) The communion which the believing soul has with Christ when grace flows out. By the account John gives, she was a guest with Christ {at supper}. John 12:2-03, “There they made him a supper, and Martha served: but Lazarus was one of them that sat at the table with her. Then took Mary a pound of ointment, very costly, and anointed the feet of Jesus.” When the soul has sweet communion with Christ, then the believer does as it were feast with Christ. He is fed with that food that Christ has prepared for his people at great cost. Christ himself sits at the table with the believer at such a time. He enjoys his company. At such time the heart is pure. And,

(2) This seems especially to represent the exercise of grace towards Christ at the Lord's table. When a Christian partakes of the Lord's Supper in a spiritual manner, then Christ always sits at the table. So he was at the table in the first sacrament that ever was. And so it is still: whenever the believer does in a spiritual manner {partake of the Lord's Supper, Christ sits there at the table}. And at such times the heart is purified, and the exercises of divine love flow out of a broken heart like precious perfumed ointment upon Christ, the head of the church; and not only so, but towards others that are his fellow members, even towards the least and meanest of them, towards not only the head but the feet.

This feast is a feast of love. And then the soul is filled with the odor {of the ointment}. And then when Christians partake in such a manner, the church is filled {with the odor}; the whole house is filled. This does most livelily represent the manner in which believers ought to partake {of the Lord's Supper}, and do partake, when in the holy exercise of grace. Then they partake with pure hearts; then their hearts are broken, humble. {They} lie at Christ's feet, and anoint his feet. Then divine love flows out as precious ointment. Then the heart is devoted and offered up in sacrifice to Christ. Then love flows out towards fellow Christians, and even to the meanest and lowest of the members of Christ.

-- 391 --

Let us in such a manner approach the table of the Lord at this time and earnestly seek to God to give us pure hearts.1

Application.

Use I may be of Instruction.

First. From what has been said, we may learn that if persons are willing to do what they can for Christ, they need not comfort of acceptance, though it be but a little that they can do. What Mary did was no way profitable to Christ; it was only an act of respect.

Anointing him did him no good any further than it showed Mary's respect to him. But yet, it was most graciously accepted of Christ. And he gives that reason for it, v. Mark 14:8, “She hath done what she could.” Though it seemed to be but a small thing that she did, yet [she] showed her good will in it, and that if it had been in her power to have done more, she would have done it. And therefore Christ manifests his great acceptance of it, and promises great rewards for it.

This may be of great encouragement to such as complain that they can do but little for Christ. They are poor and low in the world, and persons of no great abilities, and that are not much regarded. They have but little opportunity to do anything for the glory of Christ or the advancement of his kingdom. Yet let such not be discouraged. Let 'em do what they can and, if they do it with a sincere mind, it will be most graciously accepted of Christ and gloriously rewarded, though it be but little. The poorest, meanest person in this whole congregation that has a sincere love to Christ and a desire to do something for his glory, though he be a little child and can do but very little, yet need not be discouraged from attempting to do what he can, for he need not doubt of his acceptance and reward. He that sincerely gives no more than a cup of cold water, shall in no case lose his reward. There is no danger that it will be passed over and that Christ won't take notice of it because it is but a little thing. What notice did he take of the widow that cast in but two mites in the treasury of the temple, when, though it was but a very little that she contributed, yet it was as much as she could, for she had no more. Mark 12:41-44, “And Jesus sat over against the treasury, and beheld how the people cast money into the treasury: and many that were rich cast in much. And there came a certain poor widow, and she threw in two mites, which make a farthing.

-- 392 --

And he called unto him his disciples, and saith unto them, Verily I say unto you, That this poor widow hath cast more in, than all they which have cast into the treasury: for all they did cast in of their abundance; but she of her want did cast in all that she had, even all her living.”

The great thing is that there should be an heart, a willing mind, to serve Christ and glorify him according to our ability. If there be this, there is no reason to question but we shall be accepted, though it be but little that we can do. 2 Corinthians 8:12, “if there be first a willing mind, it is accepted according to that a man hath, and not according to that he hath not.” Thus it is in particular cases and on special occasions, as in public contributions for pious and charitable uses: if a man be a cheerful giver and is ready to give according to his ability, his gift, though small, is an acceptable sacrifice to God.

Indeed, it is but a little that any of us can do for Christ, and it is a wonder that Christ will accept of anything that the most able of us can do. For he stands in need of none of us, neither can we be profitable to him. But yet there are glorious rewards in reserve for us if we do but show that we are heartily desirous to glorify him, by improving what talents and opportunities we have to that end. Christ will reward us with that which shall be infinitely profitable and beneficial to us, for that which is no profit to him. Though he be now, in his state of glory, above all want, yet he is graciously pleased to speak of his being fed and clothed and relieved by the good deeds of his saints (Matthew 25:35-36). And the reward is as great as if Christ was really hungry and thirsty and sick and necessitous and stands in need of our compassion and relief.

Though it be but a little that we can do, yet if we do anything for Christ in sincerity, there is no danger of its being ever forgotten. This was promised to Mary concerning that which she did to put respect upon Christ: that her act should be remembered and published so that, wherever the gospel should be preached throughout the whole [world], this that she did should be told for a memorial {of her}. And we see it fulfilled: we see how Christ has taken care that this act of hers should be recorded in the sacred records of the Word of God, to remain as long as the church remains and to be known everywhere where Christ's own name is known.

Use II may be [of Exhortation], to exhort Christless sinners earnestly to seek two things, viz. that their hearts may be weaned from their lusts, and humbled for sin. These two things were eminently represented in Mary's box of precious ointment. The weanedness of her heart from lusts was represented by her box being a box of alabaster, a pure white substance; and her being humbled for sin was represented by her box being broken,

-- 393 --

as the heart of one truly humbled is broken for sin. These are the two great things that you stand in need of: to have your heart thoroughly broke off from the objects of your lusts, and so purified from them; and to have your heart humbled for sin, and so to have a broken heart and a contrite spirit. These things are what sinners are exceeding difficultly brought to. They are difficultly brought to the former, wholly to forsake the objects of their lusts. And so they are exceeding hardly brought to the latter, to be truly humbled for sin. There is worldlimindedness stands in the way of the former, and there is self-righteousness stands in the way of the latter. And those two are the main difficulties that stand in the way of their salvation: the one stands in the way of their having a pure heart, and the other stands in the way of their having a broken heart. These two things are the main things wherein the difficulty of sinners' salvation consists. On the one hand, they have a carnal worldly spirit, a disposition to place their happiness on the objects of their worldly lusts. And they can't bear wholly to break off from and forsake these things. And on the other hand, there is a self-righteous disposition that, wherein they do deny themselves, they are lifted up with it and are proud of it, and think God obliged to 'em for it.

Man can't bear to quit the world and let that go, wholly to forsake all the pleasures and profits and honors of it. And then he can't bear to quit himself, to renounce his own worthiness. And for these reasons, many that seek are never able to obtain. In these two things it is that the straitness of the gate that leads to life chiefly consists. Many stick long at these difficulties. They stumble at these stumbling stones and they never get over these blocks in their way, and so never enter into life. They never come to have a pure heart and a broken heart.

They never get over the former of these difficulties, viz. a forsaking all their worldly lusts. Their lusts are dear to 'em, and though they are brought to forsake some of the objects of their lusts, yet {they are not brought to forsake all}. Some particular lust is dear, [some] sweet morsel. [They might forsake it] for a season, but not {permanently. They might} restrain [it], but not {relinquish it}. 'Tis a very hard thing indeed for sinners. And so they never come to have pure hearts. They may cleanse themselves from some particular sins, but yet they are under the dominion of their lusts. They will not wash their hearts from wickedness, but still their vain thoughts lodge within 'em. And so their hearts never come to be like Mary's box of alabaster.

And so also they never get over the latter of these difficulties. They never come to be truly humbled. Their pride and self-righteousness always stands in the way. They can't bear to quit all their own glory. If they

-- 394 --

seem to do it, yet in the same act they do the contrary. If they seem to humble themselves, yet that is the thing that lifts 'em up. That is what they make a righteousness of, that they are so weaned from their own righteousness.

Let sinners in this congregation that are under awakenings and fears of hell, and are seeking their salvation, take notice of it, that these two things are what they must be brought to, or else they never shall enter into the kingdom of God. Your heart must be brought wholly to renounce all worldly lusts. You must sell all for Christ, or else you never will have a pure heart and never can be an Israelite indeed in whom there is no guile. And then you must renounce all your own righteousness so as to acknowledge from your very heart that, when you have done in religion, whatever pains you have taken, it would be just with God wholly to disregard all and withhold any mercy, to cast you into hell, notwithstanding all your morality and religion.

And I now desire you that are seeking your salvation to observe that these two things are the most difficult things in the way of your salvation. These two things are the main things that you must be intent upon, earnestly seeking that you may be indeed taught; these are the difficulties that you are especially to cry to God to help you over. You need great convictions chiefly to bring you to these two things, viz. to renounce the world and to renounce your own righteousness, that so you may have both a pure heart and a broken heart.

Thorough convictions have a tendency to break men off from the world, from expecting happiness in the enjoyments of their lusts. And so thorough convictions have also a tendency to break men off from themselves and their own righteousness, and to bring 'em to despair of helping themselves.

If you were brought to these two things, it would be the way to have your soul filled with divine love and holy joy, as with a sweet ointment. It would be the way to be happy indeed, to have your whole soul filled with an excellent pleasant odor, as the room where Mary broke her box of ointment.

Therefore hear this, all ye hard-hearted sinners, all ye self-righteous seekers. Earnestly strive and cry mightily to God that you may be brought to these things.

If you are brought to these things, it will be the way to have your hearts become an acceptable sacrifice to Jesus Christ, to be graciously accepted and greatly rewarded, as Mary was.

Use III may be of Direction to Christians how to seek more spiritual comfort and sweet communion with the Lord Jesus Christ, viz. by seeking

-- 395 --

more purity and more brokenness of heart, so your hearts will become more like Mary's box, which was of pure alabaster and was broken. And that will be the way for your heart to flow with love and spiritual joy, as perfumed ointment flowed out of Mary's box. This will be the way for grace in your soul to send forth an excellent refreshing odor, like that precious ointment that filled the room with its odor. That will surely be the way for your soul to pour forth itself upon Christ in the exercises of sweet love and rejoicing as he sits at meat, and to have communion with him, and to have the blessed testimonies of his gracious acceptance and inward assurances of glorious rewards, as Mary had.

You need not doubt of it. If your heart be more purified from the world and more humbled and broken, it will undoubtedly receive more light and comfort and sensible communion with Christ. This is the instruction that the story, in the text and context, carries in it and was undoubtedly designed to teach.

Therefore you that are complaining of darkness and difficulties, and are longing that you might have more light and more sweet experience, as some others have, seek it in this way: strive earnestly to break your heart more off from worldly and carnal objects, and to wean yourself more entirely from 'em, and that it may be more humbled and broken with a sense of your unworthiness. These things are the foundation of their troubles. I cannot give saints better direction in order to an increase of spiritual comfort than this. There is nothing in the world that does so much prepare the heart of a saint for sweet communion with Christ as these two things, viz. the having it more broken off and purified from worldly lusts, and the having it more humbled. As these things are carried in the soul, the wider is the door opened that lets in light and the more is the wall broken down that separates between Christ and the soul. Therefore hearken all you that love the Lord Jesus Christ in sincerity. I know you long for more communion with Christ. I know you prefer that spiritual joy, the odor of that precious ointment, above all other pleasures and delights whatsoever, yea, above thousands of gold and silver. I know that your soul longs for it, and breathes and pants after it. Therefore seek it in this way.

First. Earnestly seek that your heart may be more purified and weaned from worldly lusts. The things that defile and pollute the soul are the defilements of the world. The enjoyments of this, if our heart cleaves to 'em, are of a very defiling polluting nature; they will fill the soul with filthiness. And therefore purity of heart is described by that, viz. keeping ourselves “unspotted from the world” (James 1:27).

This is a great bar in the way of the spiritual comfort of many persons,

-- 396 --

that they {fail to keep themselves unspotted from the world}. This is an occasion of Christ long absenting himself from them, and God's shading his face. And they seek the removal of their darkness and the returns of spiritual light and comfort in vain {that fail to keep themselves unspotted from the world}. Whereas if they would {purify themselves, they might well experience the returns of spiritual light}. Seeing God is a blessing promised to the pure in heart. And as this prepares persons to see God in heaven, so as the impurity of the heart is removed that arises from a fondness of worldly profits and pleasures and glory, so the clouds vanish, the veil is removed.

The more the soul is weaned from the world, the more is it unspotted from the world, and therefore, the more shall it have of the presence of Christ. For so much as the soul is washed and cleansed, and without spot or wrinkle, so much the more will Christ present it to himself. This don't only fit it to be presented to him in another world, but {in this world as well}. The more entirely Christ's spouse abstracts herself from all other lovers, the more will she be likely to have of Christ's company. How can she expect that Christ will delight to come to her and have much communion with her?

Therefore consider this, then, whoever thou art that hast labored under difficulties, and examine how it is with you. Don't your heart cleave very much to some worldly enjoyment, some carnal object? Are you not in a very worldly or a very proud or sensual frame? Is there not some carnal appetite that {remains within you}? Be exhorted, as you would have comfort and light and such sweet experience as others have, more to purify yourself. Search diligently, and see if there be no Achan in the camp [Jos 7]. You han't that comfort that others have: it may be something is the matter.

Purify yourself with respect to past pollutions. Don't you live in some injury that you have heretofore? And with respect to present and future pollution, break off from all. Let your heart be thus purified. This will be the way to have it filled full of holy and divine affection, and as full of heavenly comfort as Mary's alabaster box was of precious odoriferous ointment.

Second. If you would obtain more comfort, seek more humility and brokenness of heart. This is another thing that exceedingly prepares the way for spiritual comfort. Though Mary's box had precious ointment in it, yet it did not send forth its sweet odor till the box was broken. But as soon as ever her box was broken, the room was filled with the odor of the ointment. So it is with the heart of a Christian. While it remains hard, and

-- 397 --

while the grace that is in it don't send forth its savor, the sweetness and comfort of it is not enjoyed. When a Christian has been for a long time in a cold, lifeless, worldly frame, new comfort don't commonly arise till there has been a new humbling. They often seek comfort a great while before they obtain. But when once the heart is humbled, then the way is prepared for comfort; then the door is oped, and then light and sweetness soon comes in. When once the box is broken, then the precious ointment sends forth its savor, and the room is full of [it]. Therefore this is what you have to seek after, that your heart may be humbled anew. If your heart has been humbled already, yet {it may need to be humbled again}. So it was with Job.

There is pride and self-righteousness in your heart that stands in the way of your comfort, that wants to be subdued, a disposition to think too highly {of yourself}, an insensibleness of your own unworthiness. These things stand in the way to keep out spiritual comfort. Therefore often persons have new convictions before a renewal of comfort.

Let all that are seeking further comfort, mark these directions and follow 'em. For I am persuaded they are the directions of the word of God, and that this is the very way in which you ought to seek comfort, and that 'tis in this way that God is wont to bestow renewed comfort on his people.

Use IV. Of Exhortation, to imitate that act of Mary that we have an account of in the text. Let us do that which her act was a representation of. Christ is now as it were here: he is present in this town at this time; he gives extraordinary tokens and signs of his presence; he is present, as it were sitting at table, feeding one and another with the bread. Now, if we would show respect to Christ, now is a fit opportunity. Now I would call upon all,

First. To come and pour forth the precious perfumed ointment of heavenly grace, of holy and divine love, upon Christ, out of pure and broken hearts. Let us not be easy till we can be in that excellent frame that Mary seemed to be in, when she did that which we have an account of in the text and which this act was a representation of, till our hearts are filled with all the fullness of God, till our hearts are filled with pure and heavenly dispositions and affections, as full as that alabaster box was with precious ointment. Let us seek to have our heart full of the grace that is ten thousand times more precious than that ointment, ten thousand times more costly, that costs not only three hundred pence, or ten thousand times as much silver and gold, but the precious blood of the Son of God, and that is ten thousand times sweeter and pleasanter than the fragrancy of that ointment. And don't only seek to have such grace in your heart, but to have it in exercise, that it may not only be in your heart, but that it

-- 398 --

may flow out of your heart; that it may be ointment poured forth, and that it may be poured forth out of a broken heart. Seek the exercises of an humble broken-hearted faith and trust in Christ, and the exercises of an humble broken-hearted love to him and a broken-hearted resignation to him, and a broken-hearted joy in him and a humble obedience to all his commands. Come, all you that love the Lord Jesus; love him and rejoice in him after this manner. This is divine love, and this is joy unspeakable and full of glory. Come, behold this glorious King, this blessed Redeemer. He is worthy that you should put such respect upon him. He is worthy to be anointed with the oil of gladness by all his dear children, by all his redeemed ones. Come, all ye that love the Lord Jesus. Seeing that he is come amongst us at this time, come and let us anoint him King over us. Where shall we find one so worthy to reign over us? Where shall we ever find one so wise, so mighty, so able to save us and so gracious and ready to deliver us? Let us agree with one accord to anoint the spiritual David King here, King over this church and over this town, resolving that, for the time to come, we will be wholly subject to him and no more rebel against him.

Here, consider further for motive:

1. God hath anointed him.

2. Christ's heart was broken with grief and the wrath of God to pour out his heart's blood for us. Shall not we therefore pour out divine love out of broken hearts for him? His heart was made a sacrifice for us.

3. Christ has anointed his saints, and shall not they mutually anoint him? 1 John 2:20, 1 John 2:27, “[But ye have an unction from the Holy One, and ye know all things.… But the anointing which ye have received of him] abideth in you, [and ye need not that any man teach you: but as the same anointing teacheth you all things, and is truth, and is no lie, and even as it hath taught you, ye shall abide in him.]”

4. If we do this, we shall be accepted and rewarded as Mary was. It shall never be forgotten.

Second. Don't only anoint the head but also the feet of Jesus Christ. Love to Christ and brokenness of heart will bring us to it.

Third. Be willing to show your love to Christ in deeds that are costly, as well as those that are cheap and easy. So did Mary. She did not excuse herself and say, “What good will it do my Lord?”

And don't content yourselves only with showing love in word and in tongue. Mary did not so. Don't show your love only by doing easy duties, but difficult duties, by taking up your cross {and following Jesus}. And if at any [time] you are called to show your love to Christ by expending something of your earthly substance, don't give way to an objecting spirit;

-- 399 --

be open-hearted and open-handed on such occasions. Dispense with a liberal hand. Be ready to distribute, willing to communicate. Love is a liberal, bountiful grace. Nothing opens the heart and hand like love. The precious oil flowed freely out of Mary's alabaster box; so does the Christian, whose heart abounds with love, freely dispense of his possessions when the glory of Christ calls for it.
