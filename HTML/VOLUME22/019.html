The Danger of Corrupt Communication Among Young People
Let no corrupt communication proceed out of your mouth, but that which is good to the use of edifying, that it may minister grace unto the hearers. Ephesians 4:29.

The words contain a precept and counsel from the Apostle to the Christian Ephesians, respecting their speech or discourse one with another, which consists of two parts:

1. A declaration, wherein he advises against something. “Let no corruptive communication proceed out of your mouth.” In which part may be observed two things:

(1) The thing dehorted from or advised against, viz. corrupt communication. The word in the original2 that is translated “corrupt” properly signifies “rotten,” as a noisome sore, or as the flesh of a rotten stinking carcass; intimating that such kind of communication as often proceeds out of the mouths of wicked men is foul and filthy, more loathsome than the corruption of a stinking wound or than the rotten flesh of carrion, which men would abhor to have in their mouths. Much more should they abhor to have such corrupt filthy talk in their mouths as wicked men sometimes have.

(2) We may observe the manner in which the Apostle advises against it: don't let it “proceed out of your mouth”; i.e. utterly avoid it, never be guilty of [it]. Let not your conscience ever have it to accuse you of, that ever any such words came forth out of your mouth. The mouth is a part of our bodies in which we abhor to have that which is outwardly corrupt, as corrupt and stinking flesh. We abhor to have it enter into our mouths. But much more should we abhor to have that which is morally corrupt and filthy proceed out of our mouths: for “not that which goeth into the

-- 159 --

mouth; but that which cometh out of the mouth, defiles the man” (Matthew 15:11).

2. The other part of the advice that the Apostle here gives is an exhortation, wherein something is exhorted to. Wherein may be observed two things:

(1) The things advised, which is virtuous and profitable discourse: that which is “good to the use of edifying”; i.e. that which [is] good in itself and tends to do good to others; such discourse whereby you promote the good of each others' souls.

(2) The good end and great benefit of such discourse, with which the Apostle enforces this advice: “that it may minister grace to the hearers.” Such discourse tends to the best and most excellent benefit of those that hear it. It tends to minister grace to them, which is the most excellent benefit that any can receive, and is a thousand times better than if it ministered silver and gold, or earthly pleasures, or temporal health, or any other good whatsoever.

Doctrine.
Professing Christians, when they meet together, should avoid all corrupt discourse one with another, and should practice that whereby they may promote the good of each others' souls.

I. They should avoid all corrupt communication or discourse, as particularly:

First. They should avoid all profane conversation, all speaking lightly of those things that are of a sacred and divine nature. This is what many persons, especially young persons, are prone to in a corrupt time. The being of a serious and solemn spirit, and showing a great regard to things of religion, is out of credit among young people; and those that appear to be of such a spirit, they are very apt to call precise and to charge with being needlessly, foolishly scrupulous, and very often with being hypocrites and proud of their religion, and having a mind to appear better than others. And on the contrary, they are ready to look upon it something credible, and what will recommend 'em to their companions, to make a jest of things of religion, or at least to speak lightly of such things with a jesting air.

Such conversation is very corrupt and very provoking to God, and is more fit for heathens than for Christians. To speak lightly of things of religion is to speak lightly of God and contemn him, and is so looked upon of God.

-- 160 --

Second. They should avoid all unclean or lascivious communication. There are many persons, especially young persons, that are full of such conversation as this. They are foul-mouthed persons, and do seem to delight in unclean songs and telling lascivious stories, and in talking with a bold air of those things that modesty forbids to mention, and seem to look upon as their honor that they dare break over rules of modesty in their talk, as if it was a great attainment, a very commendable courage, to show that they ben't afraid to speak those things that modest persons choose to avoid mentioning.

With many young people, he is the best man, that shows most of that kind of wit and boldness that appears in lascivious jesting. Their diversion consists very much in such kind of mirth.

This [is] exceeding unbecoming Christians. Such persons, how much soever they may value themselves on their impudence and unclean wit, appear more like brutes than Christians. True Christianity is abhorrent to any such thing, as light is to darkness, and a due exercise of Christian holiness will make men more to abhor to take such talk into their mouths, than they would abhor to take a piece of a dead rotten carcass full of crawling worms into their mouths. The Apostle advises that such unclean communication be not so much as once named among Christians. Ephesians 5:3-04, “But fornication, and all uncleanness, and covetousness, let it not be once named amongst you, as becometh saints. Neither filthiness, nor foolish talking, nor jesting, which are not convenient.” It requires Christians, whose bodies are the temples of God and who are persons devoted to the holy Jesus, to be pure in heart and mouth. They should follow after “whatsoever things are pure” (Philippians 4:8). Christians are the children of the light and of the day, and therefore should walk as children of the light. Romans 13:13, “Let us walk honestly, as in the day; not in chambering and wantonness.” Christians are advised by the Apostle in Colossians 3:5, Colossians 3:8, to put away all filthy communication out of their mouths; and again in Colossians 4:6, that their speech should be “always with grace, seasoned with salt.” Salt is used to preserve meats from putrefying and becoming rotten carrion, as otherwise they would do; so our words should be kept from uncleanness, which makes 'em like filthy noisome corruption.

Third. Another sort of corrupt discourse that Christians should avoid when they meet together, is talking against others. Great part of the conversation of many young people, when they get together, consists in this, in making themselves diversion with others' faults and blemishes. Very much of the mirth and laughter that young people have together, consists in talking of and laughing at these and those things that others that are

-- 161 --

absent have said and done. Many seem to take a pride to speak of others behind their backs with an air of contempt, and make sport of those things in them that they reckon show how weak and foolish they are. They spend a great deal of time in talking against other young people and also in ridiculing old people. And so they entertain one another and pass away the time that they spend together. A great many stories are told against others, and it is well if many false and slanderous reports are not told among others. At least things are set out beyond the truth to make others appear the more ridiculous and to make the more sport for themselves.

Which is very unbecoming professing Christians. When it is inquired in the fifteenth Psalm, “Who shall abide in thy tabernacle? who shall dwell in thy holy hill?” it is answered, “He that backbiteth not with his tongue”; and it is said in the one hundred and first Psalm, v. Psalms 101:5, “Whosoever privily slandereth his neighbor, will I cut off.” The Apostle advises in the fourth of Ephesians, thirty-first verse, that all evilspeakings be put away from among Christians; and so the apostle [Peter] (1 Peter 2:1). The apostle James does particularly warn Christians against this. James 4:11, “Speak not evil one of another, brethren. He that speaketh evil of his brother, and judgeth his brother, speaketh evil of the law, and judgeth the law.”

Fourth. Another sort of discourse that may be called corrupt and that professing Christians should avoid, is that which is unseasonable or unsuitable for the time; as when persons, when they are together on the sabbath, will use diverting kind of discourse, or talk of worldly and indifferent matters, as on other days. That talk, that at another time may be innocent, may become very corrupt by the unseasonableness of it.

So when persons will as it were mix religion and vain and diverting discourse together: as when persons come together under pretense of holding a religious meeting for reading and praying and such religious exercises, and will immediately, as soon as the religious exercise is over, fall to jesting and laughing, or go into vain diverting sort of talk, manifesting that they have attended religion merely out of custom, that they have done it as a task and, now it is over, are willing to get as far from it as ever they can. This is but to make a mock of duties of religion. And when they are attended after this manner, they had better be let alone. When things are thus disagreeably mixed together, it renders both their religious exercise, and their discourse, too, abominable to God. This is like the Jews' bringing their merchandises— their sheep and oxen and doves and worldly concerns— into the temple, at which Christ expressed so much indignation.

II. Christians, when they are together, should use themselves to such

-- 162 --

discourse as may be profitable, and whereby they may promote the good of each others' souls.

First. They should use themselves to such discourse as is innocent and virtuous, agreeable to the rule of God's Word, setting a watch upon the door of their lips. Psalms 141:3, “Set a watch, O Lord, before my mouth; keep the door of my lips.” Keeping their mouth as with cord and bridle. Psalms 39:1, “I said, I will take heed to my ways: I will keep my mouth with a bridle.” Using their tongues for the glory of God and as becomes reasonable creatures and Christians, avoiding all appearance of profaneness, all foolish talking and jesting, all backbiting, being blameless and harmless as the sons of God, and pure and honorable in their conversation.

Second. They should accustom themselves to conversation that may tend to each others' instruction and growth in Christian knowledge, in knowledge of the things of God. They should discourse of the doctrines and principles of religion, inquiring one of another and entering into such discourse as tends to the enlightening one of another.

They should discourse together of the Word of God, of things that are contained in the holy Scriptures, tending to engage one another in reading and searching the Scriptures, and helping each other to understand the meaning of it.

They should be discoursing together of things that they hear in the preaching of the Word, which would be an excellent means of fastening things that they hear in their memory and engaging their attention more in hearing for the time to come, and causing that the preaching of the Word should take the greater impression on their minds, and would prevent their hearing all only in a customary slighty manner, losing all immediately as soon as they come out of the meeting house. They should use themselves to discourse together of the attributes of God, his power and wisdom, {the} works of God in creation and providence, and, above all, in the work of redemption. Psalms 105:2, “Talk ye of all his wondrous works.” And Psalms 145:10-12, “All thy works shall praise thee, O Lord; and thy saints shall bless thee. They shall speak of the glory of thy kingdom, and talk of thy power; to make known to the sons of men his mighty acts, and the glorious majesty of his kingdom.” This that is here spoken of as the practice of the saints is an excellent practice, most becoming the saints.

Third. They should accustom themselves, when together, to talk together of such things as tend to the awakening one another and rousing each others' souls out [of] a careless, dull and dead frame, and begetting in each other a deep sense of the infinite importance of the things of religion; and should be much in speaking of the vanity of the things of the

-- 163 --

world, the shortness and uncertainty of life, and how great a thing it is to die; and should be talking together of the great things of another world, speaking of the day of judgment and the great things that will come to pass there; speaking of heaven and hell, and talking much of eternity.

Fourth. They should be much in talking together of such things as tend to each others' warning, speaking of the evil and danger of such and such sinful practices, the folly of ways, the corruption of the heart of men and its readiness to fall in with temptation, and the great need we stand in of divine help; and should particularly be speaking of the evil of such kind of sins as persons of their age and their circumstances are especially ready to fall into.

Fifth. They should be much in talking of such things as have a tendency to enkindle and warm holy affections in each others' souls. So they should talk much, when together, of Jesus Christ and his excellency, and the wonderful grace of God in giving him to die for sinners. They should be speaking much of the dying love of Jesus Christ. They should be speaking much of the great benefits and blessings of the gospel that are bestowed on the saints, the glorious happiness of the state of such as have truly an interest in Christ. They should be much in speaking of the saints' happiness and glory in heaven, where they will be perfectly holy and happy in the full enjoyment of God and Christ, and in perfect love and friendship one with another, forever and ever.

They should be much in speaking of things that convey vital piety, and the influence that the Holy Spirit of God has upon the souls of true saints, and of the exercises of the graces of the Holy Spirit.

Sixth. They should be much in talking of such things as tend to direct and stir up each other to their duty. It should be matter of discourse among 'em what is their duty in such and such cases, and of the difficulties and temptations that are commonly to be met with in the way of the duty, and of the need of care and of divine help in duty. They should be frequent in such discourse, so that they may inure themselves to it, and take heed that they keep their hand in, if I may so speak, that so it may always be easy and natural to 'em to fall into such discourse. For the less they use themselves to it, the more difficulties will increase in the way of entering into it; and the more they accustom themselves to it, the more easy [and] pleasant will it be.

Application.

The Use that I would make of this doctrine is to exhort those here present, especially young persons, for whose sake this meeting was principally

-- 164 --

intended, to comply with the duty recommended in the doctrine. Let me, who God has set to be the watchman to watch for your souls, now this day counsel and exhort you in the name of God to avoid all vain and corrupt conversation and accustom yourselves, when one with another, to such discourse as may be profitable, and by which you may promote the good of each others' souls.

You are come here this day to hear what counsels and direction I have to give you from the Word of God: and by coming here, you do as it were say that you desire to be instructed and directed in the way of your duty, and that you desire that I, whom am your pastor, should tell you what you ought to do for your souls' good. And this is the advice that I have to give you, which you ought to receive as counsel from Christ himself, because it is agreeable to the mind and to the word of Christ, and that which certainly is wholesome for your soul.

And I may the rather expect that you will hearken to me, because I know that many of you making a profession of loving God and Christ above all things in the world, and of having devoted yourselves to the service of God and the business of religion. Therefore let me pray you to hearken to some things that I have to lay before you as motives to stir you up to comply with this counsel, to accustom yourselves, when together, to such communication as has been spoken of.

First. Consider that the good of the soul is of infinitely the greatest concern.

Second. Speech is one great thing whereby God has distinguished us from the brute creatures and set us above them, and 'tis therefore most reasonable that we should improve it for him.

Third. Hereby you will promote your own and each others' profit and pleasure both together.

This will be the most profitable discourse, and not only so, but you will find it the most pleasant. It would make your company and conversation together pleasant, much more pleasant than to fill it up with vanity, with foolish and lewd jesting, talking against others, spending away the time in that which serves to no purpose but only to take off the heart from everything that is good. Such conversation would be pleasant because it would be profitable; the profit that you would find in it would make you love it and take delight in it.

It would be pleasant because it would naturally tend to unite you in a virtuous friendship one with another. [There is] no discourse or conversation that so unites persons together as religious discourse, that by which they may instruct and profit each others' souls. It naturally tends to knit

-- 165 --

persons together in friendship. And the reason is, that where such discourse is carried on, there the God of love and peace is present; there is Christ among them, the Prince of love and peace.

Fourth. This is the way to keep a good conscience.

Fifth. If you follow this advice, then your company won't indispose you to the duty of secret prayer.

Sixth. Then you will be in the way of the blessing of God in your company and in all other concerns. See that blessed text of Scripture in Malachi 2:16-17, “For the Lord, the God of Israel, saith that he hateth putting away: for one covereth violence with his garment, saith the Lord of hosts: therefore take heed to your spirit, that ye deal not treacherously. Ye have wearied the Lord with your words. Yet ye say, Wherein have we wearied him? When ye say, Every one that doeth evil is good in the sight of the Lord, and he delighteth in them; or, Where is the God of judgment?”

Seventh. Then you won't repent of your discourse and conversation afterwards. When you use corrupt discourse, vain {conversation or} unclean, you expect to repent; you intend it.

Eighth. This is the way not to lay a foundation in youth for calamities and sorrows all your life after. Job says, Job 13:26, “Thou writest bitter things against me, thou makest me to possess the sins of my youth.”

Seeing, therefore, there are so many motives to induce you to hearken to this counsel, let me insist upon it with you that are here present. When you come together, let Christ have a place among; don't wholly shut him out of your company; he is ready to be one of you. You ben't too good to admit him into your company. Yea, let me advise often to take him with you. He will do [no] hurt in company; he won't spoil it, but will make it every way better, more profitable, more honorable and more pleasant. Is it not a great and infinite condescension in so glorious a person, that he will vouchsafe {to be among you}? Is it not an unspeakable honor?

If you will admit Christ, there will be his love and his power and his joy among you, that will spoil no company. And it not only will be with you then, but it will follow you home and it will remain with you oftentimes afterwards.

You have heretofore used yourself {to profitable discourse}. Why have you left it off? Did it do you any hurt? Did it spoil your company? Is it better with you since you have left it off? Was God a wilderness to you, or a land of darkness, that you have forsaken him?

It may be all that now hear me won't comply with this exhortation, and it may be but a very few of this society will comply. I would therefore give advice [to] those few that are willing to hearken, in a few words:

-- 166 --

1. As much as they can, to avoid the society of those that use themselves to corrupt communication and won't hearken.

2. Seek out such company where profitable and religious {conversation is used}. Let those few that are willing, associate together. If others mock, and say 'tis only pride and hypocrisy, don't regard. God will observe it. God will hearken and hear, and a book of remembrance will be written. See if you can't find out companions that will fall in with this advice, and be much with them, and Christ will be with you.

3. Let those that you choose for your special friends and peculiar friends be those that are lively in religion and that delight in religious conversation. Special friends may converse freely without fear or suspicion one of another, without suspecting one another of pride and hypocrisy. And 'tis more natural for intimate friends to unburden themselves one to another.

4. Take heed, after you have begun, not gradually to leave off. Satan will envy your happiness. You have corruptions that will oppose. There will be many snares, but take heed of the beginnings. Keep up this practice with great care and watchfulness.
