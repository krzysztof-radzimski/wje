Like Rain upon Mown Grass
He shall come down like rain upon the mown grass. Psalm 72:6.

It is observable that the Holy Spirit, in some of the Psalms, has a twofold aim and intendment, the one more immediate and the other more ultimate. They have respect more immediately to some person that is an eminent type of Christ. But their principle and more ultimate respect is to Christ himself. So many of the Psalms have a more immediate respect to David; but the main respect is to Christ, the son of David. So some of the Psalms have a more immediate respect to Solomon, but ultimately respect Christ.

So in this Psalm, wherein is the text, it more immediately respects Solomon, as appears by the title, “A Psalm for Solomon,” which titles are part of the canon of the Scriptures. They have respect to Solomon as a very eminent type of Christ. But the main view of the Holy Ghost in the words of this Psalm is to Christ himself and, therefore, the expressions used in the Psalm are much more accommodated to the glory of Christ's kingdom than to the reign of Solomon, as vv. Psalms 72:3-04: “The mountains shall bring peace to the people, and the little hills, by righteousness. He shall judge the poor of the people, he shall save the children of the needy, and shall break in pieces the oppressor”; and vv. Psalms 72:8-09, “He shall have dominion also from sea to sea, and from the river unto the ends of the earth. They that dwell in the wilderness shall bow before him; and his enemies shall lick the dust”; vv. Psalms 72:16-17, “There shall be an handful of corn in the earth upon the top of the mountains; the fruit thereof shall shake like Lebanon: and they of the city shall flourish like grass of the earth. His name shall endure for ever: his name shall be continued as long as the sun: and men shall be blessed in him: all nations shall call him blessed.” And indeed, the whole Psalm is much more applicable to Christ than to Solomon. And there are some expressions in the Psalm that can't in any proper sense be applied to any but Christ, as particularly v. Psalms 72:5: “They shall fear thee as long as the sun and moon endure, throughout all generations”;

-- 301 --

v. Psalms 72:8, “He shall have dominion also from sea to sea, and from the river unto the ends of the earth”; v. Psalms 72:11, “Yea, all kings shall fall down before him: all nations shall serve him”; [and] v. Psalms 72:17, “His name shall endure for ever: his name shall be continued as long as the sun: and men shall be blessed in him: all nations shall call him blessed.” And the words of the text are much more applicable to Christ than they are to Solomon— “He shall come down like rain upon the mown grass”— and doubtless they are principally intended of Christ.

In the text are two things to be observed:

1. What Christ is represented by in dispensing his benefits on the children of men, viz. by2 the rain coming down from heaven. Christ, in the communication of his benefits to the children of men, is compared sometimes to a cool stream or river of water, sometimes to a fountain, sometimes to a rock sending forth water; sometimes to a tree bearing nourishing and life-giving fruit and affording a pleasant shadow, sometimes to bread from heaven or the manna that came from heaven with the dew, and sometimes to a physician dispensing healing balm; sometimes to the sun diffusing his bright and warm, pleasant, enlivening beams, sometimes to the rain coming down from heaven.

2. We may observe what those that are the subjects of the benefits of Christ are compared to in receiving them, viz. to the mown grass. The subject of the benefits of Christ is elsewhere compared to the grass receiving refreshing showers and dews, as 2 Samuel 23:4, “as the tender grass springing out of the earth by clear shining after rain”; and so Deuteronomy 32:2. But what is observable here is that those that receive Christ's benefits are particularly compared to the mown grass.

Therefore, in discoursing from these words I would explore this matter. And this may be the subject of the following discourse, viz.

[Doctrine.]
Christ, in communicating himself and dispensing his benefits, does as it were come down as the rain on the mown grass.

In showing how Christ is said to come down as the rain on the mown grass, I would do these three things:

I. I would show how Christ is said to come down from heaven; and,

II. How those that are the subjects of the benefits of Christ are fitly represented by the mown grass; and,

-- 302 --

III. How Christ, in coming down upon them and communicating himself and his benefits to them, is like the rain upon such grass.

I. I would show in what respects Christ is said to come down from heaven. He comes down from heaven in the four following respects.

First. In his incarnation. Before Christ was incarnate, he had only the divine nature. But heaven is always represented in Scripture as God's special abode. Christ was from eternity in the bosom of the Father. But when he was incarnate and became man, he did as it were come down and tabernacle with us. He made this evil, sinful and accursed world the place of his habitation. In this respect Christ is said to come down from heaven, in John 3:13, “no man hath ascended up to heaven, but he that came down from heaven, even the Son of man which is in heaven.” We are not to understand Christ's coming down from heaven in his incarnation as though his human nature came down from heaven, for his human nature never had been in heaven. It but then began to have a being. Nor are we to understand that the divine nature left heaven, so that heaven was empty of the divine nature of Christ. For you may observe Christ, when speaking of his coming down from heaven, says the Son of man is in heaven, i.e. as to his divine nature. But the meaning is that he that before was only in the divine nature, the proper place of whose special presence is heaven, henceforward was not only God but man, who is a native of this lower world, and as such dwelt here.

Second. Christ will come down from heaven at the day of judgment. 1 Thessalonians 4:16, “For the Lord himself shall descend from heaven with a shout, and the voice of the archangel.” Christ then shall come down from heaven in a different sense from what he did in his incarnation in two respects. For then, in the first place, his human nature shall come down from heaven. For now the man Christ Jesus is in heaven with his glorified body. And he with his glorified body shall come down from heaven. And second, Christ, as to his divine nature, shall then be gloriously present, be manifested upon earth. God, as to his glorious presence, is now only in heaven; but at the day of judgment God will appear in this lower world in that glorious majesty and unspeakable displays of his greatness and excellency that he doth now in heaven. The divine nature will then manifest itself at that day in the person of Christ, and in and with his glorified human nature. Therefore we have such expressions concerning Christ's second coming, as that he will “come in the glory of his Father” [Matthew 16:27], and that then the wicked “shall be punished with everlasting destruction

-- 303 --

from the presence of [the] Lord, and from the glory of his power” [2 Thessalonians 1:9].

Third. Christ is said to come from heaven in some great and very remarkable dispensations of providence, in which he appears on the behalf of his church and against its enemies. So these great dispensations of providence that were at the time of the destruction of Jerusalem are sometimes called Christ's coming, and are compared to his second coming. So also what shall be done at the destruction of Antichrist: Christ then, in his providence, will make a glorious display of his power and holiness, justice and mercy, as though he had appeared from heaven.

Fourth. Christ comes down from heaven in the saving influences of his Spirit on the hearts of men. The Holy Spirit that sanctifies and comforts the hearts of the elect is his Spirit. 'Tis he that sends him from heaven into the hearts of his. The Holy Spirit comes down from heaven. 1 Peter 1:12, “with the Holy Ghost sent down from heaven.” And he comes and acts in the name of Christ, so that Christ is represented as coming by his Spirit. John 14:16-18, “And I will pray the Father, and he shall give you another Comforter, that he may abide with you for ever; even the Spirit of truth; whom the world cannot receive, because it seeth him not, neither knoweth him: but ye know him; for he dwelleth with you, and shall be in you. I will not leave you comfortless: I will come to you.” Verse John 14:23, “Jesus answered and said unto him, If a man love me, he will keep my words: and my Father will love him, and we will come unto him, and make our abode with him.” Christ himself is said to be in the hearts of men by his Spirit's being in them. Romans 8:9-10, “But ye are not in the flesh, but in the Spirit, if so be the Spirit of God dwell in you. Now if any man have not the Spirit of Christ, he is none of his. And if Christ be in you, the body is dead because of sin; but the Spirit is life because of righteousness.”

Thus when a sinner is converted, Christ does as it were come down from heaven and enter into the heart to dwell there. 'Tis Christ's coming down from heaven in this last respect that I shall mainly insist on in my discourse on this subject. But I proceed, in the

II. [Second] place, to show how those that are the subjects of Christ's benefits and receive the blessings of his salvation are as the mown grass.

First. They are fitly compared to grass on account of their weakness and frailty. The grass is a low and feeble plant. It vastly differs from tall trees. It is easily cut down. Many hundreds of spires of grass may be cut down at one stroke of the scythe. 'Tis easily devoured or trodden under foot. It soon withers and dries up.

-- 304 --

On these accounts men are often in Scripture compared to the grass of the field. Isaiah 40:6-07, “All flesh is grass, and all the goodliness thereof as the flower of the field: the grass withereth, the flower fadeth: because the spirit of the Lord bloweth upon it: surely the people is grass.” So Psalms 103:15-16, “As for man, his days are as grass: as the flower of the field, so he flourisheth. For the wind passeth over it.” And so in many places.

Man is like grass in that respect, that he depends on the influences of the heavens. 'Tis the sun and rain that causes the grass to grow and flourish. If the rain be withheld, it soon withers. So it is with the soul of man. Its prosperity depends wholly on the influences of the Sun of righteousness and on spiritual showers.

Second. They that are the subjects of the benefits of Christ are like mown grass.

1. Mankind are first destroyed by the fall before they receive the benefits of Christ's redemption. Man in his first state was as grass that flourishes and grows up. He was in great prosperity. He was set in high honor and dignity, was an excellent creature, appeared amiable in the image of God that was the glory of his nature, was in a most happy condition. God crowned him with glory and honor.

But Satan found means to cut him down. He soon fell and lost all his glory. In the morning he was flourishing; but in the evening he was cut down and withered. Man, being in honor, abode not, but became like the beasts that perish (Psalms 49:12). When he sinned he was not only much wounded and made to languish, [but] wholly cut down as the grass that is mown down even with the ground. Satan was the mower and sin was the scythe, and he was cut down at one stroke. He at once lost all his glory. He wholly lost that image of God, and all holy and excellent principles were banished out of his heart. He wholly lost his spiritual life and became dead in sin. He wholly lost the favor of God. He lost his honor and dignity and sank into an exceeding vile state. He lost his happiness. The union was cut off between heaven and earth. He lost communion with God. He lost his pleasant outward circumstances. He was driven out of paradise. He lost his former beauty and vigor of body, and his body appeared deformed and vile so as to be not fit to be seen without a covering. The earth was cursed for his sake, and this lower world lost its pleasant and joyful appearance, and a kind of melancholy gloom was brought over the face of all nature, and everything had the marks of God's displeasure and wrath.

All mankind were cut down at this one blow. The whole race were mown down and utterly destroyed. Man, as he is by nature, is not only in a sorrowful but in a lost and ruined condition.

-- 305 --

In such a state as this does the redemption of Jesus Christ find all that are the subjects [of it]— like grass cut down with a scythe even with the ground. Upon such as these it is that Christ comes down from heaven.

2. When Christ comes to do great and remarkable things for his church, he commonly finds it as it were cut down, and that one of these two ways: either by general corruption or persecution. Thus when God came to deliver the children of Israel out of Egypt, he found his church there as it were cut down in both these respects. It was almost destroyed with corruption, being overrun with Egyptian idolatry, and was as it were cut down, dispirited and discouraged with the cruelty of their taskmasters: and so afterwards, when he raised up deliverers for them from time to time in the days of the judges, fulfilling that in Deuteronomy 32:36, “For the Lord shall judge his people, and repent himself for his servants, when he seeth that their power is gone, and there is none shut up, or left.”

When Christ came down from heaven by his incarnation, the church was like mown grass cut down and almost destroyed by the perversity of Jewish corruption, either Sadducean infidelity or Pharisaical pride, hypocrisy and superstition. So when Christ came remarkably to deliver his church and overthrow their enemies in the time of Constantine the Great, he found it as it were cut down with a most cruel persecution that had been just before, wherein innumerable multitudes all over the world were most inhumanely butchered, and the enemies of the church triumphed and thought they had gained their purpose and had utterly destroyed the Christian church. So when Christ remarkably appeared to revive his church in the time of the Reformation, he found it in a manner destroyed by the overspreading of the corruptions, darkness and persecutions of the church of Rome. So when Christ shall come to the destruction of Antichrist he will find his church in a very low state and as it were cut down by Satan, her grand enemy. So when he shall come at the end of the world— “shall he find faith on earth?” (Luke 18:8)— the church shall again be exceedingly reduced.

3. 'Tis oftentimes so, that the seasons wherein Christ most remarkably descends on particular persons in the blessed tokens of his favor, are times wherein they are as it were cut down by affliction. It is often God's manner, when his saints are under some very great affliction and are reduced to extremity, so that their hearts are ready to sink and fail, to appear remarkably for 'em. So the apostle Paul observes that God sometimes dealt with him. 2 Corinthians 1:8-10, “For we would not, brethren, have you ignorant of our trouble which came to us in Asia, that we were pressed out of measure, above strength, insomuch that we despaired even of life: but we had

-- 306 --

the sentence of death in ourselves, that we should not trust in ourselves, but in God which raiseth the dead: who delivered us from so great a death and doth deliver: in whom we trust that he will yet deliver us.” Christ's coming down for the help of his people when as it were cut down with afflictions and distress and the cruel oppression of enemies, seems to be what is especially intended in the text, by some of the foregoing verses, as particularly [v.] 2 Corinthians 1:2: “Grace be to you and peace from God our Father, and from the Lord Jesus Christ”; and [v.] 2 Corinthians 1:4: “Who comforteth us in all our tribulation, that we may be able to comfort them which are in any trouble, by the comfort wherewith we ourselves are comforted of God.”

4. At the end of the world, Christ will come down from heaven upon the dead bodies of those that have been cut down by temporal death. Death cuts down all, and is continually every day doing in the field of the world as the mower doth in the field of grass; he daily cuts down thousands. Men's being cut down by temporal death is in Scripture compared to the mowing of grass in the field. Psalms 90:5-06, “For thou earnest them away as with a flood; they are as sleep: in the morning they are like grass which groweth up. In the morning it flourisheth, and groweth; in the evening it is cut down, and withereth.” At the last day, Christ shall come down on the dead bodies of his saints that have thus been cut down, and they also, as well as their souls, shall be the subjects of Christ's benefits. 1 Corinthians 15:21-23, “For since by man came death, by man also came the resurrection of the dead. For as in Adam all die, even so in Christ shall all be made alive. But every man in his own order: Christ the firstfruits; afterward they that are Christ's at his coming.”

5. Those that Christ comes down upon in the saving and comforting influences of his Spirit on their souls are cut down by humiliation and mortification. They that are made partakers of the saving benefits of Christ and live through him, they must be the subject of a kind of death. They must be as it were killed before they are made alive. They must die with Christ that they may live with him. Romans 6:5-08, “For if we have been planted together in the likeness of his death, we shall be also in the likeness of his resurrection: Knowing this, that our old man is crucified with him, that the body of sin might be destroyed, that henceforth we should not serve sin. For he that is dead is freed from sin. Now if we be dead with Christ, we believe we shall also live with him.”

As to that which naturally flourishes in the heart of men, it must be mown down. Old things must be done away and destroyed. The old man must be cut down. Though it may have taken deep root and may have grown great and shot forth many branches, it must be cut down, down to

-- 307 --

the ground, in the circumcision of the heart. The old man must be crucified and killed. Galatians 2:20, “I am crucified with Christ.”

Man must be as it were cut off from his own stock, and from that wood3 alike of which he is naturally a branch, in order to his being engrafted into Christ. He must be cut off as to that life that he lives by nature in order to the renewed life by Christ.

(1) They that are the subjects of the saving benefits of Christ in their souls are cut down by humiliation. They are cut down by conviction of sin. They who before thought themselves well out, were secure and at ease in their sin, and pleasing and comforting themselves with vanity, have their quietness and comfort cut off. They are cut down by the scythe of God's holy law. They are brought to see themselves guilty and miserable, are convinced that they have destroyed themselves.

As man in his fallen state is really cut down and destroyed, so those that are redeemed by Christ are sensibly. They see themselves lost creatures; they are cut down in their own sense and apprehension of things. Though for a while they did as it were stand upright on their own bottoms, yet they are brought to see themselves fallen. They are cut down as to the high thoughts they had of their own strength and ability to help themselves.

They are cut down as to the high thoughts they had of their own righteousness. Though that might appear for a while as a pleasant plant appearing full of beautiful flowers and pleasant fruits, yet all the pleasing idea they have vanishes away, and their righteousness appears not as a flourishing plant, but it falls and is cut off even with the ground, and withers away and dries up, and comes to nothing in their eyes.

Their vain confidences, though built up as high as Babel, fall and come to the ground.

Their vain hopes in themselves are cut off, and they are brought to despair in themselves, and they are dead to the law and crucified to their own righteousness. Romans 7:9, “For I was alive without the law once: but when the commandment came, sin revived, and I died.” Galatians 2:19, “I through the law am dead to the law, that I might live unto God.”

And they are not only the subjects of a legal, but also an evangelical humiliation. That natural disposition that is in them to exalt them [selves] and to entertain high thoughts of themselves is cut down. Man naturally in his pride stands erect and lifts up his head on high, but they that are in Christ are made low and leveled with the ground in humility. They are become poor in spirit and persons of a broken heart.

-- 308 --

(2) They that are the subjects of the saving benefits of Christ are cut down by mortification. Their worldly lusts and carnal appetites are mortified and are as it were cut off. Galatians 5:24, “They that are Christ's have crucified the flesh with the affections and lusts.” Men, if they would have Christ, must be cut down as to their worldly happiness. Men naturally place their happiness in the things of the world. They see no better good. These things are their life, and they set their hearts upon them as their portion. And if you take 'em away, you undo them. You cut them down to the very ground. Men can't bear to part with the world and with their lust. Some natural men will dare to deny some particular lust and to refrain from some particular worldly enjoyment. But to deny all ungodliness and worldly lusts, and to part with all the world and sell all for Christ, is like death to them, for they see nothing to make up for so great a loss. Men love themselves. They can't bear to be cut down at such a rate as this. To consent to it would, in their eye, [be] a throwing themselves away.

But he that would find his life must lose it, and he that loses his life for Christ's sake shall find it.

Though sin be sweet to us, yet we must let it go. We must spit the sweet morsel out of our mouths, and the world must be crucified to us, and we to the world.

And so we must as it were forget our own country and our father's house (Psalms 45:10), and must be cut down as to all worldly expectations and dependence.

And so we must become like the mown grass, wholly cut off from the earth, which is natural to us, of which we naturally have rooting and out of which we seek the nourishment of our natural life.

Having thus shown how those that are the subject of Christ's benefits [are as the mown grass], I now come, in the

III. [Third] place, to show how Christ, in communicating himself to such subjects, is like the rain on the mown grass.4 I answer, in descending upon them he refreshes, revives and restores them as the rain doth the grass after it is mown.

Though the grass be a very weak and despicable plant, yet this is remarkable

-- 309 --

concerning it: that when it is cut down, it will sprout again when watered by the showers of heaven, not less than it did before. Cutting of it down, though it be never so often, yet don't put its case at all out [of] hope. It may be cut down year after year, and many times in a year. If it be devoured from day to day by the beasts that feed upon it, it will revive and grow by the dew and rain.

When a field is mown close to the ground, yet the rain refreshes it and as it were heals the wound of the scythe, and presently it appears green and pleasant again, and seems to rejoice and to be clothed with gladness. It as it were rises from the dead and grows and flourishes anew.

So it is with those that are the subjects of Christ's benefits. Therefore it is said in Isaiah 26:19, “Thy dead men shall live, together with my dead body shall they arise. Awake and sing, ye that dwell in the dust: for thy dew is as the dew of herbs”; i.e. you shall be revived after you are as it were killed, as the dew revives the grass or herbs after they are cut down. This matter may be more fully explained under two particulars:

First. There is enough in Christ to refresh, revive and restore those that are as it were cut down as the mown grass. Though men are cut down by the fall, though they have utterly ruined themselves, though they are dead in sin naturally, and that death be never so much confirmed by actual sin and a contracted hardness of heart, yet there is enough in Christ to give new life to such souls.

Though God's favor be utterly lost, though God has been so much provoked and never so dreadful a damnation deserved, yet there is enough in Christ to restore to the favor of God, and to bring into the state of his children and to make happy in God's everlasting smiles.

There is enough in Christ to restore rest, peace and comfort to those who are as it were cut down to the earth with terror of conscience, whose burden and distress has been so great as quite to sink the soul. And though its hope has been as it were cut off and all comfort seemed to be gone, and the soul was brought to give up itself as lost, yet there is enough in Christ to heal souls under such wounds, to raise up out of the dust with the most excellent refreshment, to give light in the greatest darkness. He can raise up as it were out of the depths of hell; he can take out of the miry clay and most horrible pit and set the foot upon a rock.

Christ is strong enough to do it. His voice has power enough in it to quicken the dead. This blessed rain has virtue enough in it to heal those that are cut down to the ground. His merits are sufficient. There is provision enough made in Christ for new life and new comfort to the most wretched and miserable and ruined creatures. And there is love enough,

-- 310 --

grace enough, in Christ for it. Such is the grace of Christ that, if once those that are disconsolate and destroyed and lost come to see it, [it] will irresistibly revive and renew 'em, it will put new life into 'em and give 'em new strength and fill them with rejoicing.5

I now proceed, in the

Second [place], to observe that Christ, in coming down upon the children of men and dispensing his benefits to them, does actually refresh, revive and restore them as the rain doth the mown grass.

Thus he restores poor, fallen man after he was cut down to the ground and there seemed to be no hope of his recovery. Whoever should have looked upon him then, while he remained in his fallen state, before God revealed his manifest design, would have pronounced his case past all hope and would have given him over for lost.

But Christ comes down from heaven on this fallen, miserable creature and gives life from the dead. He restores that which Satan had cut down. He heals that mortal wound that he had given. He soon revived the hearts of our first parents with hope, after their hope had been cut off. And by coming down from heaven in the fullness of time, he actually redeemed a great number of the fallen children of men.

He restores the image of God after it had been wholly defaced. He restores spiritual life after it had been wholly extinct. He restores to God's favor. He restores, and much more than restores, to the former state of happiness, for he brings to a better paradise and a more excellent state of honor and an higher degree of communion with God.

So he actually revives and restores his church from time to time when it comes to be as it were cut down. When the enemies of it triumph and send gifts one to another and make merry, as though they had now wholly got the victory, yet he makes his church to revive and grow again, as a shower of rain doth the grass after the scythe.

The church of God in the world is very much like grass in that respect: it can't be killed by cutting down or by being devoured by the beasts that feed upon it. Let it be cut down by the scythe or wounded by the teeth of beasts never so often, yet it will live and grow again by the showers of heaven. So it has always been with the church of God. Many a time have its enemies mowed down the people, destroyed 'em without mercy by hundreds and by thousands. But yet the church lives by the waterings of heaven. When the enemy has been cutting down the church, Christ has

-- 311 --

come down from heaven by his Spirit, and the others have quickly come up in the room of those that have been cut off, like new spires of grass springing up after the scythe.

The enemies of the church have always been thirsty for the blood of the people of God, and have greedily devoured them and never could glut their appetites, but still have gone on devouring, like the beasts of the fields that daily devour the grass and tender herb that springs out of the earth. But yet the church of God is not killed. The grass is alive still. The church has again taken root downward and bore fruit upward, through the blessed influences of the Spirit of her Savior descending from heaven like a refreshing rain upon her. Isaiah 37:31, “And the remnant that is escaped of the house of Judah [shall again take root downward, and bear fruit upward].”

So Christ often revives the hearts of his saints when they have been as it were cut down by some remarkable affliction. So when he shall come down from heaven at the end of the world, he restores the dead bodies of his saints that have been cut down by death. Then there will be life from the dead to many thousands and millions that have been mown down by that great destroyer of mankind, as the fields revive by rain after the scythe or as the tender grass springs out of the earth, and the fields look green in the spring after the grass had seemed to be long dead through the winter.

So again, Christ refreshes, renews and restores those souls that have been as it were cut down by the scythe of the law. Christ comes down upon such souls as an healing, refreshing shower. His blessed Spirit descends and enters the poor, wounded soul, the broken heart, as an healing oil. And then there is life from the dead. There is a new principle of life and comfort begun in the soul. A divine growth begins to sprout forth. After the convinced sinner is brought to despair in himself, it is begotten again to a living hope. Hope revives in a new foundation, a blessed heavenly hope.

After the soul is cut down with a sense of its own weakness, the sinner sees that all his strength fails and he does as it [were] sink and faint. The Spirit of Christ comes down into the soul and puts new strength into it, even the strength of Christ, and he that has no strength of his own is made strong with the strength of God.

After comfort seemed to fail, comfort is revived again. New comfort begins to spring, joy that before was unknown, that restores the soul. The poor, guilty, wretched, miserable creature is brought to rejoice in God and glory in a Redeemer.

-- 312 --

The hungry soul that [was] bent down with famine is filled with good things. There is “the oil of joy for mourning” [Isaiah 61:3] and a new song in the mouth of him that before was crying out of his misery [Psalms 40:3].

So the soul that is truly humbled with an evangelical humiliation is received by Christ. He says, Isaiah 57:15, “Thus saith the high and lofty One that inhabiteth eternity, whose name is Holy; I dwell in the high and holy place, with him also that is of a contrite and humble spirit, to revive the heart of the humble, and to revive the spirit of the contrite ones.”

He that comes and casts himself down into the dust in a gracious humility shall be lifted up, for God brings low and raises up (1 Samuel 2:7). And he that humbles himself shall be exalted [Luke 14:11, Luke 18:14].

And they that are cut down as to a dependence on a worldly happiness, that are brought to renounce all things, Christ will come down upon them as a refreshing shower and shall bestow upon them a better happiness. He will feed them with the meat that perishes not. They that crucify the flesh with the affections and lusts shall have those pleasures that are spiritual and far more delightful. They that renounce their covetousness and are brought to part with worldly profits for Christ shall have gold tried in the fire to make them rich. They that renounce earthly glory shall be exalted to the honor of the children of the great King and shall have the joyful prospect of a future crown of glory.

And Christ doth not only revive and restore the heart at conversion, but often afterwards. It is oftentimes with the saints as 'tis with the church. The devil is continually seeking to destroy 'em, and sometimes seems to prevail against them by his temptations— as he did against Peter— and seems to cut them down. But Christ will revive 'em again as the mown grass, as he did Peter. When the saints are renewedly convinced of sin, and humbled and brought to be level with the ground before God in self-abasement and a lowly submission to God, then is Christ wont by his Spirit to come with his divine refreshments and consolations into the heart, like refreshing showers upon the dry and thirsty earth.

And he not only begins a restoration of the soul, but carries it on, as the grass is not only made to spring out of the earth by the rain but is nourished by it from time to time, whereby it is caused to grow and to come to perfection.

The saints grow in grace and are built up in holiness and comfort through the influences of Christ's Spirit. Hosea 14:5-07, “I will be as the dew unto Israel: he shall grow as the lily, and cast forth his roots as Lebanon. His branches shall spread, and his beauty shall be as the olive tree, and his smell as Lebanon. They that dwell under his shadow shall return; they

-- 313 --

shall revive as the corn, and grow as the vine: the scent thereof shall be as the wine of Lebanon.”

Application.

Use I may be of Self-Examination, to let persons be hence put upon it to examine themselves with respect to two things:

First. Examine yourself whether ever you was brought to such a sense of things, and to such a frame and disposition of heart, that your soul became as the mown grass, as particularly:

1. Have you been as it were cut down by a despair in your own sufficiency and worthiness; that whereas formerly you was wont to think you could do a great deal for yourself, you have had all hope in your own strength cut off, so that you have been brought to be sensible of your being in the hands of God, that unless he helped you by his power, you was undone forever.

And have your former high thoughts of your worthiness been cut down to the ground? There is naturally in you a great disposition to esteem yourself and exalt yourself and look upon yourself as somebody, and your own qualifications and doings as something considerable and worthy of notice in the sight of God; a disposition to make much of your pains in religion, to make much of your prayers and other performances, and a readiness to expect much in return for them, and to wonder and murmur if God don't seem to take notice of them.

But have you been cut down to the earth as to any such thought of yourself and your performance, and such expectations of favor from God on account of them?

2. Have you been cut down and leveled with the ground in a disposition to abase yourself before God? I speak now not of any legal work, but of a gracious work of God upon your heart, making you poor in spirit; not merely forcing you out of your carnal confidences, but causing you [to] quit them with delight; not merely bringing you from your vain dependences and proud expectation, but changing your disposition, mortifying the pride of your heart and infusing a contrary principle, a lovely spirit, a disposition to abase yourself and exalt God, a spirit to delight to lie in the dust, to delight to see yourself cut down and your own worthiness made no account of, and all the glory given to Christ.

Is that the spirit that you are of, to desire with all your heart that Christ and his worthiness and his righteousness alone should be made mention of? Is God's footstool the place where you delight to be? Is that in

-- 314 --

Psalms 115:1— “Not to us, O Lord, not unto us, but unto thy name give glory, for thy mercy, and for thy truth's sake”— the natural language of your heart? Are doctrines of free and sovereign grace by the merits of a Redeemer, and of the absolute sovereignty of God in dispensing saving benefits to the children of men, sweet doctrines that are sweet to your soul? And is the way of salvation by Christ a lovely and an excellent way in your esteem, because it is such an humbling way, a way that so tends to abase men and to exalt Christ? Is your pride thus cut down? Are all your towering inclinations and high imaginations thus mown down?

A legal humiliation is all a mere farce. Men quit their own righteousness because they are driven and beaten from it. They despair of helping themselves. Not but that the self-exalting disposition remains, and to love themselves would be agreeable to 'em if they could have any hopes of it. They would love to be saved by their own righteousness if there could be any possibility of it.

But by a work of grace on the heart, the very disposition is mortified. There is another inclination. The soul delights not in self-exaltation, but abhors it. It delights in self-abasement. The way of being saved by sovereign grace, as being self-empty and accounted nothing, is what is his free choice. And 'tis great part of the glory and sweetness of heaven to a gracious soul that it is obtained in such a way.

Let all that think themselves converted, examine how it is with their hearts with respect to this matter. You that have lately taken up such an hope concerning yourselves: how do you find it? Do you find a great change in yourselves with respect to this matter, that now you are brought to delight in lying low before God? Do you find a longing of soul after more humility and poverty of spirit, that Christ may have more glory? And you that have formerly received hope: also examine yourselves. Don't certainly conclude that your hope is right merely because you have had it a great while. How has your hope wrought? What influence has it had upon you? Is it a soul-humbling, and not a soul-exalting, hope? Do you, the more you hope, find yourself the more disposed to walk humbly before God and men?

3. Have you been cut down to the ground with respect to your expectations and pursuit of happiness in the things of the world? 'Tis certain that naturally you see no better good. This is what you prize. You set your heart on what is to be enjoyed here.

But have you been weaned from the world? Have you been brought to sell all for Christ? Have [you] been brought wholly to renounce your former

-- 315 --

lusts, your former sinful pleasures and delights, and to spit out the sweet morsel without any design or expectation of taking it into your mouth again? Have you been brought to pluck out your right eye, however contrary to your inclination and hard to nature? Are you brought to a disposition to part with profit or honor and all worldly things that shall at any time stand in the way of your duty? Have you been brought to part with your ease, and to comply heartily with labor and suffering in the way of your duty, as long as you live? I know of nothing that is more abundantly insisted on as a requisite and necessary work of a sincere disciple in the Scriptures than this is.

It is a great thing to part with the world. The world is a natural man's god, and it is his all. It is a great thing for a man to be cut down in this affair, and to be willing to cut himself off from the world for Christ's sake, and so to give up all and reserve nothing. Thus you should inquire [whether] your soul is become like the mown grass.

Second. Inquire whether or no you ever have experienced the refreshing, reviving influences of the Spirit of Christ upon your hearts, as of the rain on the mown grass. Have you tasted and seen that the Lord is good? Have you, when you have thus been emptied of yourself and weaned from this vain world, found a better good? Have you had those discoveries of Christ, or that sense of his excellency or sufficiency and wonderful grace, that has refreshed and rejoiced your heart, and revived it as it were out of the dust, and caused hope and your comfort to spring forth like the tender grass springing out of the earth by clear shining after rain? Has there been light let into your soul, as the light of the sun pleasantly breaking forth out of the cloud after a dreadful storm, or as the sweet dawning of the light of the morning after long wandering in a dark night, or the bright and beautiful day star arising with refreshing beams? Have you had that divine comfort that has seemed to heal your soul and put life and strength into you and given you peace after trouble and rest after labor and pain?

Have you tasted that spiritual food, that bread from heaven, that is so sweet and so satisfying, so much better than the richest earthly dainties? Have you felt something of the divine comfort and peace, which can't be expressed and which passes all understanding? Have you tasted that in Christ that has turned the stream of your affections that way and filled you with longings after more of him?

Use II may be [of] Exhortation to those that are seeking their salvation, earnestly to seek that their souls may become as the mown grass in these

-- 316 --

two respects, viz. [first,] in being wholly cut down as to your own righteousness; and second, in being brought wholly and finally to renounce your lusts.

First. Earnestly seek that you may be cut down to the earth with regard to your dependence on your own righteousness, that your opinion of your own worthiness and the worthiness of your doings may be so cut off that you may cease to have any secret expectation that ever God should do anything for you upon the account of them, and so as not to wonder at all that he takes no notice of your prayers and performances; nor to think it at all hard dealing in God, though he should take every other person, and even those that never have taken an hundredth part of the pains that you have done, and leave you and cast you off forever.

Second. Earnestly seek that you may be as the mown grass by an entirely final renouncing all the objects of your lusts, whatever lusts they be. Be it pride, be it worldliness, or sensuality, or love of slothful ease, or hatred of your neighbor, [or] revenge, seek that they may all not only be wounded but cut off like the mown grass, so that you may never depend upon or desire any gratification of any of them any more, and so that you may finally quit those things that are most dear to these lusts; and leaving 'em all, may forever give up yourself to the work and business of seeking God and keeping his commands, being willing to bear all the labor and self-denial of it till death shall come.

'Tis hard for a person to come to this, because when a person thus wholly gives up his whole life to the business of religion, all hopes of ever indulging his slothful ease or any of his sinful appetites any more are cut off: and that is for a man to be as it were cut down at one blow.

But if you would be saved, you must be willing to be thus cut down. Your hands are fast clinched upon carnal ease and other carnal things, but yet you must let go and must forever quit all those things.

Therein many of you will but do as you have vowed you will do in that solemn covenant you have entered into with God, which covenant you have sealed this day in your partaking at the Lord's table.

I would here offer two things to your consideration as motives, earnestly to seek that you may thus be cut down in these two respects, to a renunciation of your own righteousness, and the objects of your lusts.

1. In these two things consists that great Christian duty of self-denial. For man to deny himself is to deny or renounce his own sufficiency and excellency, and to deny his own sinful desires and inclinations. In the one consists humiliation; in the other, mortification and weanedness from the world. Worldlimindedness and self-righteousness are the two great pillars

-- 317 --

of Dagon's temple, on which the house rests and which the spiritual Samson breaks, by which the temple falls and the Philistines are slain [Jdg 16:23-31].

The world and men's own righteousness are the two main idols of natural men. Both were represented by the idolatry of the Jews that provoked Christ to jealousy. Their idols represented worldly enjoyments because they were idols of silver and gold; and their idols represented their own righteousness because they were the works of their own hands.

In these two things, viz. renouncing {self-righteousness and the objects of your lusts}, consists the cutting off of a man from his own stock, from the wild olive in which they grew by nature.

In these two things consists that wound that is given in the circumcision of the hearts, which makes way for spiritual healing. 'Tis in the separation of the soul from these two things, viz. from the world and our own righteousness, that we become poor in spirit, for 'tis in these two things that men naturally think themselves rich and increased with goods. A being sensible that we are wretched, miserable, poor and blind and naked, includes a sense of vanity and nothingness of both these.

A renunciation both of {righteousness and the objects of our lusts} is included in that breaking up our “fallow ground,” spoken of in Jeremiah 4:3-04, for that represents the plowing of the heart with the plow of the law to destroy the natural growth of our own righteousness, and also the destroying the thorns of worldly affections and cares.6

Both a weaning from the world and our own righteousness are included in that weaning from the milk and drawing from the breasts, spoken of in Isaiah 28:9.

2. These two things are the great things wherein the straitness of the gate and the narrowness of the way that leads to life consist. We are commanded to “Strive to enter in at the strait gate” because “strait is the gate, and narrow is the way, which leadeth unto life, and few there be that find it” [Luke 13:24, Matthew 7:14]. This straitness mainly consists in these two things. Here it is that men stick. On the one hand, they can't bear to part with their own righteousness; and on the other, {they can't bear to part with the objects of their lusts}. 'Tis because of self-righteousness and love of the world that men are too big to enter {in at the strait gate}.

They are too big in their own righteousness; by this they are as it were swollen and puffed up. They are great in their own eyes. And by their worldly possessions they are too big. They have too great a cumber and

-- 318 --

load about them to enter such a narrow passage. They are laden with thick clay, as the expression is, Hab 2:6.

These two things are the very main difficulties that are in the way of men's entering into the kingdom of God. If you could get by these two difficulties, could be brought to renounce {their own self-righteousness} and {the objects of their lusts}, the greatest difficulty would be over. You would have passed the greatest narrowness of the passage, where it pinches most. These two are as it were the two posts of the strait gate that make the passage so strait and difficult. Get by these, and then the hardest things are surmounted, the greatest opposition in the way to your salvation is conquered.

Therefore let those that are now seeking their salvation earnestly seek that they may be brought to these two things, and cry earnestly to God that he would bring you to it.

And always remember that these are the difficulties that are before you, that you must surmount, viz. to renounce {self-righteousness} and to comply with a final parting {from the objects of your lusts}, and giving up yourselves wholly and forever to God.

III. Let me from this doctrine take occasion to invite those whose souls are wounded with a sense of their sins and miserable state to come to Christ. You are naturally in a miserable condition. You are cut down to the earth by the Fall. You have now heard of an excellent remedy in {Christ}.

You have some sense of your misery. Your souls are in a measure wounded. But, behold, here is one offered to you that will heal your wounds, will sweetly revive your hearts, renew, strengthen.

If you are thirsty after deliverance, like the dry and thirsty ground in a time of great drought, here is one {like refreshing rain}. If you are ready to fear sometime that there is no hope, your hope seems to be cut down and you are ready to say, as Ezekiel 37:11, “Our bones are dried, our hope is lost: and we are cut off from our parts”: if it be so, if you are cut down even to the very ground, yet look to Christ and he will revive you. You shall revive even from the dead. John 11:25, “he that believeth in me, yet though he were dead, yet shall he live.”
