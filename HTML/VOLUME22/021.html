Children Ought to Love the Lord Jesus Christ Above All
He that loveth father or mother more than me is not worthy of me. Matthew 10:37.

It has often been so in the Christian church, that some of the children in a family have professed the true religion and have loved Christ, and their parents have [been] enemies to Christianity, so that the children that were Christian must either renounce the Christian religion and cast off Christ, or they must forsake their parents and be cast off by them.6 Yea, sometimes parents have been so displeased with their children for following Christ, that they have themselves delivered 'em up to death; they have had a hand in their being proceeded against by wicked rulers, and so put to death. And there were especially many such instances in the first times of the gospel. This is what Christ speaks of in the two verses foregoing the text: “For I am come to set a man at variance against his father, and the daughter against her mother, and the daughter-in-law against her mother-in-law. And a man's foes shall be they of his own household.”

And therefore Christ says, as he does in the text, “He that loveth father or mother more than me is not worthy of me.” He would give warning hereby to such children as profess to his followers, that if it should be so, that they should have that trial, that their parents should threaten to disinherit them and turn them out of doors, to refuse to own 'em or treat 'em any more as their children, yea, to turn enemies to them if they would not renounce Christ: I say, Christ warns 'em that if, in such a case, they showed more love to their father than to him, and so will forsake him for fear of being cast off by their parents, he will renounce them at last as not worthy to be owned as some of his.

-- 171 --

Doctrine.
Children ought to love the Lord Jesus Christ above all things in this world.

Though when Christ says in the text, “He that loves father or mother more than me,” his words are not to be confined only to those that are in a state of childhood, or before they are grown up, yet 'tis evident he has a special respect to children while they yet remain under their parents' roof and under their covert and care: for he speaks of the division that shall be in households or families in the verse next before the text. He says, “A man's foes shall be them of his own household.” And the words are especially accommodated to the state of children while in a state of childhood, when affections of children are wont to be strongest towards their parents, as having not been so much conversant abroad among other objects as afterwards. Parents are very commonly the object of children's' greatest affections while young. So the latter part of the verse, “he that loveth son or daughter more than me is not worthy of me,” is especially accommodated to the usual state of persons after they are grown up, and are from under their parents' covert and have families of their own, when children are very commonly the dearest objects of their love.

The following reasons may be given why children ought to love Jesus Christ above things in the world:

I. He is more lovely in himself. He is one that is greater and higher than all the kings of the earth, has more honor and majesty than they, and yet he is innately good and full of mercy and love.

There is no love so great and so wonderful as that which is in the heart of Christ. He is one that delights in mercy; he is ready to pity those that [are] in suffering and sorrowful circumstances; one that delights in the happiness of his creatures. The love and grace that Christ has manifested does as much exceed all that which is in this world7 as the sun is brighter than a candle. Parents are often full of kindness towards their children, but that is no kindness like Jesus Christ's.

And he is an infinitely holy One. He is God's holy child, so holy and pure that the heavens are not pure in his sight, so that he is fairer than the sons of men, as the Psalmist says (Psalms 45:2). Canticles 5:10, “The chiefest among ten thousand,” “altogether lovely” [v. Canticles 5:16]. Because of his glorious excellency, he is compared to the sun, that is the brightest of all things

-- 172 --

that we behold with our bodily [eyes]. 'Tis he that is called “the Sun of righteousness” (Malachi 4:2).8

So he is called the “morning star,” the brightest of all the whole multitude of stars (Revelation 22:16). He is so lovely and excellent, that the angels in heaven do greatly love him; their hearts overflow with love to him, and they are continually, day and night without ceasing, praising him and giving him glory. Yea, he is so lovely a person, that God the Father infinitely delights in him; he is his beloved Son, the brightness of his glory, whose beauty God continually sees with infinite delight, without ever being weary of beholding it. Proverbs 8:30, “I was daily his delight, rejoicing always before him.”

And if the angels and God himself love him so much above all, surely children ought to love him above all things in this world.

Everything that is lovely in God is in him, and everything that is or can be lovely in any man is in him: for he is man as well as God, and he is the holiest, meekest, most humble, and everyway the most excellent man that ever was.

He is the delight of heaven. There is nothing in heaven, that glorious world, that is brighter and more amiable and lovely than Christ. And this darling of heaven, by becoming man, became as a plant or flower springing out of the earth; and he is the most lovely flower that ever was seen in this world. Canticles 2:1— there 'tis said of Christ, “I am the rose of Sharon, and lily of the valleys.”

Second. There is more good to be enjoyed in him than in everything or all things in this world. He is not only an amiable, but an all-sufficient good. There is enough in him to answer all our wants and satisfy all our desires. Children, if they come to Christ and set their hearts on him, will find that in him that is better for them and will be sweeter to them than anything that is to had in the presence and company or provision of their parents, and better than anything that is to be found in their parents' houses. Christ has9 the bread of life in him; their souls may feed and feast upon that which will be much better than anything they are ever entertained with at their fathers' tables. Christ is to those that love him as glorious clothing and excellent ornaments to their souls; those children that love him, their souls are clothed with those robes that are a thousand times as beautiful and desirable than the best clothing their parents provide for them. In him they shall have that spiritual good which will be as

-- 173 --

gold tried in the fire, that will make them richer than if they were kings' children.

Christ is the most suitable good for them. If they love him, they will find him sweeter to them than the honey and the honeycomb. In him they shall [have] enough, all that they desire. He will give 'em rest and satisfaction.

Third. Christ has a higher right to children than any in this world. Their parents have a right to them, but Christ has a higher right, for he has made them. They are under greater obligations to give Christ their hearts and devote their whole souls to him, than to any other; for he has given 'em their souls and made 'em capable of knowing more about God and Christ than the beasts, for that end, that they might love him. And besides, the children of Christian parents have been given up to Christ in their baptism; and if they therefore don't give themselves to Christ with all their hearts, and don't love him above all, they will violate what was done in their baptism.

They have been as really given up to God as Samuel was by his mother, who gave him to the Lord all days of his life, and brought him when he was but a child to the tabernacle to live there— though they han't been given just in the same manner. There are many poor children in the world that are brought by their parents and offered up to their idols and to devils, and are burnt alive, to be a sacrifice to them. Children among us, through God's great goodness to them, are not thus dealt with with cruelty, but are offered up to Christ in baptism, that they, when they come to be capable of it, may offer up their hearts a sacrifice to Christ by their great love to him.

Fourth. Children ought to love Christ above all, in that he has done more for them than all the men in the world have ever done. Their parents have done a great deal for them and have suffered a great deal for them— especially their mothers. And both their fathers and mothers have done a great deal in taking care of them, in providing for them and bringing of them up. They have been at a great deal of pains and charge for them.

But they have never done so much for them as Christ has done, for it is Christ that has kept 'em all their lives long. He has kept 'em from death, he has healed 'em when they have been sick.

And they have always lived upon that provision that Christ has made for them. It has been through the hands of their parents, but their parents could not have had it for 'em, had not Christ provided it for them. They have always lived upon Christ's provision; he has maintained 'em, and 'tis

-- 174 --

his wool and his flax that has clothed 'em. If it had not been for Christ's preservation, they would have been in the grave, and there they would have been eaten up of worms long ago. And 'tis Christ that has kept 'em out of the hands of the devil. The devils, if Christ permitted 'em, would immediately fall upon and carry them away. 'Tis Christ that has kept 'em out of hell. If it had not been for Christ, they would have been burning in hell among devils long before this time. 'Tis he that keeps 'em every night while they sleep. And, which is more than all, Christ has died for children. None can conceive what dreadful things Christ has suffered, and all this he suffered not only for grown persons but for children. All children are by nature children of wrath and are in danger of eternal damnation in hell. But children that live under the gospel have an opportunity to be delivered from hell and to become the children of God, and so to go to heaven when they die, and therefore to be happy and to shine forth as the sun in the kingdom of God, and enjoy rivers of pleasures at God's right hand forevermore.

And this opportunity they have by the blood of Christ. They could have had no such opportunity but by its costing Christ his life and his undergoing great cruelties and a very tormenting death, though he was God's only Son.

Now where is any in this world that has done so much for children as this? Where is anything that any have done for them to be compared with this? Surely, then, they ought to love Christ more than all things in this world.

Fifth. Never any in this world showed such wonderful condescension in taking notice of children, and offering himself to them, and seeking their love, as Christ has done. If some great king should be occasionally passing through a town, and should take much notice of children, and show a great affection and kindness to them, would this be wonderful. But this would not be so wonderful as that Christ, who is the king of heaven and the King of kings, should take a gracious notice of them. But this Christ doth. He graciously offers himself and all his great blessings that he has purchased by his death to them. He is willing to be theirs, so that they may say of this glorious King, “This is my Jesus, this is my Redeemer, my friend, my portion, one that has given himself to me.”

That is the voice of Christ in Proverbs 8:32, “Now therefore hearken unto me, O ye children”; and that in Psalms 34:11, “Come, ye children, hearken unto me.”

When Christ was upon earth, and they were about to bring little children to Christ that he might bless them, and others rebuked— they

-- 175 --

thought it was too much for them to expect that so great a person as Christ should take notice of little children— Christ frowned upon them that forbid it and said, “Suffer little children to come unto me,” and he took 'em up in his arms and blessed [them] (Mark 10:13-16).

So he stands as it were with the arms of his love open to receive all children. He would have 'em come to him, that he may bless them.

He won't despise their love. He is willing to accept it and to give them sweet manifestations of his love to them.

It would be a great thing for some great man to enter into communion with some poor child. But Christ is ready to receive little children into communion with him, and that, even the poor children of poor parents. Those that are despised in the world, Christ don't despise them.

He don't only offer to give himself to them, but to give 'em his kingdom and to give 'em all that he has, and to give 'em his angels, so that they shall be their angels, though they are glorious creatures. And he is very angry with any that despise one little child that believes in him. Christ says, in Matthew 18:10, “Take heed that ye despise not one of these little ones; for I say unto you, That in heaven their angels do always behold the face of my Father which is in heaven.” Thus wonderfully does Christ condescend to children. He as it were stands at their door and knocks, and says to 'em, “If ye hear my voice, I will come in to him, and will sup with him, and he with me” [Revelation 3:20].

Application.

Use I may be of Conviction, to convince those children that have no true love to the Lord Jesus Christ of their sin and misery.

Are here not many of you that are here present that never have truly loved the Lord Jesus Christ, though he be so excellent a person?

Though you have often heard of him?

Though he has done so much for you? Though you have heard often how much?

Though you have been given up to Christ in your baptism, and so greatly distinguished from other poor miserable children, {who have been} offered up to the devil?

Though Christ condescends to offer {himself to you? Though you have} heard the gospel preached? {Though you have been} taught so much at home and at meeting, and yet never [have loved Christ].

Don't flatter yourselves that you do love Christ, when you don't. Consider whether it don't appear that many of you never have loved him,

-- 176 --

by your living such a life as you have done. If you had loved Christ, would you have been so careless of doing the things that he has commanded you?

Would you not have been more careful not to sin against him? {Would you} have spent away your time in thinking so little of Christ? Persons are apt to think much of them that they love. Would you have spent your time in thinking so little of him, and of nothing but your play and the vanities of this world? Would you not have loved the word of Christ? Would you not have loved Christ's holy day? The sabbath day is Christ's day. [Would you not] have loved Christ's house?

Would you have cried in the neglect of secret prayers? 'Tis to be feared many of you that can pray, if you will, have lived very much without going to Christ alone to pray to him, and to God the Father, who are both one God. If you had loved Christ, you would love to go to him by prayer.

You have, many of you, hearkened to that devil more than to Christ. For when you have done wickedly, in telling lies and disobeying your parents, and giving way to a spirit of hatred to others and in breaking the sabbath day, then you have hearkened to the devil.

It is to be feared that many of you children that are here present have never had the least spark of true love to Christ in your hearts since you was born. If it be thus with you, this may convince you of the following things:

First. How angry God is with you. God is very angry with those that don't love Christ, {though} he is his own dear Son. God knows that he is infinitely lovely. {Therefore he is} angry when he sees that when persons hear how much Christ has done {for them, they} yet have no love to him. It is said, 1 Corinthians 16:22, “If any man love not the Lord Jesus Christ, let him be Anathema Maranatha,” i.e. let him be an accursed creature. So that if you don't love Christ, you are under God's curse, and if you never love him, will be a cursed miserable creature forever. And is it any wonder that such a curse is denounced against contending {with God}?

Second. This may convince that you can't desire that Christ should have any respect to you by anything you do. You have sinned greatly against him, in that you have not loved him and continued so long without any love to him, and have been guilty of disobeying his commands. How can such a sinful creature ever deserve that Christ should love him?

You don't at all deserve that Christ should look upon you. You deserve that he should hate you all the days of your life, and that he should cast you into hell forever. You deserve that he should loath all your prayers and all the best that you do.

-- 177 --

And let you do what you will for your own salvation, yet how can that deserve that God should have mercy upon you, when you do what you do not from love to Christ but only from love to yourself?

Third. This may convince you that you can't be saved any other way than by the blood of Christ. It shows that you can't be saved by your own righteousness, for you are so sinful and wicked a creature that none of your righteousness is worthy to be accepted. You need Christ's blood to wash away your sins, to cleanse you from this guilt of your not loving Christ and all your disobedience to him. You are so wicked that God will never accept you any other way; he won't save you on any other account but only for Christ's sake, his own dear Son that has died for poor sinners. Therefore when you pray to God to save you, you must not expect that God will ever do it unless it be for Christ's righteousness, and not for yours. He will never hear your prayers unless it be out of respect to Christ. Therefore don't expect to be heard any other way. If you could pray every day as long as you live, and spend most of your time in reading, and praying, and crying for sorrow for your sins, yet all this would be nothing; it won't make up for your sins. But Christ has made up for sin, and therefore if ever God hears your prayers, and makes you his children, and saves you from hell, it will be for Christ's sake alone— not because you take so much pains in reading and praying and doing your duty, but because of what Christ did for poor sinners.

Use II may be of Exhortation to children, to seek earnestly that they may be converted and that God would fill their hearts with love to Christ now while they are young.

First. If you have the holy principle of love to Christ in your hearts, you will be fit to live in the world, and can't be fit to live without: for they that have no love to Christ, if they do live, they'll only live in sin and the service of Satan. But they that love Christ will live to the glory of God and they will have God's blessing upon them in the convoys of life.

Second. And if you love Christ, you will be fit to die.

If you should die while you are young, and death should come upon you and find you without any love to Christ, what will become of you?

How uncertain [it is] whether you will live.

What multitudes die while children.

How do you know but that dreadful distemper [will seize you]? How dreadful it will be to you on your death bed.

And what will become of your poor soul when [you are] dead?

When you die, you will die safely and will have good ground of comfort. When you die, whom you have loved will receive your departing spirit. You

-- 178 --

will be welcomed into heaven by your dear Savior and by all those glorious saints and angels that attend him.

There are glorious promises that Christ has made to those that love him. He has promised them a crown of life. James 1:12, “Blessed is the man that endureth temptation: for when he is tried, he shall receive the crown of life, which the Lord hath promised to them that love him.” And when you come to die, then that glorious crown shall be set upon your head, which will be a thousand times more excellent than the best crown that is worn by any king or queen.

Third. If you should die in your childhood, how comfortable would it be to your parents if they have any comfortable evidences of your having truly loved Christ to think of. If they are bereaved of you by death, it will doubtless leave them very mournful and sorrowful, and if you gave no hopeful testimonies of {having loved Christ}, that will be the sting of their sorrow. They will be afraid that your poor soul is burning in hell. But if, on the contrary, {they have hopeful testimonies of your having loved Christ}, and so can with comfortable hope think of your soul as being with Christ in glory, in a joyful blessed state, that will be the best comfort in their sorrow; it will greatly support their hearts while your dead body lies in its grave.

Fourth. If you should not die in childhood, yet if you don't love Christ while you are young, your hearts will be grown hard {in sin}.

Fifth. If you do truly love Christ above all, you shall enjoy Christ's love to you. See what Christ has said in Proverbs 8:17, “I love them that love me.” Christ in those words has a special respect to children and young persons, as appears by the words that next follow: “and those that seek me early shall find me.” How blessed and happy a child is that the great King of angels and men loves!

Sixth. If you truly love Christ, all the glorious angels of heaven will love you. For they delight in those that love Christ; they love to see such a sight as children giving their hearts to Christ. There will be joy in heaven among the angels that day that you begin to love Christ. And they will be your angels; they will take care of you while you sleep, and God will give 'em charge to keep you in all your ways. And they will do it with delight, because they will love you.

Seventh. If you sincerely love Jesus Christ, it will be the way for you to live a comfortable pleasant life. Then you may have peace in your own mind and may live without fear. When other children that don't love Christ are afraid of dying because they are afraid they shall go to hell when they die, you need not be afraid.

-- 179 --

Then you need not be afraid of thunder, which is commonly very terrifying to children.1 You will [have] this to consider of, that 'tis your Father and your Redeemer that thunders, that the thunder is his voice. Thunder and lightning can never do them any hurt that love Christ, for if they are taken out of the world by it, yet it only carries them from this world to heaven, into the glorious presence of Christ.

And then it is sweet to the soul to love Christ. It is an holy affection that fills the soul with sweetness. And then you will have the pleasure of living a life of communion with Christ, which will be a very sweet life.

If you love Christ above all, it will tend to make you love everybody, which will be greatly to the benefit of your life. How much better will you live, if you live in love heartily, and more than if you live in strife and jarring with one and another.

If you love Christ, you will be safe from the devil, that roaring lion that goes about seeking whom he may devour. [He] will not be able to hurt; you shall be out of his reach. If the devil should appear to you, you need not be afraid of him, but might triumph over him. The devil knows that Christ will subdue him under the feet of such as love him. Christ will bring down that dreadful giant and cause all holy children that love him to come and set their feet upon his neck.

If you love the Lord Jesus Christ, it will have a tendency to make the sabbath day a pleasant day to you.

Eighth. And consider further, to stir you, how many have truly loved Christ while they are young. And on this occasion I will tell you what account the Rev. Mr. Whitefield has given me in a letter that I received from him this week past— the minister that you have doubtless heard of, that has had such great success in many places. I will read a passage of his letter that he sent me. […]2

Let such a wonderful work of God on children in other places stir you up earnestly to seek God, that you may partake of the same blessing.

Pray earnestly to God to give you true love to Christ. Your heart is wholly full of love to sin and enmity against Christ. {You} can't work love to Christ

-- 180 --

in your own heart. {Therefore} cry to God to draw your heart to Christ. John 6:44, “No man can come to the Son, [except the Father which hath sent me draw him].”

Avoid those things that Christ hates. If you do those things that he hates, it will tend to make you hate Christ more and more.

Be very earnest in this matter.

And remember the exhortation now given. Don't only seek a little while. That is not the way to obtain. Hosea 6:3, “Then shall we know, if we follow on [to know the Lord].”
