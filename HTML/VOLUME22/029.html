They Sing a New Song
And they sung as it were a new song before the throne, and before the four beasts, and the elders: and no man could learn that song but the hundred and forty and four thousand, which were redeemed from the earth. Revelation 14:3.

In the five first verses of this chapter we have an account of a vision that the apostle John had of the true church, that steadfastly adhered to Christ in the time of the great Antichristian apostasy wherein the world wandered after the beast and was corrupted with the delusions and idolatries of the Romish church. The Antichristian church, or the church of Rome, is in this book called the great whore, but the true church is represented as the faithful spouse of Christ. And so the souls of those men that polluted themselves with the idolatries and abominations of the church of Rome, are represented as whorish women that are false to their covenant with him to whom they had been betrothed and prostitute themselves to others. But the souls of true believers are represented as chaste virgins that kept themselves pure for Christ alone, to whom they were betrothed, that they might at last on their marriage day be presented as chaste virgins to Christ, and so were not defiled with women; i.e. when the rest of women were defiled, these kept themselves pure.

Verse Revelation 14:4, “These are they which were not defiled with women; for they are virgins. These are they which follow the Lamb whithersoever he goeth. These were redeemed from among men, being the first fruits unto God and to the Lamb.” They were redeemed from amongst men. When the greater part of men were left of God to Antichristian delusions, these were by the sovereign grace of God distinguished and saved from the infection. They followed the Lamb whithersoever he goeth. While the rest of the world wandered after the beast with seven heads and ten horns, these followed the Lamb as their shepherd wherever he went.

-- 228 --

Though in order to follow him they must go through great sufferings, must suffer cruel persecution from the Antichristian church, yet they would not forsake him.

And their reward is that, when they had gone through these sufferings, they appear with the Lamb on Mt. Zion with his Father's name written in their foreheads. And here is represented their joy that they have as the reward of their sorrows and suffering, by the joyful songs of praise they sang in the second and third verses.

Concerning that joyful singing of praises to God that John heard in his vision, we may observe:

1. Who they are that sing, viz. the 144,000, i.e. Christ's church or company of true saints whose souls were virgins that followed {the Lamb whithersoever he went}. The number 144,000 don't signify any certain number, but a very great number, though a small number in comparison of those that had apostatized. It is a mystical number. There is some mystery in this number of 144,000. We find throughout this book that the true church is always reckoned by twelves, as there were twelve patriarchs, twelve sons of Jacob, “twelve tribes of the children of Israel” (ch. Revelation 21:12), and “twelve apostles of the Lamb” (ch. Revelation 21:14). So the New Jerusalem had twelve gates and at the gates twelve angels. And the wall of the city had twelve foundations; and the measure of the city is twelve thousand furlongs; and the measure of the wall of the city was 144 cubits, which is twelve times twelve [Revelation 21:16-17]. And so it is said the tree of life in the midst of the street of the city “bare twelve manner of fruits, and yielded her fruit” each month throughout the twelve months of the year (ch. Revelation 22:2). And so in the seventh chapter 'tis said that the number of those that were sealed in their forehead with God's seal, were twelve thousand out of each of the twelve tribes of the children of Israel— of the tribe of Judah twelve thousand, and the tribe of Reuben twelve thousand, etc.— in all, 144,000, that is, twelve times twelve thousand.

And this is the number that the true mystical church of Christ is represented by in the text: 144,000 that “were redeemed from the earth.” These are they that are represented as singing praises in the text.

2. We may observe what is said of the song that they sing. And there are two things I would observe:

(1) That 'tis said to be new song. “And they sung as it were a new song before the throne.”

(2) That it was a song peculiar to them, that none but they sung and none but they could sing and none but they could learn.

-- 229 --

[Doctrine.]

And these two things that are said of the song that is sung by Christ's church of true saints are what I would make my subject of discourse this day:

I. I would show how that those that [are] Christ's redeemed ones, in the holy praises they offer up to God, do sing a new song; and,

II. How that no man can learn that song but they.

And then I would make some application.

I. I would show how that those that are true saints and redeemed from the earth, do sing a new song in the sincere praises they offer to God. And here,

First. I would observe, in the general, that “new” is a relative term and has respect to something else that, in comparison, is called “old.” So the song of praise that is sung by Christ's truly redeemed ones is called new with relation particularly to two things:

1. With relation to what was in man's first state under the first covenant. Man in his first state before the fall had a disposition to praise his Creator, for he was then perfectly holy. And therefore the love of God reigned in his heart, which will necessarily dispose to the praises of God. His heart was not only full of love but full of joy. Man was then in an happy state, without anything to trouble him, and everything smiling upon [him]; his heart was full of bliss so that then he could joyfully praise God in a paradise of delights.

And he had great occasion to praise God then, for God was exceeding bountiful to him, did great things for him, made him a little lower than the angels, crowned him with glory and honor and set him over the work of his hands, planted a paradise for him and set him in very honorable and happy circumstances. These things gave great occasion for songs of praise. But now the saints sing a new song. The song of praise that the redeemed of Jesus Christ sing to God is a new song compared with the joy and love and praise of man's first state.

2. The song of the redeemed saints is new when compared with what was in their fallen and natural state, as they were worldly, when they were in a miserable condition, exposed to everlasting ruin, and their hearts full of enmity against God. They might have their hearts full of outward mirth and might rejoice in their youth, and their hearts might cheer them in the days of their youth and might rejoice in their outward possessions, and

-- 230 --

in their sinful pleasures and their temporal advancement. Natural men may fare sumptuously, and may entertain themselves with music and dancing and sing songs for their pleasure and diversion. And not only so, but men in a natural state may seem to praise God by singing psalms and spiritual songs to him.

But the song that those that are the redeemed of Christ do sing, is a new song as compared with all that ever is in their natural state.

And the song that the redeemed saints sing to God is new with relation to these things: first, as later in order of time; second, as 'tis more excellent.

(1) 'Tis called new with respect to them, as 'tis later than they and succeeds them. Anything in common speech is said to be new, with respect to something that is called old, when it is later in order of time and when it begins or [is] introduced upon the failing or ceasing of the other. Thus if a man is said to be clad in new apparel, 'tis with respect to old that he has put off. So if he has a new habitation or has a new name, it supposes some former habitation or name that ceases and is succeeded by what now is. To this purpose is the Apostle's observation on the gospel covenant being called a “new covenant.” Hebrews 8:13, “In that he saith, A new covenant, he hath made the first old. Now that which decayeth and waxeth old is ready to vanish away.”

The song that is sung by the saints is called a new song in this respect, as it is founded in that great renewing work of God's power and grace, the work of redemption, or the restoration and renovation of things by Jesus Christ.

The first Adam failed in the work that he was to do, and God has appointed us a new surety. There is a new covenant, and a new and living way of entering into the Holy of Holies.

The world was as it were destroyed by the fall. And Christ came into the world as it were to create a new heavens and a new earth (Isaiah 65:17). The songs that are sung in this new creation are not such as were sung in the old. Everything there is new. God, when about to create the new heavens and new earth, says, Revelation 21:5, “Behold, I make all things new.”

Particularly that song is founded in the great works of God, whereby he brings to pass a new state of things.

1. That great work that was wrought, by which redemption was wrought out for fallen [man], consisting in the incarnation, life, death and resurrection of our blessed Redeemer. These things were new things, such as the eyes of angels nor men ever beheld before. That the Son of God should be incarnate, that God should become man, was a new thing. That the Lord of glory should become a man of sorrows, that he that was God

-- 231 --

over all, blessed forevermore, should die such an accursed death, were new and strange and wonderful things.

The works of God that were consequent on this were new things, such as the pouring out of [the] Spirit that was in the days of the Apostle, the calling of the Gentiles from heathenism, the conversion of the heathen world.

And there yet remain new things of that nature to be accomplished, in the bringing the whole world to the Christian faith and settling the world in that state of light, peace and holiness that shall be in the latter days, when the wolf shall dwell with the lamb. These things are spoken of as new things. Isaiah 42:9, “Behold, the former things are come to pass, and new things do I declare: before they spring forth I tell you of them.” And then it follows in the next verse, “Sing unto the Lord a new song, and his praise from the end of the earth.” And so Isaiah 43:18-20, “Remember ye not the former things, neither consider the things of old. Behold, I will do a new thing; now it shall spring forth; shall ye not know it? I will even make a way in the wilderness, and rivers in the desert. The beast of the field shall honor me, the dragons and the owls: because I give waters in the wilderness, and rivers in the desert, to give drink to my people, my chosen.” The city of God founded by this work of redemption is called the New Jerusalem. These redeemed ones, that sing the new song spoken of in the text, are the inhabitants of the city.

This glorious work of God in redemption lays a foundation for this new song, as it supplies new matter for a song of praise, things to be the subject and theme of praise that are new and such as neither men nor angels never sung of, never saw nor heard of before.

Man in innocency had many glorious works of God to sing of, works of creation and {God's goodness}. But the redeemed of Christ have a new song to sing.

Man in innocency had great instances of God's goodness to make the subject of songs of praise. But {the redeemed of Christ have a new song to sing}.

The angels had great things to sing of, and we read that they “sang together, and all the sons of God shouted for joy” (Job 38:7).

Moses and the children of Israel, when they were redeemed out of Egypt by {God}, had great things to sing of. They sang the song of Moses.

But they that are redeemed by the wonderful works which Christ has wrought from captivity to the devil, from eternal destruction, have a new song to sing. They have not only the song of Moses but also the song of the Lamb (Revelation 15:3).

-- 232 --

God gave Adam an earthly paradise and he gave the children of Israel an earthly Canaan, and they had great cause to sing praises to God. But they to whom God gives the heavenly Canaan and an heavenly paradise, have a new song to sing of works of God's love and goodness, far beyond those.

The old Jerusalem, which Israel were possessed of by the warlike acts of David redeeming it out of the hands of the Jebusites, was a glorious city. There was the temple built, a most magnificent structure, and the people of that city had great cause of praise. But they that are by Christ entitled to the New Jerusalem, have a new song to sing of the privileges and blessings of a city very diverse from that; and as hereby3 the glorious gospel of Christ is published, the world is supplied with that new word of life out of which this new song is learned; and also as hereby is purchased a new ability and disposition to sing praise to God.

2. This new song of the saints is founded in the great work of God in renewing their nature, and the benefits that attend. Both this and the other are parts of the work of redemption in its largest extent— only that that was spoken of before was the perpetration, and this is the application, of redemption. Both belong to the work of the new creation.

By this work of God in renewing the heart, the man that is the subject of it becomes a new creature or is a new man, as it is often said in Scripture. And therefore no wonder that his song is new: for we are told that “old things are passed away” and “all things become new with him” [2 Corinthians 5:17]. [The] hearts of the redeemed are new. He has a new spirit put within him (Ezekiel 11:19). Their natures are new. In some sense their bodies are new. They have new eyes. They are renewed in the whole man. They are said to be “a new lump,” having put away “the old leaven” (1 Corinthians 5:7). They are newborn babes (1 Peter 2:2). They have new garments, for their filthy garments are taken away and they are clothed with change of raiment. And they have a new name. Revelation 2:17, “He that overcometh I will give him a white stone, and in the stone a new name written, which no man knoweth saving he that receiveth it.” And ch. Revelation 3:12, “I will write upon him my new name.”

And as other things concerning 'em are new, so they sing a new song. The song they then sing is new in several respects:

a. They have a new object of praise. They now praise him that sits upon the throne and the Lamb who, though they are from everlasting, are new to them. They are newly known by them.

-- 233 --

When they were unknown, they were never truly praised by them. But now God and Christ are revealed to them in that infinite glory and goodness whereby they are worthy to be praised.

b. They have new matter of praise. And they now have an understanding of those glorious works of God's power and wisdom and infinite mercy and love that the angels desire to look into and do praise God for, which they never understood before.

The wisdom of God in the glorious work of redemption, and the sovereign grace of God, and dying love of Christ to sinners, are new things to them. They now can make 'em the matter of their songs, but never could before.

And then they have received new mercies themselves. God has done great things for them that are new things. God has delivered [them] “out of the pit wherein is no water” [Zechariah 9:11]. He has taken their feet out of the miry clay and set 'em upon a rock. Psalms 40:2-03, “He brought me up also out of an horrible pit, out of the miry clay, and set my feet upon a rock, and established my goings. And he hath put a new song in my mouth, even praise unto our God: many shall see it, and fear, and shall trust in the Lord.” He has delivered out of spiritual captivity, has by his mighty power overcome the strong man armed. He has gloriously rode forth in the chariots of salvation in their behalf. He has broken the heads of Leviathan in pieces, has subdued Satan under their feet, and caused them to tread on the lion and adder and to trample under foot the young lion and dragon [Psalms 91:13]. He has called 'em out of darkness into marvelous light. They who were far off are made nigh by the blood of Christ. God has forgiven all their sins and blotted 'em out as a thick cloud. And they have received the adoption of children. God is become their father, and they are become God's sons and daughters.

Here is new matter of praise for [them], matter enough to fill up a new song and to keep them singing to all eternity.4

c. The melody of the heart with which they sing is new. Before they knew but how to sing praise to God: they were unlearned; they could make no melody in God's ear; they had no skill in divine music; they had not learned the tune, nor could they sound one harmonious note. Their hearts were like an instrument that is unstrung and broken, wholly out of tune. The notes were most jarring and discordant. If they pretended to sing praise to God, it was howling and not singing in God's ears.

-- 234 --

But now God has put their hearts in tune. Now there is some inward harmony. Now there is divine harmony that is melodious unto God.

Thus the song that the saints sing is new with respect to what went before, as 'tis later, and as it succeeds and is introduced on the decay and removal of it.

(2) 'Tis called a new song, as 'tis more excellent than what preceded. Old things are removed, and new ones introduced, for the sake of their preferableness and superior excellency. Men are not wont to pull down an old house, to build a new one in its place, but that they may have a better habitation. So they don't put off their old garments, to put on new, but that they may be better clothed. And especially is it so in all God's dispensations, whereby he removes that which is old to make way for something new. The old covenant is abolished and a new covenant established that has a better surety and better promises. The old way into the Holy of Holies is abolished and a better way prepared, a new and living way. The old priesthood of Aaron ceases, and the new priesthood of Christ introduced that is after the order of Melchizedek (Psalms 110:4, Hebrews 5:6), established by the word of the oath forevermore. The old creation vanishes away, and God creates a “new heavens and new earth” so much more excellent than the former, that 'tis said the former shall not be mentioned “nor come into mind” [Isaiah 65:17]. The New Jerusalem is more excellent than the old.

And so the new song that is sung by the redeemed from the earth is more excellent than anything that preceded. It is the most excellent song that ever is sung by any of the children of men.

We often read in Scripture of a new song. And whenever it is mentioned, there is evidently respect to its excellency in its being so called. So Psalms 33:1-03, “Rejoice in the Lord, O ye righteous: for praise is comely for the upright. Praise the Lord with harp: sing unto him with the psaltery and an instrument of ten strings. Sing unto him a new song; play skillfully with a loud noise.” So Psalms 96:4, at [the] beginning, “For the Lord is great, and greatly to be praised.” So the beginning of Psalms 98, “O sing unto the Lord a new song; for he hath done marvelous things.” So Psalms 144:9 and Psalms 149:1, and Isaiah 42:10.

This song is more excellent than anything that was before the fall. The matter of the song is more glorious. The subject matter of the new song is principally the glorious salvation of Christ and God's infinite grace and love in that work, as appears by Revelation 5:9. “And they sung a new song, saying, Thou art worthy to take the book, and to open the seals thereof: for thou wast slain, and hast redeemed us to God by thy blood out of every kindred, and tongue, and people, and nation.”

-- 235 --

This work of God is more wonderful and more glorious by far than anything that Adam in innocency had to praise God. Yea, this is the most glorious subject that the angels in heaven themselves have for their songs. They once sang praises to God for the work of creation, when God laid the foundations {of the earth} and the morning stars sang together. But now, since they have seen the work of redemption, these morning stars sing a new song, and the work of redemption is principally the subject of their praises; as now that is their tune, “Worthy is the Lamb that was slain,” is in Revelation 5:11-12, “And I beheld, and I heard the voice of many angels round about the throne and the beasts and the elders: and the number of them was ten thousand times ten thousand, and thousands of thousands; saying with a loud voice, Worthy is the Lamb that was slain to receive power, and riches, and wisdom, and strength, and honor, and glory, and blessing.” The benefits that the saints receive by Christ are far beyond anything that our first parents enjoyed in innocency. They had happiness indeed, but they had no confirmation of it. They had no promise that they should never lose it, but {rather a threat they should lose it}.

And besides, their privileges are greater in other respects. {They have a} more excellent union {with God, a} greater manifestation of the love of God, {and} a better inheritance.

So their song is far more excellent than anything that was before their conversion, as much more excellent than all their external formal praises that they offered to God before conversion as light is more excellent than darkness, or as gold is more precious than the dirt. One word or note of this new song, as sung by a sincere heart, is worth more in God's account than all the praises of all the formal professors of religion from the beginning of the world to the end of it.5

I come, in the

[II.] Second place, to show how no man can learn that song but Christ's redeemed ones.

This will be manifest by considering two things:

First. By considering what things are requisite in order to learning this song.

Second. How these requisites are obtained.

First. By considering what things are requisite in order to men's learning this song. And there are two things: first, that they should be able to know the song; and second, that they should have ability to sing it. Learning a song implies a learning the song that is to be sung so as to have that

-- 236 --

in our minds. But that is not all that is necessary: but we must also learn to sing. Men may have learned the words of a song by heart, and yet not have learned the tune.

1. Therefore, in order to men's being able to sing this new song, 'tis requisite that they should be able to know the song. They must know what is to be sung. They must have the knowledge of those things that are the subject matter of the song. The song is spiritual, and the things that are the subject matter of it are spiritual and divine: the glorious perfections and excellencies of God, especially in that new and most excellent manifestation of them that is made in the works of the new creation by Jesus Christ; the glory of God as it shines in the face of Christ; the glory of God's power and wisdom and holiness, justice and faithfulness, as manifested by him; and especially the infinite and glorious riches of divine grace and love in a Redeemer, appearing in the incarnation, life, {death and resurrection} of Christ, and in the appointed way of salvation by Christ, by faith in him; and also as appearing in the application of redemption, the glory of God and Christ as appearing in the conversion of the soul and in carrying on the work of grace unto glory.

Such things as these are the matter of this song, and therefore, in order to the song's being learned, these things must be learned. He that is ignorant of these things is ignorant of the new song.

2. There must be an ability to sing. Not only the matter of the song must be learnt, but also the music of the song. But the music of this tune consists in the melody of the heart. Ephesians 5:19, “Speaking to yourselves in psalms and hymns and spiritual songs, singing and making melody in your heart to the Lord.” As in order to learn the music of other songs, the voice must be tuned, so in order to learn the music of this song, the heart must be tuned. The music of this new song consists in holy admiration, in exalting thoughts of the glory of God and the Lamb and the great things of the gospel; and in divine love, in loving God for his excellency appearing in the face of Christ, in holy rejoicing in God and in delight and complacence of the soul in Jesus, whereby we, having not seen him, do love him and “rejoice with joy unspeakable and full of glory” [1 Peter 1:8]. They that are unacquainted with this spiritual melody, they can't learn to sing that new song.

These are the things that are requisite to the learning this song.

Second. We may consider how [these] requisites are obtained.

1. They are the fruit of the redemption or purchase of Jesus Christ. Christ has purchased for a certain number that knowledge of the glorious excellency of God and Christ, and of the infinite riches of his grace

-- 237 --

appearing in the way of salvation. That excellent heavenly light that shines into the soul, and gives it a view and understanding of those excellent things, is a blessing purchased at the price of Christ's precious blood. And so that ability, that harmony of the soul and melody of the heart, consisting in holy love and joy and delight in God and Christ, is a precious benefit that is the fruit of the same purchase. This heavenly melody is dear; it costs Christ's precious blood.

And if these things cannot be obtained any other way than by the redemption [of Christ], then we may see the reason why none can obtain them but those that are redeemed by Christ. As 'tis said in the text, “no man could learn but the {hundred and forty and four thousand} that were redeemed from the earth.”

2. These things are imparted in the saving work of God's Spirit on the soul. In this work it is that God gives the soul that divine light and knowledge of the glorious and joyful things that the gospel reveals, which are the subject of this song.

And in this work it is that the heart is put in tune, and it is put into a capacity and disposition truly and sincerely to praise God and to make that heavenly melody, which is made in singing this new song, by exercising these divine principles of divine love and divine joy. “The love of God is shed abroad” in the heart by the Holy Ghost (Romans 5:5). And spiritual rejoicing is called “joy in the Holy Ghost” [Romans 14:17]. This saving work of God in the heart is redemption by power, as the other is redemption by purchase. And seeing it is thus— that this knowledge of the things to be sung, and the ability to make the melody of the song, is imparted no other way than by the saving work of the Spirit, whereby the soul is redeemed by power— hence we may see another reason why no man can learn that song but they that are redeemed. Other men are dumb and dead as to any such heavenly exercise as this. They can exalt their idols, but they can't exalt God. They can rejoice in the objects of their lusts, in their worldliness and in their carnal pleasures, but they can't rejoice in Christ Jesus. They can howl, but they can't sing the new song.

This song, whenever it is learnt, must be learned out of the gospel. But none can truly understand the gospel of Jesus Christ but his redeemed ones.6

But I now proceed to make application of what you have heard this day concerning this new song.

-- 238 --

Application.

Use I may be of Warning, to warn young people against learning and singing lewd songs. How very disagreeable will that be for those that hope either that they are or shall be of that blessed holy company spoken of in the text, whose souls are pure chaste virgins that follow the Lamb, and that shall be with the Lamb on Mt. Zion in white robes, and shall forever sing the new song of holy praises to God and the Lamb.

Thus it is with you: you hope to be of this company, and hope that singing this divine song shall be your eternal exercise. If you knew that it would not be so, how would you be terrified and amazed. If it should now be told you from heaven, that you never should learn this song and never be admitted into that glorious company that sing it, how would it astonish you. With what frighted amazed hearts would you go out of the meeting house, if your despair would suffer you to go out and your astonishment would not disenable you from going anywhere.

But you depend upon being sometime or other of that society and learning this song. To what purpose else is it that you come to meeting and attend any of the means of grace?

And if this be the case, why will you sing songs of so disagreeable and contrary a nature— vile, impure songs that you should abhor to take into your mouth, that tend to stir up lusts and to pollute the soul, and make it like unclean devils? Such songs are little better than songs of praise to the devil. You serve your lusts and serve Satan by them.

Some of you hope that you already belong to that holy company on Mt. Zion, and that you have already learned that song. If it be so, nothing can be more shocking than the thought of such an one employing his voice in singing vain lewd songs. Think with yourself how the mind of the apostle John would have been shocked and put into a strange surprise, when he saw the vision that we have an account of in the text and context, and saw the Lamb on Mt. Zion with that holy company with him of 144,000 that were redeemed from the earth, and heard that sweet and excellent melody of the new song they sung, if then, of a sudden, he had perceived some of the company leave off their new and holy song and break out in lewd laughter and jollity, singing some filthy, bawdy song. How amazing do you think this would have been to him? You that think yourselves of the redeemed from amongst men should abhor such a thing as bad as you would abhor hell itself. And if you are indeed of that company, as you think yourself to be, you will abhor it. 'Tis impossible it should be otherwise. You will not practice it. You will abhor to do so more than to take the

-- 239 --

corruption of the most filthy Achan into your mouth [Jos 7]. If you have a sanctified nature, such songs will stink in your nostrils and grate upon your ears more than the most hideous howlings.

[Use] II is of Exhortation to all earnestly to seek that they may learn this new song. You that never have been converted and are not of that company that are with the Lamb, but are poor perishing sinners, aliens and strangers, are far off, are the children of the night and of darkness, and are the children of that wicked one, wretched slaves of sin and Satan: let what you have heard stir up in you an earnest desire to be converted, that you may also sing that new song. Hitherto you knew not what it means; 'tis in a language you don't understand.

And you that hope you are converted, to you also is this word of exhortation. Earnestly seek that you may learn to sing this new song. If you have been taught of God, and do already know something of it, yet how very imperfectly have you learned it. You make but a poor head at least at singing this song. You are now beginners in it, and how miserably do you blunder in it. You are like children that do but lisp and stammer.

This song is a song of perfect harmony, of sweet and heavenly melody; but so imperfectly have you learned it that when you do your best at singing of it, there are a great many jarring discordant notes. There are many other sounds mixed that don't belong to the song and that do greatly mar the music. O what need have you to seek earnestly that you may better learn this excellent song, that you may sing it more regularly and make more of that excellent melody that belongs to it!

Therefore let all that are here present look upon this exhortation as belonging to them, and seek to learn this new song. And here, let the following things be considered:

First. The melody of this song is most acceptable to God. The great God is delighted with it. 'Tis acceptable to Jesus Christ. He accepts of such praise, and never rejects it. If we sing this new song in our hearts when we are alone and retired from the world, to spend some time in secret between God and our own souls, Christ will come to us and will as it were listen to the melodious notes, and will reward us with some token of his favor. If we sing this new song in our public assemblies, it will be to worship God in the beauty of holiness, and will be the way to have God delighting to be in the midst of us. In singing this new song to God and the Lamb, our thanksgiving days will be acceptable to God. This song is more pleasing to God than the offering up of thousands of rams and ten thousands of rivers of oil.

Second. This song is not only acceptable to God, but most sweet and delightful

-- 240 --

to the soul of him that sings it. It is the pleasantest song in the world. The singing of songs is used as a token of mirth and rejoicing. Singing and rejoicing are wont to go together. When carnal persons meet together to sing songs, it is in token of carnal mirth. But there is no joy like that which is expressed in singing this new song. This is that pleasure that the Psalmist speaks of in Psalms 135:3, “sing praises unto his name; for it is pleasant.” And so Psalms 147:1, “Praise ye the Lord: for it is good to sing praises unto our God; for it is pleasant; and praise is comely.” The melody is pleasant to the angels, and pleasant to the Lamb of God, and pleasant to him that sits upon the throne, and most pleasant to the soul that makes the melody. The act of praise is an abundant reward to itself. He that sings it has communion with God in it, for in the same time that there flows a stream of love out of the heart towards God, there flows a more full stream of love from God into the soul. At the same time that he praises God with a sweet voice, Christ with a sweeter voice speaks peace to the soul and manifests his love to it.

As 'tis said in the text, that no man could learn that song but the redeemed from the earth, so no man knows the sweetness or intermeddles with the joy of this song but they.

The sweetness and delight is too excellent for any but they. God will not suffer any but those that are his peculiar favorites to taste it.

How great does the rejoicing of this company that John saw in his vision seem to be in singing this sweet song. So engaged were they in it, and so loud and sweet was the melody of their united voices, that John seems not know how to express it, and therefore uses various similitudes. Says he, it was “as the voice of many waters, and as the voice of a great thunder,” and as “the voice of harpers harping with their harps” [Revelation 14:2]. The joy and praise and melody were inexpressible.

Third. This song is fit to be sung in the mournful and sorrowful times. We have always reason to sing it, whatever afflictions we may be under, and there is support and joy to be had in the melody of it in the darkest times. This is the song that God gives in the night (Job 35:10), i.e. in the night of affliction.

'Tis very proper to sing this new song on a death bed. And many of the saints have sung it then, and thereby have triumphed over death; yea, many have sung this new song in the flames of martyrdom and have exulted in the midst of torments. The joy and sweetness of it is enough to carry the soul above pain and above the cruelty of men and devils.

Fourth. This is the song that is sung by the glorious company in heaven. Singing this song is the principal employment of that assembly, in which

-- 241 --

they employ themselves continually and of which they are never weary. With the melody of this song does heaven ring, as with the voice of many waters and the voice of a great thunder, as in the verse before the text: “And I heard a voice from heaven as the voice of many waters, and as the voice of a great thunder: and I heard the voice of harpers harping with their harps.”7

So far therefore as we sing this song on earth, so much shall we have the prelibations of heaven. In this way we shall have something of heaven in our closets and in our families.8 And this will make our public assemblies some image of heaven, and will make our sabbath days and thanksgiving days some resemblance of that eternal sabbath and thanksgiving that is solemnized by that innumerable company of angels and spirits of just men made perfect.

Fifth. This song is that which is sung by Jesus Christ himself in heaven, and therefore 'tis called the song of the Lamb. As 'tis promised to believers that they shall be partakers with Christ in the honor of his new name (Revelation 3:12), so they shall also partake with him in the joy of his new song. When Christ rose from the dead and ascended into heaven, then he received a new name, i.e. he was as God-man advanced to new honor. So at the same time he began then to sing a new song, which he sings still and will sing to all eternity.

Christ is the head of the church, the leader of all the 144,000 that are on Mt. Zion with him, and when they sing that new song, they do but follow him. 'Tis he that leads them the way in it, as the elder brother and priest of the family, so far as the song is to praise God the Father. Christ, when he was upon earth in his state of humiliation, went mourning; and when he was in the garden in his agony, he groaned and cried with strong crying and tears, and when he was on the cross he cried out, “My God, my God, why hast thou forsaken me?” [Matthew 27:46, Mark 15:34]. But when he ascended into heaven, then his sorrow was turned into joy, and his mourning and crying into singing. And this joy believers shall partake with him in. John 17:13, “That my joy may be fulfilled in yourselves.”

Sixth. If you learn this song and begin to sing it here, you will sing it to all eternity. They that once learn this song never forget it. The life that Christ gives his saints is everlasting life. The new name he gives them is an everlasting name, that shall not be cut off. And so the new song that he teaches them is an everlasting song.

-- 242 --

They that now learn this new song, the time will soon come when they shall do nothing but sing. They shall cry and mourn no more. Isaiah 35:10, “And the ransomed of the Lord shall return, and come to Zion with songs and everlasting joy upon their heads: they shall obtain joy and gladness, and sorrow and mourning shall flee away.”

In a little time, and they shall sing the new song with saints and angels in heaven, and shall sing it there with Jesus Christ. And at the day of judgment, when Christ shall have gathered together all his elect and they shall be all together in complete glory, then shall they ascend up to heaven with Christ and enter into that glorious city with the voice of singing, singing praises to God the Father. Then will Christ's heart rejoice, and he will sing to God, and they shall rejoice and sing with him.

Seventh. In vain will all our singing be if we han't learned to sing this song. We may sing psalms sabbath after sabbath, we may keep thanksgiving days; but no song is acceptable to God but this. Without this, all our learning the rule of singing and endeavors to perfect ourselves in that art will be in vain. We shall make no melody to God.

Eighth. Consider how soon an end will be put to all other songs and all other singing but singing this song. As to those lewd songs that are sung by vain youth, how quickly are their mouths stopped. How soon does death come and turn their tone. And as to the formal singing of hypocrites, an end will soon be put to it, and they shall never sing anymore but, instead of that, shall curse and blaspheme with devils in hell.9 And the time will come when this world will come to an end and then, after that, there never shall be any other song or any other singing remaining but this that we have heard of.

Ninth. If you don't learn to sing this new song, the time will soon come, that when God's servants “sing for joy of heart, you shall mourn for sorrow of heart and howl for vexation of spirit” (Isaiah 65:14). You shall see your neighbors hereafter full of joy and shall hear them singing, and you shall see “many come from the east and from the west, and shall sit down with Abraham, and Isaac, and Jacob, in the kingdom of heaven” [Matthew 8:11], falling down in the kingdom of God, singing the new song, when you shall wail and gnash your teeth, and shall howl with devils with most doleful and dolorous lamentations and cries. Thus it shall be at the day of judgment, when you shall see a shining glorious company at the right hand of Christ singing the new song, and you shall be at the left among devils and shall shriek with dismal outcries at the hearing of that dreadful sentence,

-- 243 --

“Depart from me, ye cursed, into everlasting fire, prepared for the devil and his angels” [Matthew 25:41]. And consider that there never, never will be an end to that mourning and that howling.

So many and great motives are there, together with many more that might be mentioned, to induce earnestly to seek that we may learn this song.

I will conclude with three things that may be of direction to [those] that are desirous of learning this new song:

1. 'Tis Christ that must teach this song. You can learn it of no other way. 'Tis he that is the author of the song, and the skill of those that can sing it is too excellent for any other to impart but Christ. As there is none that can learn [it] but those that are “redeemed from the earth,” so there is no creature in heaven or earth that can teach it.1

Therefore you must go to God. You must cry to him to grant you instruction, to impart the knowledge of the subject matter of this song, and to put your heart in tune, [to] give you that blessed skill.

2. The way for you to learn this new song, is first to mourn with a broken heart for sin. Christ says in Matthew 5:4, “Blessed are they that mourn: for they shall be comforted”; and in the sixth [chapter] of Luke, v. Luke 6:21, “Blessed are ye that weep now: for ye shall laugh.” And 'tis said in the one hundred and twenty-sixth psalm, v. Psalms 126:6, that “He that goes forth weeping, bearing precious seed, shall doubtless come again with rejoicing, bringing his sheaves with him.”

So the greater your sense is of sin and of your own utter unworthiness upon that account, and the deeper your mourning is for it, the more perfectly are you like to learn the new song and to sing it with greater joy and exultation of spirit.

They that deeply mourn for sin shall have that fulfilled to 'em, in Isaiah 61:3, “To appoint unto them that mourn in Zion, to give unto them beauty for ashes, the oil of joy for mourning, the garment of praise for the spirit of heaviness; that they might be called trees of righteousness, the planting of the Lord, that he might be glorified.”

3. In order to learn the new song, you must hear the melody of the voice of Christ in the gospel. You have heard that the glorious gospel is that out of which this song is to be learned, and that 'tis Christ that must teach it. And this is the way that he teaches it: by causing the soul to hear the melody of his own voice in the gospel.

-- 244 --

'Tis Christ that speaks to us in the gospel. Many hear his words, but they perceive no sweetness in them. They perceive no pleasantness in his voice, in the doctrines and invitations and promises of the gospel. 'Tis all an insipid thing and dead letter to them. But to the godly, Christ's mouth is found to be most sweet. You must perceive the sweetness of the voice. You must see the glory of those doctrines, and the sweetness of those invitations, and the exceeding preciousness of those promises.

This must teach you those things that must be the subject of your song. And this must draw your heart, and put it into tune, and fill it with love and joy, which is the excellent harmony and heavenly melody of the soul.
