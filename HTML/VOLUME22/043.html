Importunate Prayer for Millennial Glory
Ye that make mention of the Lord, keep not silence, and give him no rest, till he establish, and till he make Jerusalem a praise in the earth. Isaiah 62:6–7.

This chapter seems to be one continued discourse with the foregoing chapter and, therefore, we may look upon it that the same person is speaking here that spoke there. And so we may learn who it is that is represented speaking in this chapter by the first words of the preceding chapter: “The Spirit of the Lord God is upon me; because the Lord hath anointed me to preach good tidings unto the meek; he hath sent me to bind up the broken-hearted, to proclaim liberty to the captives, and the opening of the prison to them that are bound; to proclaim the acceptable year of the Lord, and the day of the vengeance of our God” [Isaiah 61:1-02].

As 'tis Christ that speaks there, so the whole chapter is a continuation of his discourse. And so 'tis Christ that continues to speak in this chapter, and says in the first verse, “For Zion's sake I will not hold my peace, and for Jerusalem's sake I will not rest, until the righteousness thereof go forth as brightness, and the salvation thereof as a lamp that burneth.” Christ here foretells that prosperous and glorious state that the church shall be in the latter days: “the righteousness thereof [shall] go forth as brightness, and the salvation thereof as a lamp that burneth.” And in the first verse, Christ, who is the head of the church, [says] concerning himself that he will not hold his peace till this glorious state of things shall be brought to pass. And then in the sixth verse he declares that his ministers in those days should follow his example, that they should never hold their peace: “I have set watchmen upon thy walls, O Jerusalem, which shall never hold their peace day nor night.” And then in the words of the text is his command to his professing people that they should also follow his example and not hold their peace: “ye that make mention of the Lord,

-- 369 --

keep not silence, and give him no rest, till he establish, and till he make Jerusalem a praise in the earth.”

Christ will not hold his peace himself, but will continue to speak and utter his voice in two respects, viz. as the prophet of the church, to instruct her by his word and Spirit; and as the priest of his church, in making intercession for her. In the former of these respects [he] declares that his ministers shall imitate him, viz. in not holding their peace in their instructions: “I have set watchmen upon thy walls, O Jerusalem, which shall never hold their peace day nor night.” And in the latter he commands all his professing people to imitate him, viz. in intercession and prayer to God. In the text {we read}, “ye that make mention of the Lord, keep not silence, And give him no rest, till he establish, and till he make Jerusalem a praise in the earth.”

In the text may be observed four things:

1. Who are commanded.

2. The duty commanded.

3. The manner of the duty: importunity.

4. The blessing to be sought in this duty.

Doctrine.
God's professing people ought earnestly and importunately to pray to God that he would accomplish those great things that he has spoken of to bring to pass a glorious state of his church on earth.

I. Show what are those great things that God has spoken of by which he will bring to pass a prosperous, glorious state of the church.

II. I would give the reason why God's professing people ought earnestly and importunately to pray for this.

I. I would show what are those great things that God has foretold he will do, by which he will bring to pass a very glorious state of the church on earth.

But I would first observe, in the general, that we are not to suppose that this will be accomplished [but] by a succession of great events.

But now I proceed to show what great works or events they are by which this glorious state of the church will be brought to pass. God's Spirit will be wonderfully poured out on his professing people for reviving vital piety among them and to fit them for the propagation of it. God will make use of his church as the means. [In] Isaiah 41:15-16 [we read of a] threshing instrument out of Zion: “Behold, I will make thee a new sharp threshing instrument having teeth: thou shalt thresh the mountains, and beat them

-- 370 --

small, and shalt make the hills as chaff. Thou shalt fan them, and the wind shall carry them away, and the whirlwind shall scatter them: and thou shalt rejoice in the Lord, and shalt glory in the Holy One of Israel.” They are Christ's armies. Isaiah 62:6, [“I have set watchmen upon thy walls, O Jerusalem”].

I come now

II. To give the reasons why God's professing people ought earnestly to pray to God that he would accomplish those things.

First. Because those things shall be very eminently for the glory of God. The Lord's Prayer teaches us in the first place to pray that God's name may be glorified. Then God will be glorified throughout the world, the power and grace of God especially manifested most openly and publicly. And God's glory therein will be seen by the whole world to the admiration and praise of all nations. Isaiah 61:11, “For as the earth bringeth forth her bud, and as the garden causeth the things that are sown in it to spring forth; so the Lord will cause righteousness and praise to spring forth before all nations.” Then Christ as Redeemer will be glorified exceedingly, the glorious gospel preached to all nations. Christ will everywhere be known, become the desire of all nations. Then every knee [shall] bow. Then will be his glorious victory and triumph. Enemies [shall be] subdued under his feet.

Second. What should be accomplished in those times was especially the joy that was set before Christ and what he chiefly sought in his death and sufferings. Most of the success of Christ's redemption will be then. The promises made to Christ chiefly respect this time: “[Therefore will I] divide him a portion with the great” [Isaiah 53:12]; “[he shall] see his seed, [he shall prolong his days, and the pleasure of the Lord shall prosper in his hand” (Isaiah 53:10)].

Third. What will be accomplished in those times will remove that confusion and misery that now attends the state of the world and will be exceedingly for its prosperity. Darkness [shall be] removed. [There shall be] no night (Zechariah 14:6-07). Isaiah 24:23, “Then the moon [shall be] confounded.” Isaiah 25:7, “And he will destroy in this mountain [the face of the covering cast over all people, and the vail that is spread over all nations.” Isaiah 32:3-04, “The eyes of them that see shall not be dim.”

Application.

[I.] Hence we learn that we ought to prepare for those glorious times of the Christian church. We shall be inconsistent with ourselves and shall

-- 371 --

but mock God if we pray for them and don't prepare for them. The Scriptures do particularly call upon the visible church of Christ to prepare for this day. Revelation 16:15, “Behold, I come as a thief. Blessed is he that watches, and keepeth his garments, least he walk naked, and they see his shame.” When God brings this to pass it will be a great event, and there is great need of preparation for it.

But here, to induce us to prepare for those times, let the following things be considered:

First. Those times will be not be introduced without very great and general commotions and overturnings in which professing Christians will doubtless have great trials.

Second. It may well stir us up diligently to prepare for those times, that the introducing them is in Scripture compared to Christ's coming to judgment. There are three events in Scripture especially that are compared to Christ's last coming. 'Tis called Christ's coming, Revelation 16:15. There, after the sixth vial, after the devil's armies are gathered together to their great battle, and just before Christ's glorious victory over them, 'tis said, “behold I come quickly,” having respect to that overthrow of Antichrist that should be by the seventh vial. So 'tis called Christ's coming in 2 Thessalonians 2:8. Speaking of Antichrist it is said, “And then shall that Wicked be revealed, whom the Lord shall consume with the spirit of his mouth, and shall destroy with the brightness of his coming.” So in Daniel 7:13-14 Christ's coming to set up his kingdom on earth is represented as his coming with clouds of heaven, and so compared to his last coming. This shows what great need there is of care to prepare for this event. There is nothing that does so much require preparation as the day of judgment, which is called “the great day” (Jude 6).

This day of God's overthrowing the visible kingdom of Satan is also called that great day of God almighty. 'Tis spoken of as a very great day indeed, a wonderful time and doubtless a time greatly calling for preparation. Happy will those be that shall be found prepared for that great day of the overthrow of Antichrist and other enemies of Christ and setting up the kingdom of Christ through the world.

How much was Christ in warning his disciples to prepare for the day when he would come to the destruction of Jerusalem, in Matthew 24. But this event is a much greater event than that and therefore more requiring preparation for it. This day is compared to the day of judgment chiefly upon this account, because it will be a very trying time. There probably will be more then to try men and to make a manifest difference between saints and sinners than ever has been in the world. And therefore all ought

-- 372 --

to [take] great care that they may be ready. And let it be considered, in the

Third place, that now God seems in his providence, in an especial manner, to be calling us to be ready for those times. God seems to be as it were coming forth out of his place to do some extraordinary thing. We may go too far in positively determining what God is about to do or predicting future events, but there is no danger of our going too far in preparing for what may be.

We have prophecies left by God in the Scriptures, in the hands of his church, of what God will do in the latter days, which doubtless are to be of some use to the church. And one great use of them undoubtedly is to forewarn them, that they may prepare for them before they come. And therefore the prophecies, though they are not so plain that we can particularly and fully understand them or foresee the exact time of their fulfillment before the event, yet they doubtless may, together with what is seen in providence, be of so much direction to us as may be sufficient for our timely admonition to prepare for the fulfillment of those prophecies. Most of those things that are foretold in the prophecies of Scripture, that are to be fulfilled before the remarkable pouring out of the Spirit {of God}, are by the consent of all already fulfilled. And therefore, though we don't know how long it may be before {the onset of the latter days}, yet considering this, and considering what now appears in providence in so remarkable a pouring out of the Spirit of God in many places and the commotion the nations of the world seem to be in, we certainly ought to look upon it that we have warning in providence to prepare {for them}.

That we may be prepared for those great events that are to introduce the glorious times of the Christian church, the following things are requisite:

1. That [we] should make our calling and election sure; otherwise we shall not know whether we are safe, whether we shall be safe in those trials that will attend the introduction [of the latter days], know whether we have any part or lot [in the glorious times of the Christian church]. This is needful in order to our rejoicing with God's inheritance at that day, if we should live to see it.

2. In order to our preparation for those glorious times, it is necessary that we should continually stand upon our watches. They that are found slumbering and sleeping won't be prepared for the coming of the bridegroom when he comes to his espousals with his church. As was observed before, this event is called the coming of Christ. How often is watching enjoined in order to a preparation for the coming of Christ. We had need

-- 373 --

to watch lest we get into ill frames and so should not be in a proper frame to receive it and welcome our Lord when he comes.

3. In order to preparation {for those glorious times} 'tis requisite that we should be weaned from the world. Men that have their affections all over immersed in the things of the world will be in a very unfit disposition for such times as those. The blessedness of those times will be mainly spiritual and heavenly.

But here in this use I would particularly address myself to those that yet remain in a natural condition, to exhort them to do their utmost to get out of such a state, that they may be prepared for the glorious times of the church. You have heard this day that is the duty of all such as make mention of the Lord earnestly and importunately to pray for the glorious {state of his church on earth}. You are some of those {that make mention of the Lord}. But how unprepared are you in your present condition for those times. For,

(1) If you should live to see 'em and remain in your present condition, you would have no share in 'em, no share in the glory of those times. Then there will be times of glorious light, knowledge of God. But you, if you should live to see 'em and yet remain in your natural condition, would have no share in that light. {You would remain} dark in the midst of so great light. Then4 would be times of holiness, love to God, rejoicing in Christ, communion with God, holy love one to another, praising God together, [and yet you] would not be one of the company. [You would be as] an alien.

It will be glorious times with the church, the blessed company of true saints, but not with others. It will be with you as it was with Simon Magus (Acts 8:21), with Tobiah and Sanballat (Nehemiah 2:20), with the unbelieving land, with Achan, with Joab and Abiathar and Adonijah, with the Jews in Christ's time. Isaiah 65:13, “servants shall eat, but ye shall be hungry.”

Now you see and hear of great things that God is working. You have no share [in them]; [you are] unacquainted [with them]; [you] know nothing

-- 374 --

of these things; [you are] blind [with regard to them and], it may be, are ready to question [them]. Consider that place: “Beware therefore, lest that [come upon you, which is spoken of in the prophets]; behold, ye despisers, and wonder, and perish: [for I work a work in your days, a work which ye shall in no wise believe, though a man declare it unto you]” (Acts 13:40-41). [Beware lest ye be] like the barren heath [Jeremiah 17:6].

This is a time of spring. You are like a dry tree in the spring.

All things that you see and all that you hear from abroad are so many condemnations of you.

As the work increases, so much the more are you condemned.

And if hereafter you should hear of great things, as of the Jews being called, Antichrist being fallen, you can't rejoice in what [you] know [or what you] hear from time to time. What are you the better for the Spirit of God being poured out on the chief towns in the land, on both the colleges, on this town? And if there should be an universal reformation in all the towns in the land, if you remain in a Christless condition you will have no part or lot in the matter.

(2) If you should [live enough] time to see {the glorious times} and should remain in a natural condition, they would not only not be glorious times to you, but times of remarkable misery. The Scripture always represents the matter thus: that then will be a time of remarkable judgments on wicked men (Isaiah 66:14), in many outward, visible tokens of God's anger on open enemies. And on many others, there will be more of a distinction than usual. Therefore, as there was a remarkable distinction in the time of the destruction of Jerusalem, [so in the glorious times]. But whether they will be the subjects of distinguishing outward judgments or no, yet doubtless they will be of spiritual judgments, as it was with the unbelieving Jews in Christ's time.

Guilt will be dreadful. All that will then be, will be aggravations of their unbelief. {As the true saints are} exalted up to heaven, [they will be] cast down. {Their} sin [will be] more like the sin of the devils than any other. The devils sinned in heaven {much as they have sinned on earth}. They that continue in sin in the visible church at that day will be wicked in that state of things, that nearest of any to heaven.

So that, if you live {to see that day}, they will be dreadful times to you. And if you die in such a condition, you will wish you never had seen 'em.

Let these things stir up all those in this congregation that yet remain in a natural state and condition earnestly to strive and cry to God that there may be a glorious change in their own souls, which alone will fit them for that glorious change in the face of the world that we are hoping for. Take

-- 375 --

heed that you don't, at such an extraordinary season as this is, remain secure and negligent of your soul's welfare. And take heed that you don't seek your salvation in a slothful manner, without thoroughly denying your lusts. And take heed that you don't look back after you have put your hand to the plow.

You know not what is coming upon the world. It was foretold that, before Christ's coming to set up the Christian church and to call the Gentiles, that God would shake all nations (Hag 2:7); and accordingly it came to pass. So without doubt God will in as great, yea, and a much greater degree, {shake the nations in those glorious times}. And what safety can there be for you if you remain {in a natural condition}? What will become of you if there should come a time of persecution? As to the godly, they can say, as Psalms 46, “The Lord is my refuge and strength.”

II. I would improve this doctrine by exhorting all to be much in prayer to God for the glorious times of the Christian church that God has promised.5 According to my observation, this is too much neglected by the common people in their prayers to God. They insist much in their prayers on petition for personal favors, spiritual blessings to be bestowed on themselves, and, it may be too, for the reformation of God in the town and for the reformation of the land. But the state of God's church in general is commonly too much neglected. If it be mentioned frequently, 'tis but just mentioned and not much insisted or enlarged upon.

This is not becoming Christians that are citizens of Zion, that should be concerned for the whole city to which they belong and ought to prefer the welfare of God's Jerusalem above their chief joy. It savors too much of a selfish spirit, as though the blessings that were asked were not much out of regard to God's glory but only their own interest. If we regard God's glory in our prayers, God's glory and the glory of our redeemer is much more concerned in the state of the church abroad in the world than it is only in the state of our own souls and the souls of our near neighbors. The whole church is all but one family, and therefore, God's people in their prayers should not divide themselves from the rest of the body or society, but should pray for the welfare of the whole.

Herein they will much more imitate the example of their Lord and head and Redeemer, who sought the good of the whole church, died for the whole, and prayed for the whole, and now daily intercedes for the whole. If we neglect to pray for the church in general and for that prosperous,

-- 376 --

glorious state of the church that is promised, or do but slightly mention it in our prayers, we shall not do as we are taught in the Lord's Prayer, which is the special rule which Christ has given us for our direction in prayer. In that prayer we are directed, in the first place, to pray that God's name may be glorified, which teaches us that we should be more concerned that God may be glorified in the world than our private interest promoted. And therefore, in the next, we are directed to pray that God's kingdom may come, which has a special respect to the glorious state of the church which is to be brought to pass in the latter days. And the next petition, “Thy will be done, on earth as 'tis in heaven”; which respects not the doing of God's will only by ourselves and neighbors but through the whole earth, and has also a special respect to that glorious state of things foretold, when 'tis said, Isaiah 60:21, “thy people shall be all righteous.” Then will that petition be answered in the highest degree that ever it will.

Thus you see that one half of the petition is that form of prayer which Christ has given as a special rule to direct us all in our prayers. One half of the petitions do respect the state of God's church and the world of mankind: and those mentioned in the first place, before any of those petitions that respect our own selves and our own separate interests, which ought to be remembered and considered by all the professed followers and disciples of Christ.

And besides, if we neglect the state of the church of Christ in general in our prayers, or do but slightly mention it, we shall be far from obeying the command in the text, which directs God's professing people to insist very much on it, not to keep silence, and to give God no rest till {he establish, and till he make Jerusalem a praise in the earth}. God's people ought to insist on this, not only in their prayers together when they are the mouth of a praying company, as in private meetings and in family prayers, but also in their secret prayers. For the Lord's Prayer is given as a rule of direction, not only for social prayer, but for secret prayer, and that more especially, as you may see in Matthew 6:6-08, “But thou, when thou prayest, enter into thy closet, and when thou hast shut thy door, pray to thy Father which is in secret; and thy Father which seeth in secret shall reward thee openly. But when ye pray, use not vain repetitions, as the heathen do: for they think that they shall be heard for their much speaking. Be not ye therefore like unto them: for your Father knoweth what things ye have need of, before ye ask him.”

And at such a time as this, especially, ought this to be very much insisted in prayer, when there is such special and extraordinary need of such a

-- 377 --

mercy, the church and the state of religion is sunk so low, and when those things are drawing near, and we have much more reason to be in expectation of their speedy accomplishment than formerly the church of God [had], and when God is doing so many things that have a tendency to the advancement of his kingdom on earth and to the accomplishment of the church's glory, which we hear of with our ears and see with our eyes.

God hereby is putting us in mind of what he has promised, and is showing us how easy it is for him to accomplish it if he pleases, and is doing that which greatly tends to excite the expectations and appetite and earnest desires of God's people after these things, and so their prayers for them. Now all that have any love to Zion should cry to God for her. When Daniel understood by books that the time of God's people's redemption out of Babylon was nigh, it stirred up in him a spirit of prayer for the mercy, as you may see, Daniel 9 in the beginning.

I would conclude with one Direction in order to praying acceptably for the glorious times of the church of Christ: and that is that we show by our practice that we heartily desire the thing that we pray for. In order to this, two things especially:

First. That we in practice conform to that glorious state of things that we pray for, that we endeavor to be as God's people in those times will be and to behave as God's people will then behave. [The glorious times of the church will be a] time of great light. Then [all true saints will] be eminently holy. Let us seek, [therefore, now to be eminently holy]. Then [will be] remarkably a time of peace and love.

Second. That we do what we can to promote things that have a tendency [to lead to that glorious state]. God will make use of men of his church. They shall be the instruments. [God will use them] to promote religion in families, among neighbors [as an] example. All [should] set to with heart and head to promote a general reviving of religion in this town among the godly. [They should be as] Christ's armies following him, “[to the] help of the Lord against the mighty” (Jdg 5:23).6
