Keeping the Presence of God
And he went out to meet Asa, and said unto him, Hear ye me, Asa, and all Judah and Benjamin; The Lord is with you, while ye be with him; and if ye seek him, he will be found of you; but if ye forsake him, he will forsake you. II Chronicles 15:1–2.

[Azariah was sent by God to the leaders of Judah] after they and the people had enjoyed rest and quietness, just after exceeding great prosperity and an extraordinary manifestation of God's favor in answer to prayer.

1. Who they are that Prophet is sent with a message to.

2. The importance.

3. [The] message itself.

(1) A promise.

(2) A threatening.

Doctrine.
The way for those that have the presence of God not to lose it, is to take heed to themselves as to these two things, viz. that they don't forsake him, and they still seek him.

In handling this subject I would, first, particularly consider and explain these two things recommended in the doctrine; and secondly, I would show that this is the way for those that have the presence of God not to lose it.

I. I would particularly consider and explain these two things, that those that have the presence of God ought to do.

First. They must take heed to themselves that they don't forsake God.

[Those that have the presence of God are] apt to forsake God.

Hence 'tis so exceedingly and abundantly insisted on in God's Word that we should take great heed to ourselves, with earnest resolution and

-- 524 --

diligence and a strict watch, that we don't depart from God. I shall mention a considerable number:

Deuteronomy 4:9, “Take heed to thyself, and keep thy soul diligently, lest thou forget the things which thine eyes have seen, and lest they depart from thy heart all the days of thy life: but teach them thy sons, and thy sons' sons.”

Deuteronomy 5:28-29, “And the Lord heard the voice of your words, when ye spake unto me; and the Lord said unto me, I have heard the voice of the words of this people, which they have spoken unto thee: they have well said all that they have spoken. O that there were such an heart in them that they would fear me, and keep all my commandments always, that it might be well with them, and with their children for ever!”

Chapter Deuteronomy 10:12-13, “And now, Israel, what doth the Lord thy God require of thee, but to fear the Lord thy God, to walk in all his ways, and to love him, and to serve the Lord thy God with all thy heart and with all thy soul, to keep the commandments of the Lord, and his statutes, which I command thee this day for thy good?”

Job 23:11, “My foot hath held his steps, his way have I kept, and not declined.”

Job 27:6, “My righteousness I hold fast, and will not let it go: my heart shall not reproach me so long as I live.”

Proverbs 4:23, “Keep thy heart with all diligence; for out of it are the issues of life.”

John 15:4, “And abide in me.”

Acts 11:22-23, “Barnabas exhorted them all, that with purpose of heart they would cleave unto the Lord.”

1 Corinthians 15:58, “be ye steadfast, unmoveable, always abounding in the work of the Lord, forasmuch as ye know that your labor is not in vain in the Lord.”

1 Corinthians 16:13, “stand fast, quit ye like men, be strong.”

2 Corinthians 11:2-03, “I am jealous over you with a godly jealousy.”

Ephesians 6:11-12, “put ye on the whole armor of God, that ye may be able to stand against the wiles of the devil. For we wrestle not against flesh and blood, but against principalities, against powers, against the rulers of the darkness of this world, against spiritual wickedness in high places.”

Philippians 4:1, “stand fast in the Lord, my dearly beloved.”

1 Thessalonians 3:8, “now we live, if ye stand fast in the Lord.”

2 Thessalonians 2:15-17, “Therefore, brethren, stand fast, and hold the traditions which ye have been taught, whether by word, or our epistle. Now our Lord Jesus Christ himself, and God, even our Father, which hath loved

-- 525 --

us, and hath given us everlasting consolation and good hope through grace comfort your hearts, and stablish you in every good word and work.”

Hebrews 2:1-03, “Therefore we ought to give the more earnest heed to the things which we have heard, lest at any time we should let them slip. For if the word spoken by angels was steadfast, and every transgression and disobedience received a just recompense of reward; how shall we escape, if we neglect so great salvation; which at the first began to be spoken by the Lord, and was confirmed unto us by them that heard him.”

Hebrews 3:6, “But Christ as a son over his own house; whose house are we, if we hold fast the confidence and the rejoicing of the hope firm unto the end.”

And Hebrews 4:14, “Seeing then that we have a great high priest, that is passed into the heavens, Jesus the Son of God, let us hold fast our profession.”

Hebrews 10:23, “Let us hold fast the profession of our faith without wavering.”4

Hebrews 12, at [the] beginning, “Wherefore seeing we also are compassed about with so great a cloud of witnesses, let us lay aside every weight, and the sin which doth so easily beset us, and let us run with patience the race that is set before us.”

Persons may depart from God in either of these two respects: first, as to obedience to him; second, as to dependence on him.

1. As to obedience to him.

They that have the presence of God should take heed to themselves that they walk closely with God.

Keep their eye on him, on all his commandments.

Keep their eye on their hearts.

Keep their eye on their ways, to observe all by-paths. Look straight forward.

Keep their eye on the snares [they are] encompassed with.

Keep their eye on God's providence.

Keep their eye on Christ's example, on the precepts of Christianity, [on the] things [of] the Christian spirit. Always be mindful of the holy eye of God, of the strict account [they must give].

Keep hold on the hand of Christ.

2. Those that have the presence of God should take heed to themselves that they don't depart from God as to dependence on him.

-- 526 --

Second. They that have the presence of God ought to continue to seek him.

Not only take heed not to go from him that they have found, but still seek him as though they had not found him. Those that have most of the presence of God should continue earnestly to seek God still as much as if they had not found him. So the Apostle, who had found God probably in as high a degree as ever: “Not as though I had already attained, either were already perfect: but I follow after, if that I may apprehend that for which also I am apprehended of Christ Jesus” (Philippians 3:12).

They have found him but in part. [So they should] seek him as much as when they had not found him at all, as before conversion, or when under desertion.

[They should seek him] earnestly and laboriously and constantly, every day. In the following things:

(1) In earnest desires, not resting in past attainments, though never so high.

(2) Humble and earnest prayers.

(3) By renewedly relinquishing all other things for the blessings that are sought, and doing it more and more.

(4) All manner of duties should be performed in such a manner as that therein they should seek God.

Thus God should still be sought by those that have most of the presence of God, and the greatest degree of the manifestations of his favor and smiles.

So David commanded that it should be in Israel. 1 Chronicles 22:19, “Now therefore set your heart and soul [to seek the Lord your God].” The following reasons may be given why:

(1) Because those that have obtained most are vastly short of the perfection that is to be sought after.

(2) Those that have obtained most of that which is of the right kind, they see most how they fall short.

(3) They have more than others to excite their desires and appetites after perfection.

(4) They are under greater obligations than others to aim at perfection.

(5) They have more light to see their obligation.

(6) God has given 'em more strength and more talents. The more talents [they have] to improve, the more business and the greater work they have, the more to take care of, [and the] more strength [to do so].

(7) We are commanded to “seek the Lord while he may be found,” and

-- 527 --

“to call [upon him while he is near” (Isaiah 55:6)]. He is near in the highest sense.

Obj. Why should persons that have most of the presence of God seek more when some of them have more already than the frame of their bodies can well bare?

Ans. 1. God's people must seek that they may come up to the rule God has given, and leave the care of the frame of the body to the Author of it. When Christ came to the disciples after they had been toiling all night, {at his word they let down the net and enclosed a great multitude of fish (Luke 5:5)}.

[Ans.] 2.5 The great effect on the body is not only owing to the degree of the presence of God, but to many other things that are accidental: the particular manner [of it], the newness [of it], or other circumstances causing surprise. These things are found in the experience of some that have been very much overcome in their bodily strength.

'Tis probable the days are coming when God's people will have vastly more of heaven in their souls, without so great an effect on their bodily frame. That matter may safely be left with God. He does take care; none have yet died.

Thus those that have the presence of God should take great heed to themselves that they don't forsake God, and earnestly to seek him. So did Asa and the children of Judah and Benjamin, according to the word of Azariah in the text. See the ninth verse of the context and onward.

I come now to the

[II.] Second thing proposed, viz. to show that this is the way for those that have the presence of God not to lose [it].

Two things: first, God would not depart from them; second, if not, [they] may expect to lose [it].

1. If these things were observed by those that have much of the presence of God, they would not lose [it]. There is a promise in the text: “[The Lord is with you, while ye be with him; and if ye seek him, he will be found of you].”

I don't intend that it would always be alike with [everyone]. [There is] much more encouragement of the continuance of God's presence to those that have it in the use of these means, than of the bestowment of it on those that have never obtained it— though the latter seldom fail. There is a vast difference. As 'tis in temporal things: [it is] much easier,

-- 528 --

with a care, to preserve a state of health than to recover a broken constitution.

There are innumerable absolute promises, or promises that imply it How many promises [are there] in Scripture of God's hearing prayer, [of his] being found of them that seek him? The promises of giving to him that hath, of preserving his saints, not forsaking them, promises of feeding them that they shall not lack bread, [that] waters shall be found, [and their] wanting no good thing?

God is not a wilderness or a land of darkness. God is a good father, not wanting to his children. Christ is a good shepherd. The prodigal son would not have wanted bread.

There are many promises of increase. “[The righteous shall] flourish like [the] palm tree” [Psalms 92:12], “[Now there was a long war between the house of Saul and the house of David: but David] waxed stronger and stronger, [and the house of Saul waxed weaker and weaker” (2 Samuel 3:1)].

Those that have much of the presence of God han't only far greater advantages to have it still, and have it more abundantly than natural persons have to seek {the presence of God}, but than saints under desertion. [They have] greater strength, greater assistance. God is nigher to 'em. It is a time of favor with them. 'Tis an easy thing for them to keep the presence of God comparatively.

If they don't forsake him, 'tis not his manner to forsake them. Where are the men that have6 had much of the presence of [God], that have lost it and fallen into a cold dark state, unless it was by means of their forsaking God in some of those ways that have been mentioned— giving way to some lust, spiritual pride, worldliness, sloth, or some other hateful [lust]. You that have lost [it], you can't one of you say that you lost it in this way of thus taking earnest heed to keep close to God and still earnestly seeking him.

The three chief reasons why persons' great light and comfort have lasted but a little while, but they have {quickly lost it}, have been these:

(1) Rising of spiritual pride.

(2) Not being sensible of need of vigilance and circumspection at such seasons.

(3) Their coming to a stop in striving, as though now they had apprehended. If persons stop, they won't stop long before they go back. As a

-- 529 --

person's running up a stream: though the stream ben't so strong, yet there is a stream to stop [him], and a strong one, till we get to heaven. 'Tis up hill till we actually get there.

2. If persons that have the presence of God don't attend these things, they may expect to lose God's presence. If you forsake him, he will forsake you. [You] can't expect to have the presence of God any other way than in a way of walking with him. [You can't expect to] have7 the presence of God unless it be in his ways, not in our by— paths. God has given us faculties to be exercised, talents to be improved, ordinances to be used, commands to be attended [to].

Abundant experience shows that, for want of these things, God's people lose the tokens of his presence.

Obj. We can't keep close to God, and seek him when we have his presence, without his help.

Thus persons are ready to lay the blame of their losing {the presence of God} to God.

Answ. 1. There are many things we can do with no more than common assistance.

[Answ.] 2. Those that have the special presence of God, have special assistance present with them. If they will exert themselves as they may do, they can do a great deal more.

[Answ.] 3. If they therefore do these two things— be faithful in using their natural power, and in improving that special assistance they have— God won't leave 'em. When souls lose God's presence, their calamities begin through want of daily using these.

Application.

[Use] I. Hence we may learn the reason why formerly persons' first experiences and comforts used commonly to be the greatest. It is a shameful thing that [it] is so. It is not agreeable to the method laid out in Scripture.

God's people seemed to have been strangely in the dark. God seems heretofore to have hid from the eyes of his people the way of their duty.

[Use] II. Hence those that heretofore had the presence of God and have lost it, may see what they have to repent of and condemn themselves for.

Use III. Of Exhortation to those that have lately been favored with much of the sensible and sweet tokens of the presence of God, to take earnest heed to themselves in those things spoken of in the doctrine.

-- 530 --

First. Take heed you don't forsake God. Here I would particularly request of those that have lately been favored with much of the sweet tokens of God's presence, to hearken to me in the following things that may be for their direction and help to prevent their departing from God:

1. Be sensible you are not out of danger of the devil and a corrupt and deceitful heart, even in your highest flights and best frames. It is a great mistake [to think] that there no danger of going astray because God is near you. Indeed, there is great encouragement in it that you shall be succeeded in a way of vigilance and circumspection. But to think that even then you han't need of the greatest watch and care, and are not in great danger without it, is presumption. 'Tis a presumption by which many herein have been woefully answered. Many persons, through want of a sense of danger in such circumstances, and so neglecting, {depart from God}. 'Tis one principal means of persons' losing the presence [of God]: they have grown secure.

As long as [we are] in this world, we are in a world where Satan reigns, in the enemy's country. [We are] not out of his reach. And though God be willing to defend and protect, yet he expects great care and labor. This whole life is appointed as a state of labor, as a race and battle. 'Tis a great mistake [to think] that the devil can't come in. I have known it in abundance of instances: the devil has come in, even in the midst of the most excellent frames. It may seem a great mystery, but no greater than that the ark should be delivered over,8 [that the Philistines should] come into the temple at Jerusalem and carry 'em into house of their god.

Christ [was] taken and carried into the wilderness immediately after the [descent of the] Holy Ghost. Christ in the heart of saints is liable to [be] carried captive even quickly after the descent of Holy Ghost and [a] voice from heaven saying, “This is my beloved Son.”

Therefore be vigilant, you that live in high degrees of light, you who have lately had the Holy Ghost like a dove [descend upon you]. That9 direction of Christ to his disciples is never out of date in this world, Luke 21:36, “Watch and pray always, that you may be accounted worthy to escape all these things, and to stand”; and that, 1 Peter 5:8, “Be sober, be vigilant.” Ephesians 5:15-17, “walk circumspectly, not as fools, but as wise, redeeming the time, because the days are evil. Wherefore be ye not unwise, but understanding what the will of the Lord is.”

-- 531 --

2.1 Let me particularly urge you to watch exceedingly against spiritual pride.

(1) There is great danger. I know great degrees of the spiritual presence of God tend greatly to restrain and keep down pride. But yet 'tis not all grace. And though in such cases there be much to restrain one way, so there is much to tempt and provoke it another. Temptations in such cases are often exceeding great. To be highly loved and exalted of God tends to feed pride exceedingly, if there be any left. The apostle Paul himself was not out of danger (2 Corinthians 12:7).

Seeing others admire, [and] especially being much resorted to and respected as an eminent saint, better than others, [tends to feed pride].

(2) Consider that no corruption is so apt to work as pride. It was the first sin. [It] lies deepest, [is] most active.

(3) [There is] no corruption that has so many and so secret ways of working. The image of the devil chiefly consists in it. And [there is] no sin so like him for secrecy, subtilty, appearing in so many shapes, coming unsuspected and undiscovered, appearing as an angel of light.

If it is mortified and suppressed in one shape, yet it rises in another. It appears often under the disguise of great humility. It takes occasion to rise from everything, even real grace, yea, real humility.

It is a sin that has many lives. If you kill it, yet it will live still. If you think it is all gone, yet 'tis there still. If you seem to destroy it, yet there it is still.

There are a great many kinds of it, that lie in different forms and shapes, that corrupt the heart and lie one under another, like the coats of an onion; if you pull off one, there is another underneath.

You had need to have the greatest watch imaginable with respect to this matter, and to cry most earnestly to the great searcher of hearts: for he that trusts his own heart is a fool.

(4) Nothing is more hateful and abominable, nothing so contrary to the spirit of the gospel. [Pride is] the sin of the devils.

(5) Nothing is so pernicious in its consequences. The Spirit is very often quenched by it. 'Tis frequently the means of persons' losing the comfortable presence of God. In some that have been exalted high, the consequences have been most woeful. The devil commonly comes in at the door in all the delusions that zealous persons are led away with. [Pride is] me chief root of all the extravagances {we have lately seen}.

Therefore strive that you may be kept humble. Persons are never so safe

-- 532 --

as when kept truly humble and dependent, [as] little children. [And they are] never so much in danger as when spiritual pride rules.

But for your better direction in this matter, I would mention some of the ways of spiritual pride's working:

1.2 When it makes 'em censorious. [It makes 'em] talk in a censorious way, condemning others about [them], forgetting themselves. [It makes 'em] think much about others' deadness more than their own.

Humility makes it natural to esteem others better. Evangelical hypocrites pretend greatly to advance Christ, but are bitter and censorious (Shepard).3

2. [Spiritual pride] makes it seem natural to take a great deal upon 'em, as if they were the persons.

3. It adds fierceness and bitterness to zeal.

4. When persons begin to think they don't need instruction.

5. An unsuitable boldness and confidence. [They] describe what is the way, what is the truly Christian spirit.

The most excellent experience works that way, as experience as well as Scripture shows. [Spiritual pride works in] some that have been carried the highest of any whatsoever.

3.4 Take heed of a gradual degenerating of experiences from spiritual to carnal. There is such a thing. The Apostle speaks of it (Galatians 3:3). I have seen it in abundance of instances: after experiences are come to a great height, they begin to degenerate. [The persons' experiences are] less and less pure and spiritual.

It comes to pass thus in all experiences that Christians have in this world. There is a mixture. There is something natural and something spiritual. It is just like the blasting of fruit through the excess of moisture.5 Through want of distinguishing and want of watchfulness, the natural part increases and the spiritual decreases. The devil ventures the natural part to increase.

There is often a mixture of three things with great discoveries: natural passion, a degree of secret spiritual pride, and imagination. The devil

-- 533 --

works with these. And the person, being insensible of his danger and through ignorance not distinguishing, by and by their great discoveries come to little else but strong impulses, beats of animal spirits, great imaginations. And the person, because he is violently moved, still imagines he has great discoveries, and spiritual pride then is risen to a great height. Long observation confirms me in this.

The6 Apostle probably has respect to something of this nature, 2 Corinthians 11:2-03, “I am jealous over you with godly jealousy: for I have espoused you to one husband, that I may present you as a chaste virgin to Christ. But I fear, lest by any means, as the serpent beguiled Eve through his subtilty, so your minds should be corrupted from the simplicity that is in Christ.” The devil is too subtil for us. Eve thought she was getting higher in knowledge when she was eating the forbidden fruit. [She thought she was] going to be like a god.

Take heed to yourselves, that your experiences don't gradually degenerate, that they may be, on the contrary, more and more pure and unmixed.

4. Consider whereby any of you have formerly departed from God, and so lost his comfortable presence.

5. Remember that a departure from God commonly comes upon persons like a chronical distemper.

6.7 If you would not expose yourself to be led aside by the devil, don't affect to go beyond the rule. [Don't affect to go] beyond the present state. [Don't affect] utterly to root out and abolish all natural affections.8 [Don't affect] to pray no more for near relations than others.9 [They go beyond the rule who] neglect to take care of their families to spend all their time in religion.

7.1 Don't spend much time in disputing about religious offenders. 'Tis none of your business to go about to regulate disorders. Take heed that while you blame others for invading the office of a minister, you don't invade it yourself. I desire you would come to me and talk with me. I claim it as what belongs to me to regulate.

8.2 Let nothing that is said against high transports discourage the highest degree of a spiritual sense. Be filled as full as you will. Let the effect on

-- 534 --

your bodies be what they [will]. Ephesians 5:18, “be not drunk with wine.” Canticles 5:1, “eat, O friends, drink, yea, drink [abundantly].”

9. Help one another in religious conversation. How beautiful [it is] to see a company talking together of [the] things of the glory of Christ. When you meet together accidentally, if your spirits are solemnized by religious conversation, [then] sing praises.

10.3 Don't altogether neglect the duty of secret fasting. There are many special occasions they will find that are greatly engaged in religion. Matthew 6:16-18, “Moreover when ye fast, be not, as the hypocrites, of a sad countenance: for they disfigure their faces, that they may appear unto men to fast. Verily I say unto you, They shall have their reward. But thou, when thou fastest, anoint thine head, and wash thy face; that thou appear not unto men to fast, but unto thy Father which is in secret: and thy Father, which seeth in secret, shall reward thee openly.”

I have now told you the way in which you may have the presence of Christ continued, and may have it more and more. I come now to the

Second branch of this exhortation, to exhort those that have the presence of God to the second thing mentioned {in the doctrine}, viz. earnestly seek God. Thus you that have found Christ should hold Christ fast and not let him go. As the spouse says, Canticles 3 at the beginning, “By night on my bed I sought him whom my soul loveth: I sought him, but I found him not. I will rise now, and go about the city in the streets, and in the broad ways I will seek him whom my soul loveth.”

Use III of Direction to both natural men and Christians.

[First. To] natural men, to keep the common influences of the Spirit of God. [They should not] rest in what they have obtained [or in the] degree of earnestness they have.

Second. To Christians at a sorrowful distance from Christ.

Third. To Christian societies. Beware of false experiences.

1. Imagination. (Shepard.)4

2. Immediate testimony. [It is] without evidence.

Mr. Shepard does abundantly insist on the delusion of such as suppose that they have an absolute promise made to them in particular as the first ground of their faith, and thus get their assurance by way of immediate testimony from heaven, without any reflex act or having any work of the

-- 535 --

Spirit in themselves.5 Men don't know that they are godly by believing that they are godly. We know many things by faith. Hebrews 11:3, “By faith we understand that the worlds were framed by the word of God, so that things which are seen were not made of things which do appear.” “Faith is the evidence of things not seen” (Hebrews 11:1). Thus men know the trinity of persons in the Godhead, that Jesus is the Son of God, that he that believes in him will have eternal life, the resurrection of the dead. And if God should tell a saint that he hath grace, he might know it by believing the word of God. But it is not in this way that godly men know that they have grace. It is not revealed in the Word, and the Spirit of God doth not testify it to particular persons. Some think that the Spirit doth testify it to some, and they ground it on Romans 8:16, “The Spirit itself beareth witness with our spirit, that we are the children of God.” They think the Spirit reveals it by giving an inward testimony to it, and some godly men think they have had experience of it, but they may easily mistake. When the Spirit of God doth eminently stir up a spirit of faith and sheds abroad the love of God in the heart, it is easy to mistake it for a testimony. And that is not the meaning of Paul's words. The Spirit discovers the grace of God in Christ, and thereby draws forth special actings of faith and love; which are evidential, but it doth not work in way of testimony. If God do but help us to receive the revelations in the Word, we shall have comfort enough without new revelations.
