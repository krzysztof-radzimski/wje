Advice to Mr. and Mrs. Kingsley, February 17, 1743 Kingsley Case (all) Jonathan Edwards (with members of Ecclesiastical Council)

Advice to Mr. and Mrs. Kingsley

The following set of documents, all in Edwards’ hand, is the advice of an ecclesiastical council that met on Feb. 16-17, 1743, to hear the case of Hains and Bathsheba Kingsley of Westfield, Massachusetts.Hains Kingsley was born c. 1710, fought in the French and Indian War, and died in 1752; Bathsheba Kingsley died in 1748. The council of Feb. 16-17, 1743, was held at the Kingsley’s house. In his Journal for these dates, Westfield pastor John Ballantine wrote: "16. spent ye day at sergeant Kinsleys ye former part of it in prayer ye after part in hearing [and] considering ye Case with mr Edwards & some others. 17. spent ye former part of ye day at ye same place with ye same abt same business. mr Edwards preacht a lecture fro[m] Heb. 5. 7. 8." (See sermon no. 631.) (Journal of the Rev. John Ballantine, 1737-1774, Westfield Athenaeum, Westfield, Massachusetts.) The Westfield church’s attempts to discipline Bathsheba Kingsley first appear in the official record back in 1741, when, on Oct. 18, she publicly confessed to "breaking 4th 5th & 8th Commandmts in stealing a Horse riding away on the sabbath witht her husbands Consent." Apparently the labors of the council were to no avail, for on March 1, 1743, the congregation voted to bar Bathsheba Kingsley from the Lord’s Supper "on the accot of yr dissatisfaction with her." "The Publick Records of the Church at Westfield," Westfield Athenaeum. Joan B. Ackerman, Reference Librarian of the Westfield Athenaeum, graciously located and provided pertinent entries from the Westfield Church Records. The case is an illustration of the excesses that arose during the Great Awakening. Bathsheba Kingsley claimed to be receiving revelations from God, and thereby justified travelling abroad for extended periods of time and censuring individuals, including her husband. While Bathsheba Kingsley did not claim the full authority of a minister, she nonetheless took upon herself "some parts of the work of the ministry" in her public denunciations and prophecies.On Kingsley as an instance of how women during the revivals of the eighteenth century "stretched the boundaries of traditional ’feminine’ religious behavior to the breaking point," see Catherine A. Brekus, Strangers & Pilgrims: Female Preaching in America, 1740-1845 (Chapel Hill, Univ. of North Carolina Press, 1998), 23-26; quote on p. 26.

The council’s advice, for the most part, is conservative. The ministers tell Bathsheba Kingsley that she should "keep chiefly at home" and observe her station and obligations, namely, being subservient to the commands of her husband and the needs of her family. They also enjoin her to be more circumspect in her behavior and readiness to condemn others as unconverted. But they likewise tell Hains Kingsley to be mindful of his wife’s spiritual needs. He is not to hinder her from attending religious meetings and engaging in Christian conversation with her friends. Also, he is to restrain his temper in consideration of what the council interprets as his wife’s unstable emotional state.

The charged atmosphere of the revival, then at its height, influences the ministers’ advice. Mindful of the complex workings of the Spirit at the time, they seek not to stifle Bathsheba Kingsley’s zeal but to channel it. She is warned of the danger of judging the spiritual state of others, and of supposing "dreams" and "sudden impulses" to be revelations from God. Edwards was doubtless chosen to be a member of this council because of his defense of the revivals and his expertise in the "distinguishing nature of the gracious influences of the Spirit of God." Bathsheba Kingsley’s behavior borders on antinomianism, as she throws off traditional strictures and voices her opinions in an often vindictive manner. One group she singles out for her resentment is the clergy: among the people she reproves are her own ministers.

Since Edwards used unequal-sized scraps of paper that upon first inspection do not appear to belong together, the manuscript is currently in two separate locations within the Andover collection. The first part of the document, half of a folio leaf found in folder 1743.1, is on the back of a letter by Gilbert Tennent, probably written in late 1741 or early 1742.See letter C60; printed in Dwight ed., 1, 153. Tennent was a prominent Presbytertian revivalist based in New Jersey, who, ironically in the context of the Kingsley case, made his reputation through a controversial sermon entitled, The Danger of an Unconverted Ministry (1740). He responds to an inquiry made to him about the appropriateness of lay exhortation and teaching. He denounces the practice as having "dreadful Consequence to ye Chh’s peace & soundness." In the letter of May 10, 1742, to Moses Lyman of Goshen, Connecticut (letter no. A38), JE describes Tennent’s letter (Trask Library) as addressed to "one of the ministers of New England." In his letter to Lyman, JE incorporates Tennent’s advice in admonishing Lyman for "exhorting a public congregation." The second part, located in folder ND1.10b, fills both sides of a quarto-sized scrap. After he had filled this, Edwards returned to a slip of paper, a prayer bid from a Northampton parishioner,The prayer bid reads: "Ebenr Hunt & His wife having heard that their Father and mother Hunt at Lebanon with Two of their Sisters are Sick with the Long Feaver and Some of them (if a Live) dangerously Sick they desire the prayers of Gods people for them." that he had previously folded in half to approximate long duodecimo leaves in order to write rough notes for his draft. He crossed out the bid on the opposite side, inverted the page, and continued. For the final paragraph, Edwards returned to Tennent’s letter, which he turned inverted to make use of a blank space at the bottom of the page.

The fair copy is at the Congregational Library, London, MS II c 1/59. It is two folio leaves, with original signatures by the delegates and ministers of the council.

An accompanying document, meant to precede the council’s advice, is missing. Mention is made of a "foregoing promise," which apparently was a covenant to which both Mr. and Mrs. Kingsley consented.

It is to be noted that the foregoing promiseApparently a separate document, perhaps a covenant, the "foregoing promise" has not been located. was entred into and subscribed by Mrs Kingsley after a great deal of pains taken, very fully to explain it to her, that she might understand the import of it, & also after particular Care taken that she might not entangle her Conscience by it, being told that if she was already so fully determin’d that her former Practice was what God required of her, and was so far from doubt about it that she could not refer it to others advice; then she could not make this Promise with a Good Conscience. The Promise also was made after a great deal of Time given her for deliberation: And at last she declared she did it with great Freedom & Cheerfullness, and with all her Heart, or to that Purpose. And it is also to be noted that she had more than a week before this, entred into and sign’d a firm Promise to abide by the Judgment and Advice of the same Persons, in the same matters.

Mr Kingsley and his wife having thus promised, we who are the Persons to whom the matter was refer’d, after seeking to God by Fasting & Prayer for divine Help in this difficult affair, and that God would enlighten and direct us, so that we might know his mind, and Judge and advise agreably to his will, and so as should be most for their Benefit & Comfort, and after a long and very particular hearing of all that Mr Kingsley & his wife desired to say to open their Case, and much particular Enquiry; also having before, of a long time been well acquainted with their Circumstances and difficulties: we Judge and advise as follows.

We Judge that the fores’d Mrs Kingsley has for many years been greatly out of the way of her duty. And as to the Cause of it we Judge that, together with a weak vapoury Habit of Body, and want of some proper conduct and advice in the beginning of her difficulty, and Satan’s taking the Advantage, it has been owing chiefly to these following things:

1. Ignorance of the distinguishing nature of the gracious Influences of the spirit of God, which are upon the Heart giving a sense of divine things there, and exciting answerable affections, dispositions & holy Joys, and not by exciting imaginations in the Head. From whence it has come to pass that Mrs Kingsley has for some years been almost wholly under the Govern ment of a series of dreams, imaginations and sudden Impressi-[L. 1v.] ons & Impulses, supposing them to be immediate Revelations from Heaven, and so has been perpetually hurried and driven to and fro, without any Rest.

2. Want of a thorough Knowledge of her self, and being emptied of herself, & want of that Humility that disposes persons to think others better than themselves; whence she has been apt to think her Knowledge to be very eminent & superiour to that of others, and so has been apt to think that she was a proper sense Person to be improved to do some great thing in the Church of God, & that in the Exercise of s doing some parts of the work of the ministry: whence also she has great ly trusted in her self, and ready to look upon it that the Interest of Zion depended on her, and that if she was not improv’d its Cause would sink, at least in this Town; so that she has neglected her proper Business, and gone quite out of her Place to promote Religion: whence also her Behaviour has of late, in general been, exceeding contrary to that Christian Gentleness & meekness that becomes a follower of the Lamb of God, especially in her treatment of her Husband.

3. To a want of having her will subdued to God, and not complying with that great Christian duty of selfdenial, and resigning her self up wholly to God; whence her soul has been for many years in a continual Tumult, like the sea in a storm; being destitute of that Peace and Rest in God that many other Christians enjoy; and also in a great measure destitute of Joy & Thankfullness for the great things God has lately done for his People.

And with regard to Mrs. Kingsley’s particular Conduct, we Judge she has exceedingly departed from the way of her duty in the following things, viz. In very greatly throwing off the Care of her Husbands House and Family, being great part of her time gone from home, almost continually wandring about from House to House, and very frequently to other Towns under a notion of doing Christ’s work, and delivering his Messages; And undertaking sometimes to go and reprove & warn her own ministers, and denounce God’s Judgments against them, under a notion of delivering messages from God to them; and often disobeying her her Husband in going abroad contrary to his commands; and often stealing away privately, and taking her Husband’s Horse to go away to other Towns Contrary to his mind, & sometimes unwarrantably taking Neighbours Horses: And in making almost a Constant Business of talking against others wherever she goes freely, at all times, & before[L. 2r.] all Persons sorts of Persons, Censuring others, declaring her Judgment and suspicions of others, as to the state of their sous. It appears to us that her Conversation has for some years, chiefly consisted in setting forth others vileness & wickedness, and above all other Persons whatsoever, talking and inveighing against her Husband, and also in commonly talking in a very severe, harsh and reflecting manner to her Husband, before the rest of the Family & others, without any restraint, or any manner of Appearance of the duty that a wife owes to her Husband; And also sometimes reflecting on the dead, in a manner that was very p improper, & could tend to no Benefit. And it seems to us that Mrs Kingsley has of late almost wholly cast off that modesty, shamefacedness, sobriety meekness, diligence and submission, that becomes a Christian woman in her Place.

As to her Husband’s behaviour towards his wife, we Judge that under his great provocation he has considerably [-] governed his spirit; But notwithstanding, the Heat of spirit he has sometimes shown and his manner of expressing it towards his wife, we don’t think is to be Justified but Condemned.

Upon the whole we would give the following advice to Mrs Kingsley.

1. That she henceforwards keep chiefly at home, & let her main Business be within doors, within her husbands house, serving God there, and taking Care of the Family & houshold affairs, being diligent therein; And that she go no more abroad as following such impulses & impressions as she has followed hitherto, under a notion of delivering messages from God to one and another, or as being sent by God.

2. That she submit to her Husband in meekness, & Humility, and Honour him as her Head, and be obedient to him, & not go a- broad when he disallows it.

3. That she don’t commonly or frequently talk against her husband. Not that we would wholly restrain her from opening any Family difficulties, to some wise Judicious Persons, for their Counsel, if there be a necessity of it; but not to make a business of talking against her Husband, nor to do it to many Persons, & not to speak openly of his against him.

4. Not to talk to her & reprove him with rough & severe Language, and especially before others; but if she reproves that it be privately in an humble submissive loving manner.

5. That for the time to come, she don’t Judge & censure others that are visible Christians in good standing in the Christian Church, as to their spiritual state, commonly or openly, or so as shall tend to make[L. 2v.] her Censures publick.

6. That her Conversation on religious subjects be not for the future much made up of talk about, & against Opposers, and those that she looks on as the Enemies of Religion, but other things that tend more to her own & others Edification.

7. When she is in distress for the souls of others, to give vent to it chiefly by pouring out her soul before God for them; not that we would prohibit her from taking opportunities as providence shall give them, without prejudice to the Business of her station, prudently & humbly to counsel exhort & intreat others.

8. We advise that she be ready to humble her self before God and men for her foremention’d Errours, & to Particularly to the Church to which she belongs, if they require it, & so with regard to such particulars as they think are scandalous. In doing these things we think she will best fullfill the vow she made to God some years Past.

And to her Husband we give the following advice, viz. That notwithstanding all the Provocations his wife has offer’d, or may offer him, he govern his spirit & don’t suffer his Passions to rise, and treat her with Patience & Gentleness, without ha Blows or Hard words, or using any kind of violence, unless by further advice given by ministers that may Judge the Extremity of the Case hereafter to require it; and that he treat his wife notwithstanding all her ill treatment of him with Love and Tenderness, providing well for her outward Comfort, & the Comfort of her mind, by giving her reasonable Liberty to go to religious meetings in the Town, & sometimes to Lectures out of Town when if she desires it, & his circumstances will well allow of it, & also all Liberty of Religious Conversation with Christian Friends, that may at his own House, & that she ben’t wholly confined from her Liberty of visiting Christian neighbours & Brethren for mutual Edification.

And we advise that past injuries be forgotten & buried on both sides, & that henceforward both of them resolve with the utmost watchfullness, to maintain mutual Peace and harmony, each one striving to do the duty of their station & Relation, & all duties of Kindness & Benevolence towards each other, earnestly crying to God for his help that they may be enabled so to do.

And we advise Mr Kingsley if his wife should still persist in her former Practice, notwithstanding what has now been done, to make their Promise, & this advice Known in the neighbour Towns, as he shall see occasion, that others may hereafter the better Know how to conduct themselves towards her.

Westfield Febry 17th 1742/43

Mr. Kingsley and his wife having thus promised, we who are the persons to whom the matter was referred, after seeking to God by fasting and prayer for the divine help in this difficult affair, and that God would enlighten and direct us so that we might know his mind, judge and advise agreeable to his will, and so as should be most for their benefit and future comfort; and after a long and very particular hearing all that Mr. K[ingsley] and his w[ife] desired to say to open their case, and much particular inquiry; and also having before of a long time been well acquainted with their circumstances and difficulties, we judged and advised as follows.

We judge that the foresaid Mrs. K[ingsley] has for many years been greatly out of the way of her duty; and as to the cause of it, we judge that, together with a weak, vapory habit of body and want of some proper conduct and advice in the beginning of her difficulty, and Satan’s taking the advantage, it has been owenObscure form of "owing." chiefly to these three things:

1. Ignorance of the distinguishing nature of the gracious influences of the Spirit of God which are upon the heart,MS draft: "hearts of the saints." giving a sense of the excellency and truth of divine things and exciting answerable dispositions and holy affections and joys in the hearts of his children; and not by exciting imaginations in their heads, from whence it has come to pass that Mrs. K[ingsley] has for many years in her conduct been almost wholly under the government of a series of dreams and imaginations and sudden impulses, supposing them to be immediate revelations from heaven, and so has been perpetually harried and driven to [and] fro without any rest.

2. Want of a thorough knowledge of herself and being sufficiently emptied of herself, and want of that humility that disposes persons to think others better than themselves. Whence she has been apt to think her knowledge to be very eminent and superior to that of others, and so hasMS draft: "as." been apt to think that [she] was a proper person to be improved for some great thing in the church of God, and that in the exercise of some parts of the work of the ministry. Whence also she has greatly trusted in herself, and ready to look upon it that the interest of Zion depended upon her, and that if she was not improved its cause would sink--at least in this town--so that she has neglected her proper business and gone quite out of her place to promote religion. Whence also her behavior has in general been exceeding contrary to that Christian gentleness and meekness that becomes a follower of the Lamb of God, especially in the treatment of her husband.

3. To a want of having her will subdued to God, and not complying with that great Christian duty of self-denial and resigning herself up wholly to God. Whence her soul has been for many years in a continual tumult, like the sea in a storm, being destitute of that peace and rest in God that other Christians enjoy, and also in a great measure destitute of all joy and thankfulness in the great things God has lately done for his people.

And with regard to Mrs. Kingsley’s particular conduct, we judge she has exceedingly departed from the way [of] her duty in the following things: in very greatly throwing offMS draft: "of." the care of her husband’s house and family, being great part of her time gone from home, almost perpetually wandering about from house to house, and very frequently to other towns, under a notion of doing Christ’s work and delivering his messages; and undertaking sometime to go reprove and warn her own ministers and denounce the judgments of God against ’em under a notion of delivering messages from God to ’em, often disobeying her husband’s commands in going abroad and often stealing away privately and taking her husband’s horse to go away to other towns contrary to his mind, and sometimes have probably taken the neighbors’ horses; and in making almost a constant business of talking against others wherever she goes, freely at all times and before all sorts of persons censuring others, declaring her judgment and suspicions of others as to their souls’ state. It appears to us that her conversationMS draft: "conversion." has for some years chiefly consisted [of] setting forth others’ vileness and wickedness, and above all other persons whatsoever, talking and inveighing against her husband; and also in commonly talking in a very severe, harsh and reflecting manner to her husband before the rest of the family and others, without any restraint or any manner of appearance of the duty that a wife owes to her husband; and also sometimes reflecting on the dead in a manner that was very improper and tending to no benefit.MS draft: "& could tend(ing) to no Benefit." Probably a reference to the Rev. Nehemiah Bull, who died April 12, 1740, since the council had just condemned Bathsheba Kingsley’s habit of reproving and warning her own ministers. And it seems to us that Mrs. Kingsley has of late almost wholly cast off that modesty, shamefacedness, and sobriety and meekness, diligence and submission, that becomes a Christian woman in her place.

As to her husband’s behavior towards his wife, we judge that under his great provocations he has much governed himself and restrained his passions; but notwithstanding, the heat of spirit he has sometimes shown, and his manner of expressing it towards his wife, we don’t think it to be justified but condemned.

Upon the whole we would give the following advice to Mrs. K[ingsley]:

1. That she henceforward keep chiefly at home and let her main business be within doors within her husband’s house, serving God there and taking care of the family and household affairs, and being diligent therein; and she go no more abroad, as following such impulses and impressions as she has followed hitherto under a notion of delivering messages from God to one and another, or going because God sent her.

2. That she submit to her husband in meekness and modesty, and honor him as her head, and be obedient to him and not go abroad when he disallows it.

3. That she don’t commonly or frequently talk against her husband. Not that we would restrain her from opening any family difficulties to some wise and judicious persons for their counsel, if there should be a necessity of it; but not to make a business of talking against her husband, nor to do it to many persons and not to speak openly against him.

4. Not to talk to him and reprove him with rough and severe language, and especially before others. But if she reproves him, let it be privately and in an humble, submissive, loving manner, becoming one in so near a relation that, above all others, requires love and endearment.

5. That for the time to come, she don’t judge and censure others that are visible Christians in good standing in the Christian Church as to their spiritual state, commonly or openly, or so as shall tend to make her censures public.

6. That her conversation on religious subjects ben’t for the future much made up of talk about and against opposers and those that she looks on [as] the enemies of religion, but things that more tend to her own and others’ edification.

7. When she hasMS draft: "as." distress for the good of others, to give vent to it chiefly by pouring out her soul before God and crying to the compassionate Savior for his pity to them. Not that we would prohibit her from taking opportunities as providence gives them, without prejudice to the business of her station prudently and humbly to counsel, exhort and intreat others.

And to her husband we give the following advice:

1. That notwithstanding all the provocations his wife has or may offer him, that he govern his spirit and don’t suffer his passions to rise, and treat her with patience and gentleness, without hard words or blows, or any kind of violence used, without further advice of ministers that may judge the extremity of the case hereafter to requite it.

That he treat his wife, notwithstanding all her ill treatment of him, with love and tenderness, providing well for her outward comfort and the comfort of her mind by giving reasonable liberty to go to religious meetings in this town and to lectures to neighbor towns if she desires it, when his circumstances will well allow it; and also all liberty of Christian conversation with Christian friends that may come to converse with her at his own house, and that she ben’t wholly confined from all liberty of visiting Christian neighbors and brethren for mutual edification.

And we advise that all past injuries be forgotten and buried on both sides, and that henceforward both of them take up a resolve with the utmost watchfulness to maintain mutual peace, harmony and love for each one, striving to do the duty of their station and relation and all duties of mutual kindness and benevolence towards each other, crying earnestly to God for his help, that they may be enabled so to do.

And we advise Mr. Kingsley, if his wife should still persist in her former practice notwithstanding her promise and this advice, to endeavor to make their promise and this consequent advice known in the neighboring towns as he shall see occasion, that others may hereafter the better know how to conduct themselves towards her.
