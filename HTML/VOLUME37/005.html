NOTE TO "BOOK OF MINUTES ON THE ARMINIAN CONTROVERSY" (GAZETEER NOTEBOOK)

In "Efficacious Grace, Book I," Edwards calls this "Book of Minutes on the Arminian Controversy." In all likelihood, it is part of Edwards' collection of anti-Arminian materials, for here he gathers arguments to refute Arminian notions of justification, the decrees of election and reprobation, freedom of will, and original sin. The latter part of the notebook is devoted largely to notes on Whitby's Discourse on the Five Points and Taylor's Original Sin. Stylistically speaking, the book is a good example of Edwards' notetaking, consisting of memoranda to himself, short, choppy statements, and telegraphic prose with key statements that would trigger larger discussions in his mind.

*****

The manuscript is composed of issues of The Daily Gazeteer of London (thus the subtitle), cut up and stitched into a long, narrow notebook with blank margins outermost. As such, it is a fine example of Edwards' frugality. Its cover is badly tattered, hence the title at the top is difficult to read. Edwards appears to have numbered the book "VII" (cf. with notebooks on "Efficacious Grace"). Two dates visible on mastheads are Feb. 18 and Feb. 20, 1744, which provide a terminus a quo. Other dates are obliterated by clipping or stitching. Pages are numbered 1-18, 21-28, 31-32, 33-34. The leaf containing pp. 37-38 is at Princeton and published (misidentified as sermon notes and poorly transcribed) in an article in The Princeton University Library Chronicle.1 A number of entries are drawn through with vertical line, indicating JE may have used them elsewhere.

FAITH. JUSTIFICATION. Show wherein [consists] the grand and important difference between the orthodox faith concerning justification, and the opinion of Arminians and all neonomians and all opinions holding any legal way of justification. Not that our being justified depends on something that is in us, or something that we do; and so, in Scripture language, "some work of ours" (for faith is so). Not that something that we do, or some act or work of ours, properly interests us or is the proper condition of an interest in Christ. But it is the manner of its interesting us. It is [not] whether faith, or any other acts or works of ours, interests us as our good works, or by virtue of the goodness and amiableness of the act or work; but whether it be only by virtue of the relation it bears to Christ as a proper receiving of him. He that is orthodox holds that our holy practice interests us only this way, viz. as it is an expression of our receiving Christ. The others hold that faith justifies only by virtue of its loveliness. One holds that our holy works justify only as expressions of faith; the other holds that faith justifies only as an holy work.

The difference is not whether works, with respect to anything in them, do justify: for all that hold that [we are] justified by faith, hold that a work with respect to something that is in it does justify, because faith is a work. And 'tis no more legal or against the free grace of God to suppose that many works justify than one; but that one work does justify as a good work. No more does holy practice.

Mention this in the stating of the question.

Show that the difference is vastly great.

Show particularly in what respects the holy Scripture and orthodox divines seek to magnify free grace in the doctrine of justification, not being merited by benefiting God, or by goodness and amiableness, to balance by amiableness, to attract, etc.

These are the ways, and all the ways, that can be devised.
