Note to Fragment of a Notebook on the Signs of Grace Notes on Signs (HSP) Jonathan Edwards

NOTE TO FRAGMENT OF A NOTEBOOK ON THE SIGNS OF GRACE

The following document is a fragment of a notebook, devoted mainly to the signs of grace and related topics, consisting of five octavo leaves. Together, the fugitive leaves form pages 9-17 of the notebook. The first leaf of the notebook to which these leaves once belonged is apparently the one at Jonathan Edwards College, Yale University.1 Beyond the content, there are few clues on which to make assumptions about the disassembled notebook. Originally it was of a considerable length, since at one point in the extant leaves there is a reference to "p. 42." Also, the entries are not deleted with vertical lines, Edwards' usual manner of indicating he had used a given passage or that it was no longer of interest to him. The handwriting of the leaves dates from the 1740s. We can speculate that this may have been one of a series of notebooks, of which "No. 7" is extant, that Edwards drew up for writing Religious Affections.

*****

Fugitive leaves (pp. 9-17) from a notebook on Religious Affections. N.d. [c. 1745-46]. HSP, Dreer Autograph Coll., 126:1, American Clergy, v. III (3 leaves); Etting Collection, 26, p. 36 (1 leaf).

LIVING ON FRAMES. Doubting of a thing may arise from unbelief when it comes from a sight of abundance of corruption in the heart. For this may be when persons are far from being in a carnal frame.6

Faith also is the first exercise of grace by which they must emerge out of a dead frame. They must look to Christ for help from their darkness and deadness and blindness, as the poor blind man. So it is their duty to believe and trust in Christ to be delivered from darkness.

Another evidence that discoveries or a ;scSENSE OF GODS LOVE;rt is not the primary foundation of holy dispositions and affections, or not that wherein grace does fundamentally consist, is that a person may be without this and yet be perfectly holy, yea, in the highest and most exalted and supreme exercises of holiness, as Christ on the cross.

LOVING FOR EXCELLENCY. If there be any such thing as their being beautiful and lovely in themselves otherwise than as tending to an interest, then it will follow that they may and ought to be loved from that excellency. For if there be such a beauty, then it may be seen as it is; and if it be seen as it is, it will appear beautiful and amiable in itself, as it is, and consequently will be delighted in and loved: for that is implied in its appearing beautiful.

INFINITE OBLIGATION. Observe the difference between being infinitely obliged to a thing and being obliged to an infinite thing.

PRACTICAL EXERTIONS. The highest sign is grace, as carried forth into its practical exertions or into the fruit that is produced in practice.

BEAUTY OF HOLINESS. See 2 Chronicles 20:21.

SPIRITUAL TASTE.7 Objection concerning the approbation there is in natural men of virtue, etc.

In answer, to show that the natural approbation of men's consciences of virtue, and condemnation of vice, is a thing entirely distinct from the relish that is in a good man of virtue. As appears by this: that at the same time that this testimony of conscience will be in its greatest perfection, viz. at the day of judgment, wicked men will be furthest from that relish; and hatred of holiness and a wicked taste and inclination will be in its greatest perfection.

NATURAL MEN'S LOVE TO VIRTUE. The love of natural men is as their abhorrence is. A natural man abhors the sight of a rattlesnake; it is an unpleasant sight to him: which is only because he habitually connects it with the idea of hurtfulness to him. So he hates vice no other way, and loves many kind of virtues in the same, when all is originally to be resolved into self-love. There is this, and also the testimony of his conscience concerning the punishableness of vice and rewardableness of virtue. The testimony of a man's conscience of the punishableness of a thing is a different thing from a sense of the odiousness of it; and the testimony of the conscience of the rewardableness of [virtue] is different from a sense of the beauty {of virtue}. This is manifest in what will be at the day of judgment. And besides, there is a kind of association or habitual connection by education.

PRACTICE. In Psalms 105, last verse, after rehearsing over God's wondrous works that he had wrought for Israel, this is, in the fine, mentioned as God's aim in all: "That they might observe his statutes, and keep his laws." See p. 42.8

PRACTICE. Making Christ the minister of sin and improving free redemption as an encouragement to sin. Jeremiah 7:9-10, "Will ye steal, murder," etc. "and come and stand before me in this house, which is called by my name, and say, We are delivered to do all these abominations?"

The end of the JUDGMENT of the Last Day will be [to] make persons manifest, not only to the world but to their own CONSCIENCES. Jude 1:14-15, "Behold, the Lord cometh with ten thousands of his saints, to execute judgments upon all, and to convince all that are ungodly among them of all their ungodly deeds which they have ungodly committed, and of all their hard speeches which ungodly sinners have spoken against him."

EVANGELICAL HUMILIATION. Isaiah 46:12, "Hearken to me, ye stouthearted, that are far from righteousness." Psalms 119:21, "Thou hast rebuked the proud that are cursed, which do err from thy commandments."

True saints are of a MERCIFUL spirit. The word commonly used in the Old Testament for saints is Kasidim. See Psalms 116:15 and other places in Buxtorf.9

PRACTICE. He that gives his heart to Christ must needs give his body to him. It would be as absurd to suppose the heart and outward practice, soul and body, to be separated in the true and faithful spouse of Christ, so that she should indeed give her heart to him but serve other lovers with her body, as the heart and body in a temporal spouse, so that she should be indeed true and faithful in her heart and yet give her body to other lovers. Would not all the world call that man a fool, that should regard the pretenses of his wife that pretended that she was constant and true in her conjugal affections to him, and yet lead the practice of a whore?

PRACTICE. Proverbs 14:2, "He that walketh in his uprightness feareth the Lord: but he that is perverse in his ways despiseth him."

BOLDNESS for CHRIST. When the angels of God came to Jacob, and he called the name of the place "Mahanaim," i.e. "two armies," the host of angels was one army, and Jacob's train going against Esau and his host of persecuters of the church was the other army, representing the church militant; and as manner of warning against enemies and persecuters, they encounter Esau and his 400 men, not with swords and bloody weapons but with liberal presents, with great kindness, with respect, with love and friendliness and humble respect, and with entreaties, and with prayers and wrestlings with God.

PRACTICE. HUMILIATION. Proverbs 26:16, "The sluggard is wiser in his own conceit than seven men that can render a reason."

How this is true in matters of religion. How there is a sort of religion and pretended high experience and great affections that don't issue in a diligent and laborious constant practice of religion, but in settled slothfulness and neglect of the difficult parts of Christian practice that yet exceedingly lifts men up with a high conceit of their own goodness and high experience, and causes 'em to look on themselves vastly better than others, and to look down with contempt on them that are real, humble, modest Christians.

HUMILIATION. SPIRITUAL PRIDE. Proverbs 26:12, "Seest thou a man wise in his own conceit? there is more hope of a fool than of him."
