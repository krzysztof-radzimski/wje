1129. PROBATION.

Question: Since Christ has been in a state of probation and has passed through a time of trial in behalf of the elect, why is it requisite that they should be in a state of probation after him? What need of a state of trial in this world to try whether man will here accept of Christ and close with his redemption? 'Tis doubtless fit that, in order to men's interest in Christ and partaking of the benefits of his redemption, their hearts should be united to him. But why was it requisite that this union should be begun in this world? Why would it not be sufficient if persons should have their souls united to Christ after death, and should be brought by the power and grace of God to close with the way of salvation by him in another world? Why must the beginning of this union of soul to Christ needs be in the present life?

Ans. 1. 'Tis fit that some bounds should be set, some limitation of the day of grace, and not that the door of mercy should stand open to rebels and enemies and obstinate despisers of Christ and grace forever and ever, so that converting grace might be given and the hearts of Christ's enemies brought to an union with him at any time, indifferently throughout the endless ages of eternity. For this is inconsistent with a proper state of punishment. A state of probation and a state of retribution don't well consist together. As long as a day of grace lasts, sinners are not judged; they are not visibly given over to punishment and misery by the sentence of the judge. After a sentence to the deserved punishment, which is eternal punishment, there can be [no] possibility of obtaining an interest in a Savior. As long as a door of hope stands open, men are not in a state of eternal death, for such a light of hope is inconsistent with that shadow of death, that blackness of darkness. But if there be any such thing as a limited day of mercy, it seems manifest that 'tis most fit it should be in this world, in this intermediate state between extreme happiness and misery, and not either in heaven or hell, and therefore that the limits of it should be the same with the limits of life.

2. If upon what has been already said it should be asked: What need of any day of mercy at all, antecedent to the state of retribution? Why could not the elect have been first created in heaven in a state of perfect holiness and union of heart with Christ their Redeemer, and the reprobates have their first existence in hell in a state of punishment, perfect misery and utter despair? I answer: If men had first been created in this manner in another world, they never would have been the posterity of Adam, for they would not have descended from him by natural generation and so would not have been partakers of his sin, and so would not have been fallen creatures and would not have been justly exposed to punishment, and, therefore, could not have been created in hell in a state of perfect

-- 502 --

misery. And they would have stood in no need of a Savior and, therefore, could not have had any interest in one, nor their hearts united to Christ as their Savior. There could have been no redemption of the elect, either fundamental or actual. There could be no fundamental salvation of them or impetration and purchase of their redemption, for they would have had no guilt to be atoned for, no wrath to be appeased or revenging justice to be satisfied. There could be no actual salvation of them by bringing them out of a sinful, miserable state into a holy and happy state, for they never would have been in a sinful, miserable state. If there be not [a] state of trial, what are men born into this world for? Why not immediately created in another world? And if men had, after the fall, never been born into this world, then there would have been no such thing as the race of mankind on earth, no successive generation, no such thing as the natural birth; and, if so, Christ him [self] could not have properly assumed the human nature. He could not have been born or have been of the race of mankind. Yea, mankind must immediately after the fall have ceased to be here below, and so this world of mankind must have come totally to an end as soon as man fell. And then, how should Christ come into the world to save sinners? How should the Word become flesh, appear in the form of sinful flesh and be in a state of humiliation, and in such a state obey and suffer?

3. The Lord Jesus Christ and the grace of God would [have] failed of the glory that they have by saving those who have personally been sinful and exceeding unworthy of mercy and deserving of wrath and eternal misery.

4 'Tis fit that the elect church and spouse of Christ should accept of him as her Savior and trust in him for salvation before she actually has salvation by him; that the benefit should visibly be consequent on this acceptance, trust and unition of heart; that the dependence of the benefit on this act of the heart may be visible. Very much of the wisdom of God and the evidence of his moral perfection in the way of salvation consists in this connection and dependence. Herein lies much of the suitableness and glory of the way of salvation. And therefore 'tis fit that this connection should be made visible in God's actual dispensation of salvation and, therefore, that the benefit should follow the act, and not that men should have their first existence in heaven in the enjoyment of the purchased reward, or should have this reward given at the same moment that a spirit of acceptance and faith is given; yea, that they should be in heaven, the paradise of God purchased by Christ in the order of nature, before any act of acceptance of Christ. For creation is the first thing in the order of

-- 503 --

nature that belongs to a creature. Being is before action, and before active acceptance of Christ. For they would have the method and means of the procurement of their happiness after they have begun to enjoy the eternal life and glory purchased, which must be before the heart's active closing with this method and means of happiness. 'Tis fit that it should appear that the glorious reward is consequent on faith in Christ and dependent on it. And therefore, 'tis fit that Christ should be sought to for the reward and that men should come to him and trust in him for it as a thing that remains to be obtained.

5. It pleases God that faith should be in that sense the condition of salvation, that it should be the ground of the distinction made in the consequent state of men, the ground of the distinction between those that are saved and those that perish, those that are happy and those that are miserable. The fitness of the way of salvation, and the beauty of the method of God's dealings with fallen man consequent on the giving a Savior, appears much in his making that great difference among fallen men in bestowing eternal life on such as believe and leaving those eternally to perish who believe not. But the receiving or rejecting Christ could be no ground of this distinction if the elect were first created in heaven and reprobates in hell, for then the difference of their state would be antecedent to [the] difference between them consisting in faith and unbelief.

6. By faith in Christ being exercised before the receiving the reward, believers are prepared for the reward, to receive it sensibly, as from Christ, and to give him the glory of it. In this way they receive it from Christ and as the consequence of their applying to him for it the more sensibly to themselves.

7. If the elect should be first created in the possession of the purchased paradise, there would be no opportunity for those particular exercises of faith in the Redeemer wherein much of the fitness of it to an interest in the Savior and his redemption consists, and which peculiarly tend to make his redemption precious and to give him the proper glory which belongs to him as a Savior. For there would be no opportunity for any proper conviction of sin, for the elect would never be sensible of any sin in themselves, for they never would have sin but would be perfectly sinless in the beginning, and so never would by sense and experience be made sensible of their need of a Savior, nor ever would come to Christ in the proper exercises of repentance and humiliation. Nor would there be any opportunity to honor Christ by the proper exercises of that disposition to prefer Christ to all other things which is contained in true faith. This disposition

-- 504 --

could not be exercised and manifested under its proper trials. Persons would have no opportunity to come to him so as to forsake all for him, to go and sell all for the pearl of great price; no opportunity to receive Christ with his cross as well as with his crown; no opportunity to close with Christ and his religion with great labors and sufferings, as sitting down and counting the cost.

8. If man never were in a state of probation under the offers of the Savior, Christ never would have that honor, put upon him by God, that men are required to receive him on pain of God's peculiar wrath. There would be no punishment, nor threatening of punishment, for sinners not accepting Christ. By the terrible punishment of the sin of unbelief and sins against the gospel, God exalts and magnifies the Redeemer and his redemption, tending to impress the minds of saints and angels with a great sense of Christ's high dignity and the exceeding greatness and value of his redeeming grace and love.

9. If men should be born into the world and live here under the offers of Christ, and yet an interest in Christ were not only to [be] obtained in this world but before death and after death indifferently, and this were known, this would vastly encourage men's delay of repentance and turning from sin to God through Christ, and living boldly in the neglect of Christ and of the means of an interest in him, and would exceedingly encourage them to give themselves up to their own lusts.

10. If men should be born into this world, and here was no such thing as the offer of a Savior, or a probation whether they would accept of him or use any means to obtain Christ's benefits, but it was known that those that were to enjoy Christ's benefits were to be sanctified and have their souls united to Christ after death, it would take off almost all restraint on men's lust, make Christ nothing but the minister of sin to embolden them in wickedness and a means in that respect5 of turning earth into hell.

11. 'Tis fit that Christ, that was appointed to bruise the serpent's head and destroy the works of the devil, should conquer Satan in his own territories; that he should be overthrown in Christ's actual salvation, wherein he rescues men from his power on the same stage where he seemed to conquer; that he should be destroyed by the second man and head of men in the same world where he destroyed the first head of men; and that the Redeemer should set up his kingdom of grace and salvation in the same world where Satan hath set up his kingdom of sin and misery, that Christ's kingdom should be built on the ruins of Satan's kingdom.

-- 505 --

12. 'Tis fit that Christ should be glorified and exalted here in the same stage whereon he was humbled and suffered; that as he was exhibited in meanness and disgrace as a servant under the dominion of his enemies, men and devils, who exercised their dominion and tyranny over him, as it were, in his destruction as their subject and malefactor, so that here he should reign and vanquish his enemies and they should be made his footstool; that as he suffered in the world, in Satan's territories, and by the malignant influences of his domination, so here he should erect a glorious kingdom on the ruins of that dominion of the devil that he so suffered by; and as he suffered as a subject of the kingdom of men, the Roman Empire, the chief monarchy that ever was set up among men, so he should set up a kingdom here on the ruins of that kingdom and over all other kingdoms and nations of mankind.

13. If the application of redemption were not in this world, there would not properly have been any such thing as a conquest of Satan in this application in the conversion of sinners, in the preservation of a saint, and carrying on a work of grace to glory against the utmost power and opposition of Satan. For if the application of redemption were first in heaven, there Satan has nothing to do, and never comes, and has no opportunity to make any opposition. And the glory of Christ's power would have failed in so irresistibly conquering sin, Satan and the world, in beginning, upholding and carrying on the work of grace in the hearts of saints.

14. As Satan set up a kingdom in this world by his subtlety, and by the same means has all along promoted it, so God saw meet that his Son should set up a kingdom, and maintain and promote it in opposition to Satan's kingdom in the exercise [of] wonderful wisdom, that should appear to be infinitely above Satan's wisdom, baffling and confounding him in his most subtle devices, taking him in his own snare, leading him into the pit that he has digged. But occasion and opportunity for this would have failed if the application of redemption were begun in heaven, for then this application would be brought without means wisely to be ordered and disposed, and where Satan has nothing to do, and the marvelous wisdom of God—appearing in his dispensations towards his [church],6 the methods of his providence, and of the operations of his Spirit in carrying on the great designs of his kingdom in the world—never would have been seen.

15. God saw fit that sanctification itself and the whole of salvation, excepting the very first act of faith, should be given as the consequence of

-- 506 --

faith, and as dependent on it as the condition of all. But if the elect were created in a state of perfect holiness in heaven, this could not have been.

16. If there had [been] no state of probation appointed to precede the reward, there could [not] have been a diversity of degrees of glory established by any such rule (wonderfully manifesting the wisdom of God) as that which now takes place, viz. that the future glory of saints shall be greater or less according to the degrees of holiness and good works and fruits of righteousness, to the glory of God and good of fellow creatures in [an] antecedent state of probation.

17. If union to Christ and sanctification were first begun in heaven, then would have failed those wonderful manifestations of the freeness, independence, riches and immutability of the grace and love of Christ—and his faithfulness in his covenant—that are by the mercy he shows 'em under all their infirmities and failings, unsteadiness and ingratitude, after their conversion.

18. The elect being first in this world in a state of sin and misery—of which they are convinced by the Spirit—and by the experience they have of sin and suffering after conversion, are prepared for a state of perfect holiness and glory. By the knowledge of evil they have the greater and better knowledge of good, and are more sensible of the beauty of holiness, and the value of happiness, and the greatness of the benefit of salvation from sin and misery, and the value and riches of God's grace in bestowing these benefits upon them, who have such experience of their unworthiness and such a taste of what misery they deserve. Experience and sense of sin and misery serves to humble the soul and so to fit it for exaltation.

19. The saints, by the state that they are in in this world and the work they have here to do, are fitted and trained up for the purchased reward. Humbling and self-emptying virtues are exercised (and the sense and habit fixed), such as humility, submission, meekness, patience, forgiveness and obedience to a law properly so called, i.e. commands enforced by threatenings of God's awful displeasure and obedience to difficult commands. And dependence is, in many respects, exercised so as it is not in a state of perfect glory. If holiness were first begun in heaven, there would not be that foundation laid for building it up to so great a height, nor the bringing it to so amiable a beauty in the manner of its exercise, and so no foundation for so sweet a joy.

The things that have been said concerning the necessity of a state and time of probation in this world before the bestowment of the purchased reward are to be understood of the church of the elect collectively, taken7

-- 507 --

as a body, rather than of each individual person. As to elect infants that die in infancy, we have less to lead us into an understanding of God's dealings with them than of his dealings with adult persons.

But I would nevertheless observe the following things concerning them:

1. That some of those things that have been mentioned that render existence in an antecedent state necessary before the elect exist in their state of reward in heaven [are applicable to them]. Infants have an antecedent existence, and so are the posterity of Adam, and so are first in a state of sin and misery under guilt and exposed to punishment and destruction, which would never have been if they had first been created in heaven. Elect infants in heaven will fully know they have been in a state of sin and misery, and so will know that they have actually been redeemed out of such a state by Christ.

2. Although infants will not personally and actively have accepted of Christ as their Savior before they are in their state of reward, yet there will be enough to make it visible to angels and visible to the whole assembly of elect creatures that the reward is dependent on faith. For all will see that, with regard to the body or church they belonged to, it is glorified after acceptance of Christ. And their parents' actual faith (which hath influence in the infants' salvation and on the account of which in some sense the infant is saved) will precede the infants' glory. So that the visibility of the suitableness and beauty of the way of salvation in the eye of the heavenly inhabitants and in the eye of infants themselves, consisting in a dependence of eternal life on8 faith, will be sufficiently provided for.

3. Glorified infants, being members of a body that has (i.e. in its chief members) actually passed through the trials, exercises and discipline of an antecedent day of probation, and being members of this body in its perfect and glorified state, wherein the union and fellowship of all the members in one body shall be in its greatest perfection, as one body indeed: the acquaintance and communion of these infants with the other members will be so great and perfect as to give them an ineffable kind of sympathy, in such a manner as will greatly answer instead of experience and, together with the certain knowledge of what has been their own state by nature, will be near akin to consciousness.

4. We don't know how far they will have communicated to them by God a kind of consciousness of that faith that their parents exercised for them.

5. Though elect infants are saved without properly passing through a time of probation and without a personal, actual faith, yet seeing the body

-- 508 --

(the church) is saved in consequence of an actual antecedent trial, these forementioned ends are answered, viz. that Christ has the honor of men's being required to receive him on pain of God's peculiar wrath, and there being actually a terrible punishment for rejecting him and neglect and contempt of Christ. And men's giving them [selves] up to sloth and wickedness is not encouraged. And Christ bruises the serpent's head in his own territories and in the same state wherein he ruined man and procured the ignominy and sufferings of Christ, and sets up his kingdom of grace on the ruins of Satan's kingdom, and baffles all Satan's subtlety by his superior wisdom, manifested in the application of redemption and the way in which he carries on the work of actual salvation in the world. And Christ has a glorious victory over Satan in the conversion of sinners. And God shows his faithfulness and the riches and immutability of his love in his continued mercy to his saints, notwithstanding their infirmities. And a proper and wise foundation is laid for a diversity of degrees of glory in heaven, that many of the members of the society shall be higher than others.
