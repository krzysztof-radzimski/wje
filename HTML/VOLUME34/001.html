"Original Sin" Notebook Original Sin (Arnauld) Jonathan Edwards

Notebook on Original Sin

These notes are part of Edwards' apparatus for drafting his treatise defending the doctrine of original sin. As early as his epistle to the Northampton congregation of c. June 1752 appended to Misrepresentations Corrected, Edwards had lamented the influence of John Taylor's "scheme of religion," including his views on "the whole doctrine of original sin," in The Scripture-Doctrine of Original Sin, Proposed to Free and Candid Examination (London, 1740).1 But in a letter to Thomas Foxcroft of Feb. 11, 1757, Edwards writes that he had "lately been writing a defense of the doctrine of original sin; wherein I have largely handled the principal arguments I know of for that doctrine; and have particularly considered everything, of any consequence, in Dr. Taylor's book against that doctrine; a book that has done more to root out the gospel, in all this western part of New England, than any other book. I have almost prepared this for the press."2 The notes preserved here in the first part correlate to Ch. III, sec. 2, "Observations on Romans 3:9-24," discussing the nature of "collective bodies" and justification. Entries towards the end, including a draft of a printing proposal, language for the preface, and even a "motto" for the treatise on original sin, reflect Edwards' initial plan of publishing his views on original sin and true virtue in the same work.

*****

The cover of the MS notebook (BRBL, f. 1241) is made of a portion of the New York Mercury for April 26, 1756; the front cover is one piece, the back a large piece doubled over. The MS itself is composed of a disbound and mostly re-stitched copy of Antoine Arnauld's De la Frequente Communion (Paris, 1643), pp. 105-10 (loose) and pp. 145-256 (stitched), 53 leaves total. To utilize the printed pages, Edwards turned them upside down and wrote in the margins. The extant pages numbered by Edwards are pp. 35-40, 65-66, and 79-100, with the remaining ones unnumbered. His notes extend through MS p. 80.

Edwards received a copy of Arnauld's work from John Erskine in late June 1752. In thanking Erskine, he commented: "Arnauld On Frequent Communion will not be very profitable to me, by reason of my not understanding the French." It is possible these notes may have been started sometime between 1752 and 1756, since Edwards may have stitched on the cover after he began them. In all likelihood, however, the cover provides a terminus a quo for the notes.

Pp. 340-41, on Romans 3:10-19: "The Apostle is here speaking of Bodies of People, or of Jews and Gentiles in a collective Capacity. . . . P. 424, on Romans 9:30-31,33, on the Apostle arguing that Jews were rejected as God's people and Gentiles admitted: "This demonstrates, that he doth not mean that Justification, which puts particular Persons into the state of eteranl Salvation; but that Justification, which gives them the present Blessings, Privileges, Means and Honours of God's Church and Kingdom. For all the believing Gentiles, in a Body, attained to this Justification; but no body will suppose, that they all, in a Body, attained to eternal Salvation."
P. 438a, on Romans 9:33: "Therefore, as this obtaining Mercy is no other than the Election and Justification, about which he argues in this Epistle, it is certain he doth not thereby mean the Election and Justification only of particular Persons; and so as to ascertian this everlasting Salvation: But he means such an Elections and Justification as may also be applied to Bodies of Men, with respect to their being taken into the Kingdom of God, in this World." ↩
