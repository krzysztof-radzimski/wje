type="miscellany"> "The Fulfillment of the Prophecies of the Messiah" Miscellanies no. 1068.2 Misc. 1068.2 (ed.) Jonathan Edwards

25.1 It was foretold that there should be some eminent prophet that should appear a little before the Messiah, that should be his forerunner to go before him, to prepare his way; that should preach earnestly and very powerfully, and in a very awakening manner, in the wilderness; who should come in the spirit and power of the prophet Elijah, and be in many respects much like him. Is. 40:3, "The voice of one crying in the wilderness, Prepare ye the way of the Lord, make straight in the desert an highway for our God"; Mal. 4:5, "Behold, I will send you Elijah the prophet before the coming of the great and dreadful day of the Lord."

Those things were all verified in John the Baptist; and particularly was he like Elijah the prophet. Elijah mainly abode in the wilderness; he was of the land of Gilead, near the wilderness of Arabia. He was fed for a considerable time by ravens in the wilderness, and afterwards dwelt a long time with the widow of Zarephath, just without the borders of the land of Israel, in a secret, hidden place where the king of Israel, by the utmost search in Israel and all neighboring kingdoms, could not find; and therefore probably was in the wilderness, I Kgs. 17:9, etc., and 18:10. After this he was a long time in the wilderness, when he fled from Jezebel, I Kgs. 19. Elijah was of Gilead beyond Jordan, and dwelt by the brook Cherith that is before Jordan; and the sons of the prophets, of which he was the master, dwelt chiefly about Jordan. So John the Baptist executed his office chiefly in the country about Jordan, and beyond Jordan, John 1:28. We read of Elijah, II Kgs. 1:8, that "he was an hairy man, and girt with a girdle of leather about his loins." So we read of John the Baptist, that he "had his raiment of camel's hair, and a leathern girdle about his loins" [Matt. 3:4].

Elijah commonly lived on mean and course fare. He was for a considerable time fed by ravens with bread and flesh in the morning, and bread and flesh in the evening, I Kgs. 17:6, and afterwards lived upon bread and water with the widow of Zarephath, I Kgs. 17:15; and we read that he fed on bread and water again, 1 Kgs. 19:5-6. So we read of John the Baptist, that his meat was locusts and wild honey. We read of Elijah that he fasted forty days and forty nights, I Kgs. 19:8. So we read of John the Baptist and his disciples that they fasted oft, and that John came neither eating nor drinking. Elijah seems never to have married; so also it seems to have been with John the Baptist. Elijah was sent to reprove the sins of great men; so John the Baptist did freely and severely reprove the sins of Herod, and of the Pharisees and Sadducees, the chief men among the Jews. Elijah reproved the false prophets, the corrupt teachers in Israel, and derided and showed the vanity and vileness of Baal, the god they trusted and gloried in, I Kgs. 18. So John the Baptist severely reproved the Pharisees and Sadducees, the corrupt teachers of the Jews, and showed them the vanity of their own righteousness that they trusted in, and were exceedingly lifted up with a conceit of; and notwithstanding all their high opinion of their righteousness, he told 'em they were a generation of vipers. Elijah bore witness to the altar of the true God that had been neglected and despised and broken down, and to the sacrifice offered thereon, by fire descending from heaven upon it; so John the Baptist bore witness to him that was the great altar and sacrifice, the Lamb of God that takes away the sin of the world, and has his testimony immediately confirmed from heaven, by the Holy Ghost descending and abiding on him. Elijah was honored by God in being sent by him to anoint kings and prophets, I Kgs. 19:15, etc. John the Baptist was sent to anoint the great king and great prophet over the people of God, and the whole world of mankind: he was improved as the great instrument of his solemn inauguration into his office; he baptized God, at the same time with his baptism anointing him with the Holy Ghost.

Elijah was sent especially to denounce judgments against the wicked rulers, teachers and people in Israel. Thus at his word, God brought a grievous famine on the land, I Kgs. 17 and 18. He denounced terrible judgments on Ahab and Jezebel and their family, I Kgs. 21:19-24; and against Ahaziah, II Kgs. 1; and against the people in general, I Kgs. 19:17. At his word, the false teachers in Israel were miserably destroyed, I Kgs. 18, and at his word God brought fire from heaven to destroy the wicked, II Kings, 1st chapter. So the special errand of John the Baptist was to reveal the wrath of God [that] was coming on that wicked people, and on all ranks degrees and orders among them, and to warn 'em to fly from the wrath to come. Matt. 3:7, "O generation of vipers, who hath warned you to flee from the wrath to come"; v. 10, "And now the ax is laid to the root of the trees: therefore every tree which bringeth not forth good fruits is hewn down, and cast into the fire"; v. 12, "whose fan is in his hand, and he will thoroughly purge his floor, and gather his wheat into the garner, and burn up the chaff with unquenchable fire." At Elijah's word, God brought a plentiful rain in Israel, after a great and long-continued famine; so God, with the preaching of John the Baptist after a long spiritual famine, gave a great outpouring of his Spirit in Israel, which by the prophets is compared to plentiful showers of rain, Is. 44:3. Elijah was hated by Jezebel, the proud, meretricious wife of the prince of Israel; so John the Baptist soon became the object of the hatred of the corrupt, self-exalting, whorish church of the Jews, as appears by Matt. 11:16, [18], "whereunto shall I liken this generation? . . . For John came neither eating nor drinking, and they say, He hath a devil." Elijah was especially persecuted by the great men in Israel, by Ahab, Jezebel and Ahaziah, who sought his life; so John the Baptist was first imprisoned, and afterwards beheaded, by Herod.

It is foretold that this forerunner of the Messiah should prepare the way for him by promoting repentance among all sorts of persons--rulers, teachers and common people--and that he should do it by warning them [of the] wrath to come, or the impending, approaching, dreadful curse on the impenitent. Mal. 4:6, "And he shall turn the heart of the fathers to the children, and the heart of the children to the fathers, lest I come and smite the earth with a curse"; which was exactly fulfilled in John the Baptist. And John's denouncing in his preaching [an] approaching destruction on that nation, through a remarkable blast of heaven upon them, is agreeable to Is. 40:6-7, "The voice said, Cry. And he said, What shall I cry? All flesh is grass, and all the goodliness thereof as the flower of the field: the grass withereth, the flower fadeth: because the spirit of the Lord bloweth upon it."

26. Though it was foretold by the prophets Haggai and Malachi, as was before observed, §4, that the Messiah would come in a little time, yet it is often signified in the prophecies that he would come after much waiting for him among the people of God, and the time would seem long before he actually should come, after they expected him. Gen. 49:18, "I have waited for thy salvation, O Lord"; Is. 25:9, "Lo, this our God; we have waited for him, and he will save us: this is the Lord; we have waited for him, we will be glad and rejoice in his salvation"; Is. 26:8, "Yea, in the way of thy judgments, O Lord, have we waited for thee; the desire of our soul is to thy name, and to the remembrance of thee"; Is. 40:3, "The voice of one crying in the wilderness, Prepare ye the way of the Lord"; v. 9, "O Zion, that bringest good tidings, . . . say unto the cities of Judah, Behold your God!"; v. 27, "Why sayst thou, O Jacob, and speakest, O Israel, My way is hid from the Lord, and my judgment is passed over from my God?"; vv. 30-31, "Even the youths shall faint and be weary, and the young men shall utterly fall: but they that wait on the Lord shall renew their strength"; Is. 7:14, "a virgin shall conceive, and shall bear a son, and shall call his name Immanuel"; with ch. 8:10, "speak the word, and it shall not stand: for God is with us," or "because of Immanuel"; and v. 14, "And he shall be for a sanctuary but for a stone of stumbling," etc.; and v. 17, "I will wait for the Lord, that hideth his face from the house of Israel, and I will look for him"; and Mic. 7:7, "I will wait for the God of my salvation"; and many other like passages there are in the prophecies of the Messiah.

This was verified in the time of Jesus Christ. 'Tis evident by the history of the Evangelists, that the people were and had long been in great expectation of the Messiah and the kingdom of heaven when Jesus came. Thus we read concerning old Simeon, Luke 2:25, that he "was just and devout, waiting for the consolation of Israel"; and Anna, that prophetess of so great an age, Luke 2:38, that she coming in "gave thanks likewise unto the Lord, and spake [. . .] to all that looked for redemption in Jerusalem." And by all accounts of those times, the Jews had been long in great expectation of the Messiah, and had spoken so much of their expectations, that the fame of it had reached almost all the heathen nations of the Roman Empire, and the expectation of some glorious person to govern the world and bring days of glorious peace and prosperity, had been for some time gradually communicated from the Jews to other nations; so that before Christ came, there seems to have been a general expectation of such a person through the world. The Jews had long been the subjects of such things, that excited in them eager desires and expectations of the coming of the Messiah: their temple, ever since the return from the Babylonish captivity, had been destitute of those things that were the peculiar glory of the former temple, viz. the Shechinah, the Urim and Thummin, the fire from heaven, and the ark of the covenant and mercy seat that Moses made, with the tables of stone and original book of the law. And it was foretold that the Messiah should restore the glory to the temple, Hag. 2:1-9. And they had long been without any prophet among them, and without any prince of the royal house of David; and had been the subjects of many great oppressions and cruel persecutions from the heathen, particularly from Antiochus Epiphanes; and had been conquered by the Romans, and their temple profaned by them, and had been held in a dependence a great while upon them.

27. The prophecies represent as though the Messiah should be a person in some remarkable and peculiar manner free from sin, yea that he should be perfectly innocent and holy, though they represent him as being one of the race of mankind. Ps. 24:3-4, "Who shall ascend into the hill of the Lord? and who shall stand in his holy place? He that hath clean hands, and a pure heart"; with v. 7, "Lift up your head, O ye gates; and be ye lift up, ye everlasting doors; that the King of Glory may come in" (see observations on this Psalm in discourse on "Prophecies of the Messiah");2 Ps. 16:10, "For thou wilt not leave my soul in hell; neither wilt thou suffer thine Holy One to see corruption"; II Sam. 23[:4], "he that rules over men shall be just"; Is. 53:9, "because he had done no violence, neither was guile found in his mouth"; v. 11, "by his knowledge shall my righteous servant justify many"; Ps. 45:7, "Thou lovest righteousness, and hatest wickedness: therefore God, thy God, hath anointed thee," etc.; Jer. 23:5, "I will raise unto David a righteous Branch"; Ps. 86:2, "Preserve my soul; for I am holy."

28. It was foretold that the Messiah should not appear in temporal glory, or in the pomp and the outward noise and show of earthly princes; but that he should appear in outward meanness. Is. 42:1-2, "Behold my servant, whom I uphold; . . . He shall not cry, nor lift up, nor cause his voice to be heard in the street"; Zech. 9:9, "behold, thy King cometh unto thee: . . . lowly, and riding on an ass, and a colt the foal of an ass"; Is. 53[:2], "he hath no form nor comeliness; and when we shall see him, there is no beauty that we should desire him" (see note on this verse);3 Ps. 69:29, "But I am poor and sorrowful: let thy salvation, O God, set me up on high"; Ezek. 17:24, "And all the trees of the field shall know that I the Lord have brought down the high tree, and exalted the low tree."

It was foretold that he should proceed, according to the flesh, from an externally mean, low and barren original, which should be able to yield him no outward glory wealth or prosperity. Is. 53:2, "he shall grow up as a root out of a dry ground: he hath no form nor comeliness." This was fulfilled in his being born of a poor virgin of low degree, his parents of an inferior part of the land of Canaan, even Galilee, whence were very few prophets; and of Nazareth, a mean city; and born in Bethlehem, that was little among the thousands of Judah, agreeable to Mic. 5:2, "But thou, Bethlehem Ephratah, though thou be little among the thousands of Judah, yet out of thee shall come forth unto me that is to be ruler in Israel."

It was foretold that he should be weak and feeble, and very liable to suffering. Is. 53:2, "he shall grow up before him as a tender plant"; Ezek. 17[:22], "I will crop off from the top of his young twigs a tender one." So he is often called the Branch, or the Twig, as the word properly signifies.

It was foretold that the Messiah should submit to the authority of earthly rulers, in his being called, Is. 49:7, "a servant of rulers."

29. It was foretold that the Messiah should appear in a very unexpected manner to great numbers, in that respect, that he should appear in such outward meanness, and that few would believe it was he upon that account. Is. 52:14, "As many were astonied at thee; his visage was so marred more than any man, and his form more than the sons of men"; Is. 53, at the beginning, "Who hath believed our report? and to whom hath the arm of the Lord been revealed? For he shall grow up before him as a tender plant, . . . he hath no form nor comeliness," etc. But of this, more afterwards.4

30. It was foretold that the Messiah, when he came, should be generally hated and despised. Is. 53:3, "He is despised and rejected of men"; Is. 49:7, "to him whom man despiseth, to him whom the nation abhorreth"; Ps. 22:6-7, "I am . . . a reproach of men, and despised of the people. All they that see me laugh me to scorn: they shoot out the lip, they shake the head"; Ps. 69:4, "They that hate me without cause are more than the hairs of mine head"; and v. 8, "I am become a stranger to my brethren, and an alien to my mother's children"; [vv.] 19-20, "Thou hast known my reproach, and my shame, and my dishonor: mine adversaries are all before thee. Reproach hath broken my heart"; Ps. 102:6-7, "I am like a pelican in the wilderness: I am like an owl in the desert. I watch, and am as a sparrow alone upon the house top."

31. It was foretold that he should be used to sorrow, that he should live a sorrowful life. Is. 53:3, "He is [. . .] a man of sorrows, and acquainted with grief"; Ps. 102:3-5, "For my days are consumed like smoke, and my bones are burnt as an hearth. My heart is smitten, and withered like grass; so that I forget to eat my bread. By reason of the voice of my groaning my bones cleave to my skin"; Ps. 69:11, "I made sackcloth also my garment"; 69:29, "But I am poor and sorrowful."

It was foretold that he should do hard labor, and spend his strength in it. Is. 49:4, "Then I said, I have labored in vain, and spent my strength for nought, and in vain."

32. The name "Messiah" signifies "Anointed," and many prophecies represent the Messiah as being in an eminent manner God's anointed; but some of those ancient prophecies do represent that anointing as being a spiritual anointing, not like the anointing of the kings of Israel, but a being abundantly endued with the Spirit of God. Is. 61:1, "The Spirit of the Lord God is upon me; because the Lord hath anointed me." And the Messiah is represented as one that should have the Spirit of God resting on him in an extraordinary manner, in Is. 11:2, "And the spirit of the Lord shall rest upon him, the spirit of wisdom and understanding, the spirit of counsel and might, the spirit of knowledge and of the fear of the Lord"; Is. 42:1, "Behold my servant, whom I uphold; mine elect, in whom my soul delighteth; I have put my spirit upon him"; Zech. 3:8-9, "for, behold, I bring forth my servant the BRANCH. For behold the stone that I have laid before Joshua; upon one stone shall be seven eyes": which are the same with that spirit of wisdom and understanding, of counsel and knowledge, spoken of in the 11th of Isaiah, as appears by the next chapter [Zech. 4], 6th and 10th verses.

And thus it was that the Evangelists do represent our Jesus as having been anointed of God. Matt. 3:16-17, "And Jesus, when he was anointed,5 went up straightway out of the water: and, lo, the heavens were opened unto him, and he saw the Spirit of God descending like a dove, and lighting upon him"; Mark 1:10, Luke 3:21-22, John 1:22-33.

33. Christ's opening the eyes of the blind and unstopping the ears of the deaf, and loosing the tongue of the dumb and restoring the lame, feeding the hungry and raising the bowed down, are agreeable to Is. 35:5-6, "Then the eyes of the blind shall be opened, and the ears of the deaf shall be unstopped. Then shall the lame man leap as an hart, and the tongue of the dumb shall sing"; Is. 29:18, "in that day shall the deaf hear the words of this book, and the eyes of the blind shall see out of obscurity, and out of darkness"; Is. 42:6-7, "I the Lord have called thee in righteousness, . . . to open the blind eyes"; v. 16, "I will bring the blind by a way that they knew not"; v. 18, "Hear, ye deaf; and look, ye blind, that ye may see"; v. 20, "opening the ears"; Jer. 31:8, "Behold, I will bring them from the north country, and gather them from the coasts of the earth, and with them the blind and the lame"; Ps. 146:7-8, "which giveth food to the hungry. The Lord looseth the prisoners: the Lord openeth the eyes of the blind: the Lord raiseth them that are bowed down."

34. It was foretold that the Messiah should be an eminent teacher, and that much of his business should be to instruct men and preach the word of God to them. Is. 51:16, "I have put my words in thy mouth, and covered thee in the shadow of mine hand, that I may plant the heavens," etc.; Is. 49:1-2, "Listen, O isles, unto me; and hearken, ye people, from far; the Lord hath called me from the womb; from the bowels of my mother hath he made mention of my name. He hath made my mouth like a sharp sword"; Is. 59:21, "As for me, this is my covenant with them; My spirit that is upon thee, and my words which I have put in thy mouth, shall not depart out of thy mouth"; Is. 50:4, "The Lord hath given me the tongue of the learned, that I should know how to speak a word in season to him that is weary"; Is. 55:4, "Behold, I will give him for a witness of the people"; Is. 9:6, "His name shall be called [. . .] Counselor."

It was foretold not only that he should accomplish salvation for his people, but that part of his errand should be to make known and reveal the great salvation. Is. 41:27,6 "I will give to Jerusalem one that bringeth good tidings"; ch. 52:7, "How beautiful upon the mountains are the feet of him that bringeth good tidings, that publisheth peace; that bringeth good tidings of good, that publisheth salvation; that saith unto Zion, Thy God reigneth!"; ch. 61:1-2, "The Spirit of the Lord God is upon me; because the Lord hath anointed me to preach good tidings unto the meek; he hath sent me to bind up the brokenhearted, to proclaim liberty to the captives, and the opening of the prison to them that are bound; to proclaim the acceptable year of the Lord."

It was foretold that he should be a great prophet. Deut 18:15-19, "The Lord thy God will raise up unto thee a Prophet from the midst of thee, of thy brethren, like unto me; unto him shall ye hearken; according to all that thou desiredst of the Lord thy God in Horeb in the day of the assembly, saying, Let me not hear again the voice of the Lord my God, neither let me see this great fire any more, that I die not. And the Lord said unto me, They have well spoken that which they have spoken. I will raise them up a Prophet from among their brethren, like unto thee, and I will put my words in his mouth; and he shall speak unto them all that I shall command him." Though he was to be a king, yet he was to be a prophet, and so would be a prophet like unto Moses, who was both a prophet and a king. Deut 33:5, "he was king in Jeshurun."

His name, "Counselor," implies his being a prophet, as appears by comparing [Is. 41], vv. 22-23, "Let them bring forth, and show us what shall happen: let them show the former things, what they be, that we may consider them, and know the latter end of them; or declare us things for to come. Show the things that are to come hereafter"; and [v.] 26, "Who hath declared from the beginning, that we may know? and beforetime, that we may say, He is righteous? yea, there is none that showeth, yea, there is none that declareth, yea, there is none that heareth your words"; with v. 28, "For I beheld, and there was no man; even amongst them, and there was no counselor, that when I asked them, could answer a word." But the Messiah, he was such a counselor, that could show and declare things for to come, as appears by v. 27, "The first shall say to Zion, Behold, behold them: and I will give to Jerusalem one that bringeth good tidings"; and ch. 42, at the beginning, "Behold my servant, whom I uphold."

And this place in Isaiah was verified in our Jesus, who, as he was a great prophet in other respects, so he was in this, and did in this respect remarkably show that he was righteous; and he produced his cause,7 and brought forth his strong reasons, in this respect, that he showed what should happen: he showed things to come hereafter, both good tidings to the godly, and terrible thing to sinners that would not receive him, that they might be destroyed. His predictions were very great and wonderful, and most various and manifold, concerning himself, his disciples, the nation of the Jews, his own followers and his enemies, and all sorts of persons and the world of mankind. His predictions, many of them, respected events that were to [be] fulfilled in his lifetime, at his death and after his death. In that generation and future generations never appeared such a prophet, who so abounded in such plain predictions of future events, speaking in such a manner, as having a perfect view of all future things before him, speaking of himself as of the knowledge that was his own, and understanding his own predictions: and his predictions never failed, but were exactly fulfilled. See this matter largely treated of, "Miscellanies," no. 972.8 But surely God would never have suffered things to have been thus, if he had been an impostor, especially when Christ uttered predictions as of his own knowledge, not in the style of the old prophets, who said, "Thus saith the Lord," but in such a style as this, "Verily, verily, I say unto you" (see no. 1044). 9 Especially seeing foretelling things to come, declaring the end from the beginning, etc., do answer God's challenge to impostors and false gods, and are mentioned as good proofs and strong reasons of the righteousness of the pretenses of those that are prophets; yea, if they foretell as of their own knowledge, and show they have in themselves a view of the series of future events, 'tis mentioned here as a good proof of the righteousness of their pretenses to divinity. Vv. 21-23, "Produce your cause, bring forth your strong reasons, saith the King of Jacob. Let them bring forth, and show us what shall happen: let them show the former things, what they be, that we may consider them, and know the latter end of them; or declare us things for to come. Show the things that are to come hereafter, that we may know that ye are gods"; v. 26, "Who hath declared from the beginning, that we may know? and beforetime, that we may say, He is righteous?" And more especially still would not God have suffered it to be thus in our Jesus, seeing Jesus' profession was that he was the Messiah, and seeing this very thing of his being able thus to foretell things to come, is mentioned as what will prove him a true prophet and a divine person, beyond all the prophets and gods of the heathen.

35. It was foretold that this great teacher and prophet of God, should introduce a state of more glorious light, and a great increase of knowledge of the things of God, and of the clearness of understanding of the doctrines and mysteries of religion. Is. 32:1-4, "Behold, a king shall reign in righteousness, . . . And the eyes of them that see shall not be dim, and the ears of [them] that hear shall hearken. The heart also of the rash shall understand knowledge, and the tongue of the stammerers shall be ready to speak plainly"; Is. 33:6, "And wisdom and knowledge shall be the stability of thy times"; Is. 35:8, "And an highway shall be there, and a way, and it shall be called The way of holiness; . . . the wayfaring men, though fools, shall not err therein"; Is. 25:7, "And he will destroy in this mountain the face of the covering cast over all people, and the veil that is spread over all nations"; and ch. 29:18, "in that day shall the deaf hear the words of this book, and the eyes of the blind shall see out of obscurity, and out of darkness"; and 30:26, "Moreover the light of the moon shall be as the light of the sun, and the light of the sun shall be sevenfold, as the light of seven days, in the day that the Lord bindeth up the breach of his people, and healeth the stroke of their wound"; Is. 42:16, "And I will bring the blind by a way that they knew not; I will lead them in paths that they have not known: I will make darkness light before them, and crooked things strait"; and 51:4, "a law shall proceed from me, and I will make my judgment to rest for a light of the people"; and 52:8, "Thy watchmen shall lift up the voice; with the voice together shall they sing: for they shall see eye to eye, when the Lord shall bring again Zion" (see note on the place);10 Is. 54:13, "And all thy children shall be taught of the Lord"; Jer. 31:34, "And they shall no more teach every man his neighbor, and every man his brother, saying, Know the Lord: for they shall all know me, from the least of them even unto the greatest of them"; Zech. 14:6-7, "And it shall come to pass in that day, that the light shall not be clear, and11 dark: but it shall be one day known unto the Lord, not day, and12 night: but it shall come to pass, that at evening time it shall be light."

It was foretold that, at that time, the light of the glory of God should be no longer hid behind curtains, or concealed within the veil of the temple between the cherubims in the secret place of the most high; but that the veil should be removed, and God should shine forth openly, and there should be a plain revelation of those divine mysteries and arcana that had till [then] been kept hid. This seems to be implied in that, Ps. 80:1, "thou that dwelleth between the cherubims, shine forth"; and Is. 40:4-5, "the crooked things shall be made straight, and the rough places plain: and the glory of the Lord shall be revealed, and all flesh shall see it together." Here see "Scripture," note on Deut. 18:15.13

36. Christ's spending so much of his time in Galilee, there preaching and working miracles, conversing and calling disciples, and sending them forth to preach, his dwelling in Nazareth and being so conversant about the sea of Tiberias, and particularly at Capernaum, Chorazin and Bethsaida, is agreeable to Is. 9:1-2, "nevertheless the dimness shall not be such as was in her vexation when at first he lightly afflicted the land of Zebulun and the land of Naphtali and afterward did more grievously afflict her by the way of the sea beyond Jordan in Galilee of the nations The people that walked in darkness have seen a great light they that dwell in the land of the shadow of death upon them hath the light shined." In the latter part of the foregoing chapter, the Prophet had been threatening darkness and trouble and dimness of anguish in the land. In this chapter, he foretells light to succeed it in the same land, that the dimness that was in the day of vexation should not continue, but that there should be a day of glorious light. And here some particular parts of the land are specified, wherein the day of vexation had been, but [wherein] should be great light in the days of the Messiah: for it is his days, and light by his means, that are here spoken of, as is evident by v. 6, "For unto us a child is born, unto us a son is given," etc. Nazareth, where Christ spent great part of his life, was in Galilee, in the land of Zebulun. Capernaum, Bethsaida and Chorazin were upon the Sea of Galilee: Bethsaida was in the land of Zebulun, Capernaum was in the land of Naphtali, Chorazin was beyond Jordan, in Galilee of the nations; both Capernaum and Chorazin being, in a noted way, over the river Jordan by the Sea of Galilee, a bridge over the river joining the two cities.

37. It was foretold that the Messiah should be very gradual in his teaching his disciples, dealing with them as little children in that respect, teaching them by little and little, not revealing things too deep and mysterious and beyond their understandings at first. Is. 28:9-10, "Whom shall he teach knowledge? and whom shall he make to understand doctrine? them that are weaned from the milk, and drawn from the breasts. For precept must be upon precept, precept upon precept; line upon line, line upon line; here a little, and there a little." This was remarkably fulfilled in the way of our Jesus his teaching his disciples: he did not at first teach 'em the more sublime and mysterious doctrines of the gospel, but those things that were more easy and plain. Mark 4:33, "And with many such parables spake he the word unto them, as they were able to hear it." And he did not reveal the most mysterious doctrines of Christianity till after his ascension, when the Holy Spirit was poured out upon them. John 16:12-13, "I have yet many things [to] say unto you, but ye cannot bear them now. Howbeit when he, the Spirit of truth, is come, he will guide you into all truth." And when he saw their understandings confounded, he accommodated his speech to their understandings, as when one speaks to children. Mark 10:24, "And the disciples were astonished at his words. But Jesus answereth again, and saith unto them, Children, how hard is it for them that trust in riches to enter into the kingdom of God!"

38. It was foretold that the Messiah should treat his disciples and followers with great tenderness and gentleness, with their infirmities and calamities. Is. 42:3, "A bruised reed shall he not break, and the smoking flax shall he not quench: he shall bring forth judgment unto truth"; and ch. 40:11, "He shall feed his flock like a shepherd: he shall gather the lambs with his arm, and carry them in his bosom, and shall gently lead those that are with young"; Is. 49:10, "They shall not hunger nor thirst; neither shall the heat nor the sun smite them: for he that hath mercy on them shall lead them, even by the springs of water shall he guide them"; Ezek. 34:15-16, "I will feed my flock, I will cause them to lie down, saith the Lord God. I will seek that which was lost, and bring again that which was driven away, and will bind up that which was broken, and will strengthen that which was sick."

These things are remarkably fulfilled in our Jesus, who treated his disciples and followers with wonderful gentleness and tenderness. He spoke to them and of them, from time to time, as children. Mark 10:24, "Children, how hard is it for them that trust in riches," etc.; John 21:5, "Children, have ye any meat?" And as little children. John 13:33, "Little children, yet a little while am I with you"; Matt. 18:6, "But whoso shall offend one of these little ones which believe in me, it were better for him that a millstone were hanged about his neck, and that he were drowned in the depth of the sea"; v. 10, "Take heed ye despise not one of these little ones; for I say unto you, That in heaven their angels do always behold the face of my Father which is in heaven"; v. 14, "It is not the will of your Father, that one of these little ones should perish"; Matt. 10:42, "whosoever shall give to drink unto one of these little ones a cup of cold water only in the name of a disciple, verily I say unto you, he shall in no wise lose his reward."

He exercised great gentleness towards them in bearing with and pitying their unbelief. Though it was in them very aggravated, he reproved it from time to time in such gentle language as that, "O ye of little faith," Matt. 6:30, and 16:6, etc., and 8:23, etc., and 14:26. And how did he condescend to Thomas, who was so unbelieving, John 20:25. And how gentle were his reproofs of their ill behavior. Thus he reproved Martha, Luke 10:41-42, "Martha, Martha, thou art careful and troubled about many things: but one thing is needful." And we have a most marvelous instance of his gentleness towards his disciples in their most sinful, aggravated, unconcernedness and neglect while he was in an agony for them, and was going to shed his blood for them, when they carelessly fell asleep from one time to another, though he most earnestly desired them to keep awake, that he might have the comfort of their company in his extreme sorrows; he having told them that his soul was exceeding sorrowful, even unto death. He gently reproved them with only saying, "What, could not ye watch with me one hour? [. . .] the spirit indeed is willing, but the flesh is weak" [Matt. 26:40-41]. And with what gentleness did he recover Peter when he was ashamed of his Master, while he was suffering for him, and denied him with dreadful oaths and curses, when he melted him down and recovered him with a look of love. And with what gentleness did he receive Peter and the rest of his disciples after his resurrection, who had just before treated him so ill in the time of his suffering. See John 20:17 and 21:8. And indeed, the whole that the Evangelists give us an account of what he said and did to his disciples after his resurrection, shows his marvelous gentleness. And how compassionate was Christ to his disciples under their calamities and afflictions, weeping with those that wept, as he wept with Mary and Martha, John 11:35, 38; compassionately encouraging them, when he saw them under any fear or concern of mind, with such speeches as these: "Fear not," "Son, be of good cheer," "Daughter, be of good comfort," "Let not your heart be troubled," etc. And with what gentleness and compassion did he receive some that had been very great sinners on their repentance, particularly Mary Magdalene, Luke 7. And how ineffable is that grace and compassion and tenderness of heart that Christ manifested towards his disciples, the whole of his dying discourse to them, throughout the 13th, 14th, 15th and 16th chapters of John, and that prayer to his Father with which it is concluded.

39. It was foretold that when the Messiah should come, the greater part of the Jews should not acknowledge him to be the Messiah, nor should believe that it is he, nor should hearken to his instructions--his doctrine should be a stumbling block to them; and that they should hate him and reject him, though they are his own people, and continue obstinate in these things; and that it should be only a select and comparatively small number of the Jews that should be his disciples. Is. 53:1, "Who hath believed our report? and to whom hath the arm of the Lord been revealed?"; v. 3, "we hid as it were our faces from him; he was despised, and we esteemed him not"; Is. 49:4-5, "Then I said, I have labored in vain, I have spent my strength for nought, and in vain: . . . And now, saith the Lord, [. . .] Though Israel be not gathered, yet shall I be glorious in the eyes of the Lord"; v. 6, "It is a light thing that thou shouldst be my servant to raise up the tribes of Jacob, and to restore the preserved of Israel"; v. 7, "Thus saith the Lord, . . . to him whom . . . the nation abhorreth"; Is. 8:14-16, "And he shall be for a sanctuary but for a stone of stumbling and for a rock of offense to both the houses of Israel,14 and for a snare to the inhabitants of Jerusalem. And many among them shall stumble, and fall, and be broken, and be snared and be taken. Bind up the testimony, seal the law among my disciples"; Ps. 22:6, "I am [. . .] despised of the people"; Ps. 69:8, "I am become a stranger unto my brethren, and an alien unto my mother's children"; Ps. 118:22, "The stone which the builders refused"; Is. 42:19-22, "Who is blind, but my servant? or deaf, as my messenger that I sent? [. . .] Seeing many things, but thou observest not; opening the ears, but he heareth not. The Lord is well pleased for his righteousness' sake; he will magnify the law, and make it honorable. But this is a people robbed and spoiled; they are all of them snared in holes, and hid in prison houses"; Is. 29:9-10, "Stay yourselves" (or, "Be ye slow"), "and wonder; cry ye out, and cry: they are drunken, but not with wine; they stagger, but not with strong drink. For the Lord hath poured out upon you the spirit of deep sleep, and hath closed your eyes"; Is. 65:1-2, "I am sought of them that asked not for me; . . . I have spread out my hands all the day unto a rebellious people, which walketh in a way that is not good, after their own thoughts"; Is. 28:9-13, "Whom shall he teach knowledge? and whom shall he make to understand doctrine? them that are weaned from the milk, and drawn from the breasts. For precept must be upon precept, . . . For with stammering lips and another tongue will he speak to this people. To whom he said, This is the rest wherewith ye may cause the weary to rest; and this is the refreshing: yet they would not hear. But the word of the Lord was unto them precept upon precept, precept upon precept; line upon line, line upon line; here a little, and there a little; that they might go, and fall backwards, and be broken, and snared, and taken."

40. It was foretold that the Messiah should make the first offer of the blessings of his kingdom to the Jews, and that his first labors should be among them for their salvation; and that he should continue his instructions, offers and counsels to them, and labors for their good, a considerable time, before he should leave them and turn himself to any other people. Is. 49:4-5, "Then I said, I have labored in vain, and spent my strength for nought, and in vain: . . . And now, saith the Lord that formed me from the womb to be his servant, to bring Jacob again unto him, Though Israel be no[t] gathered, yet shall I be glorious in the eyes of the Lord"; Is. 65:1-2, "I am sought of them that asked not for me; [. . .] I have spread out my hands all the day unto a rebellious people."

41. It was foretold that the Jews should not own him to be the Messiah, because of the meanness of his appearance, and should be confirmed in it, that he is not the Messiah, by seeing the contempt and sorrows that he should be the subject of. Is. 52:14-15, "As many were astonied at thee; his visage was so marred more than any man, and his form more than the sons of men: so shall he sprinkle many nations; and kings shall shut their mouths at him." Here it is evident by the antithesis, that in many being astonied at him is implied that they, being greatly disappointed in his mean and contemptible appearance, should despise him; and therefore kings reverencing him, or shutting their mouths at him, is set in opposition to many being astonied at him. Again, ch. 53 at the beginning, "Who hath believed our report? and to whom is the arm of the Lord revealed? For he shall grow up before him as a tender plant, as a root out of a dry ground." Agreeable to this is the Jews' despising of him because he was of Galilee and of Nazareth, and his mother and reputed father and brethren such mean persons. It follows, "he hath no form nor comeliness; and when we shall see him, there is no beauty that we should desire him. [. . .] and we hid as it were our faces from him; he was despised, and we esteemed him not."

42. It was foretold that the Jews should esteem the Messiah a wicked man, one that would not hear or regard the law. This is implied in their supposing of him very blind and deaf, Is. 42:19-20, as such expressions in the Prophets commonly are so used: "Who is blind, but my servant? or deaf, as my messenger that I sent?" That their supposing [him] senseless and regardless of the law is what is here intended, appears by the following verses, wherein God vindicates him from their injurious opinion of him: "Seeing many things, but thou observest not; opening the ears, but he heareth not. The Lord is well pleased for his righteousness' sake" (though you hate and esteem him not, despise him as one that is not righteous but wicked); "he will magnify the law, and make it honorable" (though you reproach him as one that attempts to set aside and make void the law). And that it is the Jews that are here spoken of, appears by the next words, v. 22, "But this is a people robbed and spoiled."

How agreeable is this to the history of Jesus, who reproached him as a blasphemer, and a glutton and a winebibber, a sabbath breaker, a wizard, and a friend of publicans and sinners. They said of him that they knew he was a sinner, and therefore condemned him as not fit to be of the congregation of Israel, and agreed that if any confessed him, he should be put out of the synagogue, and looked on him as not fit to live.

It was foretold that the Jews be confirmed in their opinion of the Messiah's being a wicked man by his sufferings, and should look upon them as an evidence that he was not in favor with God, but cursed of him. Is. 53:4, "we did esteem him stricken, smitten of God, and afflicted." How agreeable is this to their wagging their heads when he hung on the cross, and saying, "He trusted in God; let him deliver him now, if he will have him: for he said, I am the Son of God," Matt. 27:43.

43. It was foretold that the Messiah should especially be rejected and hated by the chief men among the Jews, their rulers and teachers, and that those that should receive instruction and follow the Messiah should mostly be the poorer sort of people. Ps. 118:22, "The stone which the builders refused is become the head of the corner"; Is. 28:7, "But they also have erred through wine, and through strong drink are out of the way; [. . .] they err in vision, they stumble in judgment"; v. 9, "Whom shall he teach knowledge? . . . them that are weaned from the milk," etc.; vv. 14-18, "Wherefore hear the word, ye scornful men, that rule this people which is in Jerusalem. Because ye have said, We have made a covenant with death, . . . Therefore . . . I lay in Zion for a foundation a stone, . . . and he that believeth shall not make haste. . . . and the hail shall sweep away the refuge of lies, . . . And your covenant with death shall be disannulled," etc.; Is. 29:9-10, "Stay yourselves, and wonder; cry ye out, and cry: they are drunken, but not with wine; they stagger, but not with strong drink. For the Lord hath poured out upon you the spirit of deep sleep, and hath closed your eyes: the prophets and your rulers, the seers hath he covered"; v. 14, "Therefore, behold, I will proceed to do a marvelous work amongst this people, even a marvelous work and a wonder: for the wisdom of their wise men shall perish, and the understanding of their prudent men shall be hid"; vv. 18-19, "the deaf shall hear the words of this book, . . . and the poor among men shall rejoice in the Holy One of Israel"; Ps. 69:4, "they that would destroy [me], being mine enemies wrongfully, are mighty"; v. 12, "They that sit in the gate speak against me"; Zech. 13:7, "Awake, O sword, against my shepherd, against the man that is my fellow, saith the Lord of hosts: smite the shepherd, and the sheep shall be scattered; and I will turn my hand upon the little ones."

There being a combination of rulers against Christ, is agreeable to Ps. 22:12-13, "Many bulls have compassed me about: strong bulls of Bashan have beset me round. They gaped upon me with their mouths, like a ravening and a roaring lion"; v. 21, "Save me from the lion's mouth: for thou hast heard me from the horns of the unicorns." And that there should be a combination of both heathen and Jewish rulers against Christ, is agreeable to Ps. 2:1-2, "Why do the heathen rage, and the people imagine a vain thing? The kings of the earth set themselves, the rulers take counsel together, against the Lord, and against his anointed."15

44. It was foretold that the Jews in the Messiah's days should envy, hate, and persecute and excommunicate the people of God, and that they should do it from a pretended zeal for God. Is. 66:5, "Your brethren that hated you, and cast you out, said, Let the Lord be glorified: but he shall appear to your joy, and they shall be ashamed," compared with ch. 65:13, "my servants shall rejoice, but ye shall be ashamed," together with vv. 1-3, the connection of the whole discourse in these two chapters being observed; Deut. 32:21, "I will move them to jealousy with those that are not a people; I will provoke them to anger with a foolish nation"; Is. 26:10-11, "Let favor be showed to the wicked, yet will he not learn righteousness; in the land of uprightness he will deal unjustly, and will not behold the majesty of the Lord. Lord, when thy hand is lifted up, they will not see: but they shall see, and be ashamed for their envy at the people."

45. It was foretold that the Jews in the time of the Messiah, should make themselves exceeding guilty by trusting in false refuges and confidences, instead of relying on that sure foundation which God has laid in Zion, and particularly that a proud, self-exalting and self-righteous spirit should greatly prevail among them at that time. Is. 28, v. 12, "To whom he said, This is the rest wherewith ye may cause the weary to rest; and this is the refreshing: yet ye would not hear"; v. 14, "Wherefore hear ye the word of the Lord, ye scornful men"; and vv. 15-17, "Because ye have said, . . . we have made lies our refuge, and und[er] falsehood have we hid ourselves: therefore thus saith the Lord, Behold, I lay in Zion for a foundation a stone, a tried stone, a precious corner stone: he that believeth shall not make haste. . . . and the hail shall sweep away your refuge of lies, and the waters shall overflow the hiding place"; v. 20, "For the bed is shorter than that a man can stretch himself on it: and the covering narrower than that he can wrap himself in it"; Is. 57:10-13, "Thou art wearied in the greatness of thy way; yet saidst thou not, There is no hope: thou hast found the life of thy hand; therefore thou wast not grieved. . . . I will declare thy righteousness, and thy works; for they shall not profit thee. When thou criest, let thy companies deliver thee; but the wind shall carry them all away; vanity shall take them: but he that putteth his trust in me shall possess the land, and shall inhabit my holy mountain"; Is. 65:5, "Which say, Stand by thyself, come not near to me; I am holier than thou."

46. It was foretold that the teachers and chief men among the Jews, in the Messiah's time, should be notorious hypocrites. Is. 29:10, "the prophets and your rulers, the seers hath he covered," with vv. 13-15, "Wherefore the Lord said, Forasmuch as this people draw near to me with their mouth, and with their lips do honor me, but have removed their heart far from me, [. . .] Therefore, behold, I proceed to do a marvelous work amongst this people, even a marvelous work and a wonder: for the wisdom of their wise men shall perish, and the understanding of their prudent men shall be hid. Woe unto them that seek deep to hide their counsel from the Lord, and their works are in the dark, and they say, Who seeth us? and who knoweth us?" Compare this with the 23rd chapter of Matthew.

47. It was foretold that the Jews, in the Messiah's time, should greatly provoke God by so much affecting to stand at a distance from others, as being much holier than they. Is. 65:5, "Which say, Stand by thyself, come not near to me; for I am holier than thou. These are a smoke in my nose, a fire that burneth all the day." How remarkably was this fulfilled in the Jews' standing at such a distance from the publicans and the Samaritans and other Gentiles, insomuch that they would not do 'em any offices of humanity, so much as to show 'em the way in a journey, or to direct 'em to a spring when thirsty, as some of the heathen historians observe; nor would they do anything to relieve a Samaritan, if they saw them in the most lamentable and miserable circumstances through any accident, as is implied in Christ's parable of the man that fell among thieves; and greatly blamed Christ for suffering Mary Magdalene to touch him,16 because she was a sinner.

48. It was foretold that the Jews in the Messiah's time should make void the commandments of God by the tradition or commandments of men, that they should find fault with things as God had constituted them, imagining that they can mend the written institutions; and so shall change the religion and worship that God has appointed, following their own inventions instead of God's institutions. Is. 29:13-14, "Wherefore the Lord said, Forasmuch as this people draweth near me with their mouth, and with their lips do honor me, but have removed their heart far from me, and their fear toward me is taught by the precept of men: therefore, behold, I will proceed to do a marvelous work," etc.; v. 16, "Surely your turning of things upside down shall be esteemed as the potter's clay: for shall the work say of him that made it, He made me not? or shall the thing framed say of him that framed it, He had no understanding?"; Is. 65:2, "I have spread out my hands all the day unto a rebellious people, which walketh in a way that was not good, after their own thoughts"; and 66:3, "He that killeth an ox is as if he slew a man; . . . Yea, they have chosen their own ways."

49. By the ancient prophecies, the sufferings of the Messiah were to be extremely great. Ps. 102:3, etc., "For my days are consumed like smoke, and my bones are burnt as an hearth. 50. By the ancient prophecies, the outward meanness, abasement, disgrace and contempt that the Messiah should be the subject of, should be exceeding great, even to the utmost extreme, and that his enemies should greatly mock and deride him. Ps. 22:6[-7], "But I am a worm, and no man; a reproach of men, and despised of the people. All they that see me laugh me to scorn: they shoot out the lip, they shake the head"; Ps. 69:7, "Because for thy sake I have borne reproach; shame hath covered my face"; vv. 11-12, "I am become a proverb to them. They that sit in the gate speak against me; I was the song of the drunkards"; vv. 19-20, "Thou hast known my reproach, and my shame, and my dishonor: . . . Reproach hath broken my heart"; Ps. 102:6, "I am like a pelican in the wilderness: I am like an owl of the desert"; v. 8, "Mine enemies reproach me all the day"; Is. 52:14, "As many were astonied at thee; his visage was so marred more than any man, and his form more than the sons of men"; Is. 50:6, "I hid not my face from shame and spitting."

That Jesus was sold for thirty pieces of silver, and the money given to the potter, is remarkably agreeable to Zech. 11:12-13.

51. It was foretold that the Messiah should suffer greatly by the cruelty of men. Ps. 22:12-13, "Many bulls have compassed me about: strong bulls of Bashan have beset me round. They gaped upon me with their mouths, as a ravening and a roaring lion"; v. 16, "For dogs have compassed me about: the assembly of the wicked have enclosed me: they pierced my hands and my feet"; Ps. 69:4, "They that hate me without a cause are more than the hairs of my head: they that would destroy me, being mine enemies wrongfully, are mighty"; v. 14, "Deliver me out of the mire, and let me not sink: let me be delivered from them that hate me"; v. 19, "mine adversaries are all before thee"; vv. 21-22, "They gave me also gall for my meat; and in my thirst they gave me vinegar to drink. Let their table become a snare before them," etc.; v. 26, "For they persecute him whom thou hast smitten," etc.; Is. 53:3, "He is despised and rejected of men"; v. 7, "He was oppressed," with the context; Mic. 5:1, "they shall smite the judge of Israel with a rod upon the cheek."

52. Jesus being left alone in his suffering, forsaken of all his disciples, and deserted of those that a little before admired him, crying "Hosanna," etc., is agreeable to Ps. 22:11, "Be not far from me; for trouble is near; for there is none to help"; Ps. 69:8, "I am become a stranger unto my brethren"; v. 20, "I looked for some to take pity, but there was none; and for comforters, but I found none"; Ps. 102:7, "I watch, and am as a sparrow alone upon the house top." These two last places were remarkably verified at the time of his agony, when he watched and kept awake alone, and his disciples refused to watch with him, to comfort him, one hour, and when in his great distress he came to 'em once and again, seeking to be comforted by their company; but when he looked to his disciples to take pity on him, when he told 'em his soul was exceeding sorrowful, even unto death, he found none to pity, and looked for comforters but found none.

53. Jesus being compassed round by his enemies in his last sufferings, in that manner he was, making it their business to reproach him, and mock and afflict him, is agreeable to Ps. 22:12-13, "Many bulls have compassed me: strong bulls of Bashan have beset me round. They gaped upon me with their mouths, as a ravening and a roaring lion"; and v. 16, "For dogs have compassed me: the assembly of the wicked have enclosed me." And his being so compassed about by persons of various nations, Jews and proselytes from all parts of the world, from every nation under heaven, and Herod and his attendants, and heathens, Romans and soldiers and servants, probably of many heathen nations, is agreeable to Ps. 118:10-12, "All nations compassed me about: . . . They compassed me about: yea, they compassed me about: . . . They compassed me about like bees."

59. This prophecy had a remarkable fulfillment in Jesus Christ. The rulers and teachers of the Jewish church, the house of God, were prodigiously provoked by Jesus' severe reproofs for their wicked mismanagements in that church or house of God, of which they had the care and charge; their false teaching in the church, making void the commandment of God through their tradition, corrupting the worship of God's house; their proud behavior in God's house, affecting the uppermost seats in the synagogues, and setting up themselves in the room of God; their desiring to be called, "Rabbi, Rabbi," and so reproaching God, who alone was their master; and for shutting up the house of God against men, neither going in themselves and hindering those that were entering; and for joining long prayers in God's house with covetous practices and wicked extortion, devouring widows' houses, and teaching men that if they swear by the temple or by the altar, it is nothing, as in Matt. 23; and by [Jesus'] going into the temple a little before his crucifixion and casting out all them that sold and bought in the temple, and overthrowing the tables of the money-changers, and the seats of them that sold doves, charging them with making the temple a den of thieves. 67. We may reasonably conclude that in Jesus' death there was a remarkable fulfillment of that, Ps. 22:14, "all my bones are out of joint": for he, having his strength exceedingly wasted, and the sinews greatly relaxed before his crucifixion by his agony the night before, his watching all night and fasting and under constant, cruel sufferings till that time, and then bearing his cross till he sunk under it, and then hanging by his wounded hands and bearing his whole weight on them for three hours together, wasting all the rest of his strength and life, and by degrees more and more relaxing and stretching the sinews and ligaments by which the bones were held together, the joints must needs be separated and bones be drawn asunder.

73.20 That Jesus in the time of his last sufferings made intercession to his Father for those horribly wicked men, his crucifiers, even at the very time when, in the heat of their cruelty and bloodthirsty malice, and height of their contempt, they were nailing him to the cross, and pleading for them, saying, "Father, forgive them, for they know not what they do," was a marvelous expression of the full and perfect submission, patience and obedience of his soul under those sufferings, and the glorious holiness and grace and infinitely meritorious excellency of that act of his soul, in offering up himself to die for sinners; being done with perfect love, humility, meekness and love to God and sinners under all the trial that he then [endured], both from the sufferings he endured and the sin of men that was then, in its blackest color and most trying circumstances, set before him. And it was a wonderful fulfillment of that in Is. 53:12, "he bare the sins of many, and made intercession for the transgressors." The intercession there spoken of, seems to be the intercession he made in his last sufferings: for that is what is spoken of in the context, and in this verse in particular; and the intercession here mentioned is spoken of as a meritorious circumstance and concomitant of his last sufferings, and a manifestation of his great and meritorious virtue in suffering, on the account of which God would so gloriously reward his sufferings. The whole verse being thus: "Therefore will I divide him a portion with the great, and he shall divide the spoil with the strong; because he hath poured out his soul unto death: and he was numbered with the transgressors; and he bare the sin of many, and made intercession for the transgressors."

74.21 It was foretold that though the Messiah should die under circumstances of great contempt, as a wicked man and a malefactor, yet God, in some circumstances of his death, would take care to put honor upon him as a reward of his innocency and merits, by being in some respect with the rich in his death. This rich man was excited thus to honor Jesus' dead body, because he was sensible in his own mind that he suffered wrongfully, and that he had done no violence, nor was guilty of any fraud or deceit that should render him worthy to be thus punished by the magistrate.

80. It was foretold that the sacrifice of the Messiah should make full satisfaction to God's justice, such as should make all other sacrifice after that needless. Dan. 9:24, "Seventy weeks are determined upon thy people and upon thy holy city, to finish the transgression, and to make an end of sins, and to make reconciliation for iniquity, and to bring in everlasting righteousness," with v. 27, "in the midst of the week" (or "in the half of the week") "he shall make the sacrifice and oblation to cease." 'Tis evident that in v. 24, "to finish the transgression, and make an end of sin, and to make reconciliation for iniquity, and to bring in an everlasting righteousness," are various expressions to signify the same glorious and wonderful work that shall be done, and event that shall come to pass. The word in the original translated, "make reconciliation for iniquity," is the same that is used in the Law for making atonement by sacrifice, so that the words imply that the Messiah should offer such an atonement for sin as should make an end of, or consume, the transgression (as the word is), and seal up sin (as in the margin);28 i. e. quite complete the business of reconciliation, so that there should be no further occasion for going about to make reconciliation, or offer any further atonement for, or bring sin any more to remembrance as a thing to be atoned for. So that by these expressions it seems as if sacrifices for sin thenceforward must cease, and be made to cease by the Messiah; as v. 27, 'tis expressly said they should [cease] at that very time spoken of in this verse, viz. in the last half of [the] week of the seventy weeks. Putting these things together, we can't understand those prophecies otherwise, than that the Messiah should offer up such a sacrifice to atone for sin as should render all other sacrifices and oblations for sin needless, and should put an end to them.

The same thing is evidently implied in that, Zech. 3:8-9, "for, behold, I will bring forth my servant the Branch. For behold the stone that I have laid before Joshua; upon one stone shall be seven eyes: behold, I will engrave the graving thereof, saith the Lord of hosts, and I will remove the iniquity of that land in one day." A thus removing the iniquity of God's people is here spoken of as a new and extraordinary thing, some great thing whereby that shall be brought to pass that is very different from what used to be: as much as to say, "Removing their iniquity shall not be a thing continually a-doing by long and continued series of sacrifices, by which there is continually remembrance made of sins anew, signifying that a complete atonement was not yet [to] be made; but the business shall then be finished, when the BRANCH is brought forth, in one day, and shall not remain still to be done, but the work of reconciliation and atoning for sin shall be, from that day forward, forever completed."

It was foretold that in the days of the Messiah there should be a wonderful reconciliation of mercy and truth, justice and peace, so that they shall meet together and entirely agree in the same salvation of God's people. Ps. 85:9-10,29 "Surely his salvation is nigh them that fear him; that glory may dwell in our land. Mercy and truth are met together; righteousness and peace have kissed each other."

81.30 That the incarnation of the Son of God, and the Divinity's being united to the human nature of the Messiah, was in order to his making atonement for sin, is agreeable to Zech. 3:9, "For behold the stone that I have laid before Joshua; upon one stone shall be seven eyes: behold, I will engrave the graving thereof, saith the Lord of hosts, and I will remove the iniquity of that land in one day." I have before shown from the Old Testament, that the Divinity was to be united to humanity in the person of the Messiah.31 This verse seems to contain a prophecy of it: for those seven eyes, as they are interpreted in the next chapter, v. 10, "are the eyes of the Lord," or the divine Wisdom, or Logos. These are, by an immediate and wonderful work of God, inherent in a stone, so as to become the eyes of a stone, a thing in itself very far from sight, as the human nature is in itself weak and blind, and far from divinity. But the words that immediately follow32 in the end of the verse, seem to represent the end and design of God in this, is wholly and perfectly to remove the iniquity of his people.

94.48 By the ancient prophecies, the Messiah, after his rising from the dead, was to ascend into heaven, and in his exaltation (which I have shown the prophecies represent as following his humiliation and death)49 he was to have his throne in heaven.

In the three last verses of the 16th Psalm, 'tis foretold that when the Messiah shall rise from the dead, God will show him the path to that blessed abode, which is the place of his own glorious presence, and where he should be at God's right hand: "my flesh shalt rest in hope. For thou wilt not leave my soul in hell; neither wilt thou suffer thine Holy One to see corruption. Thou wilt show me the path of life: in thy presence is fullness of joy; and at thy right hand are pleasures for evermore." But the scriptures of the Old Testament do represent heaven as especially God's dwelling place, the place of his throne and glorious presence. This is very often called God's dwelling place. 1 Kgs. 8:30, "when they shall pray towards this place" (i. e. towards the temple), "then hear thou in heaven thy dwelling place"; so vv. 39, 43 and 49. Here 'tis observable that Solomon, at the dedication of the temple, often distinguishes between the temple and God's dwelling place. Heaven is called God's temple, and his throne is said to be in heaven. Ps. 11:4, "The Lord is in his holy temple, the Lord's throne is in heaven." This is called God's "holy habitation," Deut 26:15, so Jer. 25:30; and "the place of his habitation," and "his holy dwelling place," II Chron. 30:27, Ps. 33:13-14. 'Tis called his sanctuary, Ps. 102:19, "for he hath looked down from the height of his sanctuary." 'Tis said that "the Lord hath prepared his throne in the heavens," Ps. 103:19. Heaven is called God's tabernacle, Job. 36:29, "who can understand [. . .] the noise of his tabernacle?" Yea, 'tis said to be God's throne, Is. 66:1, "The heaven is my throne, and the earth is my footstool"; Job 26:9, "He holdeth back the place of his throne, and spreadeth his cloud upon it." Heaven is represented as God's dwelling place in opposition to the earth, which is spoken of as the proper dwelling place of men. Ps. 115:16, "The heaven, even the heavens, are the Lords: but the earth hath he given to the children of men"; Eccles. 5:2,50 "God is in heaven, and thou in the earth." And therefore, when God comes to dwell on earth, it is always spoken of as coming down from heaven; so he is represented as coming down on Mt. Sinai and into the temple. And his dwelling anywhere on earth is spoken of as his great condescension, stooping to dwell below his proper abode to dwell in man's abode. So Solomon speaks of it as a great wonder, that God will dwell on earth in his temple. And that God should place his tabernacle with the children of Israel, is spoken of as a wonderful act of condescension, Ezek. 37:27. And God in coming to dwell on earth, is spoken of as renting the heavens and bowing the heavens, Is. 64 at the beginning.51 Heaven is called the high and holy place where God dwells, Is. 57:15, "thus saith the high and lofty One who inhabiteth eternity, whose name is Holy; I dwell in the high and holy place." And heaven is represented as the place of God's glorious presence. Is. 63[:15], "Look down from heaven, and behold from the habitation of thy holiness and of thy glory." 'Tis one of God's titles, "He that dwelleth in the heavens," Ps. 123:1; and part of his proper glory, that he dwelleth on high, Ps. 113:5, "Who is like unto the Lord our God, who dwelleth on high." Heaven is called God's place, Hos. 5:15, "I will go and return unto my place." When Enoch and Elijah were taken to God's blissful and glorious presence, they were taken into heaven; the way to heaven was the path of life to them, by which they escaped death.

As in the 16th Psalm, 'tis foretold that God would show the Messiah the way to his blissful and glorious presence, and his right hand, so Ps. 110[:1], it is said, God would bid him sit on his right hand; and in Zech. 6:13, 'tis represented that he should sit on God's throne. Now the Old Testament knows of but two things that are called the throne of God: the one is heaven, or his throne in heaven; and the other is the mercy seat over the ark in the holy of holies, in the temple and tabernacle. The former the Old Testament represents as the proper temple and tabernacle of God, and his original dwelling place, and the proper place of his throne. Heaven is called God's sanctuary, Ps. 33:13-14 and 102:19; and his tabernacle, Job 36:29; and his temple, Ps. 11:4; and here God's throne is said to be, Ps. 11:4, Ps. 103:19, Job 26:9; yea, heaven is said to be his throne, Is. 66:1. And the latter the Old Testament represents as called God's throne less properly, and more properly called his footstool. Lam. 2:1, "and remembered not his footstool in the day of his anger"; I Chron. 28:2, "As for me, I had it in my heart to build an house of rest for the ark of the covenant of the Lord, and for the footstool of our God, and had made ready for the building"; Ps. 132:7, "We will go into his tabernacles: we will worship at his footstool"; Is. 60:13, "to beautify the place of my sanctuary; and to make the place of my feet glorious." The inferior manner in which God dwelt in the Jewish sanctuary was expressed by that, that he placed his name there; and these are represented as only the outworks or gates of heaven, Gen. 28:17. Yea, though God is represented as dwelling in holy places on earth, yet he was so far from dwelling in them as he does in heaven, that when he appeared in them from time to time, he is represented as then coming from heaven to them, as though heaven were his fixed abode, and Mt. Sinai and the tabernacle and temple, places into which he would occasionally turn aside and appear. Thus God is said to have descended in a cloud and appear[ed] to Moses, when he passed by him and proclaimed his name, though he had before that from time to time appeared there in the mount of God, and though Moses at that time had long been conversing with God in the mount. The temple is so much less properly the place of God's abode than heaven, that the Wise Man, that built the temple, speaks of men's worshipping God in the temple: he at the same [time] advises those that worship him there to be sensible that God is in heaven, and they on the earth; they are on earth in the temple, but he would have them conceive of God as not where they are, but in heaven, Eccles. 5:1-2. So when God withdrew from the land of Israel to heaven, it is spoken of, Hos. 5:15, as God's going and returning to his place, as though the land of Israel were not properly his place.

Now when the prophecies speak of the Messiah's sitting down on God's throne at God's right hand, 'tis much most reasonable to understand this of God's higher throne, and that which the Old Testament represents as properly God's throne and dwelling place, and not that which it speaks of as more properly his footstool, for these reasons:

1. Because this is spoken of in these prophecies as the Messiah's glorious honor and exaltation. Although I have shown before that the prophecies do abundantly represent the Messiah as God, or a divine person:52 but for God, in the person of the Son, to appear seated on that which the Old Testament represents as more properly God's footstool, would hardly be represented as so glorious an exaltation of him that was the Jehovah. God's being enthroned in heaven, is spoken of in the Old Testament as one thing wherein appears God's infinite highness and exaltation as the great king. Eccles. 5[:2], "God is in heaven, and thou on the earth"; Is. 57[:15], "thus saith the high and lofty One that inhabiteth eternity, [. . .] I dwell in the high and holy place"; Is. 33:5, "God is exalted; for he dwelleth on high"; Ps. 113:5, "Who is like unto the Lord our God, for he dwelleth on high." Though indeed God humbles himself to behold the things that are in earth,53 yet 'tis a great manifestation of the high honor and glory of God in the sight of men, short-sighted worms, that he has his throne in heaven; and therefore this is spoken of as a thing wherein God will make the glorious honor and dignity of the Son of God to appear. But God's dwelling on the earth in the temple at Jerusalem, is spoken of as a thing wherein God indeed puts great honor on men, and shows his great mercy, but a thing wherein he most marvelously manifests his condescension. "Will God indeed dwell on the earth?" says Solomon [I Kgs. 8:27]; and in so doing, He is spoken of as bowing the heavens, and renting the heavens, etc. [Is. 64:1]. Therefore, 'tis not to be supposed that the great honor and glory in which Jehovah should appear in the person of the Messiah, should consist in his dwelling in a temple made by men's hands in Jerusalem, especially considering,

2. That the prophecies do everywhere represent as though God would glorify himself, and would in all respects appear in his majesty, honor, and infinite dignity and excellency, in the days of the Messiah, much more than he ever had done; that the glory of the Lord should be revealed, and all flesh should see it together; that men should behold it and marvel, God's enemies be astonished and tremble, and through fear submit themselves; and God's people should admire and praise him, and boast and say, This is our God; and that now God will be great and exalted, and take to himself his great power, etc. And one way in which the prophecies do represent God as being so greatly glorified in the days of the Messiah, when his name shall be excellent in all the earth, is that he will set his glory above the heavens. Compare Ps. 148:13 and Ps. 8:1. And the way that the prophecies speak of God's thus manifesting his glory, is in the person of the Messiah, who I have showed is that divine person that is spoken of as Jehovah reigning over his people, appearing in glory to defend them, etc. Now, therefore, can any imagine that the glorious enthronement of the Messiah spoken of, in God's throne at his right hand, wherein his great honor and glory shall be so highly manifested, God saying to him, "Thy throne, O God, is for ever and ever," etc., is only Jehovah's being seated on that which in the Old Testament is represented not properly [as] the throne of God, but rather his footstool?

3. The Messiah's dominion is often represented in the prophecies as universal, extending over all nations of the world, and heaven is spoken of as the proper place of the throne of him who don't only rule over one nation, but all nations; and it is foretold that God in the days of the Messiah would appear as dwelling in heaven, because he would then appear ruling over all nations. Ps. 103:19, "The Lord hath prepared his throne in the heavens; and his kingdom ruleth over all," with v. 22, "Bless the Lord, all his works in all places of his dominion"; and Ps. 47:5-8; and Ps. 113:4-5, which I have shown is a prophecy of the times of the Messiah:54 "The Lord is high above all nations, and his glory above the heavens. Who is like unto our Lord who dwelleth on high." And God's thus dwelling on high, is55 spoken of as one thing wherein his exaltation shall appear in the days of the Messiah (here see note on Ps. 113 in "Harmony of the Old and New Testament," p. 167);56 Is. 33:5, "The Lord is exalted; for he dwelleth on high: he hath filled Zion with judgment and righteousness."

4. 'Tis unreasonable to understand this throne of God, in which the Messiah should sit at God's right hand, to be the mercy seat in the temple at Jerusalem, because what has been already observed, §24, of what the prophecies do everywhere represent of the nature and extent of the Messiah's kingdom, do show that the temple at Jerusalem, and its worship and ordinances, should not be continued for God's people at that time; and how impossible it is that God's people of all nations should come to worship there after the manner of the Jews; and how inconsistent such a manner of worship is with many prophecies.

5. What puts the matter beyond all dispute, and do fully determine that this throne of God on which the Messiah shall sit and reign at God's right hand, is that which the Old Testament represents as God's proper throne in his true temple and proper dwelling place, and not that which the same scriptures represent as more improperly is so called, is this: that the prophecies do particularly declare that in the days of the Messiah, the temple made with men's hands shall no longer [be] God's dwelling place or rest, and the golden throne over the ark should no longer be accounted his throne, but that heaven then be should be looked on as his temple and throne. Is. 66:1, "Thus saith the Lord, The heaven is my throne, and the earth is my footstool: where is the house that ye build unto me? and where is the place of my rest?" And in Jer. 3:16-17, 'tis very plainly signified that, in the Messiah's days, there shall be no such throne of God as that in the temple over the ark; intimating that it should be so was not a constitution suited to the multitude of God's people, and largeness of his church at that time. "And it shall come to pass, when ye are multiplied and increased in the land, in those days, saith the Lord, they shall say no more, The ark of the covenant of the Lord: neither shall it come into mind; neither shall they remember it; neither shall they visit it; neither shall that be done any more. At that time they shall call Jerusalem the throne of the Lord," etc.

Again, the ascension of the Messiah into heaven is foretold, Ps. 68:18, "Thou hast ascended on high, thou hast led captivity captive: thou hast received gifts for men; yea, for the rebellious also, that the Lord God might dwell among them." That the Messiah is here spoken of, and that his ascension into heaven is here spoken of, see particularly proved, in the discourse of "The Prophecies of the Messiah," §58.57

The ascension of the Messiah is also foretold, Gen. 49:9, "from the prey, my son, art thou gone up"; and Ps. 47:5, "God is gone up with a shout, the Lord with the sound of a trumpet." Concerning both which places, see also the forementioned §58 of "Prophecies of the Messiah."

Again, the ascension of the Messiah from the earth into heaven at his exaltation, and in order to receive his glory and kingdom, [is foretold]. And there was given [him] dominion, and glory, and a kingdom, that all people, nations, and languages should serve him," etc. His being here spoken of as the Son of man, implies that he is of this lower world, a native of the earth; but here he is manifested as coming to God in the clouds of heaven, and therefore this implies his ascending up from the earth to God in heaven. God, or the Ancient of days, in the 9th verse is represented as sitting on the throne of his glory, which throne is said to be "like the fiery flame," having thousand thousands ministering to him. This glorious throne, the scripture of the Old Testament everywhere tells us, is heaven. See the evidence more particularly that the Messiah is spoken of in this Psalm, and that this is a prophecy of his ascension into heaven, in the preceding discourse, §85 (see Bp. Kidder's Demonstration, Pt. 3, p. 51a).59

That honor which the king of Babylon, the city of the enemies of Zion, is represented in that 14th of Isaiah, [vv.] 13-14, just mentioned, as ambitious of--viz. of ascending into heaven and exalting his throne "above the stars of God," to sit in "the mount of the congregation," to "ascend above the heights of the clouds," and to be "like the most High"--it was the will of God should really be given to the Messiah, his own Son, the king of Zion, the city of God's people, that is a divine person and the man of God's right hand, God's firstborn, higher than the kings of the earth, to whom belonged divine honor, and to whom it was plainly promised that he should sit on God's throne at his right hand.

In Ezek. 17:22-24, 'tis promised that the Messiah after his humiliation, wherein he should be as a low and dry tree, and after his death, wherein he should be as a tender twig cropped off, God would plant him "upon an high mountain and eminent," even "the mountain of the height of Israel." This can be no other than the height of God's sanctuary, spoken of, Ps. 102:19, and the mountain of the congregation, spoken of, Is. 14:13; which, we are told, are heaven. As to Mt. Zion in the city of David, or the mountain of the temple, they neither of them were so superlatively high, as the double and treble expression, "high mountain and eminent," "the mountain of the height of Israel," signifies, according to the Hebrew idiom.

To the like purpose with the last mentioned text in Ezekiel and Ps. 24, is that in the 2nd Psalm, 6th verse, "Yet have I set my king on my holy hill of Zion"; which is represented as being done after the violent opposition he met with from men.

The supposition that the Messiah should ascend into heaven, is agreeable to what is said in Deuteronomy [34:10] of his being a prophet "like unto Moses," who ascended up into Mount Sinai to God on the behalf of the people, to transact affairs there with God on their60 behalf; and there as it were dwelt with God, saw his glory, was admitted to God into his presence, where was great joy and glory, and conversed with him "face to face."

The ancient Jews, who gave the name of "the kingdom of heaven" to the kingdom of the Messiah, seemed to have understood that when God should appear to reign in this kingdom, he should have his throne in heaven, and that the Messiah should ascend into heaven and also have his throne there with God: and from thence to have given this kingdom the name of the kingdom of heaven, because it would be a kingdom wherein the heavens should rule, and the throne of the kingdom be in the heavens. They seem to have taken it from the 7th chapter of Daniel, where the throne of the Ancient of days, when he sets up his kingdom after the four monarchies, is represented as being in heaven, and the Messiah as coming there to him, to be with him, and reign with him there over all nations.61
