1. It was foretold that the Jews, at least in the posterity of Judah, should be a commonwealth, having a government of their own, in which their own laws should take effect as being the rule of government, and that they should have among themselves power of enforcing and executing these laws; or, in short, that they should have among themselves the ROD of government, which rod should enforce and execute their own laws, till the coming of the Messiah. Gen. 49:10, "The scepter shall not depart from Judah, nor a lawgiver from between his feet, until Shiloh come; and unto him shall the gathering of the people be."

It hence follows that the Jews in the posterity of Judah, should not cease for any long period of time from being a state, a body politic, or commonwealth, having a power of government or jurisdiction within itself, before the coming of the Messiah. And therefore, since they have now entirely, and to all intents and purposes, ceased to be thus, without any intermission since the destruction of their city and land, for a much longer time together than ever they were a body politic, a much longer time than it was from the time of their deliverance from their Egyptian slavery till the destruction by the Romans; they having been all this time been entirely broken up and dispersed to the four winds of heaven, without any manner of civil power or government of their own, or any distinct society or body together, distinct from other people, without any land or any one city or town of their own; being everywhere subject to the government of other nations, without any manner of power or authority amongst them by which their own forensic laws are supported, so that the scepter has all this while departed from Judah as much as is possible (and 'tis to be noted that the distinction of tribes has for many ages ceased among them, so that they don't know who are of the tribe of Judah, and who are not): hence it follows that the Messiah is long since come, and that he came before the last destruction of their city and land by the Romans.

And 'tis to be further noted that about seventy years before this destruction, the rod of government finally departed from that people in the plain sense of this prophecy, i. e. they ceased any longer to have the rod of government in their hands, so as to have the power in their hands of maintaining and enforcing the laws of their own commonwealth, or to execute any capital punishments. This rod was no longer in the hands of any lawgiver, or ruler, or enforcer of their laws of their own, and was put into the hands of a Roman governor. They ceased to be [a] distinct commonwealth and were made a Roman province, and had no temporal king thenceforward but Caesar. This came to pass upon the death of Herod the Great. So that since the scepter and lawgiver have departed from Judah, and this people have been without any ruler with power to maintain and enforce their own forensic laws, is a longer space of time than3 it was to that time, from the time that this prophecy was given by Jacob; and about 700 years longer than it was to that time after the chief ruler of Israel began to be of the tribe of Judah, or the scepter or rod of government was given into the hands of Judah, and the lawgiver began to be from between his feet, when David first came to the crown. Therefore it follows that the Messiah did come before Judea was thus made a Roman province.

Moreover, it may be noted that this prophecy seems to signify some special privilege of the tribe of Judah, wherein it should be distinguished from other tribes.

Therefore this prophecy has a remarkable fulfillment in Jesus Christ, who came before the destruction of Judea by the Romans and just before the death of Herod, presently upon which the land was made a Roman province; and this was long after most of the other tribes of Israel had ceased to enjoy any benefit of the scepter of Israel, and the commonwealth had been for several ages chiefly constituted of the tribe of Judah. And soon after Jesus Christ came, there was a remarkable and wonderful gathering of the people and flocking of the multitudes from all parts of the world to him, and gathered into one congregation or church, one society or body under this head; and then all remains of the scepter finally departed from Judah, and they were totally overthrown from being in any respect anymore a commonwealth or body politic, agreeable to that, Matt. 24:14, "And this gospel of the kingdom shall be preached in all the world for a witness unto all nations; and then shall the end come."

It seems that the Jews themselves anciently, did universally understand this prophecy of the Messiah. Thus their three Targums expound it (see Mr. Dickinson's Reasonableness of Christianity, p. 86).4

This prophecy is fulfilled in Jesus Christ, and has been fulfilled in no other person: for there are none that do pretend, concerning any other person that came before the scepter departed from Judah, that he was the Messiah.

2. It was foretold that the Messiah should come during the continuance of the second temple. Hag. 2:2, etc., "Speak now to Zerubbabel the son of Shealtiel, governor of Judah, and to Joshua the son of Josedech, the high priest," etc., "Who is left among you that saw this house in her first glory? and how do you see it now? is it not in your eyes in comparison of it as nothing? Yet now be strong, O Zerubbabel," etc., "and work: for I am with you, . . . Yet once, it is a little while, . . . and the desire of all nations shall come: and I will fill this house with glory, . . . The glory of this latter house shall be greater than the glory of the former, saith the Lord of hosts: and in this place I will give peace, saith the Lord of hosts"; and Zech. 3:8-9, "Hear now, O Joshua the high priest, thou and thy fellows that sit before thee: for they are men wondered at: for, behold, I will bring forth my servant the BRANCH. For behold the stone that I have laid before Joshua; upon one stone shall be seven eyes: behold, I will engrave the graving thereof, saith the Lord of hosts, and I will remove the iniquity of the land in one day"; and ch. 4:6-9, "This is the word of the Lord unto Zerubbabel, [saying,] Not by might, nor by power, but by my spirit, saith the Lord of hosts. Who art thou, O great mountain? before Zerubbabel, thou shalt become a plain: and he shall bring forth the headstone thereof with shoutings, crying, Grace, grace unto it. . . . The hands of Zerubbabel have laid the foundation of this house; his hands shall also finish it"; and ch. 6:11-13, "take silver and gold, and make crowns, and set them upon the head of Joshua the son of Josedech, the high priest; and speak unto him, saying, Thus speaketh the Lord of hosts, saying, Behold the man whose name is The Branch; and he shall grow up out of his place, and he shall build the temple of the Lord: [. . .] and he shall bear the glory, and shall sit and rule upon his throne; and he shall be a priest upon his throne"; and Mal. 3:1, "the Lord, whom ye seek, shall suddenly come into his temple, even the messenger of the covenant, whom ye delight in: behold, he shall come, saith the Lord of hosts."

"Rabbi Josue, who saw the razing of the temple, said that the time of the Messiah was then past, as Rabbi Jacob in Caphtor testifies." Grotius, de Verit., Bk. 5, sect. 14, marginal note. "Rabbi Akiba, and many others, as Rabbi Solomon testifies, were of opinion that the Messiah ought to come in the second temple." Ibid., another marginal note. The Hebrews reckon for the time of the second temple, "all the time from Zerubbabel to Vespasian (as in the Talmud, chapter the last, concerning the Council; and that entitled Jorna, and that entitled Roch. Hasschana); for the temple in the time of Herod the Great, was not rebuilt from the foundation, but only gradually renewed by parts; notwithstanding which alteration, it might be called the same temple."5

3. Again, it was foretold by the prophet Daniel, ch. 9, that from the going forth of the commandment to restore and build Jerusalem, till the Messiah should "finish the transgression, and make an end of sins, and make reconciliation for iniquity, and bring in everlasting righteousness, and seal up the vision and prophecy" [v. 24], should be seventy weeks, i. e. 490 years. If the weeks are understood as weeks of days, they will much more prove that the time of the Messiah is past. But none can imagine that by this prophecy is meant that it should be seventy weeks of days, which is not a year and half, that the Messiah should come; and that when it is said that [in] threescore and two weeks "the street should be built again, and the wall, even in troublous times" [v. 25], that it is meant that the city and wall should be built again [in] a little more than a year; and that after a little more than a year, the Messiah the Prince should not only come but be cut off, and that then the people of the Prince that should come, then should destroy the city and sanctuary, etc.; and that he, the Messiah the Prince, at the end of this, that is a little more than a year, should "confirm the covenant with many for one week," i. e. seven days, and then "for the overspreading of abominations" he should make the city desolate [v. 27]. These suppositions have the greatest absurdity, and 'tis certain those things were never fulfilled, nor any thing like 'em. Nor was there ever any pretense heard of a fulfillment of these things in so short a time. The Messiah, that great Prince, did not come, was not cut off; the city and sanctuary were not made desolate. But by weeks here is undoubtedly meant weeks of years, as is common in the Old Testament visions and prophecies, to put days for years. So Ezek. 4:4-6, "Lie also upon thy left side, and lay the iniquity of the house of Israel upon it: according to the number of the days that thou shalt lie upon it thou shalt bear their iniquity. For I have laid upon thee the years of their iniquity, according to the number of the DAYS, three hundred and ninety days: so shalt thou bear the iniquity of the house of Israel. And when thou hast accomplished them, lie again on thy right side, and thou shalt bear the iniquity of the house of Judah forty days: I have appointed thee each day for a year." Seven years is elsewhere called a week in the Old Testament. So when Laban required of Jacob to serve seven years for Rachel, it is thus expressed, "fulfill her week" [Gen. 29:28]. The Jews had weeks of years as well as weeks of days, with a sabbath or sabbatical year at the end of the week. And 'tis probable those seven years were commonly called weeks by the people, as well as the week of days.6 As they had a sabbath of years at the end of every seven years, as they had a sabbatical day at the end of every seven days; so at the end of every seven of these weeks of years, they had the jubilee. As they had the Passover at the end of seven weeks of days, after the Passover they had the feast of Pentecost, Lev. 25:8, 13.7 And the Spirit of God seems to have some reference to these numbers preceding the feast of the Pentecost and jubilee in this prophecy of Daniel. As the feast of Pentecost was to be after seven weeks, or forty nine days, reckoning from the Passover the time of the redemption out of Egypt, and the jubilee was to be after seven weeks of years, or 490 years; so the commencement of the glorious kingdom of the Messiah was to be after seventy weeks--putting tens for units, or 490 years--after the restoration from Babylonish captivity and desolation.

But instead of 490 years, there has now passed above 2000 years, let us take our reckoning from what decree for the restoring of Jerusalem we will; and about 1700 years since the utter destruction of the city, after it had stood 490 years from the last of these decrees for restoration.

Now there is no other persons concerning whom the Jews themselves, or any one person on the face of the earth, so much as pretends that it is possible that these prophecies should be fulfilled, but over Jesus. But concerning him, they have so exact an accomplishment, that it is impossible to find out any way that the accomplishment might have been more exact, full and clear.8 It seems most probable that this prophecy is to be understood in a mystical sense, viz. for the restoring the church and state of the Jews in their orderly state, and the restitution of those laws, ordinances, officers, rulers and teachers which are the strength of the church, or Jerusalem of God: for the prophecies of the Old Testament are commonly to be understood in such a mystical sense. If we understand the prophecy in this sense, then the decree of Artaxerxes sent by Ezra that we read of, Ezra 7, must be intended. It can't be understood of either of the two preceding decrees, for they only respected the building of the temple, which can't reasonably be supposed to be intended in this prophecy, by the decree for the rebuilding the walls and streets of the city. That commission given to Ezra by Artaxerxes, above all others, was that which restored both the church and state of the Jews: it restored the law, it restored the knowledge of the law by the instruction of Ezra and others with him, it restored the ordinances of the law, it established magistrates and judges to execute the laws of Moses. See Ezra, ch. 7. And if we may judge by the notice Ezra takes of this decree, it was the greatest and most happy thing of that nature that was done at any time by the kings of Persia for the people of God, Ezra 7:27-28. Now from the going forth of this decree, till Jesus Christ made an end of sins by his death, was just 490 years, as Prideaux shows in his Connection.9

4. It was foretold that the Messiah should come in a little time after the times of the prophets Haggai, Zechariah and Malachi. Hag. 2:6-7, "For thus saith the Lord of hosts, Yet once it is a little while, and I will shake the heavens, and the earth, and the sea, and the dry land; and I will shake all nations, and the desire of all nations shall come: and I will fill this house with glory, saith the Lord of hosts"; and Mal. 3:1, "the Lord, whom ye seek, shall suddenly come into his temple, even the messenger of the covenant, whom ye delight in: behold, he shall come, saith the Lord of hosts." Now 'tis unreasonable to understand these prophecies any other wise than that it should be in a little time, compared with the time since he was first promised to the church of Israel, and so compared with the time since he begins to be their desire and the object of the expectations, or the time that they had already sought him and waited for him: that now his coming drew near, the biggest part of the time being expired already since he had been promised and prefigured to them as the hope, salvation and glory of Israel, and since they began to wish and wait for him as such. Thus the church of Israel, whom these prophets encouraged with these predictions, must naturally understand them, and in this sense and expectation take encouragement from them. But nothing at all can be made of those promises and encouragements, given by these prophets to the Jewish church, of its being now but a little while to the coming of the Messiah, and of his coming suddenly or very shortly, if no more could with good grounds be understood by them, than that it might be yet many ages longer before the Messiah should come, than10 it had been since the promises were first given to Abraham: as it would be if the Messiah is not yet come, it being at that time but about 1400 years since the calling of Abraham; it being now, since Haggai's time, about 2260 years, which is above twice so long as then it had been from the time of God's first incorporating the church of Israel and revealing the Messiah to them, and entering into the covenant with them in the wilderness, by more than 300 years.

5. Though it was foretold by the prophets Haggai and Malachi that the Messiah would come in a little time, yet by the plain prophecies of Daniel, the time wherein the Messiah would appear to set up his kingdom in the world, would not be till the three first monarchies were past--viz. the Babylonian, Persian and Grecian--and the fourth monarchy was established in the world. This is manifest by the 2nd chapter of Daniel, and also by the 7th chapter of Daniel. The first of these four monarchies is the Babylonian, by the express words of Daniel in interpreting Nebuchadnezzar's dream. And the second and third are the Persian and Grecian that followed, which also appears by the express explication of the angel Gabriel; compare Dan. 2:38-39 and ch. 7:4-6, 17, 23 with Dan. 8:1-8 and vv. 20-21. So that the fourth monarchy is undoubtedly the Roman. So that plainly, by the prophecies of the Daniel, the kingdom of the Messiah was not to commence till after the Babylonian, Persian and Grecian monarchies were utterly destroyed, and the Roman monarchy had succeeded and was established in the world.

And 'tis remarkable that Jesus was born just at that time when the Roman monarchy was completed and fully established in the earth, in the reign of Augustus Caesar, when the wars of the Romans, by which they subdued other monarchies and kingdoms, were ended, and the world was subdued under the Romans and settled peaceably under their authority; and when the Roman government was become a monarchy or kingdom, as it is called in Daniel, under the government of a monarch; and when the wars that were occasioned by the change of the government from a commonwealth to a monarchy were finished, and the whole empire was established in peace under one monarch, the first Roman Emperor, Augustus Caesar, then Jesus was born.11

6.12 It was foretold by the prophet Ezekiel, prophesying against the prince of the house of David, that the crown, or supreme civil power of the kingdom of Israel, should be taken of[f] from him, and from that family to which it properly belonged by God's appointment,13 and should be changed three times from one family to another, and each time perverted--that is, given to a family that had no just title to it, not being of the family of David, and so not properly of the royal family of Israel--and should be in that family no more till the Messiah should come, and that then God would give it him.

This seems to be the most natural and just understanding of that prophecy, Ezek. 21:25-27, "And thou, profane wicked prince of Israel, whose day is come, when iniquity shall have an end, thus saith the Lord God; Remove the diadem, and take off the crown: this shall not be the same: exalt him that is low, and abase him that is high. I will overturn, overturn, overturn, it: and it shall be no more, until he come whose right it is; and I will give it him." The more literal translation of the 26th and 27th verses, which makes the sense more easy, is thus: "Thus saith the Lord God; Remove the diadem, and take off the crown: this shall not be the same: exalt him that is low, abase him that is high: perverted, perverted, perverted, will I cause it to be (also this shall not be), till he shall come to whom it is rightly adjudged; and I will give it him."

Here, God is said to cause the crown to be perverted, i. e. according to the idiom of that language, given to, or seized by them that have no right to it, unjustly adjudged to them, or usurped by them. 'Tis here foretold that God in his providence would order the crown to be thus perverted time after time, changed from one family to another, abasing those that are high and have the possession of the crown, and then raising another family that is low, and then another, and so removing the crown from one to another; the diadem not having any rest in any of these families, because it is not yet settled where it belongs. God shall say in his providence, concerning those different families, "This family or this kingdom or power shall not continue"; and after that has possessed the crown a while, he shall remove it again and say, "Also this shall not be"; and so shall continue to do, till he comes to whom the diadem of the house of David properly belongs: and it shall be given to him, there be settled, and removed no more. And 'tis particularly here signified that the diadem should be thus perverted or overturned three times before the coming of this person.

10.24 It was foretold that he was to be, in some respect, of the posterity of Zerubbabel the son of Shealtiel. Hag. 2:23, "In that day, saith the Lord of hosts, will I take thee, O Zerubbabel, my servant, the son of Shealtiel, saith the Lord, and will make thee as a signet: for I have chosen thee, saith the Lord of hosts." The day here spoken of, I have already shown,25 is the time of the Messiah. This prophecy, where God tells Zerubbabel that he had chosen him, to make him as a signet, implies that God had chosen him26 out from all the people, to be exalted above all the rest, as chief in dignity and honor among the people; he that is as God's signet on his right hand, is he that is next to God and in chief dignity. See Jer. 22:24. This therefore implies that, as Zerubbabel was now chief among the people, being their governor, so then at the time of the Messiah, he should be chief and be advanced higher still, to be a glorious king over Israel. And because nothing is more manifest than that in the days of the Messiah, the Messiah himself is to be the King, the Prince, God's chosen and anointed, to be beautiful and glorious, excellent and comely, highly exalted, held in God's right hand, etc.: hence it follows that Zerubbabel at that day was to be the Messiah.

And seeing this can't be intended of Zerubbabel's person, hence the most easy and reasonable construction that can be put upon the prophecy, is that the Messiah should be Zerubbabel, in the same sense in which he is often, in the prophecies of Scripture, said to be David, viz. that he is the seed and heir of Zerubbabel.

And 'tis remarkable that, according to the account that the Evangelists give of Jesus, he was the seed of Zerubbabel the son of Shealtiel or Salathiel, both legally by Joseph and really by Mary, or that both Joseph and Mary were of the posterity of Zerubbabel. See Matt. 1:12-13 and Luke 3:27. In him, two genealogies unite.

And here it is worthy to be remarked, that soon after Jesus came, the Jews lost their genealogies, and they have now for many ages been without any distinction of families or tribes; so that the case has now for a long time been such, that upon the supposition that the Messiah is not already long since come, it would be impossible that those things, viz. his being of the posterity of Zerubbabel, or of the seed of David, or of the tribe of Judah, should be any proper notes by which he should be known and distinguished: because it could not be known without an immediate revelation from heaven, whether he had those things appertaining to him or no. But that can be no proper mark or note to make the Messiah known, that needs a revelation from heaven to make itself known. If we suppose the Messiah is yet to come, and that when he comes it shall be revealed from heaven that he is the Messiah, and so that he is of the tribe of Judah, of the seed of David and Zerubbabel: in this case, his being of this lineage would be no note at all to make him known, or in any wise to confirm the knowledge of him; it is not these marks that do at all make him known, but only that immediate revelation from heaven: for the knowledge of these marks themselves depends entirely on that revelation; they derive all their evidence from that revelation, and therefore can add no evidence to the revelation. Therefore, an immediate revelation from heaven that he is the Messiah, is not strengthened at all by them.

One main end of the prophecies that went before of the Messiah, was to give evidences of the Messiah, and in that respect to prepare the way for his coming. The prophets were sent as forerunners, to bear witness of the Messiah, to declare him and describe him, that he might be known when he came. And therefore, we may suppose that what they said of the time, place and circumstances of his coming, and of his person, lineage, etc., was declared as that by which he might be known; but what they said concerning his being of such a tribe and such a family, can be of no such use, unless the Messiah be already come.

14.31 It is evident by the prophecies of the Messiah, that he was to be God. Is. 9:6, "For unto us a child is born, unto us a son is given: [. . .] and his name shall be called Wonderful, Counselor, The mighty God, The everlasting Father, The Prince of Peace"; Zech. 6:12-13, "Behold the man whose name is The Branch; and he shall grow up out of his place" (i. e. out of the place of Joshua, mentioned in the foregoing verse), "and he shall build the temple of JEHOVAH: even he shall build the temple of JEHOVAH; and he shall bear the glory, and shall sit and rule upon his throne; and he shall be a priest upon his throne: and the counsel of peace shall be between them both." Here, when it is said "he shall bear the glory," there is doubtless reference to one of these two things, either:

1. That he should have upon him that shining brightness which the Jews called the Shechinah, which is so often in the Old Testament called the glory of the Lord, which was in the former temple above the mercy seat but was wanting in this latter temple; which the Jews greatly lamented, for though the word in the original ben't the same with that commonly used to signify the Shechinah, yet 'tis a word of the same signification. And if this be the meaning--if it was to be so that the Shechinah was to rest upon him; if he was to bear the glory of Jehovah as his glory--then 'tis evident he was to be God. 'Tis exceeding evident by Is. 42:8, "I am Jehovah: that is my name; and my glory will I not give unto another"; and again, Is. 48:11, "I will not give my glory to another." Or,

2. Here is reference to the beauty and glory of the temple, wherein this latter house fell vastly short of the former; as is expressed, Hag. 2:3, "Who is left among you that saw this house in her first glory? and how do ye see it now? is it not in your eyes in comparison of it as nothing?" And if we understand it thus, it will come to the same thing. And the argument will appear the same, if we compare this place with that, Hag. 2:3, taken together with what follows in the same chapter, vv. 7-9: "And I will shake all nations, and the desire of all nations shall come: and I will fill this house with glory, saith the Lord of hosts. . . . The glory of this latter house shall be greater than the glory of the former." Here, where it is lamented that the glory of the latter house was nothing in comparison of the glory of the former, God promises to make the glory of the latter greater than the glory of the former, in that he would fill the house with glory by the coming of the Messiah, the desire of all nations: where the word in the original, is the same with that commonly used to signify the Shechinah. And when God says here, "I will fill this house with glory," without doubt the same is intended as when it is said, I Kgs. 8:11, "the glory of the Lord filled the house." So that the prophecies of Haggai and Zechariah compared together do plainly show how the Messiah was to bear the glory, and in what sense he was to be the beauty, ornament and splendor of the second temple, whereby it should exceed the former, viz. that in his being in the house, the glory of the Lord being upon him, the glory of the Lord should fill the house. And inasmuch as God has once and again declared that he will not give his glory to another, this proves that the Messiah was to be God.

Again, 'tis evident by this prophecy that the Messiah was to be a divine person, because here it is said that he should sit upon the throne of Jehovah: for when it is said, "he shall sit and rule upon his throne," the word his is a relative that can have respect to no other antecedent than that last mentioned, viz. JEHOVAH. And the words immediately following confirm it: "and the counsel of peace shall be between them both." Which must relate to him and Jehovah, whose temple he is said to build, and whose throne he is said to sit upon, in the immediately preceding words. If the Messiah was to sit on the throne of Jehovah, it shows him to be God, because God will not give his glory to another; and innumerable texts do show the absurdity of supposing that the great Jehovah admits any other to sit and rule on his throne. And this confirms what has been said on the preceding expression, "he shall bear the glory." It was there, on the throne of God in the Holy of Holies, that the glory of the Lord used to appear; now it is promised that, instead of that, the Messiah should sit on that throne, bearing the glory of the Lord in his own person. And what greatly strengthens the argument is this, viz. that it is evident by comparing these two prophecies together, that for the children of Israel thus to have the Messiah among them, sitting on the mercy seat in the temple, is spoken of as a far greater privilege, than to have God manifesting himself there as he had done in the former temple. Which we can't suppose if, instead of Jehovah himself sitting in the midst of the people on his throne as their king and there manifesting himself in his glory, they were hereafter to have a mere creature and mere man placed there as their king.

Again, the same may be argued from Daniel's vision of the Messiah in Dan. 7[:13], who, though [he] is said to be "like the Son of man," yet is said to come "with the clouds of heaven." Now the clouds of heaven were a known symbol of the Divinity among the Jews from the time of Moses.

Again, that the Messiah was to be God is further evident by Mal. 3:1-2. "Behold, I will send my messenger, and he shall prepare the way before me: and the Lord, whom ye seek, shall suddenly come to his temple, even the messenger of the covenant, whom ye delight in: behold, he shall come, saith the Lord of hosts. But who may abide the day of his coming? and who shall stand when he appeareth? for he is like a refiner's fire, and like fullers' soap."

Here it is exceeding evident that the person whose coming is here foretold, is God. And the following words show it to be God: "the Lord, whom ye seek, shall suddenly come into his temple." This is undoubtedly the same coming into the temple, to restore its glory, that Haggai and Zechariah speak of. "The Lord, whom ye seek": they were lamenting that God did not appear in this temple, as he used to in the former, and this was what they earnestly sought after. Here, it is promised that he should come in a little time. This Lord, whom they sought after, desired and delighted in, is undoubtedly the same with "the desire of all nations," that Haggai foretells should come into the temple. Thus the Messiah is here called the angel of the covenant," which plainly shows that he was God or a divine person. 23.63 There are a great many representations in the Prophets of wonderful things of an outward and temporal nature to be brought to pass in the Messiah's days, and of many externally glorious circumstances of the people of God in the time of his kingdom that, it is evident, either from the nature of the representations them[selves], or [as] may be made plain by the Old Testament, are not literally but figuratively and mystically to be understood.

To put this matter beyond doubt, I would take notice of the following things:

1. Nothing is more plain, than that it was the manner of old to utter prophecies in very enigmatical and dark speeches, making use of bold figures and allegories; and therefore, predictions uttered by inspiration from God were called parables. So Balaam's prophesies were called parables [Num. 22]. And a view of the prophecies themselves, that were uttered from time to time from the beginning of the world, do plainly show it. Agreeable hereto, the prophets used typical representations of future events in actions, as well as words.

2. It would be absurd to take that text literally, [Is. 55:]12, "the mountains and hills shall break forth before you into singing, and all the trees of the field shall clap their hand." 3. Upon a supposition that the representations of outward, glorious things in the days of the Messiah are to be understood literally, the prophecies are exceeding inconsistent one with another.

As particularly, those prophecies that foretell all nations going up to the temple at Jerusalem and bringing their offerings to the altar of God there, as the children of Israel used to do, as particularly those, Is. 2:2-3, Mic. 4:1-2, and Is. 56:6-8 and Is. 60:6-13, Zech. 14:16-19. I say, those things are inconsistent with that in Is. 19:19, 21, "In that day there shall be an altar to the Lord in the midst of the land of Egypt, and a pillar at the border thereof to the Lord. . . . And the Lord shall be known to Egypt, and the Egyptians shall do sacrifice and oblation," etc.; and that, Mal. 1:11, "For from the rising of the sun even to the going down of the same, my name shall be great among the Gentiles; and in every place incense shall be offered unto my name, and a pure offering: for my name shall be great among the heathen, saith the Lord God"; Dan. 9:24, "Seventy weeks are determined . . . to finish the transgression, and to make an end of sins, and to make reconciliation for iniquity, and to bring in everlasting righteousness, and to seal up the vision and prophecy"; v. 27, "he shall cause the sacrifice and oblation to cease."

This that is said of all nations going up to the mountain of the house of the Lord, and being gathered in a great congregation there, as the children of Israel were wont to be, is also inconsistent with Ezekiel's description of the measures of the court of the temple, Ezek. 42:14 to the end; which, though very large, yet was in no wise large enough to contain such a congregation, nor if it were a hundred times as large. And what is inconsistent with both those things--viz. with all nations being gathered to the temple, and with the whole description of Ezekiel's temple, and all that is said about it, literally understood--is that elsewhere it is represented that there should be no temple or altar literally so, in the times of the Messiah's kingdom. Is. 66:1-2, "Thus saith the Lord, The heaven is my throne, and the earth is my footstool: where is the house that ye build unto me? and where is the place of my rest? For all those things hath mine hand made, and all those things have been, saith the Lord: but to this man will I look, even to him that is poor and of a contrite spirit, and trembles at my word." This is inconsistent with what is so often represented in the prophecies of God's reigning in Jerusalem and Mt. Zion, and in the temple, in the times of the Messiah. For this prophecy represents the throne of God as not in any earthly temple or city, but in heaven, at that time; and so his throne is represented as being in heaven, in Dan. 7:9-10, 13-14. Again, Jer. 3:16-17 is altogether inconsistent with there being any temple in the days of the Messiah's kingdom: "And it shall come to pass, when ye be multiplied and increased in the land, in those days, saith the Lord, they shall no more say, The ark of the covenant of the Lord: neither shall it come into mind: neither shall they remember it; neither shall they visit it; neither shall that be done any more. At that time they shall call Jerusalem the throne of the Lord; and all the nations shall be gathered together unto it, to the name of the Lord, to Jerusalem." This is inconsistent with the nations being gathered together to the temple, to offer sacrifices before the throne of God in the mountain of the temple. If Jerusalem itself be God's throne, instead of the ark and mercy seat above [or] upon67 it in the temple, where is any room for the temple to contain and be the place of God's throne? Or shall we suppose that, instead of the house containing the throne of God, the throne of God contains the house of God? This overthrows the literal sense of all that is said in the prophecies of the times of the Messiah about the temple of the Lord, the mountain of the house of the Lord and offering sacrifices upon the altar of the Lord, and God's placing his tabernacle amongst his people, the business of the priests and Levites in the temple, the glory of the Lord in the temple.

What is said, Ezek. 45 and 46, of the ordinances of the Passover, that grand memorial of God's bringing the children of Israel up out of Egypt, if literally understood, is inconsistent with Jer. 16:14-15 and ch. 23:7-8, which imply that those memorials of that deliverance should cease. And so what is prophesied, Ezek. 46, of the sacrifices for the new moons, is inconsistent with Is. 60:20, "thy moon shall not withdraw it[self]"; i. e. it shall continue to shine upon thee without ceasing. But this is inconsistent with there being any new moons, for the moon does remarkably withdraw itself as it changes, and ceases to shine at all for many days together. And what is said in the same chapter of the sacrifices that were to be offered to God every morning, is inconsistent with what is said, Is. 60:20, "thy sun shall no more go down"; and if it no more goes down, then neither shall it any more arise, and so there will be no mornings. 'Tis also represented, in Zech. 14:6-7, that there shall be no more processions of day and night, and interchanges of light and darkness, but that it should be all one continued day, being light at the time that the evening or night used to be.

Again, these prophecies of there being no successions of day and night, if we understand all literally, are directly contradicted by Jer. 33:20-21, where is an implicit, strong declaration that there should be, in the days of the Messiah, day and night in their season. "Thus saith the Lord; If you can break my covenant of the day, and my covenant of the night, that there should not be day and night in their season; then may also my covenant be broken with David my servant," etc. Here is a plain prophecy of the Messiah, as appears by vv. 14-15. Putting these things together, therefore, the words do especially imply that that covenant of the day and night, should not be broken at the time of the fulfillment of this prophecy. Those prophecies of a continual day, are also inconsistent with the prophecies that speak of day and night in the days of the Messiah, as Is. 4:5, and 27:3, and 60:11 and 62:6.

There shall the great owl make her nest, and lay, and hatch, and gather under her shadow: there shall the vultures also be gathered, every one with her mate. Seek ye out of the book of the Lord, and read: no one of these shall fail, none shall want her mate: for my mouth hath commanded, and his spirit hath gathered them. And he hath cast the lot for them, and his hand hath divided it unto them by line: they shall possess it for ever, from generation to generation shall they dwell therein."

Again, in Is. 60:20, it is said that the "sun shall no more go down" upon Jerusalem." And yet, in the same chapter, v. 11, 'tis implied that there be a succession of day and night: "Therefore thy gates shall be open continually; they shall not be shut day nor night."

And those prophecies of the creation of new heavens and a new earth, Is. 65:17 and 66:22, are, if literally understood, inconsistent with the literal sense of other things in those very chapters, as this, ch. 65:9-10, "And I will bring forth a seed out of Jacob, and out of Judah an inheritor of my mountains: and mine elect shall inherit it, and my servants shall dwell there. And Sharon shall be a field of flocks, and the valley of Achor a place for the herds to lie down in"; and that, ch. 66:19, "And I will set a sign among them, and will send those that escape of them to the nations to Tarshish, Pul, and Lud, that draw the bow, to Tubal, and Javan, to the isles afar off, that have not heard my fame." There are all countries and places of the old earth, which are herein represented as yet remaining.

5. It seems to be intimated by God himself, that when he had exhibited things by the prophets in their visions and prophecies, he had made use of figurative and typical representations. Hos. 12:10, "I have also spoken by the prophets, and I have multiplied visions, and used similitudes, by the ministry of the prophets."

6. The prophets, in many instances when they make representations of glorious external things, do apply these representations to spiritual things, and explain 'em of them; as that remarkable prophecy in Is. 65:17, of God's creating new heavens and a new earth, that "the former may not be remembered, nor come into mind," is explained in a spiritual sense, in the words immediately following, to signify the destruction of the people of God: "But be ye glad and rejoice for ever in that which I create: for, behold, I create Jerusalem a rejoicing, and her people a joy." In Is. 2:13-14, it is foretold that "the day of the Lord" should be "upon all the cedars of Lebanon, that are high and lifted up, and upon all the oaks of Bashan, and upon all the high mountains, and upon all the hills that are lifted up." But this in the 17th verse following, is explained in a spiritual sense, for abasing the pride of men: "And the loftiness of man shall be bowed down, and the haughtiness of men shall be made low: and the Lord alone shall be exalted in that day." And whereas the prophecies often speak of a glorious throne, on which God shall appear and reign in the days of the Messiah, the prophet Jeremiah, ch. 3:17, declares what that throne is, and shows it to be spiritual, and not anything material like the golden throne over the ark, viz. the church of God: "they shall no more say, the ark of the covenant of the Lord: [. . .] At that time they shall call Jerusalem the throne of the Lord." And whereas the prophecies often speak of the great glory of the temple in the days of the Messiah,the prophet Haggai represents that glory, not to an external glory, as that which is by silver and gold, but a spiritual glory, viz. the glorious manifestations of God's presence and grace, in that place, Hag. 2:7-9, "the desire of all nations shall come: and I will fill this house with glory, saith the Lord of hosts. The silver is mine, and the gold is mine, saith the Lord of hosts. The glory of this latter house shall be greater than of the former, saith the Lord of hosts: and in this place will I give peace, saith the Lord of hosts." And so other things that appertain to the external glory of God's people that is represented in the Prophets, are explained of things spiritual, by the prophets themselves. Their strong fortresses, walls, and bulwarks and gates, are explained to be spiritual, Is. 26:1, "We have a strong city; salvation will God appoint for walls and bulwarks"; Is. 60:18, "thou shalt call thy walls Salvation, and thy gates Praise"; Zech. 2:4-5, "Jerusalem shall be inhabited as towns without walls . . . For I, saith the Lord, will be unto her for a wall of fire round about, and will be the glory in the midst of her"; and Is. 33:6, "And wisdom and knowledge shall be the stability of thy times, and strength of salvation." And so their riches, so often spoken of, is explained to be spiritual there, in the next words: "the fear of the Lord is his treasure." So their bountiful and glorious clothing is explained to be spiritual, even their holiness and righteousness. Is. 52:1, "put on thy beautiful garments, O Jerusalem, the holy city: for henceforth there shall no more come unto thee the uncircumcised and the unclean"; and Zech. 3:4, "Take away the filthy garments from him. And unto him he said, Behold, I have caused thine iniquity to pass from thee, and I will clothe thee with change of raiment." And their glorious ornaments, and beautiful crown and diadem, is explained to be spiritual, Is. 28:5, "In that day shall the Lord of hosts be for a crown of glory, and for a diadem of beauty, to the residue of this people." In Is. 4:6, it is foretold, that in the days of the Messiah, God's people should have refuge or hiding places, and a shadow from the heat and a covert from storm and from wind. But in ch. 32:2, this matter is explained, and 'tis shown to be a spiritual hiding place from the wind and storm, and a shadow from the heat, that God's people shall then have, viz. the Messiah himself: "And a man shall be an hiding place from the wind, and a covert from the tempest; . . . and the shadow of a great rock in a weary land." We often read in the Prophets of rivers of water that God will give in the parched wilderness, or dry and thirsty land. But in the same Is. 32:2, it is shown what those rivers of water in a dry land are; and 'tis explained of that which is spiritual: "a man shall be [. . .] rivers of water in a dry place." And again, ch. 33:21, "the glorious Jehovah will be unto us a place of broad rivers and streams": this further shows how we are to understand that river of living water that goes down from Mt. Zion into the desert, spoken of in the 37th chapter of Ezekiel. And as we are to understand that part of the description, which Ezekiel gives us, of the state of things in the temple and land of Israel, and of the world under the Messiah, so doubtless we are understand the whole description contained in the nine last chapters of Ezekiel's prophecy.

And whereas the glory of the days of the Messiah is frequently set forth by a great increase of light--that the light of the moon shall be as the light of the sun, and the light of the sun sevenfold, and that there shall be no interruption of darkness by successions of day and night, but a continual light without the sun's going down--but there are several passages in the Prophet that show that those things are to be understood in a spiritual sense, and that this sun and this light are spiritual. Is. 60:19, "The sun shall no more be thy light by day; neither for brightness shall the moon give light unto thee: but the Lord shall be unto thee an everlasting light, and thy God thy glory"; and Mal. 4:2, "But unto you that fear my name shall the Sun of righteousness," etc. The anointing of the Messiah is explained of a spiritual anointing; see forward, §32. The prophecies foretell glorious conquests the Messiah should make, but yet these are in effect explained of spiritual conquests, in that the prophecies themselves represent the Messiah's weapons, by which he shall conquer, to be spiritual. See forward, [§109].71

7. The Jews themselves, to whom those prophecies were first delivered, had no notion that they were to be fulfilled literally, but understood them as visionary, mystical representations of future events; and therefore excused themselves in neglecting them with this, that the meaning of them was very mysterious. Is. 29:11-12, "And the vision of all is become unto you as the words of a book that is sealed, which men deliver to one that is learned, saying, Read this, I pray thee: and he saith, I cannot; for it is sealed: and the book is delivered to him that is not learned, saying, Read this I pray thee: and he saith, I am not learned";72 v. 14, "Therefore, behold, I will proceed to do a marvelous work amongst this people, even a marvelous work and a wonder: for the wisdom of their wise men shall perish, and the understanding of their prudent men shall be hid."

8. Some of the Rabbins acknowledge that Ezekiel's temple is mystical and spiritual, and that by the city is represented the church. And the Talmudists affirm that by Jerusalem we are to understand the gathering of the Gentiles to the Messiah, or the whole body of the Messiah's people. Greenhill on the nine[teen] last chapters of Ezekiel, p. 627.73
