type="sermon"> "The Fulfillment of the Prophecies of the Messiah" Miscellanies no. Misc. 1068.4 (ed.) Jonathan Edwards

145.1 According to the prophecies of the Old Testament, all people that should acknowledge and cleave to the Messiah, whether Jews or Gentiles, or of whatever nation, should be gathered into one society or company of servants of God, and should be all united as one people of God. Deut. 32:43, "Rejoice, O ye nations, with his people." In many places taken notice of in the foregoing section, 'tis represented as though all should dwell together in the same land, as Ezek. 47:22 and Is. 49:18-20, Is. 19. 24; and as flowing together to the same city, Jerusalem, or Zion, and meeting together in the same house of the Lord, and offering sacrifice on one altar to worship, as all having a joint interest in that city and temple and altar, as Ps. 100 and Ps. 48:2, Ps. 96:6-9, Is. 2:2-3, Mic. 4:1-2, Is. 56:6-7, Is. 60:3-7, 9, 11, 13-16, Is. 66:19-21, Zech. 8:22-23, Zech. 14:16. See Zech. 14:9, "And the Lord shall be king over all the earth: in that day shall there be one Lord, and his name one"; Gen. 49:10, "and to him shall the gathering of the people be"; Ps. 102:21-22, "To declare the name of the Lord in Zion, and his praise in Jerusalem; when the people are gathered together, and the kingdoms, to serve the Lord"; Is. 66:18, "I will gather all nations and tongues; and they shall come, and see my glory"; Is. 11:10, "And in that day there shall be a root of Jesse, which shall stand for an ensign of the people; to it shall the Gentiles seek: and his rest shall be glorious"; v. 12, "And he shall set up an ensign for the nations, and shall assemble the outcasts of Israel, and gather together the dispersed of Judah from the four corners of the earth"; Is. 19:23-24, "In that day shall there be a highway out of Egypt to Assyria, and the Assyrian shall come into Egypt, and the Egyptian into Assyria, and the Egyptians shall serve with the Assyrians. In that day shall Israel be the third with Egypt and with Assyria, even a blessing in the midst of the land"; Is. 49:22, "Thus saith the Lord God, Behold, I will lift up mine hand to the Gentiles, and set up my standard to the people: and they shall bring thy sons in their arms, and thy daughters shall be carried." Here and in the four foregoing verses [Is. 49:18-21], the convert Gentiles are represented as being gathered unto one church, and all as being the children of the same church, and as it were one family: "Lift up thine eyes round about, and behold: all these gather themselves together unto thee. As I live, saith the Lord, thou shalt surely clothe thee with them all, as with an ornament, and bind them on, as a bride doth. . . . The children which thou shalt have, after thou hast lost the other, . . . Who hath begotten me these, seeing I have lost my children, . . . who hath brought me up these?" So Is. 60:3-5, 8-9; so Is. 54:1, etc., and Is. 66:20. See Is. 56:8, "The Lord which gathereth the outcasts of Israel saith, Yet will I gather others to him, besides those that are gathered to him." See also v. 3, "Neither let the son of the stranger [. . .] speak, saying, The Lord hath utterly separated me from his people." See also Jer. 12:14 to the end.

See further, in the next section.

146.2 When God's people that should be in the days of the kingdom of the Messiah, are spoken of under the names of Jacob, Israel, Jerusalem, Zion, the daughters of Jerusalem, and the daughter of Zion, and such like names that God's visible church was wont to be called by under the old testament, thereby is for the most part intended all the people of God and followers of the Messiah that should be in those days, not only of the Jews, but Gentiles and of all nations, without distinction. (Here see "Miscellanies," nos. 49, 100, 597, 601, 649, 658.)3 What has been already observed, might be sufficient to put this matter out of question: for it has already been shown, §101,4 that the prophecies do represent that not the being of any particular nation would qualify persons in the days of the Messiah's kingdom, to be received as the people of God or the people of the Messiah, but that those that were righteous and holy only should be admitted to those privileges; and (§100) that all that were righteous, of whatever nation they were, should be owned as God's people and admitted to the blessings of the Messiah's kingdom. And it has been shown in the preceding sections, that the prophets did also abundantly foretell that the greater part of the nation of the Jews, should be rejected from being God's people; and that the Gentiles should be called and owned as God's people in their room, and admitted, to all intents and purposes, to the same privileges that they had before their rejection; and that the remnant of that nation which should own the Messiah, and should not be rejected, should have [those privileges], as being united to them, as the same society and people and family of God with them. These things, put together, may be sufficient to convince anyone that when the prophets speak of God's Israel, and people of Jerusalem and Zion, that should be owned as his people in the days of the Messiah's kingdom, and should enjoy the benefits of the Messiah's [kingdom], this is the people that is intended: viz. God's people, both of Jews and Gentiles, thus united as one body, society and family after the rejection of the greater part of the Jews, and bringing in of the Gentiles to fill their room.

But yet I would observe further, in some particulars,5 how plainly this is implied in the prophecies. The people of God of the Gentile nations are represented as speaking the same language with Israel, as those that were of the same nation were wont to do. Is. 19:18, "five cities in the land of Egypt shall speak the language of Canaan." The Gentile converts are spoken of as the brethren of the people of Jerusalem and Zion, and so of the same descent, of the same nation or family. Is. 66:18-21, "and I will gather all nations and tongues; and they shall come, and see my glory. And I will set a sign among them, and I will send those that escape of them unto the nations, to Tarshish, Pul, and Lud, that draw the bow, to Tubal, and Javan, to the isles afar off, that have not heard my fame, neither have seen my glory; and they shall declare my glory among the Gentiles. And they shall bring all your brethren for an offering unto the Lord out of all nations . . . to my holy mountain Jerusalem, saith the Lord, as the children of Israel bring an offering in a clean vessel into the house of the Lord. And I will also take of them for priests and for Levites." They are the people of Jerusalem and Zion that are spoken, of whose brethren those Gentile converts are here called, as is manifest by vv. 7-13.

And as they are called "the brethren," so they are often represented as the seed, offspring or children of Israel, or the church of Jerusalem or Zion, in the next verse to those words last cited, Is. 66:22. "For as the new heavens and new earth, which I will make, shall remain before me, saith the Lord, so shall your seed and your name remain." So they are, in Is. 54:1, "Sing, O barren, and thou that didst not bear; . . . for more are the children of the desolate than the children of the married wife, . . . For thou shalt break forth on the right hand and on the left; and thy seed shall inherit the Gentiles." She that is here spoken of in this chapter as God's wife, is manifestly the same with her that is often called Zion and Jerus[al]em in this great prophecy, continued from the beginning of the 40th chapter to the end of the book, as will be most manifest to anyone that reads the chapter through and compares it with other parts of this prophecy. Again, the Gentiles are represented as her children. Is. 44, at the beginning, "O Jacob my servant; and Israel whom I have chosen: [. . .] I will pour my spirit upon thy seed, and my blessing upon thine offspring: and they shall spring up as among the grass, as willows by the water courses. One shall say, I am the Lord's; another shall call himself by the name of Jacob." In Is. 49, the Gentile converts are represented as belonging to Zion, as much as clothes or ornaments belong to a bride, and as being her children. V. 14, "Zion hath said, The Lord hath forsaken me"; vv. 17, etc., "Thy children shall make haste; . . . Lift up thine eyes round about, and behold: all these gather themselves together, and come to thee. As I live, saith the Lord, thou shalt clothe thee with them, as with an ornament, and bind them on, as a bride doth"; vv. 20-22, "The children which thou shalt have, after thou hast lost the other, . . . Then thou shalt say in thine heart, Who hath begotten me these, . . . Thus saith the Lord, Behold, I will lift up my hand to the Gentiles, and set up my standard to the people: and they shall bring thy sons in their arms, and thy daughters shall be carried upon their shoulders"; Is. 60:3-4, "And the Gentiles shall come to thy light, . . . thy sons shall come from far, and thy daughters shall be nursed at thy side"; v. 9, "Surely the isles shall wait for me, and the ships of Tarshish first, to bring thy sons from far"; Is. 66:8, "Who hath heard such a thing? . . . Shall the earth be made to bring forth in one day? or shall a nation be born at once? for before Zion travailed, she brought forth her children"; Ezek. 16:61, "and I will give them to thee for daughters," speaking of Sodom and Samaria. These Gentile converts are represented as coming of Jacob and Israel, and the fruit that springs from his root. Is. 27:6, "He shall cause them that come of Jacob to take root: Israel shall blossom and bud, and fill the face of the world with fruit"; so Hos. 2:23, "And I will sow her unto me in the earth; and I will have mercy upon her that had not obtained mercy; and I will say to them that were not my people, Thou art my people; and they shall say, Thou art my God."

The Gentile converts are also represented as some of those that belong to the land of Israel, the same land that is the dwelling place of Israel, being their proper dwelling place. Thus, Is. 54:1-3, the church of God is called upon to enlarge the place of her tent, and to stretch forth the curtains of her habitation, because "more are the children of the desolate than the children of the married wife," signifying that the old habitation of her children is too strait, her children are so many by this addition; which implies that those new children belonged to her habitation, as well as her former children. So Is. 49:17-22, the Prophet, speaking of Zion's having a new kind of children after she has lost the other, and a vast multitude gathering themselves together to her, because God would lift up his hand to the Gentiles; it is said, vv. 19-20, "For thy waste and thy desolate places, and the land of thy destruction" (i. e. the land of Canaan, that has been made desolate) "shall even now be too narrow by reason of the inhabitants" (implying that those children belonged there, and would dwell there, were it not too narrow) "The children which thou shalt have, after thou hast lost the other, shall say again in thine ears, The place is too strait for me: give place, that I may dwell." So it is expressly prophesied that the Gentiles should dwell with the children, and be owned as some of the same people, and as if born of them, and as such have an inheritance among them. Ezek. 47:22, "And it shall come to pass, that ye shall divide it by lot for an inheritance unto you, and to the strangers that sojourn among you, which shall beget children among you: and they shall be unto you as born in the country among the children of Israel; they shall have inheritance with you among the tribes of Israel." The prophets declare that, in the days of the Messiah, it should not be a belonging to the nation of Israel, but a being righteous and holy, that should give persons a portion and inheritance in the land of Israel. As to the wicked, though they are of that nation, 'tis revealed that they should be utterly excluded. Ezek. 20:38, "And I will purge out from among you the rebels, and them that transgress against me: I will bring them forth out of the country where they sojourn, and they shall not enter into the land of Israel: and ye shall know that I am the Lord." See also Is. 57:13, "But when thou criest, let thy companies deliver thee; but the wind shall carry them away; vanity shall take them: but he that putteth his trust in me shall possess the land, and shall inherit my holy mountain."

They were called the people of Jerusalem, or the daughters of Jerusalem, not only that dwelt in Jerusalem, but the people that had an interest, right and memorial there, and came there to worship, Neh. 2:20. Hence, when Jerusalem was rebuilt after the captivity, all the people of the land assisted in it, and not only the inhabitants of the city, Nehemiah, ch. 3. So the people that are called by the name of Zion, were not those only that were the proper inhabitants of Zion: for if by Zion we understand the city of David, but few of the people dwelt there, probably only those that were of the king's household, or principal officers and attendants; or, if we understand it of the mountain of the temple, there were no inhabitants there but the priests and Levites, and their constant and ordinary abode was not there, but only they came and lodged there in the courses of their ministration. But they were called Zion, or the people of Zion, that were worshippers in Zion, and that had an interest in the sacred things of that holy mountain, and accordingly have admittance there from time to time. But the prophecies do represent that in the days of the Messiah's kingdom, it should not be a being of the nation of the Jews that should give any a right to that privilege, but a being holy and righteous. Is. 35:8-10, "And an highway shall be there, and a way, and it shall be called The way of holiness; the unclean shall not pass over it; but it shall be for those: . . . the redeemed of the Lord shall walk there: and the ransomed of the Lord shall return, and come to Zion with songs and everlasting joy upon their heads"; Is. 33:14, etc., "The sinners in Zion are afraid; . . . Who among us shall dwell with the devouring fire? . . . He that walketh righteously, . . . He shall dwell on high: his place of defense shall be the munitions of rocks: . . . Thine eyes shall see the king in his beauty: they shall behold the land that is very far off"; v. 20, "Look upon Zion, the city of our solemnities: thine eyes shall see Jerusalem a quiet habitation"; Ps. 69:35-36, "For God will save Zion, and will build the cities of Judah: that they may dwell there, and have it in possession. The seed also of his servants shall inherit it: and they that love his name shall dwell therein"; Is. 52:1, "Awake, awake; put on thy strength, O Zion; put on thy beautiful garments, O Jerusalem: for henceforth there shall no more come into thee the uncircumcised and the unclean." And as 'tis foretold that only the righteous should have this privilege, so it is foretold that all the righteous, of whatever part of the earth, should have this privilege. Ps. 24, at the beginning, "The earth is the Lord's, and the fullness thereof; the world, and they that dwell therein. For he hath founded it . . . Who shall ascend into the hill of the Lord? who shall stand in his holy place? He that hath clean hands, and a pure heart." And those that shall thus, on the account of their being righteous, have this privilege of entering the gates of Zion, are spoken of as a distinct nation by themselves, in Is. 26:2, "Open ye the gates, that the righteous nation which keepeth the truth may enter in." And what nation can this be, but that Israel of God, spoken of as having the benefits of the Messiah's kingdom?

Moreover, 'tis often more expressly signified by the prophets that the Gentile converts should have a part in Jerusalem, and should enter into Zion and God's holy mountain, as some of the worshippers and servants of God there. Jer. 3:17, "At that time they shall call Jerusalem the throne of the Lord; and all the nations shall be gathered unto it, to the name of the Lord, to Jerusalem"; Is. 2:2-3, "And it shall come to pass in the last days, that the mountain of the Lord's house shall be established," etc., "and all nations shall flow unto it. And many people shall go and say, Come ye, and let us go up to the mountain of the Lord, to the house of the God of Jacob"; and Mic. 4:1-2, the same words; Ps. 96:6-10, "Honor and majesty are before him: strength and beauty are in his sanctuary. Give unto the Lord, O ye kindreds of the people, give unto the Lord glory and strength. . . . bring an offering, and come into his courts. O worship the Lord in the beauty of holiness: fear before him, all the earth. Say among the heathen that the Lord reigneth"; Ps. 100, "Make a joyful noise unto the Lord, all ye lands. . . . come into his presence with singing. Know ye that the Lord he is God: . . . we are his people, and the sheep of his pasture. Enter into his gates with thanksgiving, and into his courts with praise"; Is. 56:6-8, "Also the sons of the stranger that join themselves to the Lord, . . . and taketh hold of my covenant; even them will I bring to my holy mountain, and make them joyful in my house of prayer: their burnt offerings and their sacrifices shall be accepted on mine altar; for mine house shall be called an house of prayer for all people. The Lord which gathereth the outcasts of Israel saith, Yet will I gather others to him, besides those that are gathered to him." God says to Zion, Is. 60:3, etc.,

the Gentiles shall come to thy light, and kings to the brightness of thy rising. Lift up thine eyes round about, and see: all they gather themselves together, they come to thee: thy sons shall come from far, . . . the abundance of the sea shall be converted unto thee, the forces of the Gentiles shall come unto thee. The multitude of camels shall cover thee, the dromedaries of Midian and Ephah; all they from Sheba shall come: they shall bring gold and incense; they shall show forth the praises of the Lord. All the flocks of Kedar shall be gathered together unto thee, and the rams of Nebaioth shall minister unto thee: they shall come up with acceptance on mine altar, and I will glorify the house of my glory. Who are these that fly as a cloud, and as the doves to their windows? Surely the isles shall wait for me, and the ships of Tarshish first, to bring thy sons from far, their silver and their gold with them, to the name of the Lord thy God, and to the Holy One of Israel, because he hath glorified thee;

v. 11, "thy gates shall be open continually; they shall not be shut day nor night; that men may bring unto thee the forces of the Gentiles"; Ps. 102:21-22, "To declare the name of the Lord in Zion, and his praise in Jerusalem; when the people are gathered together, and the kingdoms to serve the Lord"; Is. 25:6, "in this mountain shall the Lord of hosts make unto all people a feast of fat things, a feast of wines on the lees, of fat things full of marrow, of wines on the lees well refined"; Ps. 48:2, "the joy of the whole earth, is mount Zion"; Zech. 6:14-15, "And the crowns shall be to Helem, and to Tobijah," etc., "for a memorial in the temple of the Lord. And they that are far off shall come and build in the temple of the Lord"; Zech. 8:20-22, "there shall come people, and the inhabitants of many cities: and the inhabitants of one city shall go to another, saying, Let us go speedily to pray before the Lord, and to seek the Lord of hosts: I will go also. Yea, many people and strong nations shall come to seek the Lord of hosts in Jerusalem, and to pray before the Lord"; and ch. 9:1, "the eyes of man, as of all the tribes of Israel, shall be towards the Lord," i. e. as the tribes were wont to look to God's holy temple in Jerusalem, and go up thither to worship. See Ps. 122:4. Zech. 14:16-17, "every one that is left of all the nations," etc., "shall go up from year to year to worship the King, the Lord of hosts, and to keep the feast of tabernacles. And it shall be that whoso will not come up of all the families of the earth unto Jerusalem to worship the King, the Lord of hosts, even upon them shall be no rain."

God, in the 65th chapter of Isaiah, speaking very expressly and largely in that chapter of the rejection of the Jews and calling of the Gentiles in their room, says, in the 16th, 17th, 18th, [and] 19th verses, "and ye" (i. e. the rejected Jews) "shall leave your name for a curse unto my chosen: for the Lord God shall slay thee, and call his servants by another name: that he who blesseth himself in the earth shall bless himself by the God of truth," etc. "For, behold, I create new heavens and a new earth: . . . for the former shall not be remembered, nor come into mind. But be you glad and rejoice for ever in that which I create: for, behold, I create Jerusalem a rejoicing, and her people a joy." Here, this new people called by a new name, after the Jews were rejected and slain, are evidently the people of this new creation that God here calls the joyful people of JERUSALEM, that he would create; and then it follows in the next verse, "And I will rejoice over Jerusalem, and joy in my people." And these are said to be "the seed of the blessed of the Lord," v. 23.

In the 22nd Psalm, they that fear the Lord, the meek, they that seek the Lord, the Messiah's brethren, the congregation of the Lord, "the seed of Jacob," "the seed of Israel," the seed that should serve God, a people born to God, and the generation of the Lord, all seem to be used as synonymous expressions; and it is there declared that the Gentiles should be of them that should seek and serve God, and plainly intimated that of them should be this seed and generation of God. Vv. 22-23, "I will declare thy name unto my brethren: in the midst of the congregation will I praise thee. Ye that fear the Lord, praise him; all ye seed of Jacob, glorify him; and fear him, all ye seed of Israel"; vv. 25-31, "My praise shall be of thee in the great congregation: I will pay my vows before them that fear thee. The meek shall eat and be satisfied: they shall praise the Lord that seek him: your heart shall live for ever. All the ends of the world shall remember and turn to the Lord: and all the kindreds of the nations shall worship before thee. For the kingdom is the Lord's: and he is the governor among the nations. All they that be fat upon the earth shall eat and worship: . . . A seed shall serve him; it shall be accounted unto the Lord for a generation. They shall come, and declare his righteousness unto a people that shall be born, that he hath done this." And in the 24th Psalm, as 'tis signified that all of the inhabitants of the world that have clean hands and pure hearts, shall dwell in Zion in God's holy hill,6 so it [is] intimated that they should belong to Jacob. "The earth is the Lord's, and the fullness thereof; the world, and all that dwell therein. . . . Who shall ascend into the hill of the Lord? . . . He that hath clean hands, and a pure heart"; v. 6, "This is the generation of them that seek thee, that seek thy face, O JACOB." In the 50th Psalm, it seems to be pretty plainly declared that God's saints, or holy ones, only in the Messiah's days shall be called God's people and ISRAEL; but that the wicked shall be reputed as those that have nothing to do to take God's covenant into their mouth, as though they were some of God's Israel. Vv. 5-7, "Gather my saints together; those that have made a covenant with me by sacrifice. And the heavens shall declare his righteousness: . . . Hear, O MY PEOPLE, and I will speak; O ISRAEL, and I will testify against thee: I am God, even thy God." And so God goes7 on to speak to his saints and his Israel in a continued speech, to v. 16; and there Gods says thus: "But to the wicked God saith, What hast thou to do to declare my statutes, or that thou shouldst take my covenant into thy mouth?"

In Is. 63:16-17, those that are there represented as being of the tribes of God's inheritance, are spoken of as those that are indeed the children of God, but those that Abraham is ignorant of, and Israel acknowledges not. "Look down from heaven, and behold from the habitation of thy holiness and thy glory: . . . Doubtless thou art our father, though Abraham be ignorant of us, and Israel acknowledges us not: thou, O Lord, art our father, our redeemer; thy name is from everlasting. O Lord, why hast thou made us to err from thy ways, and hardened our heart from thy fear? Return for thy servants' sake, the tribes of thine inheritance."

In Is. 44, the Gentile converts are represented as calling themselves by the name of Jacob and Israel with God's approbation, and as expressing a great privilege God should give them. "O Jacob my servant; and thou, Jesurun, whom I have chosen. . . . I will pour my spirit upon thy seed, and my blessing upon thine offspring: and they shall spring up as among the grass, as willows by the water courses. One shall say, I am the Lord's; and another shall call himself by the name of Jacob; and another shall subscribe with his hand unto the Lord, and surname himself by the name of Israel" [vv. 2-5]. 'Tis evident that they herein take a name and honor that God gives them: for their so doing, is represented as what they are brought to by his Spirit poured upon them, and it is a privilege that is the consequence of his blessing; and the name, according to this scripture, belongs to them, for they are here represented as the seed and offspring of Jacob.

In Zech. 9, it is not only foretold, v. 1, that "the eyes of man should be towards the Lord, as all the tribes of Israel," but in v. 7, 'tis represented as though the Gentiles, even of the Philistines, should be accounted as belonging to Israel. 'Tis said, Ekron shall be "as a Jebusite"; but the Jebusites, who were hewers of wood and drawers of water for the house of God, were reputed, according to the Law of Moses, as some of God's covenant people, of the men of Israel, and of the posterity of Abraham, Isaac and Jacob. Deut. 29:10-13, "Ye stand this day all of you before the Lord your God; your captains of your tribes, your elders, and your officers, with all the men of Israel, your little ones, your wives, and thy stranger that is in the camp, from the hewer of thy wood unto the drawer of thy water: that thou shouldst enter into a covenant with the Lord thy God, and into his oath, which the Lord thy God maketh with thee this day: that he may establish thee today for a people unto himself, [. . .] as he hath said unto thee, and as he hath sworn unto thy fathers, to Abraham, to Isaac, and to Jacob." 167.49 'Tis evident by the prophecies of the Old Testament, that after the Messiah should come, his people should for a long time be in afflicted, persecuted circumstances before that time of their peace, prosperity, dominion and glory, so often foretold, should come.

'Tis evident by very many predictions that the people of the Messiah should be greatly maligned by the men of the world, and should suffer very exceedingly, and be brought very low by their persecutions; and that by the Messiah, they should obtain a glorious conquest over their persecutors, and be delivered from them. And it is evident from the prophecies that the people of the Messiah should be in a suffering, persecuted state after he should come. This is reasonable to suppose, from what the prophecies declare of the sufferings of the Messiah himself: if the prince shall suffer exceedingly, and be brought very low in his war with his own and his people's enemies, we must naturally suppose that the people suffer with him, as Moses and his people, and afterwards David and his followers suffered affliction together, and as a prince and people either prevail or suffer together in war; and not only is it reasonable to suppose this, but the prophecies do evidently represent that the people of [the] Messiah shall be made partakers of his sufferings from the hands of his enemies, and that in his victory and deliverance they shall be delivered and have victory. This is plainly supposed in that prophecy of the Messiah's sufferings and victory in the 22nd Psalm, in the 20th verse: "Deliver my soul from the sword; my darling from the power of the dog." It is supposed that in the deliverance of Christ's soul, his darling, or his church, is delivered from the power of the same cruel, persecuting foe; and so in what there follows, is set forth how his people that behaved with meekness under their suffering should rejoice, triumph and praise God with him. So when the great sufferings and deliverance of the Messiah are foretold in the 69th Psalm, his people, [which] are represented as being poor and prisoners with him, and bearing their sufferings in meekness, and crying to God for help, are delivered in his deliverance. Vv. 32-33, "The humble" (or "the meek") "shall see this, and be glad: and your heart shall live that seek God. For the Lord heareth the poor, and despiseth not his prisoners." So in the 89th Psalm, the Messiah, the prince of God's people, is represented as suffering, vv. 38 to the end: his crown cast to the ground, his hedges broken down, his strongholds brought to ruin, being spoiled by all that pass by, and being a reproach to his neighbors, his glory being made to cease, and his throne cast down to the ground, etc. And this his persecution, is represented as being the reproach and persecution of God's people, v. 50, "Remember, Lord, the reproach of thy servants; how do I bear in my bosom the reproach of all my neighbors?" And the matter is very plainly represented in the 102nd Psalm, as though Zion was persecuted with the Messiah her head, and suffered long in conformity to his sufferings: but at last, at the set time, has deliverance in a participation of the Messiah's deliverance. In the 11 first verses is livelily set forth the great sufferings of the Messiah from the cruelty of his enemies; and then, in the 12th and 13th verses, we have the antithesis: "But thou, O Lord, shalt endure for ever; and thy remembrance unto all generations. Thou shalt arise, and have mercy upon Zion: for the time to favor her, yea, the set time, is come"; which implies as much as this: "And though I suffer so much from the hands of my persecutors, and Zion, my darling, suffers with me, and in conformity to my persecutions, suffers very long, even through many generations, yet thou, her God, endurest unchangeable through all this long season; thou remainest through all generations; and though the time to favor Zion seem to be long delayed, yet there is a set time to favor, which will come at last." Then it follows in the next verse, "For thy servants take pleasure in her stones, and favor the dust thereof"; i. e. they take pleasure in her remains, though it be nothing but old rubbish stones and dust, the city having long lain waste. And then it is said, vv. 16-17, "When the Lord shall build up Zion, he shall appear in his glory. He will regard the prayer of the destitute, and not despise their prayer"; and vv. 19-20, "For the Lord looked down . . . to hear the groaning of the prisoner; and loose those that are appointed to death." That the church shall suffer with the Messiah, and be exalted in a participation of his exaltation, is also manifested by Zechariah, 3rd chapter: there, God, to encourage the Prophet with a prospect of a future glorious deliverance of his church from her enemies, exhibits to his view Joshua (or Jesus) standing before God, and Satan standing at his right hand to resist him; and the angel rebukes Satan, saying, "The Lord rebuke thee, O Satan; [. . .] is not this a brand plucked out of the fire?" And Joshua, as a type of Christ, has his filthy garments taken away, and is clothed in change of raiment, and having a fair or beautiful mitre set upon his head [vv. 1-5]. The Messiah and his people suffering together, and triumphing together, is compared to Moses' and his people's suffering and triumphing, in Zech. 10:10-11, "I will bring them also again out of the land of Egypt, . . . and will bring them again into the land of Gilead and Lebanon; . . . And he shall pass through the sea with affliction, . . . and the scepter of Egypt shall pass away."

The scattering of the people of the Messiah as a flock, and their being tried in the furnace of affliction, is very plainly spoken of as following the Messiah's sufferings, in Zech. 13:7-9. "Awake, O sword, against the man that is my fellow, saith the Lord of hosts: smite the shepherd, and the sheep shall be scattered: and I will turn mine hand upon the little ones. And it shall come to pass, that in all the land, saith the Lord, two parts therein shall be cut off and die; but the third shall be left therein. And I will bring the third part through the fire, and will refine them as silver is refined, and I will try them as gold is tried: and they shall call on my name, and I will hear them: I will say, It is my people: and they shall say, The Lord is my God."

In the 12th chapter of Daniel, the greatest time of the trouble and persecution of God's people of all that ever was, is represented as being after the appearance of Michael (or the Messiah), the great prince that standeth for God's people [v. 1]. And this their persecution, that should be after his coming, is represented in that chapter as of very long continuance.

Again, if we compare the prophecies of the Old Testament one with another, and with that history which is allowed by all, Christians, Jews and heathens, it may certainly be inferred that the people of the Messiah should be greatly persecuted long after he should appear in the world. It is foretold in the 9th of Daniel that it should be seventy weeks, or 490 years, from one of the edicts of the kings of Persia to restore and build Jerusalem after the captivity, unto the coming of the Messiah. And it is evident from history, that that time was out (let us reckon from which of the edicts we will), in the time of the fourth or Roman monarchy, while this monarchy was subject to one head or monarch, and that it continued many ages after that, before it came to be divided into ten kingdoms, as is foretold in that book of Daniel, chs. 2 and 7. But it is evident by the 7th of Daniel, that after this division of the fourth monarchy into ten kingdoms, there should arise an extraordinary power in the Roman Empire that should extraordinarily persecute the saints of the most High, and should continue to do so for a very long time, vv. 21 and 25.

168.50 It was foretold in the prophecies of the Old Testament that there should appear an extraordinary and new kind of sovereign in the Roman Empire, that should arise after this fourth monarchy should be divided into many kingdoms, that should set up a form of government very diverse from all others, and very noted for his craft, cruelty, pride and boasting, and pretending to great things: pretending to a great superiority above other princes of the Roman Empire; whose boasts and high pretenses should be of a religious nature; assuming divine honor, encroaching on the prerogatives of the most High; making new laws in religious matters, as though he had power to disannul or dispense with the laws of the most High, and introduce new ones; that he should be the heart of the fourth monarchy under its last form of government; and that he should continue a long time, and that this sovereign should arise from very small beginnings, Dan. 7:8, 11, 20-22, 24-26. And it is evident that there has arisen just such a kind of sovereign as this in the fourth monarchy, since this monarchy has been broken into many sovereignties, concerning whom all these things are exactly verified.51 Now it is foretold that this power should exceedingly persecute the saints of the most High, so as to wear them [out]. Doubtless, therefore, if we observe what people that is that have, in a peculiar manner and far above all others, [been] persecuted by this power, we may conclude that they are the true people of God. But the people that have been thus persecuted by this power, and that with such exceeding cruelty as no history can parallel, are those that are the professed followers of Jesus, that trust in him as the Messiah. Therefore, this people is the true people of God, and that Jesus they trust in, is the true Messiah: for this people is the people that are not only called the saints of the most High, but is the people that are represented as the people of the true Messiah in this prophecy, who shall with him triumph over this persecuting power, and should reign in great glory with the Messiah, vv. 13-14, 18, 21-22, 25-27.

172.56 The Messiah in the prophecies is represented not only as the Savior of God's people that should be then, after he should come into the world and set up his kingdom; but also the Redeemer of the saints in all ages, of those that lived many ages before his coming into the world, and even before the Mosaic dispensation. So God comforts Daniel, with respect to the many and great transgressions and iniquities of the church of the Jews that he had been confessing, with that, "Seventy weeks were determined upon thy people [. . .] to finish the transgression, and to make an end of sin, and to make reconciliation for iniquity" [Dan. 9:24]. And surely it is not natural to understand this exclusively of those sins of his and his people that then were, which he had been confessing and bewailing before, which was the very occasion of God's making this comfortable revelation to him. So the church of the Jews, when in great trouble, as being threatened with destruction in Ahaz's time, is comforted with that promise that "a virgin should conceive, and bear a son," etc., Is. 7[:14]; and this is mentioned to them as an evidence that they should be saved from the destruction they were now threatened with. So God's sending the Messiah into the world, is chiefly insisted on by the prophets Haggai and Zechariah to comfort the church of God at that day, with respect to the destruction they were threatened with from their numerous enemies that compassed 'em about. In the 3rd chapter of Zechariah, Joshua, that was then under Christ, the head of the church of the Jews, and is there spoken of as representing the whole church, is comforted with respect to the calamities that church was then subject to for their sins, and the destruction that they were threatened with, that God would forgive his iniquity, or cause it to pass from him, and so save him and his people from these calamities. V. 4, "And he answered and said to those that stood before him, saying, Take away the filthy garments from him. And unto him he said, Behold, I have caused thine iniquity to pass from thee, and I will clothe thee with change of raiment." And then the promise of the Messiah's coming and making full and perfect atonement for sin, is brought in as a confirmation and reason of this great benefit promised to Joshua and his people. Vv. 8-9, "Hear now, O Joshua the high priest, thou, and thy fellows that sit before thee: for they are men wondered at" (i. e. "Hear and believe this promise I have made of taking away your iniquities and those calamities that are the fruits of them, that make 'em a wonder to their neighbors): "for, behold, I will bring forth my servant the Branch. For behold the stone that I have laid before Joshua; upon one stone shall be seven eyes: behold, I will engrave the graving thereof, saith the Lord of hosts, and I will remove the iniquity of that land in one day." And in the next chapter, the Messiah, represented by this stone laid before Joshua as the foundation on which he should build the temple, having seven eyes graven on it: I say, the Messiah, represented by this stone, is here and in the next chapter represented as the Savior of that Jewish church that Joshua was then the head of, and even then building up. He is represented as the head or chief foundation stone of the temple Joshua was then building, in this 3rd chapter, 9th verse, and 4th chapter, 7th verse; and that church of the Jews is represented as being preserved and saved by these seven eyes that were graven on that stone, v. 10. And in the 6th chapter, the success and prosperity of Joshua in building of the temple, and restoring the church of the Jews, and the honor he and his fellows should receive, is represented as owing to the man whose name is the Branch. Vv. 10-12, "Take of them of the captivity, even of Heldai, and Tobijah, and of Jedaiah, which are come from Babylon, and come thou the same day, and go into the house of Josiah the son of Zephaniah; then take silver and gold, and make crowns, and set them upon the head of Joshua the son of Josedech, the high priest; and speak unto him, saying, Behold the man whose name is the Branch; and he shall grow up out of his place, and he shall build the temple of the Lord." And in Mal. 3:1,57 the Messiah is promised to the Jewish church, thus: "the Lord, whom ye seek, shall suddenly come into his temple, even the messenger of the covenant, whom YE DELIGHT IN"; which, according to the idiom of the Old Testament, implies as much as if it had been said, "the messenger of the covenant, whom ye trust in as your defense and savior": for delighting and rejoicing in a person or thing, is often used in the Old Testament for trusting in them for salvation. So Ps. 22:8, "He trusted on the Lord that he would deliver him: let him deliver him, seeing he delighted in him"; here the word rendered "delighted," is the same as in Malachi. Ps. 37:3-5, "Trust in the Lord, and do good; [. . .] and verily [. . .] thou shalt be fed. DELIGHT thyself also in the Lord; and he shall give thee the desires of thine heart. Commit thy way unto the Lord; trust also in him; and he shall bring it to pass"; Is. 8:6, "Forasmuch as this people refuseth the waters of Shiloah that go softly, and rejoice in Rezin and Remaliah's son"; Ps. 63:7, "Because thou hast been my help, therefore in the shadow of thy wings will I rejoice"; Hab. 3:17-19, "Although the fig tree shall not blossom, . . . yet I will rejoice in the Lord, and joy in the God of my salvation. The Lord God is my strength."
