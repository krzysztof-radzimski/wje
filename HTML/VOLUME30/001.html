"Prophecies of the Messiah" Miscellanies no. 1067.2 Misc. 1067.2 (ed.) Jonathan Edwards

61. ISAIAH 8:9-10, 14-17, AND CHAPTER 9:1-7. There are several passages in the 8th and 9th chapters of Isaiah, between those two great prophecies of the Messiah that have already been spoken of in Is. 7:14-15 and Is. 9:6-7, that are manifestly connected with those prophecies and relate to the times of the Messiah. I observed before, §19, that these two great prophecies seem to be connected one with the other, and that the discourse from one to the other seems to be continued. And here I would observe that nothing is more plain than that the 9th and 10th verses of ch. 8 are connected with the 14th verse of the foregoing chapter. The conclusion of the 10th verse shows [it]: "speak the word, and it shall not stand: for God is with us"; in the original it is Ki Immanuel, with a plain reference to, and connection with, ch. 7:14 foregoing. And the association of the enemies of the church in the 9th verse, is not chiefly that between Rezin and Pekah, but a much greater association in future ages, of which that was but a type. This is manifest, because here, those that are spoken of as joining together against the church of God are "all those of far countries." Such a combining and associating of the heathen and foreign enemies of the church against her in vast multitudes in the Messiah's days, and taking counsel and exerting themselves in vain, and being broken in pieces by the Messiah, is exactly agreeable to too many passages in formentioned prophecies to be now enumerated.

The 14th and 15th verses of this chapter are plainly connected with the 9th and 10th verses, as may be easily seen by the conjunctions that unite every sentence. So that he that it is said, v. 14, "shall be for a sanctuary," is the same "God with us," spoken of, v. 10; and those that he is a sanctuary to, are those that he is said to be with. 'Tis added, "but for a stone of stumbling and for a rock of offense to both the houses of Israel, for a gin and for a snare to the inhabitants of Jerusalem. And many among them shall stumble, and fall, and be broken, and be snared, and be taken." How agreeable is this to ch. 28:13 (These verses, as Stapferus, Theolog. Polem., Tom. III, p. 142, says, the Jews themselves interpret of the Messiah),1 "the word of the Lord was unto them precept upon precept; . . . that they might go, and fall backward, and be broken, and snared, and taken," connected with vv. 16-17 following, "Therefore thus saith the Lord God, Behold, I lay in Zion for a foundation a stone, a tried stone, a precious corner stone, a sure foundation: he that believeth shall not make haste. . . . and the hail shall sweep away the refuge of lies," etc., with other prophecies of this book of the rejection of the greater part of the Jewish nation.

The [words of the] 17th verse, "I will wait upon the Lord, that hideth his face from the house of Jacob, and I will look for him," are very much like those words relating to the salvation of the Messiahbefore taken notice of,2 Gen. 49:18, "I have waited for thy salvation, O Lord."

The beginning of the 9th chapter is plainly connected with the latter part of this 8th chapter, as appears by the conjunction, and is also most evidently connected with that great prophecy immediately following, 6th and 7th verses (before observed),3 as a part of it.

The 2nd verse, "The people that walked in darkness have seen a great light: they that dwell in the land of the shadow of death, upon them hath the light shined," is agreeable to Is. 42:6-7, "I [. . .] will [. . .] give thee for a light of the Gentiles; to open the blind eyes, to bring out the prisoners from the prison, and them that sit in darkness out of the prison house"; Is. 49:9, "That thou mayest say [. . .] to them that are in darkness, Show yourselves"; ch. 42:16, "I will bring the blind by a way that they knew not; I will lead them in paths that they have not known: I will make darkness light before them"; Mic. 7:8, "when I fall, I shall arise; when I sit in darkness, the Lord shall be a light unto me"; Is. 50:10, "Who is among you that feareth the Lord, and obeyeth the voice of his servant, that walketh in darkness, and hath no light? let him trust in the name of the Lord, and stay upon his God"; Is. 60:1-2, "Arise, shine; for thy light is come, the glory of the Lord is risen upon thee. For, behold, the darkness shall cover the earth, and gross darkness the people: but the Lord shall arise upon thee, and his glory shall be seen upon thee."

What is said, v. 3, "Thou hast multiplied the nation, to him increased the joy" (as it is in the margin):4 they joy before thee according to the joy in harvest, and as men rejoice when they divide the spoil," and also what is said of God's delivering his people from bondage to their enemies, v. 4, is agreeable to too many passages of forementioned prophecies to be enumerated.

Corollary. That there is so evident a connection of all the discourse from ch. 7:14-15 to ch. 9:6-7, with prophecies so much interspersed, so agreeable to other prophecies of the Messiah, is a great evidence that that prophecy, ch. 7:14, of a virgin's conceiving and bringing forth a son whose name shall be called Immanuel, has respect to the same child born and son given, whose name shall be called "Wonderful, . . . The mighty God, . . . the Prince of Peace," spoken of, ch. 9:6, as was before supposed, §19.

62. ISAIAH 19:18 TO THE END. In the 19th of Isaiah, from the 18th verse to the end, there seems to be a prophecy of the Messiahand his times. And that which is a sufficient evidence that the times of the Messiahare here spoken of, is that this is a prophecy of the calling of the Gentiles to "speak the language of Canaan, and swear to the Lord of hosts," and to offer sacrifice unto him, and pray to the Lord and know the Lord, and shall be acknowledged by God as his people and inheritance, and the work of his hands, and shall have the same privileges as the Jews. Nothing is more plain by preceding prophecies, than those are the great events of the Messiah's days, and peculiar to them.

'Tis here particularly foretold that Egypt and Assyria, two great nations that had been enemies and opponents of God's church, should be brought into his church, which is agreeable to many other prophecies of the Messiah's days, as Ps. 87:4, "I will make mention of Rahab and Babylon to them that know me: behold Philistia, and Tyre, and Ethiopia; this man was born there"; Ps. 68:31, "Princes shall come out of Egypt": Is. 45:14, "The labor of Egypt shall come over unto thee"; Is. 60:14, "The sons also of them that afflicted thee shall come bending unto thee; and all they that despised thee shall bow down themselves at the soles of thy feet; and they shall [call] thee, The city of the Lord, The Zion of the Holy One of Israel."

Here is foretold a change of the law, whereby other nations and lands shall have those privileges that by the law were peculiar to the land of Israel, so that there should be an altar in the land of Egypt, and they should offer sacrifices, and Egypt and Assyria should be holy lands as well as the land of Canaan. This is agreeable to other prophecies of the days of the Messiah, as Is. 66:21, "I will also take of them for priests and for Levites, saith the Lord." And God, in Is. 2 and Mic. 4, speaks of all nations going up to the house of the Lord, as the tribes of Israel were wont to go up.

That in the 23rd verse, "In that day shall there be an highway out of Egypt to Assyria," is agreeable to ch. 11:15-16, "And the Lord shall utterly destroy the tongue of the Egyptian sea; and with his mighty wind he shall shake his hand over the river, and shall smite it in the seven streams, and make men go over dryshod. And there shall be an highway for the remnant of his people, which shall be left, from Assyria; like as it was to Israel in the day that he came up out of the land of Egypt."

And there is all reason to think that the Savior and the great one spoken of, v. 20, whom God would send for the deliverance of the elect Egyptians from their oppressors, when they should cry to God, is the Messiah, who is everywhere spoken of as the great deliverer of his people in all nations from their oppressions. And it is very agreeable to the character everywhere given to him, that he is called a great one.

63. MALACHI 1:11. We have a plain and evident prophecy of the same times in Mal. 1:11, "For from the rising of the sun even unto the going down of the same my name shall be great among the Gentiles; and in every place incense shall be offered unto my name, and a pure offering: for my name shall be great among the heathen, saith the Lord of hosts." Here are the same distinguishing characters of a prophecy of the times of the Messiahthat were observed in the foregoing section. The calling of the Gentiles and changing of the law, bringing the Gentiles on a level with the Jews in privileges, their offering incense and a pure offering in other countries as well as Judea, is agreeable to the last-mentioned prophecy, that speaks of an altar to the Lord in the land of Egypt. That their offering should be pure is agreeable to Is. 60:7, "All the flocks of Kedar shall be gathered together unto thee, the rams of Nebaioth shall minister unto thee: they shall come up with acceptance on mine altar." And that God's name should be great among the Gentiles "from the rising of the sun even to the going down of the same," is agreeable to innumerable prophecies that speak of all nations being brought to know and serve God, and particularly Ps. 46:10, "I will [be] exalted among the heathen, I will be exalted through the earth"; and Ps. 113:3-4, "From the rising of the sun unto the going down of the same the Lord's name is to be praised. The Lord is high above all nations"; and Is. 59:19, "So shall they fear the name of the Lord from the west, and his glory from the rising of the sun."

64. ISAIAH 24, FROM VERSE 13, AND CHAPTERS 25, AND 26, AND 27, 28, 29, 30, 31, 32, 33, 34, 35. We have a continued prophecy of the times of the Messiah, from the 13th verse of the 24th chapter of Isaiah to the end of ch. 28, and from thence resumed from time to time with reproofs and threatenings interspersed to the enemies of the church and wicked professors, to the end of ch. 35. Here we have like prophecies of the great things that God in future ages would do for his church, introduced in like manner with the threatenings of approaching judgments on the land, and predictions of destruction to their oppressors, as other prophecies of the Messiahin this book that have been already taken notice of.

What is said, ch. 24:16, of God's people's rejoicing and singing praise in the utmost parts of the earth, and glorying in God, is agreeable to innumerable forecited prophecies.

Ch. 24:15, "glorify [. . .] the name of the Lord of God of Israel in the isles of the sea," don't agree to the time of the restoration of Israel from the Babylonish captivity. And the enlightening the isles and bringing them to give glory to God, is very often in other prophecies mentioned as a peculiar event of the times of the Messiah.

That terrible shaking of the earth and shaking of the nations, and punishing the kings and high ones of the earth, before the introduction of the glorious day of the church's peace and light, spoken of from v. 17, is agreeable to Hag. 2:6-7, 21-22, and Joel 3:16, and Ps. 46, and many others. Compare vv. 18-20, 23 with Is. 2:19, 21.

God's gloriously appearing in Mt. Zion and Jerusalem as the light of his church, far more glorious than the moon or sun [v. 23], is agreeable to Is. 2[:5], "O house of Israel, come, let us walk in the light of the Lord"; Is. 60:1-2, "Arise, shine," etc.; Mal. 4[:2], "to you that fear my name shall the Sun of righteousness"; and to that, Ps. 89:15,5 "Blessed are the people that know the joyful sound: they shall walk, O Lord, in the light of thy countenance"; and Is. 60:19, "The sun shall no more be thy light by day; neither for brightness shall the moon give light unto thee: but the Lord shall be unto thee an everlasting light, and thy God thy glory."

Ch. 25:3, "Therefore shall the strong people glorify thee, the city of the terrible nations shall fear thee," is agreeable to ch. 19:23-25, and Ps. 87:4, and Ps. 22:29, and many other prophecies.

And this verse, together with the 6th and 7th verses, are agreeable to innumerable prophecies that speak of the calling of the Gentiles as a great event, peculiar to the Messiah's days.

I need say nothing of vv. 2 and 4 and 12, that speaks of the terrible destruction of the enemies and oppressors of the church.

And in v. 8, another great event often spoken of as peculiar to the times of the Messiahis spoken of, viz. the resurrection of the dead and the bestowment of eternal life: "He will swallow up death in victory." See Ps. 22:29, Job 19:25-27, Is. 66:14, Ezek. 37, and Hos. 13:14.

The latter part of v. 8, "and the Lord God will wipe away tears from all faces; and the rebuke of his people shall he take away from off all the earth: for the Lord hath spoken it," is agreeable to Is. 51:11, "Therefore the redeemed of the Lord shall return, and come to Zion with singing; and everlasting joy shall be upon their head: they shall obtain gladness and joy; and sorrow and mourning shall flee away"; and to Is. 40:1-2, "Comfort ye, comfort ye my people," etc.; and Is. 54:9, "this is as the waters of Noah unto me: . . . I have sworn I will not be wroth with thee, nor rebuke thee," and many others. V. 9, "Lo, this is our God; we have waited for him, and he will save us: this is the Lord; we have waited for him, we will be glad and rejoice in his salvation," is agreeable to Ps. 46:1, 5, 7, 11, and 48:14, and Is. 40:9. (See next page.)6

The treading down of Moab, v. 10, is agreeable to ch. 11:14 and Num. 24:17.

Ch. 26:1, "We have a strong city; salvation will God appoint for walls and for bulwarks," is agreeable to ch. 60:18, "Thou shalt call thy walls salvation"; and Ps. 48:12-14, "Walk about Zion, and go round about her: tell the towers thereof. Mark ye well her bulwarks, consider her palaces; that ye may tell it to the generation following. For this God is our God for ever and ever."

What is said of the bringing down of the high and lofty, ch. 26:5, is agreeable to Is. 2:11, etc.

What is said, v. 6, concerning God's people's treading down their proud enemies, is agreeable to Mic. 7:10, and Ps. 68:23, "That thy foot may be dipped in the blood of thine enemies"; Ps. 47:3.

Ch. 26, v. 2, "Open ye the gates, that the righteous nation which

keepeth the truth may enter in," is agreeable to Is. 4:3; Is. 52:1, "henceforth there shall no more come to thee the uncircumcised and the unclean"; and Is. 60:21, "Thy people also shall be all righteous: they shall inherit the land for ever, the branch of my planting, the work of my hands, that I may be glorified"; and Joel 3:17.

What is said, ch. 25:9, "this is our God; we have waited for him," and also ch. 26:8-9, "Yea, in the way of thy judgments have we waited for thee; the desire of our soul is to thy name, and to the remembrance of thee. With my soul have I desired thee in the night," is agreeable to Gen. 49:18, "I have waited for thy salvation, O Lord," and Is. 8:17. Ch. 26:4 is agreeable to Is. 45:17, 24-25.7

Ch. 26:17, etc., the deliverance of the church is described as brought forth by the church's being first as it were in travail, which is agreeable to Jer. 30:6, 9, and Mic. 5:3.

V. 19, the resurrection of the dead is again foretold, as in ch. 25:8. (See observation on this verse in the last page.)8 And here God speaks of his dead body's rising, and his church reviving with him, which can be no other than the Messiah, and is agreeable to other prophecies of the Messiahalready mention[ed], as Is. 53[:10], "He shall see his seed, he shall prolong his days," with the context, that speaks of his death; and Ps. 16, latter end; Ps. 22:15 with the context.

Ch. 27:1, "In that day the Lord with his great and sore and strong sword shall punish leviathan the piercing serpent, even leviathan that crooked serpent; and he shall slay the dragon that is in the sea," is agreeable to the first prophecy of the Messiah, "it shall bruise thy head" [Gen. 3:15].

Vv. 2-3, "In that day sing ye unto her, A vineyard of red wine" (i. e. of the choicest wine; Prov. [23:31], "Look not on the wine when it is red"). "I the Lord do keep it; I will water it every moment," is agreeable to Gen 49:11, "Binding his foal to the vine, and his ass's colt unto the choice vine." And this verse, together with v. 6, "He shall cause them that come of Jacob to take root: Israel shall blossom and bud, and fill the face of the world with fruit," are agreeable to Hos. 14:5-7, "I will be as the dew unto Israel: he shall grow as the lily, and cast forth his roots as Lebanon. His branches shall spread, and his beauty shall be as the olive tree, and his smell as Lebanon. They that dwell under his shadow shall return; they shall revive as the corn, and grow as the vine: the scent thereof shall be as the wine of Lebanon"; and Ps 72:7, "In his days shall the righteous flourish," or "bud" or "branch forth," as the original word signifies; together with v. 16, "There shall be an handful of corn in the earth upon the top of the mountains; the fruit thereof shall shake like Lebanon: and they of the city shall flourish like grass of the earth."

V. 12, "And it shall come to pass in that day, that the Lord shall beat off from the channel of the river," etc., is agreeable to ch. 19:23 and ch. 11:15-16.

V. 13, "And it shall come in that day, that the great trumpet shall be blown, and they shall come which were ready to perish in the land of Assyria, and the outcasts in the land of Egypt, and shall worship the Lord in the holy mount at Jerusalem," is agreeable to Ps. 89[:15], "Blessed is the people that know the joyful sound."

Ch. 28:5-6, the words "In that day," with which these verses are introduced, refer to the same time spoken of in the same manner, ch. 27:13, "And it shall come to pass in that day, that the great trumpet shall be blown," etc.; and v. 14 and v. 1; and ch. 26:1; and ch. 25:9, and 24:21.

What is said, v. 5, "In that day shall the Lord of hosts be for a crown of glory, and for a diadem of beauty unto the residue of his people," is agreeable to Is. 60:19, "thy God thy glory."

And that, v. 6, "and for a spirit of judgment to him that sitteth in judgment, and for strength to them that turn the battle to the gate," is agreeable to ch. 11:2, "and the spirit of the Lord shall rest upon him, the spirit of wisdom and understanding, the spirit of counsel and of might."

In v. 9 and following verses, we have an account still of what shall be done on that day.

And9 so still, v. 16. And here it is to be noted, that what we have now observed of the connection of this 16th verse with the preceding part of the prophecy contained in these chapters, as a part thereof, and there being in them so many things that make it evident that these are predictions of the times of the Messiah, is a great confirmation of what has before been supposed, §20, that the precious tried stone there spoken of is the Messiah; and the connection being considered, those other arguments there mentioned that that was the Messiah, is a mutual evidence that these chapters are a prophecy of the times of the Messiah.

V. 21, it is said, "the Lord shall rise up as in mount Perazim, he shall be wroth as in the valley of Gibeon." This refers to that in II Sam. 5:20, "And David came to Baal-perazim, and David smote them there, and said, The Lord hath broken forth upon mine enemies before me, as the breach of waters. Therefore he called the name of that place Baal-perazim"; and to the destruction of the Amorites by great hailstones, with the sun standing still in the valley of Gibeon, in Joshua's time. This therefore is agreeable to v. 2, "Behold, the Lord hath a mighty and strong one, which as a tempest of hail and a destroying storm, as a flood of mighty waters overflowing, shall cast down to the earth with the hand."

Ch. 29:6-7, etc., "Thou shalt be visited of the Lord of hosts with thunder, and with earthquake, and with great noise, with storm and tempest, and the flame of devouring fire," etc. See also Is. 30:27-28, 30, and 32:19. Those things that are said of the manner of the destruction of the enemies of the church, is an argument that what is ultimately and chiefly aimed at is not the destruction of Sennacherib's army: for that was not in such a manner, but was by a secret plague inflicted by an angel in the night, whereby they were struck dead at once, so that when they arose in the morning, behold, they were all dead corpses. But 'tis most manifest that punishment and destruction that is threatened to be brought upon the host of the high ones, the lofty city, etc., is not chiefly intended [of] the Assyrians that warred against Jerusalem in Hezekiah's days, by those events that are spoken of as being consequent on that destruction. Compare ch. 24:21 with v. 23; and ch. 25:2, 5 with vv. 6-8; and ch. 25:12 and 26:5-6, 14 with v. 19; and ch. 27:1, 10-11 with vv. 12-13.

The marvelous work and wonder spoken of, ch. 29:14, seems to be the work of redemption by the Messias, which is often in the prophecies of the Messiahspoken of as the greatest of all wonders, as Num. 23:23 and Mic. 7:15-16, II Sam. 7:19, Ps. 118:23, Joel 2:26; and is often spoken of in this book of Isaiah as an exceeding great wonder, ch. 64:4, and 52:15, and 7:14, and 9:6 and other places. It seems here to be foretold that work, being so wonderful, hence it should not be understood nor acknowledged by many of that people, vv. 9-14; which is agreeable to other passages in this book, as particularly ch. 52:14 and 53:1-2.

Ch. 29:17, "Is it not yet a very little while, and Lebanon shall be turned into a fruitful field" (see also ch. 32:15), "and the fruitful field shall be esteemed as a forest." There seems to be a prophecy of the rejection of the Jews, for their not knowing and acknowledging the marvelous work and wonder that God would do; which is agreeable to other prophecies in this book, as ch. 49:4-8, ch. 53 at the beginning with [ch.] 54 at the beginning, and 65:1-15 and 66:1-5.

V. 18, "the eyes of the blind shall see out of obscurity, and out of darkness" (see also ch. 32:3), is agreeable to ch. 42:7, "To open the blind eyes, to bring out the prisoners from the prison, and them that dwell in darkness out of the prison house."

V. 19, "The meek shall increase their joy in the Lord, and the poor among men shall rejoice in the Holy One of Israel," is agreeable to ch. 61:1, "the Lord hath anointed me to preach good tidings unto the meek"; Ps. 22:26, "The meek shall eat and be satisfied"; Ps. 68:10, "thou hast prepared of thy goodness for the poor"; Ps. 72:4, "He shall judge the poor of the people, he shall save the children of the needy"; and v. 13, "He shall spare the poor and needy," etc.; and Ps. 45:4, "ride prosperously because of truth, meekness and righteousness"; Is. 11:4, "with Righteousness shall he judge the poor," and other places.

V. 22, "Jacob shall not be ashamed, neither shall his face now wax pale," is agreeable to ch. 45:17, "But Israel shall be saved in the Lord with an everlasting salvation: ye shall not be ashamed nor confounded world without end"; and ch. 49:23, "kings shall be thy nursing fathers," etc., "for they shall not be ashamed that wait for me"; and 50:7, "I know that I shall not be ashamed"; and ch. 54:4, "Fear not; for thou shalt not be ashamed: neither be thou confounded; for thou shall not be put to shame."

Ch. 30:19, "thou shalt weep no more," is agreeable to ch. 25:8, "and the Lord will wipe away tears from off all faces"; and also to Is. 51:11, and 40:1-2 and 53:9.

What is said in the 22nd verse, of the people's forsaking idolatry, is agreeable to many other prophecies of the times of the Messiah.

What is said in the 23rd and 24th verses, of the great plenty that shall be, is agreeable also to many of the forementioned prophecies. See especially Ezek. 34:26-27.

V. 25, "And there shall be upon every high mountain, and every high hill, rivers and streams of waters," is agreeable to Is. 41:18, "I will open rivers in high places, and fountains in the midst of the valleys: I will make the wilderness a pool of water, and the dry land springs of water."

V. 26, "Moreover the light of the moon shall be as the light of the sun, and the light of the sun shall be sevenfold as the light of seven days, in the day that the Lord bindeth up the breach of his people, and healeth the stroke of their wound," is agreeable to Is. 60:1-2, 19-20 compared with ch. 24:23, and among other prophecies that speak of a great increase of light to the church in the days of the Messiah.

[That in] vv. 27-28 is agreeable to ch. 29:6-7. And God's tongue being as a devouring fire, and his breath as an overflowing stream to destroy his enemies, and v. 31, "through the voice of the Lord shall the Assyrian be beaten down, which smote with the rod," is agreeable to ch. 11:4, "and he shall smite the earth with the rod of his mouth, and with the breath of his lips shall he slay the wicked."

71. EZEKIEL, CHAPTERS 38 AND 39. In vv. 8, 12, the restoration of Israel "to the mountains of Israel," that have long lain waste, are spoken of. See also ch. 39:28, which is evidently the same that had been spoken of, ch. 36:4, 19, 24, 28, 34-35, and ch. 37:12, 14, 21, 25.

In these chapters, the kings and nations of the world are represented as gathered together against God's Israel from the four quarters of the earth, or the four winds of heaven: Magog and Meshech and Tubal, Gomer and "Togarmah of the north quarters," ch. 38, vv. 2, 6; Persia from the east, v. 5; Ethiopia or Cush, and Lybia or Phut, Sheba and Dedan from the southeast, south and southwest, vv. 5, 13; the merchants and "young lions" (i. e. princes) of Tarshish from the west, vv. 13 and ch. 39:6. And therefore this we may certainly infer, that in many of those places in the former prophets, where we have prophecies of a great combination and gathering of many nations to invade the church of God, and their being defeated of their design by God's suddenly and with great manifestations of his power and wrath confounding and destroying, and working glorious deliverance for his church: I say, we may infer that many such prophecies, in the former prophets, are the same thing that is the subject of these two chapters. And since we are told that the former prophets did actually often prophesy of the same thing, we may also judge, with good reason, when we find things foretold in other prophets agreeing in many things with what is here foretold, and in nothing inconsistent, that those are things that here the prophet Ezekiel (or the Spirit of God in him) has respect to here, ch. 38:17.

73. THE PROPHECY OF OBADIAH. 'Tis apparent and obvious that the short prophecy of Obadiah does, part of [it], respect times of the Messiah, because it speaks of the time wherein the captivity of God's people should be returned, v. 20; and the day wherein God would revenge the quarrel of his church upon all the heathen, vv. 15-16; and the time wherein God would set up his kingdom upon earth, v. 21; and there should be deliverance and salvation in Zion, v. 17; and God's Israel should dreadfully consume their enemies, v. 18; and should possess the heathen lands, vv. 19-20.

And [if] we carefully consider this prophecy, it will be found that the whole of it chiefly respects the times of the Messiah. Though it seems to be mainly against Edom, yet it seems evident that by Edom here is mainly intended the same thing as in many forementioned prophecies of the church, viz. the chief of the enemies of the church that formerly had been brethren, with whom other enemies of the church shall be in combination, and in whose destruction they shall be involved, as in Is. 34 and 63, and Ezek. 35. The same envy and hatred against Israel is here spoken of, vv. 10, etc., that is spoken of, Ezek. 35:5, 11, 15; and the same thing is here threatened to Edom, v. 15, "as thou hast done, so shall it be done unto thee," [that] is threatened, Ezek. 35:15, "As thou didst rejoice at the inheritance of the house of Israel, because it was desolate, so will I do unto thee: thou shalt be desolate, O mount Seir, and all Idumea."

Here the destruction of Edom is spoken of as attended with the destruction of all the heathen that were enemies of Israel, vv. 15-16. So it is, Ezek. 36:5, "Surely in the fire of my jealousy have I spoken against the residue [of the heathen], and against all Idumea." So Is. 34, vv. 1-2, "Come near, ye nations, . . . For the indignation of the Lord is upon all nations, and his fury upon all their armies: he hath utterly destroyed them, he hath delivered them to the slaughter"; vv. 5-6, "For my sword shall be bathed in heaven: behold, it shall come down upon Idumea, . . . the Lord hath a sacrifice in Bozrah, and a great slaughter in the land of Idumea." So Is. 63:1, "Who is this that cometh from Edom," etc.; v. 6, "I will tread down the people in mine anger."

V. 15, "the day of the Lord is upon all the heathen." This is agreeable to Ezek. 39:8, "this is the day whereof I have spoken," and Is. 34:8 and 63:4 and 61:2.

V. 17, "But upon mount Zion shall be deliverance." This is agreeable to Is. 63:4, "the year of my redeemed is come." This deliverance is that great salvation that the Psalmist wishes were come out of Zion, and that is so often mentioned in other prophecies. See §51.

V. 17, "and the house of Jacob shall possess their possessions." This is agreeable to what follows the prophecy of the destruction of Idumea in Ezek. 36.

V. 18, "And the house of Jacob shall be a fire, and the house of Joseph a flame, and the house of Esau for stubble, and they shall kindle in them, and devour them; and there shall not be any remaining of the house of Esau." This is agreeable to Is. 41:15-16, "I will make thee a new sharp threshing instrument," etc., and Mic. 4:11-13, "Arise, thresh, O daughter of Zion: I will make thine horn iron, and thy hoofs brass," etc.

V. 19, "they of the south shall possess the mount of Esau," is agreeable to Num. 24:18, "Edom shall be a possession, Seir also shall be a possession." See also Amos 9:12, "that they may possess the remnant of Edom."

V. 21, "And saviors shall come to judge . . . and the kingdom shall be the Lord's," is agreeable to Hos. 13:10 and the many other prophecies already mentioned that speak of God's being king, and his reigning, etc., in the time of the Messiah.

74. EZEKIEL, CHAPTERS 40, AND 41, AND 42, AND 43, AND 44, AND 45, AND 46, AND 47, AND 48, ESPECIALLY 43:1-4, AND 44:1-3, AND 45:13 TO THE END, AND 46:1-11. The nine last chapters of Ezekiel are evidently a description of the state of God's Israel after that restoration from captivity, that resurrection as it were from the dead, and that re-establishment of them in their own land, and that great destruction of their enemies that had been spoken of in the six preceding chapters: and consequently is a prophecy of the times of the Messiah. Here we have a particular account of the land resettled, and how it should be settled and divided, and of the city rebuilt, and of the ordinances and worship of God restored. Here is a particular account of God's return to the people, and to the sanctuary, to dwell amongst them, ch. 43:1-7, of whose departure we have an account, ch. 9:3, and 10:18 to the end, and 11:22-23. And here we have a particular account of that which had been promised before, ch. 37:26-28, "I [. . .] will set my sanctuary in the midst of them . . . My tabernacle also shall be with them: . . . And the heathen shall know that I the Lord do sanctify Israel, when my sanctuary shall be in the midst of them." Here we have a particular description of that sanctuary of God.

Ch. 43:7, "And he said unto me, Son of man, the place of my throne, and the place of the soles of my feet, where I will dwell in the midst of the children of Israel for ever": and v. 9, "and I will dwell in the midst of them for ever"; and ch. 48:35, "and the name of the city from that day shall be, The Lord is there." These are evidently predictions of the same things that had been foretold, ch. 34:30, "Thus shall they know that I the Lord their God am with them, and that they, even the house of Israel, are my people, saith the Lord God"; and ch. 37:26-28, "and I [. . .] will set my sanctuary in the midst of them for evermore. My tabernacle also shall be with them: yea, I will be their God, and they shall be my people. And the heathen shall know that I the Lord do sanctify Israel, when my sanctuary shall be in the midst of them for evermore"; and ch. 39:29, "Neither will I hide my face any more from them: for I have poured out my spirit upon the house of Israel, saith the Lord God."

And that, ch. 43:7, "and my holy name shall the house of Israel no more defile, neither they nor their kings, by their whoredom, nor by the carcasses of their kings in their high places," is plainly a prophecy of the same thing with that foretold, ch. 37[:23], "Neither shall they defile themselves any more with their idols, nor with their detestable things, nor with any of their transgressions: but I will save them out of all their dwelling places, wherein they have sinned, and will cleanse them"; and ch. 36:25, "Then will I sprinkle clean water upon you, and ye shall be clean from all your filthiness, and from all your idols will I cleanse you."

That which is prophesied of, ch. 37:15, etc., and 34:23, viz. that the whole house of Israel should be one nation and should be no more two kingdoms, and should be united under one prince, we have here in these chapters a particular account, ch. 48.

And this prophecy in these nine last chapters of Ezekiel, is agreeable to other prophecies of the times of the Messiah, in those particulars that have been already mentioned, wherein it agrees with passages in the six preceding chapters (which may be seen in places referred to in past observations on those passages), and also in other things. As particularly here, it is foretold that there should be a new measuring out and dividing of the land, and a new laying out of the plan of the city, which is agreeable to Jer. 31:38-40, and Obadiah, vv. 18-20. And who can doubt whether God's return to his temple, here spoken of, ch. 43:1-7, be the same that is spoken of, Mal. 3:1, "the Lord whom ye seek, shall suddenly come to his temple."

And what is said, ch. 47, of the living waters that proceed from the house of the Lord and go forth eastward towards the east country, is agreeable to Joel 3:18, "and a fountain shall come forth of the house of the Lord, and shall water the valley of Shittim," which Shittim was eastward from Jerusalem, on the other side Jordan. Ch. 47:8, 'tis said those waters "go down into the desert," which is agreeable to those many passages that have been already mentioned in other prophecies of the Messiah's days, of fountains, streams and rivers in the desert, wilderness and dry places. And what is said, v. 5, "Afterward he measured a thousand, and it was a river that I could not pass over: for the waters were risen, waters to swim in, a river that could not be passed over." This is agreeable to Is. 33:21, "there the glorious Lord will be to us a place of broad rivers and streams." What is said of the healing virtue of those waters, and of the leaves of the tree that grow on the banks of the river, they should be for medicine [v. 12], is agreeable to Jer. 33:6, "Behold, I will bring it health and cure, and I will cure them, and will reveal unto them the abundance of peace and truth." So what is said, vv. 8-9, of the waters going into the [sea] and healing of the waters of the great ocean, and healing and giving life to everything, is very agreeable to what is elsewhere said of turning the barren land in[to] a fruitful field, and making the wilderness to bud and blossom as the rose; and the Messiah's giving light and life and salvation to all nations; and changing the nature even of those things that are poisonous, that they shall become harmless; and restoring all things, making a new heavens and new earth, and the like.

Here in these chapters it is foretold that the Law of Moses, as to what concerns the Mosaic constitution of worship, shall be set aside and give place to a new constitution (see "Miscellanies," no. 1034);30 which has often been observed to be a sure note of a prophecy of the times of the Messiah.

Again, it is here foretold that the Gentiles that shall turn to God shall be admitted to equal privileges with the Jews, insomuch that they shall have a lot with them in the division of the land of Israel. Ezek. 47:21-23, "So shall ye divide this land unto you according to the tribes of Israel. And it shall come to pass, that ye shall divide it by lot for an inheritance unto you, and to the strangers that sojourn among you, which shall beget children among you: and they shall be unto you as born in the country among the children of Israel; they shall have inheritance with you among the tribes of Israel. And it shall come to pass, that in what tribe the stranger sojourneth, there shall ye give him his inheritance, saith the Lord God." This has often been observed to be a plain and certain note of a prophecy of the times of the Messiah. See Ezek. 16:61, and see many other places mentioned, §68.

Ch. 44:9, "Thus saith the Lord God; No stranger, uncircumcised in heart, nor uncircumcised in flesh, shall enter into my sanctuary, of any stranger that is among the children of Israel," is agreeable to Is. 52:1, "henceforth there shall no more come into thee the uncircumcised and the unclean"; and Joel 3:17, "then shall Jerusalem be holy, and there shall no stranger pass through her any more"; also Is. 26:2, and 34:8, and 60:21 and 4:3.

Ch. 48:35, "and the name of the city from that day shall be, The Lord is there." This is agreeable to Is. 7[:14], his name shall be called Immanuel; and Is. 8:10, "God is with us"; and Ps. 46:4-5, "the city of God, the holy place of the tabernacle of the most High. God is in the midst of her"; and so vv. 7 and 11, "The Lord of hosts is with us"; and [Ps.] 48:2-3, "Beautiful for situation, the joy of the whole earth, is mount Zion on the sides of the north, the city of the great King. God is known in her palaces for a refuge"; v. 14, "For this God is our God for ever and ever"; Is. 25:9, "And it shall be said in that day, Lo, this is our God; we have waited for him, and he will save us: this is the Lord; we have waited for him, we will be glad and rejoice in his salvation"; and Jer. 33:16, "in those days shall Judah be saved, and Jerusalem shall dwell safely: and this is the name wherewith she shall be called, The Lord our righteousness"; and many other places.

Ch. 43:5, "So the spirit took me up, and brought me into the inner court; and, behold, the glory of the Lord filled the house." This is agreeable to Hag. 2:7, "the desire of all nations shall come: and I will fill this house with glory, saith the Lord of hosts."

"Á Lapide tells us, that many Rabbins and Jews refer this temple and city to the Messiah, expecting that he should build them; and, because this third temple and new city are not yet built, they think the Messiah is not yet come. This argument was objected by the Jews to Galatinus." Greenhill on Ezekiel, last vol., p. 279.31

As to the prince spoken of in the 44th and following chapters, although the whole of that authority and rule or power of dominion that God should set in his Israel, may be included in what is designed to be here represented by the prince, yet there are several things that make it manifest that the Messiah, the head and source of all that authority and power, is especially intended:

1. The prophecies in the preceding chapters, of the same times and this same state of God's people, which the Spirit of God continues to describe in those last nine chapters, do in effect declare plainly that this prince over God's people is the Messiah; as ch. 37:24-25, "And David my servant shall be king over them; and they all shall have one shepherd: they shall also walk in my statutes, and observe my judgments, and do them. And they shall dwell in the land that I have given unto Jacob my servant, wherein your fathers have dwelt; and they shall dwell therein, even they, and their children, [. . .] for ever: and my servant David shall be their prince for ever." Here we are expressly told who their prince should be, and we are told they should have but one, v. 24, "and they all shall have one shepherd." And we are not only told that there should be no other that should be contemporary with him, or that he only should be their prince for a time; but that they should never have any other prince, or that he only should be their prince forever, v. 25, "and my servant David shall be their prince for ever." This, as the context shows, shall be when God has brought 'em out of captivity and resettled 'em in their own land, and united the two kingdoms of Judah and Israel, and God has purged them from their idols and detestable things, and set his sanctuary in the midst of them; which is the time and state described in those last chapters. Therefore it follows that this prince here spoken of, is that David there spoken of, who is also spoken of under the same appellation of THEIR PRINCE, ch. 34:23-24. "And I will set up one shepherd over them" (one and no more), "even my servant David; he shall feed them, and he shall be their shepherd. And I the Lord will be their God, and my servant David A PRINCE among them; I the Lord have spoken it."

2. This prince was to do the part of an high priest among the people. He was to offer all the sacrifices of the nation. They were to bring their sacrifices to him, ch. 45:13-16. They were to go through his hands, and by him to be offered to God: their daily and their weekly and their monthly sacrifices, and their sacrifices on all their feasts and special solemnities, ch. 45:17. "And it shall be the prince's part to give burnt offerings, and meat offerings, and drink offerings in the new moons, in the sabbaths, in all solemnities of the house of Israel. He shall prepare the sin offering and the meat offering, and the burnt offering, and the peace32 offering, to make reconciliation for the house of Israel." See also v. 21 of the same chapter to the end, and ch. 46:4-7. These offerings of the sabbaths and new moons that the prince was to offer, were the weekly and monthly sacrifices of the nation, instead of those appointed, Num. 28:9, etc. This shows plainly who this prince was, viz. the same with that David spoken of, chs. 34 and 37: the antitype of David the son of Jesse that was girded with a linen ephod, the king over Israel; and of him spoken of in the 110th Psalm [v. 4], to whom the Lord swore, "Thou art a priest for ever after the order of Melchizedek," i. e. both a king and a priest; and the same with the man whose name is the Branch, spoken of, Zech. 6[:12-13], who should "sit and rule upon his throne, and be a priest upon his throne."

3. It is said, ch. 44:2, of the eastern gate of the temple, "This gate shall be shut, it shall not be opened, and no man shall enter in by it; because the Lord, the God of Israel, hath entered by it, therefore it shall be shut." And yet it is said in the next verse, it shall be for "the prince, he shall sit in it to eat bread before the Lord; he shall enter by the way of the porch of that gate, and shall go out by the way of the same." And so afterwards we have accounts of the prince's entering on the sabbaths and new moons by that gate, ch. 46. This argues that this prince was more than a mere man, one on whom divine honor was conferred; as 'tis said, Zech. 6[:13], "he shall bear the glory." He was to enter into God's temple at that gate at which Jehovah had entered, and which none other man entered in at but he, and which it was not lawful for any man to enter in by, because Jehovah the God of Israel had entered by it. This shows that he was not a mere man, infinitely distant from God, a worm or grasshopper, as all the kings of the earth are before God, who "accepteth not the person of princes, nor regardeth the rich more than the poor, for they are all the work of his hands" [Job 34:19].

It is no solid objection against the Messiah's being chiefly intended by this prince, because princes are spoken of in the plural number, ch. 45:8-9. For this is evidently paralleled in other prophecies of the Messiah, as Is. 32:1, "Behold, a king shall reign in righteousness, and princes shall rule in judgment." So in Jer. 23:4 'tis said, "I will set up shepherds" (in the plural number) "over them which shall feed them: and they shall fear no more, nor be dismayed, neither shall they be lacking, saith the Lord"; and then in the next verse it follows, "Behold, the days come, saith the Lord, that I will raise up unto David a righteous Branch," etc.; and of this Branch of David it is said, Ezek. 34, and [vv.] 23-24, and 37:24-25, that he only shall be their shepherd. So in Obadiah's prophecy of the Messiah, v. 21, it is said, "saviors shall come upon mount Zion to judge the mount of Esau." In all these, there may either be a kind of Enaltage numeri,33 prophetically (and so very figuratively) representing the Messiah by such a succession of princes or judges or captains as were a type of him in Israel. Or, as I said before, this prince in the prophecy may represent the rule, authority and dominion that God will set in his Israel, which, though it chiefly respects the Messiah, the head and source of it all, yet may include subordinate rulers, or all such as do partake of any part of his dominion.

"Rabbi David, in his commentary, saith that the Hebrews interpret this prince to be the Messiah; so doth Jerome." Greenhill on Ezekiel, last vol., p. 407.34

75. PSALMS 60 AND 108. The 60th and 108th Psalms, which are in great part the same, seem to be prophecies of the times of the Messiah. The first verse of Ps. 60 speaks of a time of great displeasure against Israel, and casting them off and dispersing them in the world, preceding the glorious things that are spoken of in the Psalm. "O God thou hast cast us off, thou hast scattered us, thou hast been displeased; O turn thyself to us again." So v. 10; and Ps. 108, v. 11, "O God, which hadst cast us off, and thou, O God, which didst not go out with our armies"; and [Ps. 60,] v. 3, "Thou hast showed thy people hard things: thou hast made us to drink the wine of astonishment." This don't agree with the time of that victory of David over his enemies, mentioned in the title of the Psalm, but it is agreeable to the greater part of the forementioned prophecies of the Messiah.

V. 2, "Thou hast made the earth to tremble; thou hast broken it," is agreeable to Haggai's prophecies of shaking the heavens and the earth, and shaking all nations, Hag. 2:6, and also vv. 21-22; and to Ezek. 38:19-21; and Is. 24:18-20, "the foundations of the earth do shake. The earth is utterly broken down, the earth is clean dissolved, the earth is moved exceedingly. The earth shall move to and fro like a drunkard, and shall be removed like a cottage; and the transgression thereof shall be heavy upon it"; and Joel 3:16, and many others.

V. 4, "Thou hast given a banner to them that fear thee, that it may be displayed because of the truth," is agreeable to Is. 59:19, "When the enemy shall come in like a flood, the Spirit of the Lord shall lift up a standard against him"; and Ps. 20:5, "in the name of our God we will set up our banners."

Ps. 108:3, "I will praise thee, O Lord, among the people: and I will sing praises unto thee among the nations," supposing David here speaks in the name of the Messiah, agrees well with other prophecies that foretell the Messiah's being the light of Gentiles, and his making known the true God to them.

Ps. 108:4, "For thy mercy is great above the heavens: and thy truth reacheth unto the clouds," is agreeable to what is said in other prophecies of the Messiah, of God's great mercy and faithfulness manifested to his people in the time of the Messiah.

Ps. 60:5 and 108:6, "That thy beloved may be delivered; save with thy right hand, and answer me." Here the deliverance of God's beloved or spouse, is spoken of as a thing dependent on his being saved, and his prayers answered, that speaks; which is agreeable to Ps. 22:19-21, "be not thou far from me, O Lord: O my strength, haste thee to help me. Deliver my soul from the sword; my darling from the power of the dog. Save me from the lion's mouth: for thou hast heard me from the horns of the unicorn."

Ps. 60:6-7 and 108:7-8, "God hath spoken in his holiness; I will rejoice, I will divide Shechem, and mete out the valley of Succoth. Gilead is mine, and Manasseh is mine; Ephraim also is the strength of my head; Judah is my lawgiver." Here is a prediction of God's acknowledging Israel anew as his people, after he had cast 'em off (as Ps. 60:1, 10 and Ps. 108:11), and his settling of them anew in their own land, after he had scattered 'em, as v. 1; agreeable to most of the foregoing prophecies of the Messiah. And here is also a prophecy of a new laying out and division of the land, agreeable to Ezekiel, the nine last chapters, and Jer. 31:38-40, and Obadiah, vv. 18-20. These things are plain notes of a prophecy of the Messiah's times, and was no consequence of that victory of David, spoken of in the title of the 60th Psalm, nor did they agree to David's times. Particularly, here is a prediction of the restoration and resettlement of that part of the land that was beyond Jordan, as the land of Gilead and Manasseh, that were subject to captivity and desolation before other parts of the land, and had lain desolate much longer. This is agreeable to Is. 9, at the beginning.

Ps. 60:8 and 108:9, "over Edom will I cast out my shoe," and Ps. 60:12 and 108:13, "he it is that shall tread down our enemies." This is agreeable to Is. 63[:1-3], "Who is this that cometh from Edom, with dyed garments [. . .], with thy garments like him that treadeth in the winefat? I have trodden the winepress alone; [. . .] I will tread them in mine anger, and trample them in my fury."

Ps. 108:9 and 60:8, "Moab is my washpot; over Edom will I cast out my shoe; over Philistia will I triumph," is agreeable to Is. 11:14, "But they shall fly upon the shoulders of the Philistines towards the west; they shall spoil them of the east together: they shall lay their hand upon Edom and Moab"; and Obadiah, vv. 18-19, "And the house of Jacob shall be for a fire, . . . and the house of Esau for stubble, and they shall kindle in them, and devour them; . . . And they of the south shall possess the mount of Esau; and they of the plain the Philistines." See also Is. 25:10 with 34:5-6, and Num. 24:17-18, "a Star shall arise out of Jacob, and a Scepter shall rise out of Israel, and shall smite the four corners of Moab, and destroy all the children of Sheth. And Edom shall be a possession, Seir also shall be a possession for his enemies; and Israel shall do valiantly." These last words are agreeable to Ps. 60:12 and 108:13, "Through God we shall do valiantly."

Ps. 60:8, "Philistia, triumph thou because of me." Here is a prediction of the calling of the Gentiles, even of some of the worst of them, that had been great enemies to the church, and bringing them into a covenant relation to the God of Israel as their God. This is agreeable to what is said of Egypt and Assyria in the three last verses of Is. 19; and especially is this agreeable to Ps. 87:4, "I will make mention of Rahab and Babylon to them that know me: behold Philistia, and Tyre, with Ethiopia; this man was born there."

Ps. 60:9 and 108:10, "Who will bring me into the strong city? who will lead me into Edom?" Here, the conquest and destruction of the enemies of Israel is represented as chiefly consisting in subduing Edom and the strong city. This is agreeable to Is. 25:2-3, "For thou hast made of a city an heap; of a defensed city a ruin: a palace of strangers to be no city; it shall never be built. Therefore shall the strong people glorify thee, the city of the terrible nations shall fear thee"; and v. 12, "the fortress of the high fort of thy walls shall he bring down, lay low, and bring to the ground, even to the dust"; and ch. 26:5-6, "For he bringeth down them that dwell on high; the lofty city, he layeth it low; he layeth it low, even to the ground; he bringeth it even to the dust. The foot shall tread it down, even the feet of the poor, and the steps of the needy"; and ch. 30:25,35 "in the day of the great slaughter, when the towers fall," together with ch. 34:5-6, "For my sword is bathed in heaven: it shall come down upon Idumea, . . . for the Lord hath a sacrifice in Bozrah" (which was the strongest city in Idumea), "and a great slaughter in the land of Idumea."

76. PSALM 67. The 67th Psalm is very evidently a prophecy of the times of the Messiah. For though what is expressed in it is chiefly by way of pe[ti]tion, yet those petitions seem to have the force of predictions: for it appears to have been of old the manner to prophesy of future events in the form of petitions, or language of prayer. See Gen. 27:28-29, and Deut. 33:6-8, 24, and Judg. 9:20.

What is said, v. 1, of God's causing his face to shine to shine upon his people, is agreeable to Ps. 89:15, "Blessed are the people that know the joyful sound: they shall walk, O Lord, in the light of thy countenance"; and what is said, Is. 6,36 of God's people's walking in the light of the Lord; and in Is. 60[:2, 19], of God's glory's being risen on his church, and the sun's being no more her light by day, but God's being her everlasting light; and in Mal. 4[:2], of the Sun of righteousness rising on his people; and many other such things in forementioned prophecies.

What is said, vv. 2nd, 3rd, 4th, 5th, [and] 7th verses, of the calling of the Gentiles, and God's appearing to judge the people and to govern the nations upon earth, and all nations being taught God's ways and having God's saving health shown to them, and all nations rejoicing and singing praises to God on this account, and all the ends of the earth fearing God, are certain and clear notes of a prophecy of the days of the Messiah, and are all things agreeable to so many passages everywhere in other prophecies, that it would be both endless and needless particularly to show them.

V. 6, "Then shall the earth yield her increase; and God, even our own God, shall bless us," is agreeable to Is. 30:23-24, and Jer. 31:12, and Ezek. 34:27, 29 and 36:29-30, and Amos 9:13-14 and many other prophecies.

77. PSALM 138. The 138th Psalm has that clear evidence of being a prophecy of the Messiah. There, in vv. 4-5, the calling of the Gentiles is foretold with like circumstances as in other prophecies of the Messiah. "All the kings of the earth shall praise thee, O Lord, when they hear the words of thy mouth. Yea, they shall sing in the ways of the Lord: for great is the glory of the Lord." And this is here spoken of as a consequence of a great deliverance, granted to him that is represented speaking in this Psalm, in answer to his prayer. See vv. 3-5, which is agreeable to the manner in which the same thing is foretold, Ps. 22:19-21 with vv. 27-29, and Ps. 60:5-9 and 108:6-10.

Vv. 1-3, the Psalmist declares that in consequence of his great deliverance in answer to prayer, that he would praise God with his whole heart and sing praise to him before the gods, and worship towards his holy temple and praise his name; [which] is agreeable to Ps. 22:20-22 with v. 25, and 108:3 with v. 6.

That, v. 2, "for thy lovingkindness and for thy truth: for thou hast magnified thy word above all thy name," is agreeable to, and is brought in in like manner with, Ps. 108:3-4, "I will sing praises unto thee among the nations. For thy mercy is great above the heavens: and thy truth reacheth unto the clouds."

V. 2, "thou hast magnified thy word above all thy name," i. e. "In what thou hast done for thy people in fulfillment of thy promises, thou hast exceeded all the fame that we have heard of thy great works of old." This don't well agree with anything that came to pass in David's time, but well agrees with the great works which it is often foretold God would do for his people by the Messiah, and well agrees with what is expressly said of those works in many forementioned prophecies, as Is. 43:16-18 and 64:3-4, and Jer. 16:14-15, and Is. 65:17-18. This is a clear evidence of this Psalm's being a prophecy of the times of the Messiah.

V. 6, "Though the Lord be high, yet hath he respect unto the lowly," is very agreeable to Is. 57:15, "For thus saith the high and lofty One," etc., and Is. 66:1-2, "The heaven is my throne," etc.

V. 8, "The Lord will perfect that which concerneth me: thy mercy, O Lord, endureth forever: forsake not the work of thine own hands." This is agreeable to Is. 66:9, "Shall I bring to the birth, and not cause to bring forth? saith the Lord: shall I cause to bring forth, and shut the womb? saith thy God."

78. PSALM 85. That the 85th Psalm is a prophecy of the redemption of the Messiah, there are many evidences.

The grand subject is the bringing back the captivity of Jacob, and forgiving the iniquity of his people and covering all their sin, and taking away all his wrath and turning the people again to God; which is the description everywhere given of the Messiah's salvation.

The redemption and captivity here spoken of, is after a long-continued manifestation of God's anger, so as to make their case appear to an human eye almost desperate, vv. 5-6, agreeable to many forementioned prophecies that speak of perpetual wastes and the desolations of many generations, when their bones were dried, their hope lost, etc.

V. 6, "Wilt thou not revive us again." The Hebrew signifies "restoring life." This is agreeable to Ezek. 37, and many other prophecies.

This Psalm speaks of that which is eminently God's salvation, vv. 7 and 9, agreeable to the places taken notice, §51, and other places.

Vv. 10-11, "Mercy and truth are met together; righteousness and peace have kissed each other. Truth shall spring out of the earth; and righteousness shall look down from heaven." This is agreeable to Ps. 138:2, and Ps. 108:3-4, and Ps. 72:3, and Is. 45:8, "Drop down, ye heavens, from above, and let the skies pour down righteousness: let the earth open, and let them bring forth salvation, and let righteousness spring up together; I the Lord have created it."

V. 12, "Yea, the Lord will give that which is good." This is agreeable to Jer. 33:14, "Behold, the days come, saith the Lord, that I will perform that good thing which I have promised unto the house of Israel, and to the house of Judah."

V. 12, "and our land shall yield her increase." This is agreeable to Ps. 67:6, "Then shall the earth yield her increase; and God, even our own God, shall bless us"; and Is. 30:23-24 and Jer. 31:12, Ezek. 34:27 29 and 36:29-30, and Amos 9:13-14, and many other places.

79. PSALM 69. The 69th Psalm is very parallel with the 22nd Psalm, and there is much evidence that the same things are the subject of it, that it has already been shown are spoken of in that Psalm. Here is described a like suffering state of the person that is represented speaking, and a like deliverance, and like consequences of the deliverance.

What is said here of the multitude of the Psalmist's enemies, v. 4, is parallel with Ps. 22:7, 12, 16.

What is said here of the great reproach that he suffered, vv. 7, 9-12, 19-20, is parallel with Ps. 22:7.

V. 20, "Reproach hath broken my heart," is agreeable to Ps. 22:14, "my heart is like wax; it is melted in the midst of my bowels."

V. 3, "I am weary of my crying: my throat is dried: mine eyes fail while I wait for God," is agreeable to Ps. 22:15, "My strength is dried up like a potsherd; and my tongue cleaveth to my chaws."

V. 20, "I looked for some to take pity, but there was none; and for comforters, but I found none," is agreeable to Ps. 22:11, "there is none to help."

Vv. 13, 1637 are parallel with Ps. 22:24.

Vv. 30, 32, "I will praise the name of God with a song, and will magnify him with thanksgiving. . . . The humble shall see this, and be glad; and your heart shall live that seek God," is almost the same with Ps. 22:25-26, "My praise shall be of thee in the great congregation: I will pay my vows before them that fear him. [. . .] your heart shall live for ever."

V. 34, "Let the heaven and earth praise him, the seas, and every thing that moveth therein," is parallel with Ps. 22:27.

V. 36 is agreeable to Ps. 22:30.

This Psalm is said to be a Psalm of David in the title, but David did not speak here in his own name, for the things spoken in the Psalm are not applicable to David. They are not applicable to any of the troubles that David was the subject of by Saul's persecution, or before David was exalted to the throne of Israel: for Zion is spoken of, v. 35, "For God will save Zion, and build the cities of Judah," etc. But Zion did not belong to Israel, but was a heathen city, till David was king over all Israel, and in great prosperity, II Sam. 5:1-9. See SSS on the argument of this Psalm.38

Nor does what is said in this Psalm seem to agree to David's circumstances in the time of Absalom's rebellion, for this is spoken of as a very long-continued affliction, v. 3. But David's troubles in the time of Absalom's rebellion were very short. In vv. 4 and 21, the Psalmist speaks of himself as being in the hands of his enemies, and obliged to do what his enemies enjoined him--that is, it must be so understood, if the words are understood of David. And in vv. 7 and 9, the Psalmist speaks of those troubles as what he suffered for God's sake, and as the fruits of a persecution that was brought upon him by his zeal for the worship of God. But this was not the case in Absalom's rebellion. Again, v. 20, he speaks of himself as being wholly forsaken of all, having none to pity or comfort him; but this was not the case with David in the time of Absalom's rebellion, for he then had the most valiant men in Israel about him to defend him. And then the imprecations in vv. 22-28, than which we have none more terrible in all the book of Psalms, are what can't be supposed to be uttered by David in the case of Absalom's rebellion: for Absalom then was the chief cause of all his troubles, and the head of his enemies. But it is plain that David was as remote as possible from uttering such curses against him, for he both exceedingly longed and also hoped for his deliverance and welfare, with the bowels of a most tender, compassionate father. He here prays that his enemies may be "blotted out of the book of the living" and perish forever in another world, and their families be extirpated, vv. 24-25, 28. But how much otherwise was the case with the last with David, as to Absalom.

In v. 32 to the end, the Psalmist speaks of the saving of Zion and her being rebuilt, and the rebuilding the cities of Judah and God's people's being restored to the possession of it, after they had been in a state of bondage and captivity, as the consequence of the deliverance of the person that is the subject of this Psalm, out of trouble; which don't agree with any deliverance of David, and by innumerable passages in other prophecies of the Messiah, shows plainly that it is the Messiah that is here spoken of.

V. 13, "in an acceptable time: O God, in the multitude of thy mercy hear me, in the truth of thy salvation," is agreeable to Is. 49:8, "Thus saith the Lord, In an acceptable time have I heard thee, in a day of salvation have I helped thee."

V. 5, "O God, thou knowest my foolishness; and my sins are not hid from thee," is no sufficient objection against this Psalm's being spoken in the name of the Messiah: because we learn by the 53rd of Isaiah, that although the Messiah was God's righteous servant, yet he bore trouble and the terrible fruits of God's displeasure for sins that were laid upon, and reputed in God's dealing with him, as if they were his own. And this is what is meant by God's Knowing his foolishness, and his sins not being hid from him; i. e. God, by punishing, brought them to remembrance, did not hide them by forgiving, but found out his sin and beheld iniquity, and punished it, and set the sins in his view to excite his wrath. God forgave nothing to the Messiah, but exacted the uttermost farthing; he would know and behold all his iniquity, and none of his guiltiness (as it is the original, in that 5th verse) should be hid from him, agreeable to the 53rd of Isaiah. See "Miscellanies," no. 1035.39
