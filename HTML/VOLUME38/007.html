Northampton Church Committee on the Petition of the Minority, [c. 1751] N'ton Chh on Minority Petition Ebenezer Pomeroy

Northampton Church Committee on the Petition of the Minority

This March 5, 1752, report by the Northampton Church Committee, in the hand of Ebenezer Pomeroy, responds to the petition of the "aggrieved brethren" that explained why they absented themselves from communion. While acknowledging and apologizing for some heated statements in their Remonstrance, the Church Committee here, as in later documents such as "Reflections on the Remonstrance," defends its criticism of Edwards and his adherents.

*********

The MS (ANTS f. 1752E.7) is two folio leaves, the fourth page blank, with damage affecting the right and left margins of pages 1-2.

Here followeth some Remarks upon what those that called themselves aggrieved Brethren of the first Chh. in Northampton ---- In ye first Place they Justifie themselves, because they cant Join in Communion with those that are not santified contra=ry to ye Doctrine we were long taught by ye late[?] Stoddard, that santifying Grace was not necessary in Order to ye lawfull Attendance upon ye Lords Supper &c But this they pretty much give up, and so we shall say no more about it ----- But now ye Remonstrance itself they say contains a great Degree of unchristian Bitterness, [-] against Mr. Edwards and tho[se yt] Adhere to him, and particularly against Coll. Dwigmarginal with Respect to what was said in ye Remonstrance respecting Mr. Edwards contradicting Himself; which to explain it will not bear a Dispute, that Mr. Edwards has often and again declared his Willingness to be dis=missed from this Chh & Precinct, if they continued their Aversion to Him: But how has He since his Dismission by his Preaching privately & publickly endeavoured to be resettled again over an Handfull of People? this indeed is too plain to be disputed, and altho' they go on in a long Discourse how Mr. Edwards is wrong'd in the Remonstrance which can be no Reason for 'em at all, for withdrawing Com=munion from this Chh for if Mr. Edwards be abused by this Chh. he hath his Remedy either in the Law or Gospel or both to right Himself, for these pretended aggrieved Bre=thren have no Business with it; for he is not their Pastor, neither do they belong to his Chh. for ye Matter belongs to Him and not to 'em; and wherein this Chh hath wronged Mr. Ed=wrds they stand ready to give Him any Xtian Satisfaction. They say that in ye Remonstrance, ye Chh says they are guilty of Schism. Answer. We account yt to be Schism in a Chh. when any Member of a Chh. withdraws Commu=nion from ye Chh. they belong unto, upon ye Account of any Errors in sd. Chh. which are not fundamental, that that is Schism: But indeed if yt be not Schism, tis nothing more but an Error in Judgment, which is not scandalous nor criminal

And thus they go on to say, that something came marginal from Mess. Woodbridge & Williams respecting Cold-Spring Chh. and this Chh. not well agreeing. Answer. Let 'em answer for themselves, and not this Chh for 'em. And then once and again they take Notice how Mr Edwards hath been wronged, as also Col. Dwight in our Remonstrance. In answer Unto all which we would say it seems to be ye profest Intent & Design of this Interview of ye Chh. Committee and ye aggriev'd Brethren, to make Peace, and live in U[n]ity and Brotherly Love according to the Doctrines taughht [by] our Lord Jesus Christ, and we very much question whe[=t]her that be ye Way for Peace to descend into all ye Particu=lars and send & prove: but this we would say in generall that the Remonstrance was voted by ye Chh. very sudden=ly in a Day of Temptation, and tis possible, nay and very probable some Things in it too harsh, and not well di=gested by ye Chh. and for yt we would have our Brethren pass over and forgive, according to ye Example of our blessed Lord Jesus Christ, and that is ye Way for him to shower down his Blessings upon us; and that is it we should pray for, and earnestly seek more than any outward Blessing whatsoever, and if we be at Peace among our selves that is ye Way for ye God of Peace to dwell among us: which would be such a rich Blessing yt it well becomes every true Christian to be much in Prayer to God for.

And what is said in ye Answer by ye aggrieved Bre=thren with respect to particular Persons offensive Words, particularly mention'd in Answer to yt we say yt what they alledge was said by particular Persons, and cant be Matter of Offence to 'em any more than to ye Rest of the Brethren of ye Chh. and we would refer yt Matter to ye Chh, what Reflections & Satisfactions they should give to ye Chh: for every Member of ye Chh. hath as good a Right to Satisfaction for those particular Offences as the aggriev'd Brethren have. Mind what ye great Apostle said in 2. Cor. 13. 11. Finally brethren farewell be per=fect, be of good Comfort, be of one Mind, live in Peace and the God of Love and Peace shall be with you.

And now Brethren, we are sensible thamarginal true hearted Son of Zion is seized with a Sense of [the] Judgments of God upon us, and yt they come upon us readily for our Sins, in that we have been so long as Sheep without a Sheepheerd, and now we see ye Distemper let [us be] in earnes for a Cure of ye Disease: And first would [say] negatively, tis not the Way to be healed of this Malady to be finding Fault one with another and charging the Blame [upon] others. But, secondly, let every one search his ow[n] Heart, what he hath done to provoke a holy God marginal out against us; and to be much in Prayer yt God would guide & direct us in our Duty; and we can think of no Ot=her Way than that which ye People of Nineveh took when God sent Jonah to pronounce an awfull Judgment against 'em, now the Course they took was a universal Determination of Fasting & Prayer, and to cry mightily to God, yea and to turn every one from his evil Way, and from the violence yt is in their Hands, now let us instead of saying, from ye Violence that is in our hands, say, from the Violence that is in our Words, and we then have good Reason to think yt God would be reconciled to this Chh. and Town, but most certainly tis worth while to try as the People of Nineveh did, and had Success, with only that Encouragement, Who can tell, and God heard 'em and diverted the Judgment. ---- These Reflections we have offered to our aggrieved Brethren, and are willing the Chh. should concur therein.

Northampton March 5 1752
Ebenezer Pomroy Joseph Wright Com= Jonathan Strong=mittee Noah Wright Ebenezer Hunt

[2. Edited version.]

Here followeth some remarks upon what those that called themselves aggrieved brethren of the First Church in Northampton {have offered for their vindication in absenting themselves from communion}. In the first place, they justify themselves, because they can't join in communion with those that are not santified, contrary to the doctrine we were long taught by the late[?] Stoddard, that sanctifying grace was not necessary in order to the lawful attendance upon the Lord's Supper, etc. But this they pretty much give up, and so we shall say no more about it. But now the Remonstrance itself they say contains a great degree of unchristian bitterness, against Mr. Edwards and tho[se that] adhere to him, and particularly against Col. Dwig[ht], with respect to what was said in the Remonstrance respecting Mr. Edwards contradicting himself; which to explain it will not bear a dispute, that Mr. Edwards has often and again declared his willingness to be dismissed from this church and precinct, if they continued their aversion to him: but how has he since his dismission, by his preaching privately and publicly, endeavored to be resettled again over an handful of people? This indeed is too plain to be disputed; and although they go on in a long discourse how Mr. Edwards is wronged in the Remonstrance--which can be no reason for 'em at all, for withdrawing communion from this church, for if Mr. Edwards be abused by this church, he hath his remedy either in the law or gospel, or both, to right himself--for these pretended aggrieved brethren have no business with it; for he is not their pastor, neither do they belong to his church, for the matter belongs to him and not to 'em; and wherein this church hath wronged Mr. Edwards, they stand ready to give him any Christian satisfaction.

They say that in the Remonstrance, the church says they are guilty of schism. Answer: We account that to be schism in a church, when any member of a church withdraws communion from the church they belong unto, upon the account of any errors in said church which are not fundamental, that that is schism: but indeed if that be not schism, 'tis nothing more but an error in judgment, which is not scandalous nor criminal. And thus they go on to say, that something came from Messrs. Woodbridge and Williams respecting Cold Spring church, and this church not well-agreeing. Answer: Let 'em answer for themselves, and not this church for 'em. And then once and again they take notice how Mr. Edwards hath been wronged, as also Col. Dwight, in our Remonstrance. In answer unto all which we would say, it seems to be the professed intent and design of this interview of the church committee and the aggrieved brethren, to make peace, and live in u[n]ity and brotherly love according to the doctrines taught [by] our Lord Jesus Christ; and we very much question whe[t]her that be the way for peace, to descend into all the particulars, and send and prove: but this we would say in general, that the Remonstrance was voted by the church very suddenly in a day of temptation, and 'tis possible, nay, and very probable, some things in it [are] too harsh, and not well-digested by the church; and for that we would have our brethren pass over and forgive, according to the example of our blessed Lord Jesus Christ, and that is the way for him to shower down his blessings upon us; and that is it we should pray for, and earnestly seek more than any outward blessing whatsoever; and if we be at peace among ourselves, that is the way for the God of peace to dwell among us: which would be such a rich blessing that it well becomes every true Christian to be much in prayer to God for. And what is said in the answer by the aggrieved brethren with respect to particular persons' offensive words, particularly mentioned: in answer to that, we say that what they allege was said by particular persons, and can't be matter of offense to 'em, any more than to the rest of the brethren of the church; and we would refer that matter to the church, what reflections and satisfactions they should give to the church: for every member of the church hath as good a right to satisfaction for those particular offenses as the aggrieved brethren have. Mind what the great Apostle said in II Cor. 13:11, "Finally, brethren, farewell. Be perfect, be of good comfort, be of one mind, live in peace; and the God of love and peace shall be with you."1 And now brethren, we are sensible tha[t every] true-hearted son of Zion is seized with a sense of [the] judgments of God upon us, and that they come upon us readily for our sins, in that we have been so long as sheep without a shepherd; and now [that] we see the distemper, let [us be] in earnest for a cure of the disease. And first would [say], negatively, 'tis not the way to be healed of this malady, to be finding fault one with another and charging the blame [upon] others. But, secondly, let every one search his ow[n] heart, what he hath done to provoke a holy God [to lash] out against us; and to be much in prayer that God would guide and direct us in our duty. And we can think of no other way than that which the people of Nineveh took when God sent Jonah to pronounce an awful judgment against 'em. Now the course they took was a universal determination of fasting and prayer, and to cry mightily to God, yea, and to turn every one from his evil way, and from the violence that is in their hands; now let us, instead of saying, from the violence that is in our hands, say, from the violence that is in our words; and we then have good reason to think that God would be reconciled to this church and town. But most certainly 'tis worth our while to try as the people of Nineveh did, and had success, with only that encouragement, "Who can tell?," and God heard 'em and diverted the judgment2. These reflections we have offered to our aggrieved brethren, and are willing the church should concur therein. Northampton, March 5, 1752
Ebenezer Pomroy
Joseph Wright
Jonathan Strong Committee
Noah Wright
Ebenezer Hunt
