Petition of the Northampton Minority, [c. 1751] 1751 N'ton Minority Petition Timothy Dwight

Petition of the Northampton Minority, c. 1751

This undated document, written by Timothy Dwight sometime after May 1751 on behalf of the Northampton minority that supported Edwards, was composed to justify their absenting themselves from the Lord's Supper due to the many injurious statements made by leaders of the church. Apparently, this vindication would have been addressed to the church or one of its committees.

*****

The manuscript (ANTS f. 1750.9) was originally two folio leaves, but the bottom half of leaf two is now missing.

[1. Manuscript transcript.] We the subscribers members of the first Church in Northampton Offer what follows for our vindication in Absenting from the Co`munion at the Lords Table first The practise which this church is Obstinately & resolutely fixed in with regard to its own con :stitution & the Admission of its members --- we dont suppose that every corruption in the Constitution of A Church will Justifie A withdrawing from its Communion, Yea we Allow that A very great Corruption in this respect in many cases may & ought to be born with for A long time, rather than make an Open break & all means to be used for A reformation of the Church in Union & Communion with it but when it is the Establish'd principle of A Church that such ought to be Admitted its members & may be reciev'd as its Officers even as its pastors as make no pretence to any part of the distin guishing Essence of the religion of Jesus Christ or any true friendship to him & when they continue Ireclaimable in this practise & A declared resolution of Continueing in it when all possible means have been Used to Convince & reclaim them by Such as are most proper to Exhibit [true?] light to 'em & their reformation is quite hopeless & they have proceeded to utmost Ex[trem?] ities in their Opposition to means of conviction even to A great degree of persecution & in an publick renunciation of their Pastor for a Contrary Opinion & not only refuseing convicttion but constantly refuseing in any Just Manner to hear & consider reasons Offered. When this is the Case with a Church we think it will Justifie Such as have been members of Such a Church in withdrawing communion from them, because if they continue their communion they can have no prospect of any Other than of Always seeing the Sacred ordinances of Christ greatly prostituted & prophaned Every time they Attend them & this done by the Chh they Join with & also can have no prospect of any Other but being Obliged Always to participate of holy ordinances with Such as are not visibly their brethren, & friends to Christ. Yea without any means Used or pretended to tending to prevent its comeing to that that the greatest number & Governing part of the Church shall be such as those, naturally Exposeing the church to its own utter ruin as to those thing that are the main Ends & designs of the Christian Church. We say we think that when A Church is so infected & Corrupted in its very constitution with so fatal & incurable a disease considered considered as a body its vital & governing parts it will Justify any of Zions friends & Such as have a proper regard for the intrest of Christs Kingdom in withdrawing from Such a Church

We hereby signifie that we dont Offer what is Above in expectation of Satisfaction from the Church but as A vindication of our selves & Justification of our Opinion & that light may be Offered to the Church's consideration But 2dly: A great difficulty we have in Joining with this Church is what is contained in the Remonstrance Sent to the Revnd Mr Clark in May last which we Apprehend contains in it a great degree of unchristian Bitterness, censoriousness, Revileing, & Slandering Against Mr Edwards & those that Adhered to & particularly Against Col Dwight; therein the Chh by a publick & deliberate Act of theirs in a most Open manner reviled & Slandered Mr Edwds in Accuseing him with a Conduct to the highest degree unaccountable & Shocking particularly in Saying he contradicted his farewell Sermon & that he notwithstanding his declaration of dismission pretended to be not Only de jure but de facto pastor of the Chh from which he was dismissd & that he Set out on a Journey to procure a Council [to] Install him here, about so long before, as he himself knew the people were About to send for a Candidate, as that the news would probably spread to the Total preventing their Success therein, & in refuseing so important a place as Stockbridge where he might in All probability Settle in peace, for the Sake of Settling here in the fire of Contention with a hand full, & that he shew a great inclination Yea an Exceeding engagedness of Spirit to Settle at Northampton at the Expence of the Peace & prosperity of the greater part of the Town. These things are All denied by Mr Edwards & Several of them we know to be false Accusations

And the Church go on & Charge those of that Adhered to Mr Edwards with Schism which they themselves say is the most grievous & heavy Sin, & we Apprehend there was no Sufficient foundation at All for Such a Charge

And then with a flout & Sneer deride us & Say that in Some of our Moods & to Serve partic particular purposes, we & our Abbettors pretended there was but a little difference be[2]tween Mr Edwards's, & the Church's Opinion, & that Great & Good men had differed in the same point & Yet nevertheless had Always held Communion together. But we dont remember we ever heard of this, till it came out of Messrs Woodbridges & Williams's letter of Advice to the Church of Coldspring, & so it happens come out of the Mouths of the Church's Abbettors & not of ours. & how well this Advice Agreed with that, the Same Gentn gave this Church, in A Similar case is well known. & then mention that Some of us condemd our Selve, by partaking of the Lords Table the Sabbath immediately before the last Councill Sat here, which we don't think was so great an inconsistency, for we thôt it reasona ble, when we Expected A Council in A few days to Advise us Under our difficulties, that we should wait for their Advise, & not make A determination for our selves Just at that Juncture.

And then in page Sixth besides What has been taken notice of before as Said Against Mr Edwds there is a very large & unwarrantable liberty taken in revilings & bitter reflections Against those that Adhered to Mr Edwards, Charging of with A Conduct unaccountable, that Savoured of the reverse of a Spirit of Meekness Gentleness Sincerity & Charity which we made Such high pretensions to, & particularly, the violent Efforts we made against their Obtaining Another Minister, & Especially the Primum Mobile, in the Same breath confessing they could which was the was the first most violent mover. & our violent Efforts to hinder the getting Another Minister, Pr fas aut ne fas, & that let Northampton Sink or Swim, Mr Edwards should be settled over them Again, & of the rong representations made by Col Dwight, (as we Suppose is meant by Singleing out one in particular) in Say ing that the greatest part of the Church & parish could be content, & really chose to have Mr Edwards for their pastor, his late principle notwithstanding, & that the Church & Parish were Nosed, & forced from their true inclination, by a few particular rash & hot headed men. & then declare that they Acted on principle but lamented the necessity of it, & Still lamented it, And lookd upon it as A frown of Heaven upon 'em. & then declare that Such representations are no Other than down right falsehoods, & the Church would be glad they had reason to think they were not wilfully such and then in page 8th; there a long Harrangue Against Mr Edwards Again, for manifesting an unea siness at his being dismiss'd, & that he Shew A Strong inclination to be Settled here again in the work of the Ministry, After he had manifested such A willingness to be dismissd in his letter to Deacon Cook & otherwise, as thô he had been guilty of A great deal of Con Deceit in it. All which may be Justly & Easily Accounted, the Abominable Abuse & Injury Mr Edwards Suffered respecting his part of the Council in the pains taken to prevent yr Coming, & finally his having one in number less than the Church had, without which the Council never could have got A vote for his dismission, & now Mr Edwards see the Spirit & temper the Church & people, he had So long & so faithfully Served were of, & how resolute to get rid of him & that &c here the Pr fas et ne fas might have been mentioned with the greatest propriety thô it was wholly lost Just now

Defamation thô only of A private brother, by A private person, in private conversation, Yea in Saying things not Absolutely false, is look'd upon to be A thing that disqualifies A person for Communion, till repentance is Manifested, but we think it is A thing inexpressibly more Aggravated, for A Church as A body, in A deliberate & publick Act & vote, & in A Writeing drawn up, (which is A proper matter of record to remain to future Generations) not only thus to defame, but Slander A Minister of the Gospell & one that had long ben their pastor, & so much their Spiritual father, before Some of the most Eminent Ministers & the Elders & messengers of Some of the principal Churchs in the Land, which is further Exceedingly Aggravated, with the most peremptory, repeat ed, Obstinate, refusall of any Opportunity, for Mr Edwards's & our having any Opportunity vindicating our Character, before them to whom we were Accused, our Accusers be [3]ing face to face with us

We cant think our selves Obliged, to Join in the Ordinance of the Lords Supper, with a Church, unless there be some reason, or at least room to think that Christ will be with that Church in that Ordination that ordinance, Afording his presence & owning & blessing them in their Administration, but we think there is no room to Expect this with this Church under its present Circumstances, for we think the Church has to a very high degree disqualified themselves, by Acting as A body in the vote Above mentioned.

We have Objections Also to make against Sundry particular members of the Church, The word of God forbids Christian Churches to Tolerate Railers in their Communion & one of the Chief of the brethren of this Church did on Novr. 20th: 1749 in the Open Church Meeting, revile Mr Edwards,then our Pastor, by saying it was Apparent he regarded his own Temporal intrest, more than the good of the Church, & althô this was Openly done in the presence of great numbers, no notice was taken of it. The same Gentn: did Openly before the Church & Council that Sat here in June 1750 Abuse & vilifie the witnesses, whose evidences Mr Edwards produced to vindicate his Character. Saying there was not A word of truth in All that they had Said, Thô some of those witnesses were ministers, & persons of Eminent Character Yet the Church have never taken any notice of this

Another of the brethren of this Church viz Lt John Miller, Uttered openly & in the hearing of Several, Such revileing Expressions concerning Mr Edwards our late Pastor as these viz It would be well if his head was Seven feet under ground but he thôt Six would do his turn & Concerning Col Dwight What follows (viz) that men [. . .] they Suppose unqualified, not thrô any gross neglect or maladministration of the Chh, but only A Supposed Error in their Judgment, but we think the case to be farr Otherwise, when the Church as A body in their publick Acts, have so greatly disqualified themselves by such A manifestation of A Spirit so Contrary to that Christian love, requisite to A proper Attendance of this holy feast of love.

We are not Obliged to Attend the Lords supper under such circumstances as render it impossible to use it for the purposes for which it was instituted now tis very Evident that this holy feast was intended as A Mutual Christian Charity & friendship & so as A Testimony of the Communicants recieving & Accepting one Another as the Objects of that Charity which is due to Such. But it is impossible to give that Testimony towards those that we think live obstinately in Offences that are A Just Barr in the way of persons being recd as members in good standing.

As this is A feast of Christian friendship tis the design of the Ordinance that the communicants should not Only give Testimony of their Christian friendship to their fellow Communicants but Also that they in partaking with them should signifie their Mutually Accepting the Testimony which their fellow communicants give of their Christian friendship to them but tis impossible Solemnly to signifie or Shew A Cordial Acceptance of A pretended Testimony of Christian friendship from them that we know are at the Same time Openly Acting A part directly contrary to friendship that live in Open Alienation & bitterness manifested by reproaches revileings &c

If it be said that our withdrawing from this Church is contrary to our Covenant with this Church wherein we promised to be subject to the Government of this Church during our Abode here --- we answer that it is very unreasonable to understand that in Such an unlimited Sense that we wil continue in Communion with [. . .]

[2. Edited version.]

We the subscribers, members of the First Church in Northampton, offer what follows for our vindication in absenting from the communion at the Lord's table.

First, the practice which this church is obstinately and resolutely fixed in with regard to its own constitution and the admission of its members. We don't suppose that every corruption in the constitution of a church will justify a withdrawing from its communion; yea, we allow that a very great corruption in this respect in many cases may and ought to be borne with for a long time, rather than make an open break, and all means to be used for a reformation of the church in union and communion with it. But when it is the established principle of a church that such ought to be admitted its members, and may be received as its officers, even as its pastors, as make no pretense to any part of the distinguishing essence of the religion of Jesus Christ, or any true friendship to him; and when they continue irreclaimable in this practice and a declared resolution of continuing in it, when all possible means have been used to convince and reclaim them by such as are most proper to exhibit [true] light to 'em, and their reformation is quite hopeless, and they have proceeded to utmost ex[trem]ities in their opposition to means of conviction, even to a great degree of persecution and in a public renunciation of their pastor for a contrary opinion; and not only refusing conviction, but constantly refusing in any calm or just manner to hear and consider reasons offered: when this is the case with a church, we think it will justify such as have been members of such a church in withdrawing communion from them, because if they continue in their communion they can have no prospect of any other than of always seeing the sacred ordinances of Christ greatly prostituted and profaned every time they attend them, and this done by the church they join with; and also can have no prospect of any other but being obliged always to participate of holy ordinances with such as are not visibly their brethren, and friends to Christ--yea, without any means used or pretended to, tending to prevent its coming to that, that the greatest number and governing part of the church shall be such as those, naturally exposing the church to its own utter ruin as to those great things that are the main ends and designs of the Christian church. We say we think that when a church is so infected and corrupted in its very constitution with so fatal and incurable a disease considered [in] its vital and governing parts, it will justify any of Zion's friends and such as have a proper regard for the interest of Christ's kingdom in withdrawing from such a church.

We hereby signify that we don't offer what is above in expectation of satisfaction from the church but as a vindication of ourselves and justification of our opinion, and that light may be offered to the church's consideration. But

Secondly, a great difficulty we have in joining with this church is what is contained in the Remonstrance sent to the Rev. Mr. Clark in May last, which we apprehend contains in it a great degree of unchristian bitterness, censoriousness, reviling, and slandering against Mr. Edwards and those that adhered to him, and particularly against Col. Dwight: therein the church, by a public and deliberate act of theirs, in a most open manner reviled and slandered Mr. Edwards in accusing him with a conduct to the highest degree unaccountable and shocking, particularly in saying he contradicted his farewell sermon and that he, notwithstanding his declaration of a willingness for a dismission, pretended to be not only de jure but de facto pastor of the church from which he was dismissed; and that he set out on a journey to procure a council [to] install him here, about so long before, as he himself knew the people were about to send for a candidate, as that the news would probably spread to the total preventing their success therein, and in refusing so important a place as Stockbridge, where he might in all probability settle in peace, for the sake of settling here in the fire of contention with a handful; and that he shows a great inclination, yea, an exceeding engagedness of spirit to settle at Northampton at the expense of the peace and prosperity of the greater part of the town. These things are all denied by Mr. Edwards, and several of them we know to be false accusations.

And the church go on and charge those of [them] that adhered to Mr. Edwards with schism, which they themselves say is the most grievous and heavy sin, and we apprehend there was no sufficient foundation at all for such a charge.

And then [they] with a flout and sneer deride us, and say that in some of our moods, and to serve particular purposes, we and our abettors pretended there was but a little difference between Mr. Edwards' and the church's opinion, and that great and good men had differed in the same point and yet nevertheless had always held communion together. But we don't remember we ever heard of this, till it came out of Messrs. Woodbridge's & Williams' letter of advice to the church of Cold Spring, and so it happens to come out of the mouths of the church's abettors and not of ours. And how well this advice agreed with that, the same gentlemen gave this church, in a similar case, is well known. And then [they] mention that some of us condemned ourselves, by partaking of the Lord's table the sabbath immediately before the last council sat here; which we don't think was so great an inconsistency, for we thought it reasonable, when we expected a council in a few days to advise us under our difficulties, that we should wait for their advice, and not make a determination for ourselves just at that juncture.

And then in page [the] sixth, besides what has been taken notice of before as said against Mr. Edwards, there is a very large and unwarrantable liberty taken in revilings and bitter reflections against those that adhered to Mr. Edwards, charging of them with a conduct unaccountable, that savored of the reverse of a spirit of meekness, gentleness, sincerity and charity, which we made such high pretensions to; and particularly, the violent efforts we made against their obtaining another minister, and especially the primum mobile, in the same breath confessing they could not tell which was the most violent mover; and our violent efforts to hinder the getting another minister, per fas aut ne fas, and that let Northampton sink or swim, Mr. Edwards should be settled over them again; and of the wrong representations made by Col. Dwight (as we suppose is meant by singling out one in particular), in saying that the greatest part of the church and parish could be content, and really chose to have Mr. Edwards for their pastor, his late principle notwithstanding; and that the church and parish were nosed, and forced from their true inclination, by a few particular, rash and hot-headed men. And then [they] declare that they acted on principle, but lamented the necessity of it, and still lamented it, and looked upon it as a frown of heaven upon 'em; and then declare that such representations are no other than downright falsehoods, and the church would be glad they had reason to think they were not willfully such. And then in page [the] eighth, there [is] a long harangue against Mr. Edwards again, for manifesting an uneasiness at his being dismissed, and that he show[ed] a strong inclination to be settled here again in the work of the ministry, after he had manifested such a willingness to be dismissed in his letter to Deac. Cook and otherwise, as though he had been guilty of a great deal of deceit in it. All which may be justly and easily accounted for: the abominable abuse and injury Mr. Edwards suffered respecting his part of the council, in the pains taken to prevent their coming, and finally his having one in number less than the church had, without which the council never could have got a vote for his dismission; and now Mr. Edwards see[s] the violent spirit and temper the church and people, he had so long and so faithfully served were of, and how resolute to get rid of him, and that, etc.: here the per fas aut ne fas might have been mentioned with the greatest propriety, though it was wholly lost just now.

Defamation, though only of a private brother, by a private person, in private conversation, yea, in saying things not absolutely false, is looked upon to be a thing that disqualifies a person for communion, till repentance is manifested; but we think it is a thing inexpressibly more aggravated, for a church as a body, in a deliberate and public act and vote, and in a writing drawn up (which is a proper matter of record to remain to future generations), not only thus to defame, but slander a minister of the gospel, and one that had long been their own pastor, and so much their spiritual father, before some of the most eminent ministers and the elders and messengers of some of the principal churches in the land; which is further exceedingly aggravated, with the most peremptory, repeated, obstinate refusal of any opportunity, for Mr. Edwards' and our having any opportunity vindicating our character, before them to whom we were accused, our Accusers being face to face with us.

We can't think ourselves obliged, to join in the ordinance of the Lord's Supper, with a church, unless there be some reason, or at least room to think that Christ will be with that church in that ordinance, affording his presence and owning and blessing them in their administration; but we think there is no room to expect this with this church under its present circumstances, for we think the church has to a very high degree disqualified themselves, by acting as a body in the vote above-mentioned.

We have objections also to make against sundry particular members of the church. The Word of God forbids Christian churches to tolerate railers in their communion, and one of the chief of the brethren of this church, viz. Moderator [Ebenezer] Pomeroy, Senior, did on Nov. 20th, 1749, in the open church meeting, revile Mr. Edwards, then our pastor, by saying it was apparent he regarded his own temporal interest, more than the good of the church; and although this was openly done in the presence of great numbers, no notice was taken of it.

The same gentleman did openly before the church and council that sat here in June 1750 abuse and vilify the witnesses, whose evidences Mr. Edwards produced to vindicate his character, saying there was not a word of truth in all that they had said, though some of those witnesses were ministers, and persons of eminent Character: yet the church have never taken any notice of this.

Another of the brethren of this church, viz. Lt. John Miller, uttered openly and in the hearing of several, such reviling expressions concerning Mr. Edwards our late pastor as these, viz. "It would be well if his head was seven feet underground, but he thought six would do his turn," and "Mr. Edwards was just like his old cow lowing after a good mess"; and concerning Col. Dwight what follows (viz.), that men [. . .]

[. . .] they suppose unqualified, not through any gross neglect or maladministration of the church; but only a supposed error in their judgment, but we think the case to be far otherwise, when the church as a body in their public acts, have so greatly disqualified themselves by such a manifestation of a spirit so contrary to that Christian love, requisite to a proper attendance of this holy feast of love.

We are not obliged to attend the Lord's Supper under such circumstances as render it impossible to use it for the purposes for which it was instituted. Now 'tis very evident that this holy feast was intended as a mutual Christian charity and friendship, and so as a testimony of the communicants' receiving and accepting one another as the objects of that charity which is due to such. But it is impossible to give that testimony towards those that we think live obstinately in offenses that are a just bar in the way of persons being received as members in good standing.

As this is a feast of Christian friendship, 'tis the design of the ordinance that the communicants should not only give testimony of their Christian friendship to their fellow communicants, but also that they in partaking with them should signify their mutually accepting the testimony which their fellow communicants give of their Christian friendship to them; but 'tis impossible solemnly to signify or show a cordial acceptance of a pretended testimony of Christian friendship from them that we know are at the same time openly acting a part directly contrary to friendship, that live in open alienation and bitterness manifested by reproaches, revilings, etc.

If it be said that our withdrawing from this church is contrary to our covenant with this church, wherein we promised to be subject to the government of this church during our abode here, we answer that it is very unreasonable to understand that in such an unlimited sense that we will continue in communion with [. . .]
