A193. Letter to Thomas Prince, May 10, 1754 193.JE-T.Prince 5-10-54 Jonathan Edwards

A193. JE to Thomas Prince, 5/10/54. MHS, Ms. Large Collection.

Stockbridge May 10. 1754.1

Rev. and honoured sir,

I thank you for your Favour of April 17 which came Safe to

Hand the last with Mr. Hollis's Money ; which was brought from

Northampton by my Son.

I find by the Contents of your Letter that there is Need of your

having laid before you a particular Account of many Things re-

lating to the past & present State of Mr. Hollis's School, that you

may have some tolerable Idea of the Circumstances of the

Affair.

Mr. Hollis has maintain'd a school in this Place for about nine

Years Three years before Capt. Kellogg was improved & years six years

before one Mohawk was taken into the school on Mr. Hollis's Account.

After the school was set up and had been continued for some Time,

consisting only of Stockbridge Boys, The stockbridge Indians, excited

by Mr. Sergeant, gave of their own Land, a Farm of two hundred Acres,

of an excellent Soil, within half a mile of the Meeting House, for the

use of the Boarding school, with a special view to the Benefit of

their Children ; for the school had then alwaies consisted of such

only ; and it was long before any of the Mohawks look'd this

way. The Farm is very conveniently Situated, being a Peninsu-

la, encompassed on three sides by the River ; and is of great value.

The Indians, besides giving this Farm, helped to clear some of it,

and were at the trouble and Cost of building a Bridge over the

River to accommodate the school. Afterwards an House was

built for the school on this Farm ; and about 500£ laid out & upon [it,]

which had been charitably contributed in stockbridge & other

Places ; besides 500£ more given by the Commissioners; and all

this before the Mohawks so much as talked of coming hither.

When the Mohawks first came down in the Fall of the year 1750.

Capt. K. did not take any of 'em on Mr. Hollis's Account : But

not having heard from Mr. Hollis a long Time, & not knowing

but he was dead, He dismiss'd all Mr. H---s's scholars, (stockbridge

Boys) & in their Room took Mohawk Boys, to look after; not on Mr.

Hollis's Account, but on the Account of the Countrey, & was paid

by the Province. When the Mohawks first came, Capt. K.

was mightily taken, and was very full of his Promises to them what

great things should be done for them & their Children, how well

they should be fed & Clothed and instructed, and looked after in

all Respects ; raising their Expectations very high. Which Promises the

Indians remember'd, and often put us in Mind of 'em afterwards.

---- Capt. K. went on with the Mohawks at the Charge of the Pro-

vince 'till the next summer 1751; when, Mr. Hollis having been

informed of the Mohawks coming, Capt. K. received a Letter

from Him , wherein He writes as supposing his school still in

Being consisting of 12 Boys, agreable to his former Orders which

related only to stockbridge Boys, and he now ordered him to

add 12 Boys to more to the Number. far from ordering Him

to

to dismiss his 12 St---ge Boys ; but his Letter plainly implying the marginal

Contrary. On which Capt. K. now having no Boys at all on Mr.

Hollis's Account, goes about to get his 24 Boys ; and to make up the

Number he took up some Mohawks, and some St---ge Boys ; But the

greater Part M-ks ; for by this Time the St---ge Indians were

heartily sick of Capt. K---gs managements, and but few of their

Boys could be obtain'd by him : so that He could not make up his

full Number of 24. as yet. --- By this Time, there began to ap-

pear an Uneasiness among the St---ge Indians ; that whereas

they had given the Farm, built the Bridge &c. to accommodate

the school, with a special view to the Benefit of their own Chil-

dren, their Boys had the Fall before been turn'd away, and Mo-

hawks taken into the House and upon the Farm in their stead. And

we had several Meetings with 'em on this Affair, before they could

be quieted with respect to it. Finally they were made easy with

this, that there was no design of excluding their Children, or

taking away the Land from them ; but that special Regard should

be had to their Children still, according to their Intention in the

sequestration of the Farm ; And that the M---ks were like to im-

prove their Land but a little while, the Design being that they

should be removed to the Hop-Lands.

In the month of Aug. of that summer 1751. was here in stock-

bridge, a grand Conference of the Committee of the Gen. Court

with the sachems of the Mohawks ; who came down from their own

Countrey with a great Train of about 100 young & old, being invited

hither to confer about the Affair of the Mohawks settling in the

Countrey, and putting their Children to school here. In the Confe-

rence they objected that the Promises of the English concerning their

Care for the Instruction of their Children in Times Past had not been

fulfilled. They mention'd a Treaty which they had had with this

Province many years ago, wherein the English had promised to send

them a missionary to preach to 'em ; and a schoolmaster or masters to

instruct their Children ; and produced a silver Box with the Pro-

vince seal in it, which they said was given 'em to confirm these

Promises : which yet had never been fulfilled. Moreover they

said, that when some of their People came down hither the preceding

Fall, they were promised that their Children should be handsomly

clothed, and in other Respects well looked after ; whereas they found

their Children Naked, & Nasty, and in a worse Condition than

they were in in their own Countrey ; and that they observed a great

Neglect & woful Confusion in the met manner of their Instruction,

quite diverse from what they had observed in English schools.

In the Reply. Col. dwight acknowledged and lamented that

their Children had been neglected ; But told 'em that if their

Children stay'd they wo thorough & speedy Care should be taken

that there should be a thorough Amendment, and they should

soon see it with their own own Eyes. And finally, after much

deliberation, the Indians consented to leave their Children ; But

were careful to inform the Committee in a dependence on these

Promises being fulfill'd and that they should be deceived no more marginal

with fair Promises without fulfilment Performance.

Nevertheless, things still went on as they did before in the same

Negligence and Confusion. Col. dwight was much in observing how

that Capt. K. was perf quite incorrigeable, And that there was

no Hopes of any Order or success in the school as so long as He

had the Care of it ; and that therefore there was a great Ne-

cessity of a New Master for the Boys. Accordingly the Commissi

oners were applied to with Brigr d--ts Zealous Concurrence.

But it was a long Time before another Master could be obtain'd,

& so a long time before there was any happy Alteration in the

school agreeable to the Expectations of the Mohawks, & the Commit-

tees Promises. so that many of the Mohawks were much discouraged,

many that were coming from the Mohawk Countrey turnd

back again ; and Hendrick, the Chief speaker of the Mohawks,

declared he would never have any thing more to do with

the stockbridge Affair. But in Feb. 1752. A master was

obtaind, viz. Mr. Hawley ; whom I, by the Commissrs Order,

introduced into the school, informing the Indians how he

would now set up a regular school. at which they appeared

highly pleased ; said this was what they had waited and

longed for. And when Mr. Hawley began his school, and the

Mohawks observed the Regularity of his Managemts, And

the Order of his school, there were the greatest Testimonies

of Satisfaction & well-Pleasedness that ever I saw in 'em on

any Occasion. But this fair Weather lasted but a very little

while : Col. dwight had by this Time got far in his Ac-

quaintance, and Friendship with the Family of the Williams's,

who were greatly attached to Capt. Kellogg, and against his

being removed. so that now all at once the Brigadier seem'd

strangely changed, & was for continuing Capt. K. and soon

appeared openly against me and against Mr. Hawley. so that

Capt. K-- was Continued, and Claim'd most of the Boys as

his own. so that Mr. Hawleys regular school was soon bro-

ken all in Pieces, and things reduced to as great and greater

Confusion than ever ; and all the Joy, and pleasing Expecta-

tions which appeared in the Mohawks on Mr. Hawleys first

coming were dash'd in Pieces. so that the number of the

Mohawks soon began to diminish ; many went home to re-

turn no more. And in the following April. 1753. above 20

of the Indians of the five Nations, who came from Onohoh-

quaughe (who had appeared the best disposed of any of the Indi-

ans) went away at once, Openly declaring before many Wit-

nesses, that it was from no dissatisfaction with me, or any of

my People, that they went away, but wholly from dislike of

the managemt of Col. d. & Capt. K. The Mohawks that

remov'd expressed great dissatisfaction also at the same things & in

the same manner.

The Uneasiness of the stockbridge Indians also now increased;

insomuch that they had a Meeting, & sent their sachem to warn

Capt

Capt. K. off from their Land which they had sequester'd for the Use marginal

of the Boarding-school.

Then it was in the spring of the last year that a stockbridge Indian

was kill'd in the woods by a Couple of Englishmen or Irishmen;

which put our Indians into a very great Ruffle.2

The summer following, the Heads of the Families of the Mo-

hawks that were left were at my House ; and in a long Conversa

tion, br declared before several Witnesses their great Uneasiness

here by Reason of Col. D---ts and Capt. K--gs Managements,

and their Resolution of all going away, finally to leave St---ge

e'er e're long. After this they were at my House again and

declared the same before several of the People of the Town, who

happen'd to be at my House at that Time : and being then parti-

cularly enquired of whether their design of going away was from

any Uneasiness with me or any of my People, they declared it was

not, but wholly & only the Managemts of Col. d. & those that

join'd with Him. This they declared in a Variety of Expressions.

After this, the same summer, Hendrick the Chief speaker of

the Tribe came down from the Mohawk Countrey, and being at

my House with some other Mohawks, declared the same things

again, Concerning their Resolution of all going away, finally to leave

St---ge in a short time, mentioning the like Grounds of their

Uneasiness ; and among other things he mention'd as Occasions of

their dissatisfaction was Col. d---ts so much opposing me.

In sep the september following, came on the Trial of the men

that had kill'd the Indian ; when they were acquitted of mur-

der, & not executed. On which by degrees appear'd more &

more Uneasiness among the Indians ; both the stockbridge Indi-

ans & Mohawks ; who often were expressing their Resentmts in

their Conversation.

That Fall Capt. K. died at Newington. And Brigr D. took

the Care of Mr. Hollis's school ; undertaking to do the Part of

a school-master Himself : the school still Consisting partly of

Mohawks, & partly of st---ge Indians. --- But I could not

perceive that the Indians were any better pleas'd than before.

After this, One and another of the Mohawks were at my

House from Time to Time, and often spoke of their design of going

all away in the spring never to Come again.

After some Col. d- had pretended to keep school Himself for

Some Time, He got a young Gentleman, one Mr. Smith, to

come and keep the school ; who kept it 'till Mr. Hollis's Letter

to me arrived. But still the Mohawks persisted in expressing

the same Resolution of all going away in the spring ; And went

away a considerable Part of 'em just before Mr. Hollis's Letter

arrived, near half the scholars (by Mr. Smiths account;) the

rest intending to follow assoon as the Time for making sug

Maple-sugar was over.

Mr. Hollis's Letter arrived just as about the Time that the Indi

ans began to go into the Woods to make sugar, and at a Time

when the Uneasiness of the Indians, both Mohawks and st--ge

Indians; and their dissaffection towards the English on Account

of the Mens escaping wi being acquitted that kill'd greatly

increased

increased, and in some was risen to an extreme hight, (tho' secretly) marginal

occasion'd by some young Indians who had been abroad among some

Nations of Indians which have been lately been very much under the

Influence of the French ; and had in their Resentment & Revenge

entered into a Conspiracy, with some other Indians abroad (who

are much dissaffected to the English) to destroy all the English in

stockbridge, and burn the Town all their Houses, and go away

to Canada. And there are some things that make it look very

probable that they had drawn some of the young Mohawks, who

were reckon'd as of Mr. Hollis's school into the Conspiracy.

When Mr. Hollis's Letter came to Hand there were ten left

in St---ge, who were accounted as belonging to the school, tho'

not all Mohawks ; F some of them very unsuitable members of

the school, were taken in not agreeable to Mr. Hollis's Orders to

Capt. K.-- when they were Old, Men-grown, two of them

married men and had Children. When I went to the

school to enquire into the state of it, Mr. Smith told me that

4 out of the 10. had declared they would never come to school any

more ; some of these old ones had lately appeared very cross &

surly. 'Tis probable that some of them they had entered in then into

the bloody design before mentiond with some of the st--ge

young Men.

When I received Mr. Hollis's Letter, I proceeded with the

greatest Precaution and Prudence I was capable of : I was here in-

trusted alone ; but I took the best advice I could come at. ----

when I informed Col. d. of Mr. Hollis's Orders, He offered to

Continue things along in the school, in the state they were then

in (If I would be at the Charge of it) 'till I could be ready to

take the Care of them my self. It being impossible I should be ready to

take the immediate Care of 'em in an Instant, I accepted this offer,

and especially that I might have time to inform my self whether

the Mohawk scholars were to be obtain'd under a new Regulation;

It being now just the Time when they were all going out a sugaring,

wives and Children (as their Manner is) when they would be wholly

absent from the school, as they alwaies are at such seasons ; And

then (if they persisted in their long fix'd, & often declared Resolution)

assoon as they had done sugaring They would immediately leave

the Countrey ; & so could not possibly at any time at all, be in the

school. It was therefore necessary to let things continue in the

as they were 'till I could have Opportunity to enquire into that

Matter, before I pretended to put the Affair on a new Footing.

But assoon as I could, I sent for the Mohawks, all, Men, Women

& Children to my House and desired the English People of the Town

that were my Friends to be there, to be Witnesses, and assisting

in the Conference. When all were met, I told the Indians the

Orders I had from Mr. Hollis ; made 'em the Offer of taking their

Children ; discoursed the matter very largely ; some of my Friends

assisting me. I told 'em as much of the Care I would take of their

Children as I thought was best. Mr. Woodbridge discoursed

very largely to them, & used many arguments with them to induce

em to leave their Children under my Care ; told 'em what a pro- marginal

bability there was that the school would be under better Regula-

tion than it had been. But they replied, that they had heard

such Promises concerning the school so often, and had been so often

disappointed that they were discouraged ; And said, that Besides

the affair of their Removal had been determined by a C. the

a Council of the sachems of their Nation, which they could not de-

part from without carrying the Affair to them again. It is a

vanity, (to the Utmost degree) for any to pretend that I, or my

Interpreter misunderstood them: It was not a transient saying

th only, that was suddenly taken up: the Matter of their go-

ing away was very largely discoursed, & repeated, with Reasons

given very particularly in a long Conversation on the subject;

besides the same thing said over and over for three Quarters

of a year before, as before observed. As to their, or their

Childrens weeping, or shewing themselves grieved that I would

not take Care of their Children, 'tis a Misrepresentation of the

grossest sort. for not only was there Nothing of that Nature;

But no such thing was hinted at as that I would not take Care

of their Children.3 Indeed, afterwards I sent Col. d. word that

I should no longer desire Him to take Care of em on my Account,

& or woul or chose not to be at the Charge of paying for what

He should do hiring the Master he had provided &c. for that

would have been a perfect throwing away Mr. Hollis's Money.

The Indians were then going away, with all their Children, into

the woods, to be absent from the Wholly from the school. and

assoon as they returned, appeared fix'd in their Resolutions to go

leave the Countrey, to be absent forever from the school. However

it may have been the manner of those that had the Care of those

things, to draw Pay from Mr. Hollis for Masters, for many Months,

while they were not at all employed in the Business, 'tis what

I alwaies have born a Testimony against ; and therefore could

not with a good Face go into the same Practice my self.

What stories some of my Enemies might tell the Indians con-

cerning my designs, to make 'em Cry, I can't tell: I know it has

been the Manner of some to take almost an unbounded Liberty

in stories they have told the Indians to disaffect 'em to me. And

I have good Reason to think there are some in the Town would

do their utmost to alienate the Indians from me, and to discou-

rage the Mohawks from letting their Children come under my

Care. ----- Besides what I said to the Mohawks at the fore-

mention'd Conference, I afterwards, at every Opportunity, re

renewed my Offers of taking their Children ; and offered for

the present to take 'em to my own House, & look after 'em as

my own Children. Yea and have actually taken one to

of them to live at my House before the others went away.

and because the Parents were none of 'em willing to leave

their Children unless s they could live here themselves,

I have laid out my self to get some Land for 'em to improve, marginal

& also to get it plowed for 'em ; and to this End have been at

the Trouble of a great deal of Consultation with the English & the

stockbridge Indians. ---- The Representation made in the Letter

you speak of is of a Piece with what is daily done here. There are

some here that industriously spread abroad such Representations, and

say that the school was in flourishing Circumstances 'till it came

into my Hands, And that I immediately threw up the school after

I had taken it. & that I had broken up the Mohawk Affair &c. &c.

They have been this several years murdering the Mohawk

Affair themselves with Cruel Hands ; and now just as it is ex-

piring, they lay to me the mischief which they have most ap-

parently done themselves; and are indefatigably endeavouring

to possess every body with an agreeable Opinion. And I have no

Reason to expect any thing else of them, as long as they stay here,

but that they will with all imaginable Industry and Craftiness,

not to say Guile & deceitfulness seek, by all means, to clog 'em-

barrass & Ruin my Affairs. And from the Representations which

are sent to Boston we may easily Conjecture what Representations

will be sent to Mr. Hollis. ---- But I would intreat you sir,

for the future, in Justice to me, to pay no Regard to the Repre-

sentations of my known and open Enemies. And I desire sir that

you shew the Person or Persons to whom the Letter you mention

was written ad about my grieving the Mohawks, what I have

here written concerning that Matter, and the Testimonies about

it which I herewith send.4

But to resume my Narrative. Tho' the Mohawks seemed

to be so much fix'd in their Resolution of all leaving the Place,

yet some of 'em, on further Consideration came and offered to deliver

their Children to me, as soon as they should return from sugaring.

which offer I readily accepted, and told 'em they should come &

live at my House 'till I could get a good Master to come and

board 'em & look after 'em. After this the Mother of two of

the Children that were offered came to be me again with

her Eldest son (one of those Fellows I mention'd before as dissaffected

& probably joining in the horrid designs of some of the young Indians

of the stockbridge Tribe) and told me that this her son would

not consent to her leaving any of her Children.*5 I then renewedly

used Arguments to perswade her. The woman seemed to have

a great Mind her Children should be stay, and seemed to argue

the matter much with her son ; But He would by no means Consent.

But finally she promised she would return some Time in the sum-

mer, & bring back her Children, & put 'em under my Care : And so

left some of her things at my House as a Pledge of it.

Now there was left but one Mohawk Boy ; whom I have taken to

my own House. Besides the Mohawks, there were a Number of

stockbridge Boys which had belonged to Mr. Hollis's school;

which therefore, If I tied my self exactly to Mr. Hollis's Instruc-

tions, I should have collected together. But I knew most of 'em

were wholly unfit to answer his design, never having been at all

Cultivated

marginal

under Capt. Kellog's but rather had grown more rough & wild

than when he first took them, and were now past their form-

ing Age, There were two of em that were not past a Capacity

of Cultivation these therefore I took. And instead of those

others (some of which had a Mind to come in) I took three

new Ones that were young, such as I thought likely & promising.

I thought if I pretended to keep the school in Being, I m could

not take less than six. so that the school now consists of

one Mohawk, and five st---ge Boys. The Mohawk is at

my House ; the others live all at the House of Mr. Joseph Wood-

bridge ; a very desireable Family ; The Man one of the best

sort of Men that I am acquainted with, the most faithfull &

thorough in Business, and of the most generous Christian and

publicK spirit. the Boys at present go to school to Mr. W---ge

Timothy Woodbridge, whom I have desired to take extraordinary

Pains with them, till I can get another Master for 'em.

This sir, is what I have done And I will now give you the Reasons

of my Conduct. ----- It was apparent that it was absolutely necessary

that the school should be kept in Being. To let it sink for the present

under a Notion of reviving it again some Time hence hereafter,

would be the way to have it sink finally, especially as to the Mo-

hawks. To have let fall the school wholly, because now Mohawk

Boys are not to be obtain'd, of hopes of grad getting half a dozen

Mohawk Boys hereafter to begin the school with them, would

have been to have proceeded on a vain & unreasonable Expectation.

They have been so often disappointed with Respect to the sat state of

the school, and so continually & long deceived with fair Promises

of a better state of it, that 'tis now apparent that, not the hearing of the

Ear, but the sight of the Eye only will convince 'em. there is not

the least hope of ever regaining Mohawk Children to the school

any other way than this, viz. by giving them to see, Not only that the

school is upheld, but that tis well settled, & indeed in good Cir-

cumstances, & established under a happy Regulation than it used

to be : And this they must see for some time, before their Pre-

judices will be removed. If they see this indeed brought to Effect,

& that the school is no established, & continues in good Circum-

stances, then perhaps, by Mr. Hawley's Help, in Length of Time,

a number, yea a Considerable number of Mohawk Boys might

be obtained. But how should they see this if the school had no Be-

ing? And when the Mohawks at present refuse to leave their Chil-

dren, how shall we give the school a Being, in order to gain

that the Mohawks may see it, & see its flourishing state, in order

to our gaining their Children, than by our constituting the school

at present of such Children as can be had? And it was of

vast Importance that the school, now it is come into new

Hands, should be soon amended & settled under a happy Regu-

lation. For if the Indians, after they have seen it so long in such

miserable broken Circumstances, and have had their Patience &

Hopes so worn out by multiplied disappointments, should see

it continuing in its broken state still, now it is got into quite other

Hands, it will tend to their utterly despairing of ever seeing

it

marginal

it otherwise ; and by not taking speedy Care that it might be other-

wise, would be to give the finishing stroke to that important affair

that was before just Expiring.

Therefore that now the Indians might see the school happily settled,

under a good Regulation, the first thing to be the school must

be kept in Being, & the first thing to be done must be to seek a

good Master to settle in the Business, who should board 'em, & every

way take Care of 'em. Accordingly this was the Thing I immediately

applied my self to. I went to dr. Ashley,6 and proposed the Affair

to Him, conversed largely with him upon it, left it to his Considera-

tion, & then visited Him again at Westfield, And finally prevailed

so far that he consented to come up to stockbridge and bring his

Wife to see the Place. and accordingly they came up, and were

the last week, & spent the Sabbath here. But when the doctor came

to see your Letter to me, it seem'd to discourage Him, and put an End

to his entertaining any Thoughts of coming, for the at least 'till the

affairs of the school should be further settled by Mr. Hollis. And

indeed I could not have the Face to desire it of him. ------ But I

exceedingly regret it, that the settling the school in proper form is

thus hindred. --- Tis a thousand Pities. --- Tis to be fear'd the Conse-

quences will be very unhappy. ----- I take the doctor to be the fittest

Man for the Business that can be found in N. England. I have

long been acquainted with him, have had intimate Conversation

with Him as to his souls Concerns : I take Him to be a man of real

Experimental Piety. He is a Gentleman of liberal Education. He

has a most established Character, for a man of the greatest Integrity

& Fidelity, a publick spirited, generous Man ; One of great Concern

for the Interest of Religion; of great faith & activity in the Manage-

ment of Business, and having a singular Talent at instructing

& governing Children, and one that thoroughly understands farm-

ing. He might probably have been a great Blessing, not only to

that school, but to the Town in general with Respect to all it's

important Affairs.

With Regard to the schools Consisting at present chiefly of St--ge

Boys, it is further to be considered that Mr. Hollis's Letter imports

no such thing, as that his school should consist only of Mohawks,

only in Case his sc I find his school wholly broke up ; otherwise

He directs me to collect together all that used to be of his school

and his school has for the most part consisted wholly of stockbridge

Boys ; and in all it's stages from the Beginning, partly of

them. And Mr. H. had no Reason to think from his any

other from his past Instructions to Capt. K. but that there

were twelve of his St--ge scholars to be found, to be collec-

ted by me. Indeed there were more of his scholars of this

of this sort than I have taken up. but only (some of which would

be glad to be accepted) But only instead of old uncultivated

ones, on whom Mr. Hollis money would be thrown away, I

have taken three Young ones of a proper Age to have Mr.

Hollis's Ends answered upon them. I found Mr. Hollis's school

consisting of ten, when Mr. H his Letter arrived ; and we may

well

marginal

well suppose that Mr. H. expects that I should carry on the

school with as many as I find in it ; and not only so but also

collect others that are scattered from it. But instead of that

I have not taken made it to consist of more than half so

many ; having taken up but six, besides the two Children

Mohawk Children that are promised to come in the summer

(whose coming is uncertain) and if they come there will

be eight.

If it had been so that I could immediately have got six Mo-

hawk Boys, yet to have fill'd the school only with Mohawks,

& wholly to have neglected the St---ge Children, would have

been of immediate fatal Consequence. You see sir, how much

the St---ge Indians have done for the school with a special

Respect to the Benefit of their own Children in it; and how jea-

lous they have been of the Interest they Claim, and indeed may

well claim, and with what difficulty they have been made

easy that the Mohawks should have so much of the Benefit as

they have had -- And now on my taking the school, their

Jealousy immediately appear'd : they manifested that they ex-

pected to be consulted in the Affair. accordingly I called a meeting

of them : At the meeting they put me in mind of their giving

the Land, with a special Respect to their Children. To have

excluded their Children would have been a fatal Consequences

especially at this Time which when they have just been in so great

a Ruffle about the man that was killd. -- I would have by

no means had any thing to have done with the school on these

Terms, as to be obliged to constitute it only of Mohawks. -- It

would have been to expose my self to great Trouble ; and the

Ruin of my Usefulness among the Indians.

I should not have thought sir, that I had run any Venture in Proceeding

as I have done, had it not been for your Letter. But I am here alone

intrusted with this Business, and could do no otherwise than according

to my own best discretion, with the Advice of my Friends that are

here, who see the state of things and have known Mr. Hollis's

& Mr. sergeants Manner from the Beginning ; who thought it no

venture to do as I have done : They say Mr. sergt, who undoubtless

edly was a very honest faithful man, ever went on those Principles

that to attend Mr. Hollis's Instructions as far as the state of things

(which he Knew and which it was impossible for Mr. H. to know)

would allow. But to proceed so as not to defeat Mr. Hollis's main

design, for fear of departing from his Instructions in some Punctilios,

And that Mr. sergt took a far greater Liberty than I have done

in this Respect, and was never blamed for it by Mr. Hollis.

But we in our great Caution least we should not be punctual to

Mr. Hollis's Instructions, are knocking on the Head the thing

which He mainly has his Heart upon, and taking a Course

to spend his money to little Purpose, in comparison of what might

be, if we acted as we see the state of Things absolutely requires.

I know not what I shall do. I have been endeavouring to perswade

the

the Mohawks to let me have their Children with such Argu- marginal

ments, that probably the school would soon be under much better

Regulations than it used to be. But if they see it long in an un-

settled state, I shall soon be ranked with others that heretofore

have deluded 'em with such Oromises, and shall have no more

Credit with them. But now I must wait for new Instructions

from Mr. H. And you see sir, how long it has been in Times past

because we could have any new Instructions from Him. For five or

six years past, his Letters have not arrived but about once in two years.

And we find it is commonly two or three months before we can

have a Return from Boston. ---- I can't But think Mr. Hollis

will be grieved when he comes to understand the state of things;

and will be ready to blame us for needless & excessive Caution, when

he comes to understand the bad Consequences of it.

This Affair comes into my Hands under the most difficult perplex'd Cir-

cumstances imaginable, just as all was falling into Ruin &c. -

you was pleased sir, in your former Letter kindly to warn me against

triumphing on the Receipt of Mr. H---s's Letter -- I took the Caution

well. But really, at present I have much greater Temptation to be

heartily sorry, that I have any Concern in a matter embarrassed with

so many discouraging, perplexing Circumstances, than to triumph in

it .

I desire sir, that you would lay these Things before some of my

Friends in Boston and take their Thoughts. ----- And I desire

that assoon as possible, you would write to Mr. Hollis, giving

him some Account of these Affairs, in such a Manner as you shall

think most prudent. I had rather trust your Judgment than mine own

as to the manner of informing Mr. H. in Order to the best Influ-

ence on his Mind. But 'tis very necessary that He should make a

Return with the utmost possible Expedition, and give me some

more full and explicit Instructions concerning the school. I wish

sir, that you would urge that, if possible, we may have a Letter

before Winter. Since dr. Ashley's coming is put by, I have sent

into the Jerseys for a young Gentleman to come from thence to in-

struct the scholars this summer, while they are boarded at private

Houses as at present. But tis absolutely necessary that I should

have new Orders from Mr. H. For since I have received your Letter

I have determined to proceed no further with the school without

new Instructions, than only so as to spend the Money which I have

received ; for to be at the Charge of the school my self ; will presently

break my small Estate and ruin my Family. And please to desire

Mr. H. if he intends the Affair shall go on, to make new Re-

mittance, which may arrive, the at furthest, the next spring.

for much will be saved by having ready money to pay for the

Necessaries of the school. ---- I shall give Mr. H. particular &

exact Accounts what I do with his Money. ----- The sachem of

the st---ge Tribe has a son that He has a great desire should be

in the school. I am exceeding sorry I did not know it before

I had compleated my Number of six. He being the chief Man

of the whole Tribe, it would be happy if his son might be in

the

the school. --- Mr. H. in his Letter to me says " If any of marginal

"the Boys should appear capable, I would have them educated for

"the Ministry, that they may be Preachers to their Countrey-men.

I want to know whether Mr. H. will allow to educate St--ge

Boys ; for I know some such that would be likely & promising, whose

Parents would be glad they might have such an Education. ---

'Tis absolutely necessary that Mr. H. should not so tie my Hands,

But that I may have some Liberty to act as I see the necessity of

affairs requires, otherwise I shall be often extremely perplexed &

embarrass'd, and his Money will be lost. --- It has been grievous

to me to see others throw away his money ; but it will be more

grievous to have a Hand in doing so my self. --- I want to be able

to tell a Master that I may agree with for a settlement in the Busi

ness, what dependence he might have for a support in Case Mr.

H. should be removed by death. This Question was put by dr.

Ashley, and I may expect the same Enquiry will be made by

any other that I shall endeavour to obtain to settle in the Business.

---- 'Tis needful that Mr. H. should be informed that Col. dwight

refuses to deliver up the Cattle & Houshold Furniture which belongs

to the Boarding school, which was purchased with his Money. The

Reason He gives is, that Mr. H. is in debt to hi Capt. K. and to

Him, and it Concerns him to look to Himself. ----- dr Ashley

talked of going to Boston at the End of this month. I wish you

could see him, and would endeavour to prevent to his giving over

all Thoughts of coming to st---ge, tho' our Affairs are unsettled

& under difficult Circumstances at present. ------ And pray sir,

Let me have a Letter from you speedily. There If there is no

Opportunity sooner, there will be one by Capt. Ashley of sheffield,

or dr. Ashley, who are both coming to Boston in latter End

of May. And there will be often Opportunities to send to my

son Capt. dwight of Northampton, which may be known of by

Enquiring of Mr. dwight of Boston. --- My Wife is gone to

N. Jersey. --- Please to present my humble Regards to Mrs Prince.

My daughter Lucy joins with me in presenting Respects to Mrs

Sally if she be returned from N. Jersey -- And pray for me

sir, that I may have the divine Assistance in the difficult Affairs

which lie so heavy on my feeble shoulders. I am

Honoured sir,

your most respectful

son & servt

Jonathan Edwards.

The Rev. Mr. Prince.
