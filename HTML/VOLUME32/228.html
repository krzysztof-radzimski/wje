A203. Letter to John Erskine, April 15, 1755 203.JE-J.Erskine 4-15-55 Jonathan Edwards

A203. JE to John Erskine, 4/15/55. ANTS, f. 1755, #1, 4. Addressed "To the / Rev. Mr. John Erskine / Minister of the Gospel / at Culross in Scotland."

Stockbridge April 15. 1755.

Rev. & dear Sir,

The last year in the Spring I received, without a Letter, a Pacquet,

containing the following Books ; [------------------------------------],1

Casaubon on Enthusiasm, Warburton's Principles of natural & reveled

Religion, Merrick on Christ the true Vine, Campbell's Apostles no

Enthusiasts, discourse on the prevailing Evils of the present Time,

Remarks on Apostles no Enthusiasts, Moncrief's Review & Examina-

tion of Some Principles in Campbell's Apostles no Enthusiasts, Gilbert

of the Guilt and Pardon of Sin, Hervey on the Cross of Christ, An Ac-

count of the Orphan School &c. at Edinburgh, Memorial concerning

the Surgeon's Hospital, Gairdners Account of the Old Peoples Hospital, State

of the Society in Scotland for propagating Christian Knowledge, Abridge

ment of the Rules of Said Society, Regulations of the Town's Hospital

at Glasgow, Annals of the Persecutions of the Protestants in France.2

In the Beginning of last december, I received another Pacquet

without a Letter, The wrapper superscribed with your Hand. In this

were the following Pamphlets ; A sermon by a Lay Elder before the

Commission, A Letter to a Gentleman at Edinburgh, Resolution of the

gen. Assembly of May 22. 1736. Rutherford's Power of Faith and

Prayer, Enquiry into the Method of Settling Parishes, The Nature of the

Government and Constitution of the Chh of Scotland, Essay on Gospel

& Legal Preaching, Necessity of Zeal for the Truth, A vindication of

the Protestant doctrine of Justification against the Charge of Antino-

mianism[3]. The last week I received a Letter from you dated July 11. 54.4

which was found at Mr. Prince's by one that went to Boston from hence,

and had lain there Mr. Prince could not tell how long. In this

Letter you make mention of these last mention'd Pamphlets received

last december. I now return you my hearty thanks for this Letter,

& these generous Presents. I should have wrote to you long ago had

I not been prevented by the longest & most tedious sickness that ever

I had in my Life ; I being followed with Fits of the Ague, which came upon

me about the middle of last July, and were for a long Time very se-

vere and exceedingly wasted my Flesh & strength, so that I became

like a skeleton. I had several Intermissions of the Fits by the use

of the Peruvian bark ; but they never wholly left me 'till about the

middle of last January. In the mean time, I once several Times

attempted to write letters to m some of my friends about affairs

of Importance ; But found that I could bear but little of such writing.

Once in attempting to write a Letter to Mr. Burr, a Fit of the

Ague came upon me while I was writing; so that I was obliged

to lay by my Pen. when my Fits left me, they left me in

a poor weak state, all over bloated; so that I fear'd whether I

was not going into a dropsy. I am still something swell'd, & much

overrun

overrun with scarbutick Maladies. Nevertheless I have of late gradu- marginal

ally gain'd Strength.

I lately received a Letter from Mr. McLaurin, dated Aug. 13. 54.5 which

Mr. Prince sent me with a Letter from Himself, wherein He informed

that the Captain of the same ship that brought the Letter also brought

an Account of Mr. McLaurin's death, that He died w very suddenly with

an Apoplexy, a little before He left Glasgow. since I received that Letter,

I sent to Mr. Prince desiring to know more of the Certainty of the Ac-

count : Mr. Prince writes back, that He sees no Reason to doubt of it.

---- This is an affecting Piece of News: It is an Instance of death which

I have much Cause to lament. He has long shewn Himself to be a

very worthy, kind & obliging Friend & Correspondent of [-] mine. And

doubtless the Church of Scotland has much Cause to lament his death:

There is reason to think that He was one of them that stood in

the Gap, to make up the Hedge in these evil Times. He was a

wise, steady & most faithful Friend of Gospel Truth, and vital Piety,

in these days of great Corruption. I wish that I may take warning

by it, as well as by my own late sickness to prepare for my departure

Hence.

I have nothing very comfortable to write concerning my own success

in this Place. The Business of the Indian mission, since I have been

here, has been attended with strange Embarassments, such as I

never could have expected or so much as once dream'd of ; ari of

such a Nature, and arising from such a Quarter, that I take no de-

light in being very particular & explicit upon it. But besides what

I especially refer to, some Things have lately happened that have occasion'd

great disturbance among the Indians, and have tended to alienate

'em from the English. b As particularly the killing of one of them

in the Woods by a Couple of Travelers, white men, who met

Him , And the contended with Him. And tho' the men

were apprehended & imprisoned, yet on their Trial, they escaped

without sentence of death ; One of them receiving a lighter Punish-

ment, as guilty of Man-slaughter : By which these Indians, and

also the Indians of some other Tribes, were greatly displeased and

disaffected towards the English. Since this, the last fall, some

Indians from Canada (doubtless instigated by the French) broke

in upon us on the sabbath, between meetings, and fell upon

an English Family, and kill'd three of them, which occasioned

a great alarmed in the Town & in the Countrey. Multitudes came

from various Parts for our defence, that night & the next ; and

many of these Conducted very foolishly towards our Indians on

this occasion, suspecting them to be guilty of doing the Mis-

chief, charging them with it, & threatning to Kill them, &

the like. After this, a Reward being offered by some private

Gentleman, to some that came this way as soldiers, if they

would bring them the scalp of a Canada Indian ; Two men w

ere so extremely foolish & wicked that they, [--] in the night, dug marginal

one of our Indians, that had then lately died, out of his Grave, to

take off his scalp; that by pretending that to be the scalp of a

Canada Indian, whom they had met & killed in the Woods,

they might get the promised Reward. When this was discovered,

the men were punished. But this did not hinder but that such

an Act greatly increased their Jealousy & disaffection towards the

English. Added to these things, we have many white People in

the neighbourhood, Many dutch & some English, that will at all

Times, without any Restraint give them Rum ; which is a Constant

Temptation to their most predominant Lust.

Tho' I have but little success, & many discouragements here at Stock-

bridge, yet Mr. Hawley, Missionary among the six Nations (who went

from N. England to Onohohquaughe, a Place more than 200 miles

to the westward from hence) has of late had much Encouragement.

Religion seems to be a growing spreading Thing among the savages in

that Part of America, by his means; And there is an hopeful Prospect

of way being made for another Missionary in those Parts. which

may have happy Consequences, unless the six Nations should go over

to the French ; which there is the greatest Reason to expect, unless

the English should exert Themselves vigorously & sucessfully against

the French in America, this year. They seem to be waiting to see

whether this will be or no, in Order to determine whether to

entirely to desert the English, and Cleave to the French. and if the

six Nations should forsake the English, it may be expected that

the stockbridge Indians, & almost all nations of Indians in North

America will follow 'em. It seems to be the most Critical

season with the British dominions in America, that ever was since

the first settlement of these Colonies. and all probably will de-

pend of the warlike Transactions of the Present year. what will

be done I can't tell. We are all in a Commotion from one End of

the British America to the other : one to and various Expeditions

are Projected, and preparing for, One to Ohio, another to the

French forts in Novatia Scotia, another to Crown Point. But

these affairs are not free of Embarrassments : great difficulties arise

in our Present most important affairs, through the disunited

state of the several Governments. 'Tis hard for them to g agree

upon means & measures. And we have no Reason to think

the French are behind us in their Activity & Preparations.

A dark Cloud seems to hang over us : we need the Prayers of all

our Friends, & all Friends to the Protestant Interest. stockbridge

is a Place much exposed, and what will become of us in the

struggles that are coming on, God only knows.

I have hear'd that Messrs. Tennent & davies are arrived in marginal

America, having had good success in the errand they went upon.6

Mr. Bellamy is not like to go to N. York, principally by Rea-

son of the Opposition of some of the Congregation, and also of some

of the neighbouring Ministers. I have heard they have lately

unanimously agreed to apply themselves to Mr. McGregore of

New-Londonderry,7 alias Westfield in N-England, to be their

Minister ; who is a Gentleman that I think, if they can obtain

Him, will be likely to suit 'em, and well to fill the Place.

And I have heard that there has been some difference in his

own Congregation, that has lately made his situation there

uneasy : If so, he will be more likely to consent to the mo-

tion from N. York.

My wife joins with me in respectful & affectionate salu-

tations to you and Mrs Erskine. I am sir,

your affectionate & obliged Brother

Jonathan Edwards.

Sir,8

In a Journey I went to Northampton the last April, I carried the

foregoing Letter, with others for scotland so far, seeking an Opportunity to send

'em from thence to Boston: But there I met another Letter from Mr. Prince,

with a Joyful Contradiction of his former Account of Mr. McLaurin's death:9

which Occasion'd my bringing my Pacquet home again. Nevertheless, after I

had broke open & revised this Letter, I thought best to send it along, in-

closed in a Wrapper to Mr. McLaurin; who I hope is yet living,

& will convey this to you. I am dear sir,

your affectionate B. J. Edwards

st-ge. June 2. 1755.

Mr. Erskine
