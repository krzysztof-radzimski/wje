To speaker Hubbard

Stockbridge Aug. 29. 1752.

Sir

The special End of my Writing to your Honour at this Time is humbly

to propose to you that you would use your Influence that deac. Wood

bridge may be added to the general Courts Committee for the taking Care of

the Mohawk Affair in this Place. Probably you will not wonder that

I should propose it, when you have understood the present State of Things

here, by which the necessity of it appears. I informed you something of the

Calamities which attend the Mohawk affair in my Letter of last spring;

which, as I then Signified, were owing very much to the affairs falling almost

entirely into the Hands of one man, who was entring into a strict Alliance

with Col. Williams's Family, by his Engagements with his daughter

Mrs. Sergeant, & who was almost constantly here, while the rest of the

Committee kept at a distance. Our Confusions continue, and grow worse &

worse, and from the same Cause. Col. dwight takes up his Constant

Abode here; and as we are informed was lately married to Mrs. Ser-

geant, and so is now become Col. Williams's son in Law, and appears in

all Respects to be strongly engaged attached to and engaged with that Fa-

mily. He claims, and has used a sort of sovereign arbitrary Power here

with Regard to the Mohawks, and both the boarding schools. He utterly

denies all Authority of the Commissrs in these Affairs, and says all is in the

Hands of the general Courts Committee; and that He only of the Commit-

tee being resident here, it must be look'd upon that They leave affairs

with Him. Accordingly, he has done many Things of Consequence of his own

Head, and according to his own Pleasure, without the voice of either of

the other Gentlemen of the Comttee since the Comttee were first ap-

pointed. And almost every Thing has been done in an exceeding un-

happy Manner. He of his own Head Instead of building two school

Houses, agreable to the vote of the Assembly, has framed two large

Houses with various Apartments, each of which will doubtless cost as

much as three school-Houses : For what Purpose, I know not. The state

of things is apparently such, as makes it very doubtful whether we

shall have any Occasion for for so much as school-House for the Mo-

hawks any where in N. England ; And if any where, probably not here,

but at the Hoplands. I think the Case required the mature Conside-

ration of the Committee [ ] first [ ] most Part before the Founda-

tion of any school House, school House or other House, had been laid,

and it should been Enquired of the Indians, whether, if They settled

in the Hoplands, They would be willing their Children should be

at school here. For I think there is little probability of this. The

Col. has determined the Place, as well as the Form of these Houses by

Himself alone3 if I am rightly informed. He has of his own Head in

I think in a very precipitant Manner framed a large House for a

female boarding school, on his wifes Home lot, near her House; when

it remains yet undet undetermin'd whether she shall be the settled mis-

tress, and when indeed there is not the least Prospect of her ever having

a school. One would think They have made Trial enough to Convince them

that the Indians,4 both stockbridge Indians & Mohawks, have such a Preju-

dice against her, that They will not let her have their Children. The

House is framed big enough, not only for a school-House, but to entertain &

Lodge all the schollars. Tho' the society in London granted 10L sterl. to

prepare her House for their Entertainment & Lodging. That House has marginal

been lately finish'd in a pretty sumptuous manner ; but not with Lodgings

for Indian Girls. But He nevertheless expects the 10£ Sterl. to help defray

the Charge of it.  He in the winter, of his own Head sent for some of

the Chiefs of the Mohawks to come hither from their own Countrey. And

sent for Capt. Jos. Kellogg of Suffield in Order to a Treaty with Them: which

Treaty He managed by Himself alone. And the chief Business of it

was to know what we should do to secure the six Nations in the Brit-

ish Interest : than which no Business could have been devised more un-

happy for m our great & important Affairs with them in this Place.

For they are exceeding apt to suspect, that in all we do for them, we

act from any Kindness to them, but from a Regard only to our own Inte-

rest, & because we need their Help. Which they have often express'd

since they have been here. I have laboured much to Convince 'em

that in this Affair we evidently acted the Part of Friends to them

& sought their real & best Interest : and They seem'd in some measure

convinced ; but such a Treaty as this, in the boarding school here,

By one They supposed acted & spoke in the Name of the Countrey,

tended fully to establish Them in their former suspicion, and so to de-

feat our own designs. The Col. singly, & according to his own dis-

cretion distributed the 200L O. T. the general Court granted in the

winter, to Onoh-quaugas, who were newly arrived ; not all to them:

but some to them, and some to the Chiefs of the Mohawks, in such

a manner as did more to create uneasiness among Them, so far as

I could learn, than to gain their Affections. And so far as it was used

to the last-mentioned Purpose, it was to gain their Affections to Him-

self, and to establish his own Interest with Them, & not to excite their

Gratitude to the Government, if the Onohquaugas understood Him

right, & speak true. For they all (i. e all their Heads of Families)

with one voice declared to me, that He deliver'd the Present to Them

in his own name, & as his own Kindness to 'em ; That He said to 'em

that He had gathered up a little in one Place, and a little in ano-

ther, till He had got so much for them, which He now gave them.

The Col. of his own Head did also again send for Capt. Jos. Kellogg,

& kept Him here about 10 weeks in the spring & summer,

without any visible Business & Employ whatsoever : And as to his

secret Business we can but Guess at no that. And many other

Things have been done of great Consequence, and which have had

a most unhappy Influence without the voice of the Rest of the

Comttee, if Capt Ashley may be credited. 'Tis Apparent that

this Affair cannot prosper under his Hands, if it be should [-] conti

nue to under his Management as it has been. And indeed it is

already brought to the very brink of Ruin, and will soon be past

Recovery, and all the Cost the Countrey & Commissrs will be lost

if there ben't some Alteration. He is The Col. is too sanguine & self-

sufficient & Confident in his too hasty & precipitant in his measures, too

unsteady, of too passionate & fiery a Temper, which tends to keep every

Thing in a Ruffle and Tumult ; Too sovereign & arbitrary. He He is

not of a disposition at a and manner that at all suits Indians, who don't

seem at all pleased with Him, but have shewn much of the contrary.

They dont Love a man of his sovereign & forbidding Airs, nor have

They at all liked his Conduct. Tho' he has taken Pains to gain an ascendant marginal

in their Esteem : He is of a very suspicious, Jealous temper; which was remarka

bly the Temper of the Family at Northampton, which He came from on

the mothers side. And He is too uxorious ; & has had too many Things

in view for the promoting his private Interest in the management of

our affair, to Consist with his being so free of Temptation, as those ought

to be who have the management of stockbridge Affairs.  He expec

His wife expects the salary of 30L sterling as Mistress of the Girls school, without

immediately doing the Business of a school-mistress, as she has manifested to

me : And whether the mistress under her was to be one of the Family

I cannot tell. Two of their Children They expect should be both boarded

& educated at the Countreys Charge. And He has had it in view, not only

to be a Comttee-man (and so chief manager of all affairs) But also steward

of both boarding schools, as He has signified to me ; which would have

been a great Profit to Him, as a shopkeeper & otherwise, besides what He would

Expect from the Countrey, as the Reward of his services. He had a design

that his son should be master of the male-boarding school, and actually

Sent Him up from Brookfield to stockbridge for that End, with a Letter

to me, desiring me to introduce Him into the school, tho' it was only un-

der the Pretence of a necessary present supply. I leave t it to others to Judge

whether He would not have been for continuing Him if he had been

introduced. But it happen'd, that before his son came, I had a Letter

from the Commissrs, to send for Mr. Hawley. so that his son was forced

to go back again. He has also had a Scheme for the getting Mr. Hol-

lis'S moneys into his own Pocket. Capt. Kellogg told Mr. Hawley in

the spring, that it was proposed that He should resign Mr. Hollis's

Affair into the Cols Hands, when things were ripe for it, or to that

Purpose. I suppose the design was, That He was to have the Care

of Mr. Hollis's Boys, and would see that they should be instructed

by his son, and would Himself take Care of 'em as steward, and so draw

the Pay for the whole. Besides his wife had a design, that the

Farm her House stands upon should be bought by the Countrey, for a

School Lot, for the female school (for she signified this to me)

And so they would have had the Advantage of selling it for good

Pay, & a good Price, and yet still to have lived upon it, & upon the

Incomes of it, as Officers concerned in the Business of the School ; and

the Family & the school being blended, the Provision of the school

would have been their Provision, And the servants of the school

to take Care of the School Farm, would have been their servants.

Besides all these Things, the Brigadier has had it in his Mind (as

He intimated to me) to turn the Channel of the Kings Bounty of 500£

sterl. to the six Nations, from going by the way of N. York. He did

not say that He intended it should come thro' stockbridge, and through

his Hands, as chi the resident Agent for the Government, and be dealt

forth to the Indians, out of his shop. -- If all these things could

have been brought to bear, the Profit to Him & his Family would

have been great indeed.

In the Course Things are now in, every Thing is going into such a Channel,

and forming into such a shape, as tends to establish a dominion of the Family

of the Williams' over stockbridge Affairs, and bring all Things in a great

measure into their Hands ; As tis apparent that this is what they are ea- marginal

gerly grasping at : By their natural disposition they are sufficiently apt to assume

& engross all Power Profit & Honour to Themselves. Col. W--ms of

Weathersfield, since his Arrival has been very busy about our Affairs.

Has been abundantly resorted to by his Friends in stockbridge : He has

lately spent near a Fortnight here ; and assumed more to Himself than

I thought He had a Right to do, in demanding Accounts concerning our af-

fairs.

These things shew the Necessity of some other Committee man, that

shall be resident here, and have a hand in the management of Af-

fairs, besides Col. dwight, for the Family of the Williams's for his Coun-

sil. Tis apparent the other Gentlemen of the Committee don't In-

cline to have any Thing to do with Col. dwight, without greater strength

on their side. Col. Pynchon has not been here since last October : &

I have [ ] the Re I have Reason to think the Cause is, He don't like

such a Companion in the Committee. And Capt. Ashley has said to me

several Times, that He don't care to have any Thing to do, without Col.

Pynchon. I know Col. P. would rejoyce to be Join'd in Commission with

Mr. Woodbridge. The Latter is a Person well capable of the Business, and

in some Respects better than any man in N. England ; for He has more than al

most any man in these western Parts of New-England, been acquainted with In-

dians, and has demonstrated that He has great Skill in managing Them,

by the vast Ascendant He has gain'd and constantly maintains over them. And

He has certainly the Character of strict Integrity, among all that have been

longest acquainted with Him. That He has no disposition to lay schemes

for his private Interest to the Prejudice of the Indians, He has demonstrated

by the best Proof, viz. long Trial. He has had the fairest Opportunities to

have made an Estate by the Indians, of any man in these Parts, But He

has not taken his Opportunities ; but on the Contrary, has often given up his

own Interest for their sakes. Much has been said against But if Reports

should be followed, all would probably [be] found to come from one Family to

whom He has been obnoxious ; as standing in the way of their particular

designs. He is known to be a Gentleman of good discerning, and there is no

great danger of his being wheedled, and made a dupe or Tool by such as

craftily built up Themselves on the Ruin of publick designs. Col. Pynchon is

acquainted with Him, & knows his Character. I would propose to your Honour

to discourse with Him about the Matter. Perhaps adding deac. W--ge to

the Comittee is the only Expedient that is feasable for the delivering stock-

bridge Affairs from present dreadful Embarrassments. It may be none will care

to move that Col. d. should be turned out ; But if Mr. W--ge is added,

it may be as effectual a Relief as if the other were dismiss'd. Then Col. P. &

Capt. A. will be encouraged to come to Stockbridge, & be active in the Busi-

ness of the Comttee : And in all Probability the Brigr will soon dismiss Himself

& have no more concern in the Business. But if we have no Redress at all, 'tis

past all doubt, the Mohawk Affair will soon be past Recovery.

I leave it to your Honour to make use of this Letter, as your Wisdom shall di-

rect. I asking your candid Interpretation of the Freedom I have used in

this Case of Extremity. ---- My wife joins with me in most respectful &

affectionate Salutations to you Madam & your Family. - I am

Honoured sir,

your obliged & most humble servt

Jonathan Edwards.

Octob. 14.
