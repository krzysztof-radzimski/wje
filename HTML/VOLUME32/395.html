C10. Timothy Edwards to East Windsor Church Committee, [October 1715] C010.TE-ChhComm 10-15 Timothy Edwards

C010. Timothy Edwards to "Gentlemen" [Church Committee], 10/15. BRBL, f. 1551.

Gent m. Brethren & Christian Friends.

I have Something to Offer to y r Consideration respecting my State and Condition Among you with reference to a Livelihood, &

1. with respect to time past; in which that So you may rightly take up & understand my Case, you may Consider the dificult Circumstances, and Disadvantages that it hath been all along attended with: particularly to mention Some of them, (and I shall do a Little more then Just mention them) your Giving me So Small a Summ for my Settlement together with ye manner of the payment of a Considerable part of it: My being paid for the greatest part of the time I have dwelt here, So much in the Meanest Grain in the Country, and So little in the Choicest and best; my being paid So much in Grain or Country pay, especially Indian corn, when it has been Cheap & Low in the Market, and when I have Lost by it, & at Such times Receiving So Little mony; & again my receiving So Little Corn when It hath born a high price in the Market & when I might have Gain’d by it, & my being So Commonly then paid in two thirds in mony for what has been due to me from my Neighbours; your Giving me So Little Land eithe when I first Setled amongst you or ever Since, Are all of them things whereby I have been disadvantaged in My Outward estate, and Some of them In no Small degree have been So, and especially this Last: for our Giving me almost no Land, Nor enabling me to buy any for my Self hath been attended Several Inconveniences, and Dificulties, very Considerably to the Disadvantage and Detriment of my family, as may easily be Conceiv’d, which I shall not now Stand particularly to Mention.

obj: We have no Land to Give in this place; all the Lots are taken up and possest by others, and were So when you first Came Amongst us:

A. 1 Several of my Neighbours have Bought Land, and that in Our meadow for themselves Since I came hither, And Surely It would have been as easy for a whole people to have bought Land for their Minister, had they Seen Cause to have Laid out themselves that way, as for one of them to buy it for himself.

2ly. I shall Next offer somethings to your Consideration, concerning my present outward State.

1. I have been Serving the People with whom I now dwell in a Constant manner, in The best work that any One is capable of doing for people for Near One and twenty years; Neither have my wife and Children eat the bread of Idleness, or been extravagant that I know of in Spending what we have receiv’d of our neighbours, niether have We (Such has been the goodness of God to us) Met with any great Losses in all this time, and yet in all this Long space of Time I have Got but a very Small estate. Several of my younger brethren in the Ministry, & Some much younger, & I suppose Some others that begun with nothing, are Got far above me in that respect, & that in a Much Shorter time.

2. With all that I receive from the People, and all that we do by our Own Labour (which is a very Great help to the Support of the family) We can but Just get through the year, Nor that Neither in a Sutable manner: what ever We have, or can Get we Spend, or at least need to Spend and Much More: I dont know that I have been able to get Sixpence aforehand, to Lay out to buy Land, or Lay up for my Children, for More than this Six teen years. It is much My family as it is with a Company of men in a Boat or vessel, that have So Scant a wind without, that with all their Rowing and Labour, within, Can but Just Get heavily, and Slowly along Shore. 3. I am Not able to Get through what is before me in bringing up my Son to Learning, which I desire to devote to the Service of Christ and his people in the work of the ministry; which will be a very Chargeable thing, and except my State be much bettered, it islike to prove an Insupportable burden to me; and I think it is pity that Such a Concern Should miscarry and Sink, or my family Sink under the burden of it, for want of Strength and ability to bear, or undergo it. May it not well Seem a hard Case when A man hath for many years Serv’d a Christian People in the work of the Ministry, marginal and hath but one Son, that for want of maintenance he Should [—] be able to Give him Neither Mony, Land, Nor Learning for his Portion.

4. As the Case Now Stands with me I am not able honourably to pay my debts. It is a very unsutable unbecoming thing, for Ministers of the Gospel to trespass upon the Patience of their Creditors by keeping them Out of their Mony, and to Ly in debt from year to year So as to wrong themthat trust them: but except my present outward State be Much bettered & amended, I cant See but taht I must to my Grief and Shame be forced So to do, or Go without the things that from year to year ar every needfull for the Supply of my family wants.

5. I neither am, nor as the Case is Now with me, can be in any way Sutably to provide for My Own, which the Law of God and Nature not only allows, but also Obliges all Christians to as one of their Necessary Cares, and My Neighbours Round about me, are Carefully endeavouring after. And if it Should please God under our present Circumstances to Remove me by death, to what a Low and Sorrowfull [-] Condition in an Ordinary way, would my family be Soon Reduc’d for want of the Comforts of this life, and which of my Neighbours would Care to be at Charge to bring up their Children to Learning, and put them Out of all other wayes to Live in the world to Serve a People all their dayes upon the terms of exposing themselves to dificulty while they Live, and their Nearest and Dearest Relation to poverty when they die.

And because an Objection May possibly Arise in the minds of Some against Doing any thing more for my Comfortable Subsistence in this place, from what hath been added by this Society both formerly and Lately to my maintenance, I shall in the Next place Observe Something to you Concerning those additions, in what now follows, viz

formerly you gave me Eighty pounds in pay for my yearly Maintenance, & upon My Complaint of the Insufficiency thereof, you added that which hath been Called twenty pounds more; but when you made this Addition Brethren you may please to remember that you So rais’d the price of Gace, that what you then added to My Maintenance was very Inconsiderable, So that if you had that year paid me but Twenty bushells of Rie, thirteen Bushells of wheat, and the rest In Indian Corn, it would have been in pay but, 03 - 07 - 0, which makes in Moy Just . 2 - 4 - 8 . this is the true Accout of it, if I have not Misreckon’d, which would not have been to the Inhabitants of this place take them One with another, quite 6 d in Mony a Man. I acknowledge that Seeing Some of this was paid me in two thirds in Mony, It was more to me then the Summ that I have Now Mentioned, but yet it was far, yea very far, from Making my Maintenance twenty pounds better then it was before, according to the Name and Sound of it; and much further Still from being a Competent Supply and help to one tha Laboured under the Dificulties of an Insufficient Maintenance, as I thn did, and have done ever Since.

And as for that eight pounds that you added to my Salary this Last year and the year before, I desire it may be Consider’d that any Summ is realy added to a Ministers Maintenance, it may be properly and truly Said that his Maintenance is made So much more and more better then it was before as that Summ doth Amount unto, but it is far from being So with me in this Case; for by your votes Concerning my rate for the Abovesaid years (if I rightly understand them) the Inhabitants of this place were left at Liberty to me in that we use to call Country pay at the Market price, or two thirds in Mony: now the price of provisions these two Last years hath been greatly advanced in the Market, ciz Indian Corn the year before this from three to four and six pence, and this year from three to three and Nine pence, and both these years Rie from three and Sixpence to six and Wheat from Six to Nine and pork to Sixpence a pound in pay. And what I have receiv’d of my Neighbours in provisions was Generally paid me at these prizes, Only they Let me have pork (Some of them at Least) a little Cheaper; and Some men both Last year and his abated me something in other things, but Generally According to my remembrance I have I have given for Such pay, to the height of what has been Call’d the Market price Among us; marginal Which by the enquiry I have made by examining my Book, (which I have Carefully done & that more than Once or twice) hath taken all tha teight pounds Lately added to my maintenance, and if I mistake not considerably more back again in mens particular payments: for all that I have receiv’d of my beigh bours I have Sent in housekeeping. So that this Supposed additin, hath in effect prov’d to be no additoin at all, and indeed by the account that I have taken, wherein If I be not mistaken, it may be truly be Said to be Nothing but an empty name and Sound, and rather a Substraction of Something from then an addition of anything to My Maintenance, and So not only worth Nothing but worse then Nothing to me. And this as far as I can find upon a carefull examination is the true account

3. In the of the Matter. Next place I shall further take Notice of Somethings Concerning your Making my Rate by your publique votes, not only in former years, but also these two Last years no better then two thirds in Mony, togethe with your asking me So very high for what I have receiv’d of you, in that which formerly went by ye name of Country pay.

1. Thereby I conceive my maintenance, [-] instead of being rais’d, and made better, is made Considerably Less then it was fourteen, or fifteen years Ago, when you Gave me Eighty pounds a year in pay: for tha eight pounds added these two Last years being nothing as I have Shewed already, the most that I have at any time receiv’d of my Neighbours besides what they allow me for wood, & what particular Neighbours have done in a way of Kindness in getting wood for me, is a hundred pounds in pay, which Amounts to, 66 - 13 - 4. in Mony, and no More. Now in the Judgment of all that I have Mett with, Mony now, is no better then pay was then: and I have discours’d with Some that Say it Cant be So good. Sure I am It would not these two Last years go near So far in housekeeping as Good pay would in former years. And by this Account which as far as I know all Agree in, what you have Lately allowed me is 13 - 6 . 8 Less then what you allowed me for my Maintenance, when my Salary was but fourscore pounds a year, and that though My Necessities and your Abilities Are both Since that Time Much Increased . which Brethren I desire that you would well Consider of.

2. In paying me in two thirds in Mony, Or that which is no better, you dont allow me to Share with you in the blessings, and advantages that hrough th egood ness of God you do enjoy. When God is pleas’d to Give you a Competently Good harvest at a time when by reason of a Scarcity in Other parts of the Country, provisions bear a very high price in the Market, he is then in a distinguishing man ner kind and Good to you, in tha tyou enjoy his blessings when others want them, and herein Divine providence makes that hwich is a Calamity to Some of your Christian brethren to be a benefit to you. and at Such a time God in his goodness bestows a double blessing upon you, in giving you a Competent portion of his bounty in the fruits of the Earth, and increase of the field, and in making these blessings of his Goodness, to be of far greater worth & value to you, then they have usually been at other times: which must be Own’d to be a very Considerable ad vantage. but as the Matter is Order’d I dont Share with you in this advantage, for you han’t given me Any Land, whereby I may raise Corn of My Own, and So As providence offers Opportunity partake with you in Such a benefit that way; and then again you ask me So high for Grain and Such like pay that you Cut me off from it that way also. And herein I conceive brethren that you have not dealt by me as God in his goodness hath dealty by you. God hath made provisions both this year and Last in Some Species of them at Least to be full as good, if not better then Mony to you; but you have Seen Cause to make them no better then two Thirds as good as mony to me.

3. As you dont allowme to Share with you in that advantage that providence hath afforded in the Great price that provisions have of Late born in the Market, So you So deal with me, that you rgreat advantage in this thing, is my great disadvantage. The great Rise and Advance of Grain in the price it bears in the Market, is Certainly a great benefit and advantage to marginal them that have it to Sell, or to pay their debts with: Sure I am it hath been so to My Neighbours both this Year and the Last, in paying what they have been Indebted to me. Ahout three or four years past, three bushells of Rie, would have paid to me but a Rate of ten and Sixpence, and in former years when you gave Me but fourscore pounds a year, it would have paid but One of Nine Shillings, & this year and the Last it hath paid a Rate of Eighteen. & About four years Ago three bushells of wheat would have paid a Rate of eighteen Shillings in pay, and in former years but One of Twelve, but these two Last years it hath paid One of Seven and twenty. for Many Years Since I came to this place, tha tquantity of wheat and Rie that would pay but a Rate of twenty Shllings, would Of Late have paid one of more then forty. So that its very Apparent that the dearness of provisions these two Last years hath been very much to your advantage in your dealings with me.

Again this May be Consider’d with respect to me, and it will be found Greatly to my disadvantage: for by your Asking me the Market price ofr provisions, housekeeping is Made far more Chargeable to me then it was a few Years ago, And it Now cost me very Near if not full as Dear again as it did for many years Since I came first to this place: When my family was very Small and my charge Comparatively Little and burden light in pro viding for it, I could Live at Near, if not full as Cheap a Rate Again, as of Late I have been able to do, though Now the Charge and Burden of Supporting and Maintaining it be So greatly Increased. I Now Need help and ad vantages for Living in this world more then ever I did Since I knew what it was to provide for a family, And Yet now by reason of ye Dearness of provisions and almost every thing (I speak with respect to this Last year & ye year before( I Labour under the Greatest difficulties and Disadvantages upon that accont in Some Respects especially that ever I did Since I kept house.

4. By Such a Method as this, the Ministers of the Gospel are more hardly dealt with then the Generality of other Men, and that by their own People, who Certainly Should use them very Kindly for their work and for their Masters Sake. Other Men have a way to help themselves Now provisions & goods are So Dear, hence all Sorts of men rise in the work of their Calling, and you your Selves ask Dearer for your wood, flax, work of your Trades & Teams, and for your Labour in husbandry then you did in former years, but I have No Such advantage in my hands, for My Maintenance when you gave me fourscore pounds a year when My family was Considerably Less was not Sufficient, And Now though a much Greater Charge Lies upon me, and you are more able to hlelp me then you were then, yet as [—] I have already Observ’d instead of raising my maintenance, and making it better in poroportion to the Dearness of the times, According to the Common Account and estimation (excepting only the kindness that is Shewed me in woodd) It is Now more then thirteen pounds in Mony Less then it was fourteen years ago. So that while all men in General, and my own Neighbours round about me, rise in the price of their Labour, and work of their Callings, I alone am forc’t to fall: and the truth is it Seems to me that [——] [————] herein the work of the Ministry is much undervalued, and that I am made very much an underling, and in this thing depressed, and put down almost below all men.

These things Brethren I must Confess, when I think of them Are Something Grievous to me, and that in them, I cant but Judge my Self to be hardly dealth with.

and in the Last place I shall offer Some things to your Serious thoughts In Several particulars viz

1. It is Certainly the duty of a Christian People to do their true Endeavour to Afford a Minister that Labours Among them in marginal the Work of ye Gospel an honourable Maintenance. God hath not Left his people at Liberty, but Laid them under a bond and tie of duty, as Much in this thing, as, in any other. as diligently and faithfully to Serve a People is the duty of a Minster, So honourable to Support a Minister is the duty of the People. the office and work of a Minister are honourable, therefore their Maintenance Should be So also. this is evident from the very light of Nature, ye dictates & Sentiments of the Common Reason of mankind and from the word of God both in the Old Testament and the New, & in p rticular that in 1 Cor: 9. 14. Even So hath the Lord Ordained that they that preach the Gospel, Should Live of the Gospel. that is not only Live, but Live Sutably, according to the Dignity of their office, and excellency of their work. Not only the honour of Reverence, but also the honour of Maintenance is due to the Ministers of Christ. 1. Tim: 5. 17. 18. Now a poor mean maintenance is No honour, but rather a dishonour to a Minister: therefore &c—

2. As it is the duty of a People, So it is greatly for their Interest and advantae to afford the ministers of Christ an honourale Maintenance, and that every way. By this as well as other things a people please and honour God, & therefore the practice of this as well as other duties, tends to bring down the blessing of God upon them & their Childre, and that both in Body and Soul. this is One way to have the Means of Grace bles’t in a place, which is above all things to be Desir’d. And this is a way and means also of the outward prosperity of A people. It is Observable that the People of Israel did Never So abound in wealth and Riches, prosper and flourish in Outward Respects as they did when they Laid out most on th ehouse and worship of God, and Gave the greatest encouragement to his Ministers; the Reigns of David Solomon, Jehosaphat, [—] Hezekiah and Josiah are a Clear & Convincing Instance of this Truth. God also doth very expressly give great encouragement in his word to the practice of this duty. Mal. .3. 10. 11. 12. Pro. 23. Pro. 3. 9. 10.

3. The Neglect of this Duty, is Greatly to ye Disadvantage of a People. as it is greatly to the disadvantage of a Minister, not only in his outward estate but also in his Ministry, & that by filling his mind with disquieting and Distracting worldly Cares, taking him much off from his Studies, exposing his person and Ministry to Contempt, Grieving and Discouraging his heart, and weakning his hands in his work: So for a People to keep a Minister Low in the world, by not taking that Care they ought to take, for his Comfortable Subsistence tends greatly to their Own Loss and disadvantage, for it can neither be to ye honour of the Lord Jesus Christ or pleasing to God, it being direct ly Contrary to his Word, or to ye Credit and honour of a People, or the way of their prosperity upon any account for them So to do. this is not the way to have the means of Grace Accompanied with the blessing of Christ to the Saving Good of the Souls of those that do enjoy them but rather the Contrary. & this is evident from the word of God in Mal: 3. 8. 9.

4. It can be no Just ground of offence for Ministers to desire and expect from their People, that they endeavour to the best of their Ability honourable to maintain them . for Ministers that have Numerous families, So to exercise Natural affections as not to be willing to See them that God and Nature have made above all others in the world, near and Dear to them, and to desire to be in Some Good way Sutably to provide for them, Can be the breach of no Divine Rule. that which is the desire, Care and duty of all Christians, Certainly cannot be amiss in a Minis ter. Again for a Minsiters to desire that which by the Rule of equity and Common Justice, and by the Appointment of the Lord Jesus Christ is their due, and but the Just reward of their Labours, the doing of which would be of great advantage to them in Serving of a People, and promoting their Spiritual and Eternal Good, also for the honour of the Lord Jesus Christ, and for the honour of a people, and also for their benefit and advantage both upon Spiritual & Temporal accounts, Cant Just ly expose them to Reproach or Censure Amongst a Christian People.

5. A Christian People Should willingly and Chearfully Lay out marginal themselves Comfortably and honourably to Support and maintain their Ministers. forasmuch as a People expect that Ministers Should willingly & with readiness of mind Lay out themselves in the Service of ther Souls, and forasmuch as a Ministers being So Maintained that he may Live after a decent Comely man & ner in the Workd, is as hath been already observ’d for the honour of God the Lord Jesus Christ, and for the honour and advantage of a People, they Should do this Duty not of Constraint but willingly, not with a backward, but with a ready Chearfull Spirit. And Indeed Considering what a Mercy of God it is to a people to Send the Gospel amongst them, & afford them the enjoyment of his word and Ordinances, & how Miserable notwithstanding all their outward enjoyments they would be without it, & what they owe to God for this , and all their other Mercies, and Considering Again what the Lord Jesus Christ hath done & Suffer’d to Make them ost hap py and blessed to all Eternity, and the vast Obligations they Lie under to him, and that a gospel Ministry is above all things in the world the Means of Saving them and theirs from endless Misery, and bringing them to the enjoyment of all this happiness, I say Considering these things it Seems to me to be an unsutable unbecoming thing for a People professing Christianity, and Living under the Gos pel to Showe a Backward & unwilling Spirit, to So plain & Reasonable a duty as this is.

6. As it is the duty of a Minister to be affectionately Concern’d for the Spiritual and Eternal wellfare of his People and their Children; So it is the duty of people to be affectionately Concern’d for the temporal pros perity, of their Minister and his family . that the Souls of a people may prosper and flourish, and that they and their Children mayin the enjoyment of Spiritual and Eternal blesings, be happy and blessed for ever, Should be very much the Desire, prayer, Care and Study of the Minister, and also that a Minister, and his Children after him, may have wherewithall comfortably to Live in In the world in the enjoiment of the blessings of this Life Should be the hearty desire and affectionate Care of a People. The Reason is Ministers and their People Should be very Dear one to another, and according to the Several Relations wherein they Stand, Should affectionately Seek one Anothers wellfare, and also the priviledges of the Gospel, the Means of Salvation and Eternal Life are of far greater value and Much More highly to be priz’d then earthly things.Such a Spirit in Christians towards their Ministers, the holy Apostle greatly desir’d and rejoic’d to See in the Christians of his time towards himself. Phil. 4. 10. 18. but I rejoyced in the Lord greatly, that now at the Last your Care o fme hath flourished again, wherein ye were also carefull &c —— but I have all, & abound, I am full, having received of Epaphroditus the things that were Sent from you, An odour of a Sweet Smell, a Sacrifice acceptable well pleasing to God.

7. Sometimes the Case is So, that the ministry of the Gospel, or the enjoyment of the word and Ordinances of God, is in a great Measure the Means of the building up and Making of a place: Were it not for this Some places that are come to be Considerable among their Neighbours, Might have been to this day in Comparison but Small Obscure villages, or at least not near what they are Now. Now Brethren might it not well Seem hard and Grievous to A Minister, if a People Shouls Suffer that very thing wherein he hath Serv’d them, and for Many years Sought their Good, & in which he hath been made by God very Much an Instrumental Cause of their tem poral Prosperity, as well as of their enjouing of Spiritual Blessings and advantages, To be An Occation of Impoverishing and in a great measure undoing him & his for this World: and Should not a Christian People be very Carefull, that Such a thing as this may Never happen, through any Neglect of theirs.

8. If that which is Due to the Kings officers by the King’s Laws, be either wholly or in part denied them wrong is done them. So when that is wholly or in part denied to, or withheld, form the Minsiters of Christ, (that are the officers of the King of Kings) that by his Laws is their due, as being appointed by him to be the Reward of their Service from the People whom they Serve, which is an honourable Maintenance they are manifestly wrong’d. this is So plain, that One would think it must needs be evident to Any Christian but of Ordinary understanding even at the first Sight. Now Such a maintenance I have not had for Many years; that which I have receiv’d from the Inhabitants of this place hath much fallen Short of it, as may appear from these two things as has been already observ’d viz marginal

1. It comes greatly Short of enabling me in any Sutable manner to provide for my family for the future.

2. It falls much Short of enabling me Sutably to Supply mine and my families wants, and Live as becomes a Minister of the Gospel for the present.

And if I am wrong’d in this thing, I desire Brethren that you would Consider that this wrong hath Laid upon me for a Long time, and hath been very considerable every year; for Many years together: and then it is a very extensive wrong, in respect of the persons that are Subjects of it, it reaches not only my Self, but all mine, Many at Once Suffer by it, and that more wayes then one. and if it Should be Continued without any Redress, My Children May greatly Suffer by it, not only while I Live, but Also Long after I am Dead.

Hon rd: and Beloved.

I must Confess I have thought it high time, to take Some thorow and effectula Care, for the Comfortable Subsistence of my Numerous family, and therefore have now endeavour’d to give you a true account of my State in this place with respect to a Livelyhood. the Case being very weighty, I have thought it Calls for a free & full opening of my mind to you. Its Common with men to Spends dayes and weeks time, & considerable Summs of mony besides in a Course of Law, & with all needfull freedom and and plainness to Speak to their Cause & that [—————] in Cases as I conceive of far Less weight then this without blame. I take it to be the undoubted & indisputable right of the Inhabitants of this Government when they Conceive themselves to be Sufferers for want of that which the Law of the Commonwealth gives them a Just claim unto with freedome to Spread their Case before the General assembly, & the com mon priviledge of his Majesties Subjects in a Sutable manner to Lay their grievances before their Sovereign. It Seems therefore but reasonable that a Christian People and the Minsters of the Gospell Should be allowed the Like Liberty as there may be Occation with respect to one another: So that I cant but hope that I have given no Just Cause of offence in what I have Now Read to you, to any person whatsoever.

In what I have here written I have not that I know of exprest my Self rashly or Inconsiderately, or With a disturbed edged Spirit, or personal reflections on any of my Neighbours. I have Laboured to Lay open the Case that I am Now Concern’d in in, with as much mildness and Gentleness, as the matter will well bear, and as Setting things in a true light before you: Wherein I have declared to you my real Sober Serious thoughts. when I ssay I take my Self to be wrong’d with respect to my Maintenance, I would not be taken in the harshest & worst Sense, but in the most Charitable and best Sense: my Meaning therein is not that I think any of my Neighbours have Gone about to defraud me of my due and So willfully and designedly to do me wrong; but it is plainly this, as I ahve already intimated, that for want of what the Lord Jesus Christ hath in his word appointed for me and made my right, & that is an honourable Maintenance, I conceive I have for many years Much Suffer’d, & So Lain under great wrong. And this I take to be in a great measure from want of light and understanding in my neighbours, & not that they have Acted herein against their light. I am far from thinking So hardly of them. I freely acknowledge that Some of my Neighbours have been these two Dear years more Moderate in their prices in what they have paid me in, then Ma ny others have been, and do in Charity think concerning them that asked me dearer, tha they did what they thought was right therein.

I also do readily and thankfully Acknowledge the kindness of a Considerable Number of my Neighbours in Getting wood for me for Several years of Late and that they have therein done tha twhich hath been a Considerable benefit to my family, and which I have taken very Kindly at their hands. I do also own that I have receiv’d kindness from Several, & especially from Some of my Neighbours in Other respects, which I have taken a very particular notice of and I shall not forget, but be ready at all times upon proper and Sutable occation to Make a gratefull mention of it to their honour. And my desire and prayer for them is, and I shall be that the God of all Grace would Return the kindness they have Shewed to me, More then Sevenfold into their own Bosomes, in the bestowment of a Rich blessing both upon them and theirs. but yet I must Still Say, that In My Maintenance I cant but take my Self upon the marginal most Impartial Consideration of it that I am Capable of, to have been dealt hardly with, and that my neighbours in that thing which is a great thing, have too Little Consider’d, & to Much Neglected me, whereby I have Considerably Suffered, and been attended with dificultires in my outward Condition in this place, for the Greatest part of One and Twenty years, which I Desire Brethren, that you would with a Christian Spirit Seriously think of.

I am y r affectionate friend & Serv t In the work of the Gospel

Tim o: Edwards

Oct r: — 1715

Postscript

There are a few things Concerning my outward State amongst you, that I desire brethren you would well weigh, & Seriously Consider of, some of which I have Mentioned already, but that So you may the better take them up, and Remember them, I shall Lay them more briefly before you, in this Short postscript.

1. The Most that I have at any time Receiv d: of my neighbours for Amounts to but 66: 13: & 4 d as Mony (that 8 ll: that they have Late ly added Coming to Nothing as I have already observed) will not afford my family take us One with Another above four pounds fif teen Shillings and threepence apiece, And Indeed if I have Cast it right quite So Much for food & Raiment both for a year, Reckoning nothing out of this for finishing, and furnishing my house, buying necessary household Stuff, Repairing my fences and huildings, buy ing Books, Charge of Journeys for my Self, and Others of my family, en tertainments of Strangers, and friends, Acts of Charity & helpfullness to the poor (in which a Minister Should have wherewithal to enable him to be a Shining example to his people as well as in other vertues) Expences upon the Account of Sickness, & all other charges Whether More Stated or Occational & accidental, which in Such a family as Mine Must needs be very Considerable: So that what you allow me for my whole maintenance would not near pay for the Board of Such a Number as my family Consists of I mean of persons of their Age & Bigness taken one with another. And It is well known how Little I have had, besides the Labour of our Own hands in the family More than this to depend upon for a Livelyhood in this place.

2. Though the Calling that I Serve the Inhabitants of this place in, be in its Own Nature, very honourable, Yet it has notmade my out ward Condition, as to my worldly estate honourable, but rather mean amongst you. and though it be a Calling that tends greatly to the benefit & advantage, and profit of a people & that every way, yet with respect to the blessings, and Comforts of this Life it has been No very profitable Calling to me, for though I have been Serving this people in it, for very Near One and Twenty years, yet in all this Long Space of time I have Got but a Small estate by it.

3. Though many other Men, and Several of my Own Neighbours, have marginal Risen, and are still Rising in the world, & that by the work of their Callings, & that though their Callings are in Comparison o fmine but of a private nature, and for their own, & their families benefit, and advantage; Yet though My Calling be of a publique Nature, and in it god and your Selves have Call’d me to take the Care of Several hundreds of persons, & to make it my work and business to Serve & every way to Seek the Good of this whole People, Yet for want of an Answerable & Agreeable Reward of my Service, and Labour among you, it hath Long kept, & Still doth keep me Low.

4. I dont only not Share with my Neighbours (those of them I mean that have had Corn to Spare) in that which providence hath these two Last dear years turn’d greatly to their advantage, namely the great price provisions have born inthe Market, but this very thing is So orderd by them, that it is turnd greatly to my disadvantage, it making house keeping full, or at Least very Near as Dear again to me as it was in former years, as hath been already Observ’d, So that my Neighbours benefit proves my detriment. My Brethrens Gain in ye Dearness of what we use to Call Country pay (they asking me the market price, as they Generally have done) hath proved my Great Loss. I Intreat you Brethren Consider this

5. God in his providence by Calling me to the work of the Ministry In this place, hath made me instead of Another and better an Instrumental Cause of the Increase and Growth of this place. for I suppose that there is No One thing that canbe Mention’d that hath been a great Means of your outward prosperity then your having for So many years had the Ministry of the Gospel & means of Salvation amongst you. Now Brethren I hope you will not (if you can help it) Suffer this very thing wherein I have So Long Serv’d you, and which hath been Such a benefit & blessing to you, to prove an Occatin of impoverishing, and so In a great measure of undoing me and mine.

6. Supposing that Mony Now, is No Better then Good pay was in for mer years, (which is the Opinion as far as I can understand of the Generality of men) and which I think none Can deny) then what I have of you Now for my Maintenance and Support in the work of the ministry Is More then thirteen pounds in Mony Less then what you gave me about fifteen or Sixteen years ago, when my Salary was but 80 ll in pay & when that thought My Necessities & your abilities are both Much Increas’d [—] Since that time.

7. If god Should Call me by Death to Leave my family under its present Circumstances except Ghe Should find Some other way besides what my estate Could afford for its Releif, Let my Christian Neighbours Judge whether or no it would not be in as Low a Condition, and as Much put put to it to Live as most families in this place yea as any excepting only the very poorest? and Brethren pray Consider, would this be to ye honour of Christ, or to the honour of this place, or Can any that Love us and heartily wish and Seek our wellfare and prosperity, be willing that it Should be so with my wife and Chidren after my Death.

Brethren, You have Invitted & Call’d me to Settle amongst you in this place in the work of the Ministry, and I have Long Since accepted of your Call; and in my Settlement here in that work I have under God Cast my Self upon your Love and faithfullness, for a comfortable Support. you have I must acknowledge, and I desire to do it humbly, that I have greatly marginal fallen Short of Serving Christ & yourselves therein as I ought to have done, but yet this I hope through Grace I can truly Say, that accord ing to my poor Measure I have unfeignedly & heartily desir’d & Sought the Good & wellfare of you & yours. Seeing therefore that you have as it were embarq’d & Ship’t me in this Bottom, I desire that after So Many years Spent therein in your Service that you would not See me Sink it. Yea Let me further Say that if it please God to Give you a right understanding & Sense of my present Case that I cant but hope that you will not do it, but that you will take effectuall Care to prevent it.

Brethren

The Greatest part of my Opportunity for providing for my family, So that they may Live Comfortably in the world, both while I am with them, and also after I am dead and Gone from them, is not only Spent, but also in a great measure Lost. The Sun of my Life is not only past its meridian but is far declined, and onward in its going down, that it is Some years Since I have entred uopn the afternoon of it. And though I very freely acknow ledge that in Some respects I have great yea abundant Cause to admire the Goodness of God to me Since I came to this place, Yet [—————] [—————] I may truly Say that for want of Such an outward estate, & Support, as is Sutable & proper, yea realy needfull for one of my calling and Occations, the greatest part of the time by far that I have dwelt here, hath been Spent and passed away under considerable difficulties. I desire therefore if it might Stand with the Goo dwill and pleasure of the most wise orderer, and disposer of all things, that I might Spend the Latter part of my Short & uncertainLife more comfortably, I mean with a Great freedome from those disquieting Cares and distractions, that the want of a Comfortbale outward Subsistence, is ordinarily attended with, and that So I may not only Live more becoming my place in the world & be better able to provide for my family, but also be under advantage more fully to give my Self to my ministerial work in the Reading and Study of the word of God, & care of the Souls that the Lord Jesus hath commit ted to my Charge.

After a Little while Brethren, you and I Shall have no further Oppor tunity of doing any thing for the help or Comfort one of Another, and how Short this time is, is known only to him with whom is the number of our moneths, and who hath Appointed our Bounds that we Cannot pass. I Desire therefore that while we do Continue together, we may in all respects So walk with, & behave ourselves one towards Another, and in particular in a faithfull & Chearfull discharge of our Selves, in all those Relative duty’s, that we Owe one to another, that we may the more groundedly, and Comfortably hope for and expect ye gracious presence and blessing of the Lord Jesus Christ, in the enjoyment of his word and ordinances which is above all things to be desir’d, and wherein our happiness Chiefly Consists.

I do freely own it to be my duty diligently to Serve you in the work that Christ hath Call’d me to amongst you, and therein earnestly to desire and Seek your good and wellfare every way, and especially upon Spiritual accounts; & to take heed to my Self, and to the Ministry that I have received of the Lord that I fullfill it, and that in all the parts of it; and it is my desire that by the help of Divine Grace I may ever be found faithfull in the performance of this great duty. And that which I desire of, and expect from your Selves is this, viz, that you Settle upon me Such a yearly Maintenance as may with the blessing marginal of God be Sufficient at Least with our own diligence in the family to enable me to Live as becomes one of my place and Calling for the pre sent, and that you do that for me wherby I may be in a Capacity com fortably to provide for those that committed above all others to my Care for time to Come; whom I am Oblig’d to care for as I am a Christian, & whom to neglect & expose to poverty and want, would render me therein worse then an Infidel. 1 Tim: 5. 8 — I think therefore Brethren that you cant reasonably desire that I should expect Less from your Selves then this, which is No more then What your Selves, and the best Christians in the world Desire and endeavour to obtain, and which very agreeable both to Scripture and Rea son. I dont Desire that you Should make me rich and wealthy, or do that for me that would afford me a Superfluity; Do but that which is Sutable and proper for a Christian People to do for a Minister of Christ that desires to Live and their diligent and faithfull Servant, and as far as I Know my heart I am Contented, I desire no More.

And if it Shall please the most high to Incline your hearts So to Consider my Case as to this for me, it will be an encouraging and refreshing thing to my Spirit, and that not only as it would be a Benefit, and advantage to me in my outward estate, but also as I should take it as a Testimony of your Christi an regard for me as a Minister of Christ; and I think it would help to revive my fainting Languishing hopes that God would yet bless my Labours amongst you more then of Late years he hath done. but if upon tryal it be found that this is not likely in any Good Measure to be attain’d in this place, it is like to be a Subject of Melancholly thoughts to me, and mat ter of no Small Discouragment.

Obj: If we Should do that for you that you Seem to expect, it would be a burden to heavy for us to bear; It would Tend to Impoverish the place.

A 1. The Abilities of This whole place in point of estate I mean are Certainly Abundantly above mine; and Consequently you are far more able to do that for me, that will Comfortably Supply my outward wants, then I am Still to Ly under those wants without receiving tha thelp and Releif which in a Manner no burden to [————] the united Strength of a whole People con sisting of about a hundred and twenty men, many of whom are good Livers, & Some of them Thriving, and forehanded men, that to One that is Low, and behind hand in the world, and has but a little Strength, would be a burden quite In supportable. the burden that Now Lyes upon me in Maintaining & providing for my great family, my neighbours can much More easily Contribute or afford that assistance and Strength that is needfull to enable me Comfortably to bear it, then I can Still bear it alone.

2. Though I would be far from making too light of the publique Charge and burdens of this place, yet upon the most Serious and unbyast thoughts of the matter that as far as I know I am Capable of, I cant but think that if it would please God to Incline, and dispose my neighbours in a General way, to Act for me in this thing in a good measure with that willingness and readiness of mind, that they act with in their Own Concerns, they would do as much for me as I Desire, and yet Never find Any reason to Groan under the burden of it.

3. I am So far from thinking that a Peoples Laying out themselves as far is needfull to afford Such a Maintenance to a Minister, as may be proper and Sutable for him (which is all that I desire) tends to impoverish them, that I rather think it tends to enrich them; knowing that it is the blessing of God maketh rich, Prov: 10. 22. and when a People do this they honour marginal God; and that when they honour God, they may humbly hope, and that from his own word, that he will honour and bless them. Mal. 3. 10. 11. 12. bring ye the Tithes into the Storehouse, tha there may be meat in mine house, an dprove me now herewith Saith the Lord of hosts, if I will not Open to you the windows of heaven, and pour you out a blessing, that there Shall not be Rom enough to Receive it ———— and all Nations Shall Call you blessed, For ye Shall be a Delightsome Land, Saith the Lord of Hosts. We have no Cause to be afraid of making proof and tryal of God, & of trusting of him in the way of our duty; It is a Safe thing for us to Obey, and honour God, though it put us to Some Charge. The practice of our Duty is Certainly the way of our wellfare and prosperity.

Honoured and beloved

I Shall trouble you but with a few words further, and they are these viz that whatever Some may have Imagined, or whatever Opportuni ties I may think I might possibly have had for my advantage of that kind, had I been ready for them, I never had th eLeast thought that I know of, of Leaving this place for the Love of Lucre, or for Gains Sake; for A Minister to Leave his People, meerly or chiefly because he May have More of the world elswhere when he may Live Comfortably where he is, is that which I highly disapprove of, and abhor; And If a Covetous Craving Spirit, or the Love of mony, be the thing y t has mov’d me to do what I have now done, I must confess that in this thing I am a great Stranger to my Self: as far as I know my heart I would, & hope in Some measure I do, therein Look at that which will be greatly for your profit and benefit as well as mine.

And If It please God So to Move upon your hearts, that you Shew your Selves a Kind Loving People to me by expressing a due Regard to mine & my families wellfare in the present Case, I can without flat tery Say that It is my unfeigned Desire to be still for you, & for no other People but your Selves in the world, in that work and Service y t The Lord Jesus hath Long Since Called me to Amongst you, & therein to be a far greater blessing both to you & yours then ever I have yet been, and so to Remain, and that we may Live, and walk one with another, that there may Never be any Occation for our parting till Death Shall part us.
