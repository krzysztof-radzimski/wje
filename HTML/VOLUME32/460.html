C69. Thomas Clap to A Friend, December 10, 1744 C069.T.Clap-Friend 12-10-44 Thomas Clap

C069. Thomas Clap to "A Friend in Boston,"12/10/44. Printed as A Letter from the Rev. Thomas Clap . . . to a Friend in Boston (1745).

A L E T T E R FROM The Rev. Mr. Thomas Clap, &c.

Sir,

IAm informed by sundry Persons, that the Report of Contradiction between the Rev. Mr. Edwardsof Northampton and my self, has occasioned much Talk, and various Reflections in the Country, and especially in the Town of Bo- ston; and that he has wrote a Letter to Boston, which is to be printed, in which, it is said, there is a Denial of some Things which I reported.

This is very unexpected to me; especially con- sidering the Letters which have passed between us upon that Subject. And since it may be some Time before I shall see what he has wrote, I think it may be proper, in the mean Time, to give you this brief Information.

About two Months ago, when I heard the Report marginal that Mr. Edwards had denied some Things which I had said, I wrote a Letter to him, wherein I put him in Mind, that when we rode together toward Boston, in May,1743. we had a long Dis- course about Mr. Whitefield, and the State of Reli- gion, and that both of us seem’d to be well agreed to condemn sundry Errorsand Disorders, which some had fallen into and practised: And among other Things, I said, that it appeared to me, that there had been a Design to turn the generality of Ministers in the Country out of their Places, and where they could not make the Party strong enough to do to that at once, they would, in the mean Time, set up a Separation, hoping in Time to bring over the Major Part: Upon which Mr. Edwards said, Mr. Whitefield told me, that he designed to turn the generality of Ministers in the Country out of their Places, and bring over Ministers from England, Scotland andIreland, and place them in their Room. I also informed him of what he farther said up- on it, the Substance of which was, that he told Mr. Whitefield, that he disliked that, and some other Things; after which he did not seem willing to have much Discourse about it.

Now I am very clearand positive, that Mr. Edwards said to me the Words above recited, not only as to the Sense and Substance of them, but even as to mostof the Principal Terms:And though I am sensible of the Im- marginal perfection of humane Nature, that a Man may sometimes be mistaken, tho’ he seems very positive; yet in this Case I can’t at present think, that I was under any Mistake, for these three Reasons.

1. Because I took very good Notice of the Words, and laid them up in my Mind, and soon related them to others; and that, because I never before had any direct Information, but only conjectured about that part of the Design relating to the bringing over of Foreigners, &c;———tho’ as to the other Part, which respects the turning out, or separatingfrom Un- convertedMinisters, it was no News to me at all.

2. Because it was notorious to the World, that Mr. Whitefield, and other Itinerants did endeavour to perswade People, that the gene- rality of the Ministers were Unconverted, Pha- risee Teachers, &c. and that all unconvertedPer- sons were half Beastsand half Devils, and could no more be the Means of any Man’s Conversion, than a dead Man could beget a living Child; from whence it necessarily followed, that People ought to discard them, or separate from them, as accordingly they did, in a short Time, in a multitude of Places in the Country; especially in Connecticut, and that with the Ap- probationof the Itinerants,witness Mr. Ten- nant’s Sermon upon the Danger of an uncon- marginal verted Ministry, Pag. 18. &c.

3. Because Mr. Edwardshimself is, in the main, of the same Opinion with me, and the generality of others, in this Point, as appears by sundry fair and ingenuous Concessions in his Letters to me on this Subject, some Clauses of which I shall transcribe verbatim. "I have also (says he) on some Occasions, mention’d it as my Opinion, that Mr. Whitefield was formerly in the Opinion and SCHEME of People’s forsaking unconverted Ministers, which possibly you might hear of from others."——— "I heard Mr. Whitefield say, that he had Thoughts of bringing over a Number of young Men from Englandinto the Jerseys, and Pennsylvania, that he intended should be ordained by the Tennants."———Indeed Mr. Edwards, in his Letter, seems not willing to allow that he mentioned Ireland, because he never heard of any Interest Mr. Whitefield had there, &c. But I remember I had hearD he had, and thought of it when Mr. Edwards mention’d Ireland; and it is well known, that the Tennants, and the generality of those whom they have ordain’d, came originally from that Country; and it is most probable they would chuse to Ordain such as they had done, and that Mr. Whitefield should chuse to gratify them in some, at least,of those whom he should send over. But Mr. Edwards, in his marginal Letter, says, that Mr. Whitefield did not say that the Persons thus ordained in the Jerseys, were to be sent into New England. I think it no ways material whether he said so or no, since the constant Practice of the Tennants, and their Presbytery, makes it evident to the World, that thatwas their Design; for they sent Mr. Finley, Mr. Sacket, and sundry others to preach to the Separationsin New-Haven, Milford, and other Places in New England; and I have often heard it said, that theTennant’s and their Presbytery assumed as much Authority to send Missionaries intoNew-England, as the Bishop of London: And if so many have come from thence heretofore, we may presume that a greater Number would come, when they had a new Supply of Foreigners. But to go on. Mr. Ed- wards, in a second Letter, says "I have some- times said to others, as I inform’d you in my late Letterto you, that I supposed Mr. Whitefieldwas formerly of the Opinion, that unconverted Ministers ought not to continue in the Ministry; and I do suppose, that he en- deavour’d to propogate this Opinion, and a Practice agreeable to it. It is also true, that I talked with Mr. Whitefield about judging other Persons to be unconverted. Tho’, as I said, I remember no Conversation about Mr. Whitefieldat that Time; yet ’tis possible I "might say to you, that I believed Mr. Whitefield did marginal aim at People’s forsaking unconverted Ministers, and to endeavour that there should be a Supply of converted Ministers as fas as in him lay, or something to that Purpose. In the same Discourse, it is possible I might mention what Mr. Whitefieldtold me of his Design of bringing over some young Men from Englandto be ordained by the Tennants in the Jerseys; and might say, that I thought I had dealt more plainly with Mr. Whitefield about his Errors than any other Mi- nister; and when I talk’d with him about Impulses, he did not seem to like to have much Discourse about it." And I think it is reAsonable to presume, as I understood Mr. Edwards at first, that he had this Knowledge of Mr. White- field’s SCHEME of People’s forsaking unconverted Ministers, &c. from Mr. Whitefield’s own Mouth.

Thus, it appears that Mr. Edwards and I are, in Sub- stance, of the same Opinion in this Point: and tho’ he has not, in the above Letters, us’d exactly the same Terms, yet he has used a variety of those which are equally fulland strong; and if we still differ, it is only about some particular Terms, and inconsiderable Circum- stantials, which are not worthy of any Dispute.

I desire you would send me what he has printed, and in the mean Time you may make what Improvement of this Letter you think proper.

I am, Sir,

Your humble Servant,

New Haven, Dec 10 1744

Thomas Clap.
