B45. Letter from Thomas Clap, April 1, 1745 B045.T.Clap-JE 4-1-45 edited Thomas Clap

B045. Thomas Clap to JE, 4/1/45. Printed as A Letter from the Rev. Mr.Clap . . . to the Rev. Mr. Edwards (Boston, 1745).

A LETTER, &c.

Reverend Sir,

SOME Months ago, when I heard a Report of a Contradiction between us, I wrote some Letters to you in a pacific Manner, representing the ill Consequences of it to Religion, and endeavouring to come to a right Understanding. In your Answers, it appeared to me, that you plainly conceded to the Substance of what I had said, and yet seem'd unaccountably to insist upon a Contradiction; tho' I could see but little or none, but only between the several Parts of your Letters. And you sent a Letter to Boston to be printed, containing sundry Reflections upon me, which necessitated me to write a Letter to a Friend, giving him a short Account of the Case, and which was tho't necessary to be printed in my Vindication. This Letter charg'd you with no Crime, entred into no Controversy with you, but rather compromised Matters, and represented that there was no Contradiction between us so great, as to be worthy of any publick Controversy, Clamour, or Dispute.

This pacific Representation has exasperated you so much the more; and has occasioned you to write in a bitter Manner, declaring, that there was a great Contradiction between us, and that it was your indispensible Duty publickly to contradict me.

The State of the Case is thus:
I suppose Mr. W- had a Scheme or Design to turn out the generality of Ministers in the Country, by perswading People to discard or separate from them as being unconverted, and to get a Supply of converted ones, many of which were to be Foreigners.

After discoursing upon this with you, I reported, that you said these marginal Words, "Mr. Whitefield told me, that he designed to turn the generality of Ministers in the Country out of their Places, and bring over Ministers from England, Scotland, and Ireland, and place them in their Room."

Upon which, you in your Letters to me say these Words.

"Tho' I remember no Conversation about Mr. W-- at that Time, "yet I have on some Occasions mentioned it as my Opinion, that Mr. W-- was formerly in the Opinion and Scheme of People's forsaking unconverted Ministers, which possibly you might hear of from others.-- I heard Mr. W-- say, that he had Thoughts of bringing over a Number of young Men from England, to be ordained by the Tennant's. I have sometimes said to others, as I inform'd you in my late Letter to you, that I suppose that Mr. W-- was formerly of the Opinion, that unconverted Ministers ought not to continue in the Ministry; and I do suppose that he endeavoured to propagate this Opinion, and a Practice agreeable to it. It is true also, that I talked with Mr. W- about judging other Persons to be unconverted. Tho', as I said, I remember no Conversation about Mr. W-- at that Time; yet it is possible I might also say to you, that I believed Mr. W-- did aim at People's forsaking unconverted Ministers, and to endeavour that there should be a Supply of converted ones as far as in him lay; or something to that Purpose. In the same Discourse it is possible I might mention what Mr. W-- told me of his Design of bringing over some young Men from England, to be ordain'd by the Tennant's in the Jerseys."

These Words I quoted in my printed Letter, and said, that tho' you had not used exactly the same Terms (in expressing Mr. W--'s Scheme) as I had done; yet you had used a variety of those which were equally full and strong. And if we still differ, it is only about some particular Terms, and inconsiderable Circumstantials, which are not worthy of any Controversy or Dispute.---And I am of the same Mind still.

Now this agreeing Representation has very much exasperated you; and in your printed Letter you say, "that it appears you have owned no part of my Report, but most solemnly denied every Article of it." And proceed to make a most solemn Declaration or Oath in these Words, "I have no Remembrance that ever Mr. W-- said any Thing to me-- of any Design of his turning out any Ministers, or ever so much as hinted of any Thing of that Nature, or of his bringing over any Candidates from Scotland or Ireland, into any Part of America, or marginal from any other Country in Europe into New-England, or that ever I reported any such Thing to Mr. Clap, or any other Person, or that ever any thought of Mr. W-d's saying or hinting any Thing like either of these Things ever entred into my Heart, 'till I heard them as coming from Mr. Clap."--
‘--There is a great Contradiction between us.’--
‘--And it is my indispensable Duty publickly to contradict him.’

This is the State of the Case, and the principal Question upon it is, Whether this great Contradiction lies most between you and me, or between you and yourself.?

Now in order to determine this Question, I shall particularly go over my Words and yours, and compare them together.

I express'd Mr. W-d’s SCHEME in these Words, [that he design'd to turn out the Ministers,-- &c.] You express it in these Words ["Mr. W- was in the Opinion and SCHEME of People's forsaking unconverted Ministers; that unconverted Ministers ought not to continue in the Ministry; that he endeavoured to propagate this Opinion and a Practice agreable to it; that he aimed at People's forsaking unconverted Ministers, and endeavour'd that there should be a Supply of converted ones as far as in him lay."]--Here you have expressed Mr. W-d's Scheme in much fuller and stronger Terms than I did, especially as to the Way and Method by which it was to be effected; and this is exactly agreable to what I and others always supposed, viz. that Mr. W- designed to turn out the Ministers in this Manner, by laying a SCHEME that People should forsake them, by advancing this Opinion, that unconverted Men ought not to continue in the Ministry; and endeavouring to propogate this Opinion among the People, and the Practice agreable to it, i. e. their forsaking them accordingly.

Now since you have here in such full and strong Terms expressed and declared Mr. W-d's SCHEME, it seems very unaccountable to me, to see you so flatly deny it, and I think you msut have in your own Mind some strange Quibble or Equivocation.

Perhaps you'l say, that Mr. W- did not design to turn them out himself, but laid a Scheme how the People should do it by forsaking of them. But is not this in effect, the same Thing?

Suppose General PEPPERIL should be taken and carried into Ca-marginal nada, and there examin'd about the present Designs against Cape Breton, and he should make a solemn Declaration or Oath, that he never heard the Governour of Boston say any Thing of any Design of his to turn out the Governour of Cape Breton, or to take the Place, or any one Man in it; and that no such Thought, or any Thing like it ever entred into his Heart, till he heard of it at Canada, and had this Salvo in his own Mind, that the Governour had no Thought of doing of it himself, but only laid a Scheme how it should be done by the Soldiers, and endeavoured to propagate this Opinion and Practice among them; would not the World think that he had been to School to the Jesuites?

Perhaps you'l say, that Mr. W--d's Scheme was, that only Part of the People (perhaps the converted) should forsake their Ministers, and the rest adhere to him, and so he should not be turn'd out. But his is contrary to your own Account of Mr. W--d's Scheme; for if unconverted Ministers ought not to continue in the Ministry, they ought not to continue to be Ministers to Part, any more than to the Whole. And Mr. W-- endeavoured to propogate this Opinion and Practice, that People ought to forsake unconverted Ministers among all Men in general, without any Restriction to some. Suppose the Pretender should make another Invasion into England, and should declare himself to be the right Heir to the Crown and that no other Person ought to continue in the Possession of it, and endeavour to propagate this Opinion and Practice, and thereupon a considerable Number should cast off their Allegiance to his Majesty King GEORGE, and own the Pretender for their King; but afterwards the Pretender being apprehended, should solemnly declare, that he had no Design to dethrone the King, or to dispossess him of his Crown; that no Thought of that Nature, or any Thing like it, ever entred into his Heart (any more than to dethrone the Grand Turk, as in your Comparison) and that he only intended to protect a few Englishmen who voluntarily and out of Conscience submitted to him, but intended to leave King George in the full Possession of the Throne and Crown notwithstanding. What Credit do you think the World would give to such a Declaration?--

But perhaps you'll say, "that if every Man forsook the Minister, yet if he was not by Force turned out of the Meeting House, but suffered to preach there (tho' to the bare Walls) you might very fairly and solemnly deny that he was to be turned out." But, dear sir, it is amazing marginal to me to think that a Gentleman of your Sincerity and Integrity should use any such solemn Quibbles and Equivocations, to amuse and deceive the World. And yet I must say, that I cna imagine no other Way to reconcile your solemn Declaration with your Letters, or with the least appearance of Truth.

In P. 5th you say, "I have sometimes said to others, that I supposed Mr. W- was formerely of the Opinion that unconverted Ministers ought not to continue in the Ministry" And I do suppose he endeavour'd to propagate this Opinion, &c. But all that ever I said about it, has been only as expressing my Opinion, and not any declared Design of Mr. Wh-d." But what can you mean by this Distinction? Must we suppose that Mr. W-d laid this Scheme, and propagated this Opinion and Practice, and yet had no Design in it? or that this Design was secret and not open and declared? I never supposed that he made an open and formal Declaration of it; calling God to Witness to the Truth of it. But only that he expressed himself to you is such Terms as were plainly to be understood. Or do you mean that this was a meer Opinion or Supposition in you, founded upon no sufficient Evidence? Many others supposed there was sufficient Evidence of it from his preaching and Journals, and I presume, as I always understood you, that you had it from his own Mouth. And certainly I must presume that you had some very good Reason and Evidence for it, or else you would never have taken up such an Opinion of Mr. W--d, and publish'd it to others, as you own you did. And if you did rashly take up and publish such Opinion of him, meerly upon Report or evil Surmising, without any sufficient Foundation, you ought to make a publick Recantation and Acknowledgement for abusing Mr. W-d. I shall be ready to do it, when I am convinced that that Opinion is groundless: And since you insist upon a publick Recantation for the Vindication of Mr. W-d, I'll make you this offer, that if you will make a publick Recantation of this Opinion, and declare (without calling God to witness) that you now believe that Mr. W-d never was in this Scheme of People's forsaking unconverted Ministers, that he never did endeavour to propagate this Opinion, that unconverted Ministers ought not to continue in the Ministry, nor any Practice agreable to it, and that he never did aim at People's forsaking unconverted Ministers, nor endeavour that there should be a Supply of converted ones, as far as in him lay, and that you wrong'd Mr. W- in taking up such an Opinion, and speaking of it to others; then I also will make a publick Recantation for reporting such a Thing from a Gentleman marginal whose Opinion and Judgment in that Matter could not be depended upon, and desire the World to take no Notice of it as coming from you.

However, since you acknowledge, that this was once your declared Opinion, how then can this be true, "that no Thought of Mr. W-d's "saying or hinting any Thing of any Design of his of turning out any "Ministers, or any Thing LIKE IT ever entered into your Heart, till "you heard of it as coming from Mr. Clap?" I must leave others to re concile it if they can. But to use your Words, it is one of the most - amazing Things to me that ever I met with in my Life.

Again you say, "Mr. W. was formerly in this Opinion and Scheme, &c." which implies that you suppose that he has lately laid it aside. I wish I knew what Evidence you have of that. 'Tis certain he has never made any publick Recantation of that Scheme; but those who have practiced upon it by forsaking their Ministers, still adhere to him, and he still encourages them by preaching at the separate Meetings, as the Prints inform us. If you could have produced any Evidence that Mr. Whitefield had recanted this Scheme, it would have been much more for his Honour, than to have pretended to deny that he was ever in it. And I can't see how you can, consistent with your self, countenance his preaching, until it appears, either that you were mistaken in your Opinion of his being formerly in this Scheme, or that he has laterly made a Recantation of it.

But to proceed, my Words are [and bring over Ministers from England, Scotland and Ireland] You say from England only, and have no Remembrance of Scotland or Ireland, Here is some Difference in our Memories, but this is an immaterial Circumstance in the Scheme, and not worthy of any Dispute. For if any Part of the present Ministry were to be discarded, and a Number of Foreigners brought over, it matters but little whether they were all to come from England or some from Scotland, Ireland or the Orphan House. Indeed in my former Letter, I mention sundry Reasons why I do verily believe, that you mentioned Scotland and Ireland, and that Mr. W- did intend to Bring some Candidates from thence, and could now mention more; but as this is an immaterial Point, I shall wave it. And indeed you mention it and pass it over as if you laid but little Weight upon it. P. 11.

My Words are [and place them in their Room] Your Words are marginal

["Mr. W--d did aim at People's forsaking unconverted Ministers, and endeavour that there should be a Supply of converted Ministers"] Now this new Supply must necessarily be in the Room or Place of such as were forsaken. 'Tis true you say in your Letter, "that Mr. W- didn't say one Word of any Design of these Foreigners supplying the Pulpits of other Ministers, made vacant by their being turned out." But this in the Circumstance of Things is necessarily implied, whether he particularly express'd it or not. For when any People have forsaken and discarded their Minister, they must have another in his Room, or else they must live without; and many of these must be Foreigners, because our Colleges are in such a dark State, as he represented them, that but a little Supply could be expected from them. Perhaps you will say, "that these Foreigners were to settle only where Ministers died, or in new Parishes, or to be confin'd to the Jerseys and Pensylvania, and not come into New-England." But I would appeal to the Common Sense of Mankind, whether there be any rational Foundation for either of these Suppositions. And the constant Practice of the Tennant's in sending their Missionaries to our Separations proves the contrary. And therefore you might have spared that Clause in your Letter and Declaration, wherein you say, that Mr. W- did not say that the Persons ordained by the Tennant's were to come into New England; since I never said that you did say so, or that you mentioned New England in particular, but the Nature of the Scheme, and the Practice of the Tennant's makes it evident that they were to come here as well as to other Places.

In the last Place, I observe, that you say, P. 3. that tho' "you have on some Occasions mentioned it as your Opinion that Mr. W- was in this Opinion and Scheme, which possibly you might hear from others, yet I have no Remembrance of my having any Conversation with you about Things of this Nature." Yet in P. 5. you say, "that might be, and I might forget it," and in P. 9. "tho' I Remember no Conversation about Mr. W-- at that Time, yet it is possible I might say to you, that Mr. W-- did aim at People's forsaking unconverted Ministers, and endeavour that there should be a Supply of converted ones, &c." Yet in P. 14th you seem to think, that 'tis not very likely that you should reveal such a SECRET to Me. I have before observed, that a principal Part of this Scheme that Mr. W-endeavoured to propagate this Opinion and Practice of People's forsaking unconverted Ministers, and that he intended some where or other to get a Supply [10] ply of converted ones, was no Secret to me; and this you own you said to others; but as to this Part or Circumstance, viz. that Mr. W-- told you that he intended to bring over a Number of young Men from England to be ordain'd by the Tennant's--&c: This was truly a Secret to me 'till I had it from you, and must have remain'd a Secret 'till the Great-Day, unless you or some other Person had revealed it to me. Now as you say in another Case, I challenge the whole World besides you, to declare, if ever they gave me this Information; and therefore I think it falls but little short of Demonstration, that you must be the very Person who revealed this Secret to me, unless I had it by immediate Revelation.

And thus I have considered your Words (by me quoted) in all the Constructions which they can possibly bear, as they are in themselves, and they stand related to any other words in your Letters, which can possibly explain or alter the Sense of them, and have considered all the Evasions which you have made, or might make, and after all am of the same Opinion I was at first, that your Words must and will speak the same Sense in Substance with mine, and can't be avoided without the plainest Quibbles and Equivocations: and I leave the World to judge, whether I deserve any ill Treatment from you for being of that Mind.

That the Reader may have a comprehensive View of the Whole, I shall set down my Words and yours in Contrast in three Columns.

Now let every Reader compare the Words as they here stand (a little abridg'd for want of Room) and as they stand in your printed Letters, and see whether this great Contradiction lies between the first Column and the second, or between the second and the third.

And I leave the World to judge, whether you have not treated me in a very unhandsom and unchristian Manner, in making such a publick Noise and Clamour, and amusing the World about a great Contradiction between us, and casting such Reflections, and endeavouring to begin a publick and personal Controversy with me, altho' you could not do it without contradicting yourself. I could with the greatest Justice return back many of your unhandsom Reflection in your own Words, but the Scripture as well as my own Inclination forbid me to render Evil for Evil, and Railing for Railing; and I am so far from it, that I freely forgive you, tho' you never ask it, or see Need of it. I hope that the future your Time will be better employ'd.

I remain, Rev. Sir,
Your humble Servant,
Thomas Clap.
New-Haven, April 1. 1745.
