A215. Letter to William McCulloch, April 10, 1756 215.JE-W.McCulloch 4-10-56 Jonathan Edwards

A215. JE to William McCulloch, 4/10/56. ANTS, f. 1756-59A, #2-3.

Stockbridge April 10. 1756

Rev. & dear Sir,

I thank You for your Favour of Aug. 1755. with Mr. Imries Letter,1

which came to Hand in the latter Part of the last month. ---

It recommends a Man, especially a Minister of the Gospel, to me, to

see Evi in Him Evidences of a disposition to be searching into the Pro-

phecies of Scripture, relating to the future Advancement of Christ's

Kingdom on Earth : It looks as tho' he were a Man of Concern

for Christ's Kingdom & Interest in the World, and were one of those

who took Pleasure in the Stones, and favoured the dust of Zion.

But it has proved by Events, that many divines that have been of

this Character, have been over forward to fix the Times & Seasons,

which the Father hath put in his own Power. However, I will not

positively charge Mr. Imries with this, before I see what he has to

say in proof of those Things which he has advanced. I think that

it neither I, nor any other Person that knows no more than what is

contain'd in his Letter of the Reasons that He builds his Opinions

upon, have any Opportunity to judge of those Opinions. And there

fore I sh should think it a Pity, that his private Letter to Mr. Hogg

was publish'd to the World, before his Reasons were prepared for the

Press. This Letter has been reprinted in Boston : But coming abroad

with so little Mention of the Grounds of his Opinion, gives occasion

to the Profane to reproach and ridicule it, and its Author.

With respect to Mr. Brainerd and Mr. Hawley, & their Indians, con-

cerning which you desire to be informed; The Correspondents have altered

their determinations from time to time with respect to Mr. Brainerd

& his Indians. They seemed inclined at first to his their removal to

Wawwomung alias Wioming; & then to Onohohquaughae; & then

to Wioming again. and finally, about a Twelve-month ago, they

d wholly dismiss'd Him from his Employ as a Missionary to the In-

dians, and Pastor to the Indian Church at Bethel. I can't say

I am fully satisfied in their Conduct in doing this so hastily; nor do

I pretend to know so much of concerning the Reasons of their Con-

duct to have sufficient Grounds positively to condemn their Proceed-

ing. However, that Congregation is not wholly left as Sheep without

a sheperd, but are in Part committed to the Care of Mr. William

Tennent ; who lives not far off and is a faithful zealous minister;

who visits them, and preaches to 'em once a week ; but I think not

often upon the sabbath. The last Fall, I was in N. Jersey and Phila-

delphia, and was present at a meeting of the Correspondents when

Mr. Tennent gave an agreeable account of the then present State

of those Indians with respect to Religion ; and also of their being

in better Circumstances as to those Lands than they had been. Mr.

Brainerd

Brainerd was then at Newark with his Family; where he marginal

had been preaching as a Probationer for settlement, ever since

Mr. Burr's dismission from that Place on Account of his Busi-

ness as President of the College. But whether Mr. B---d is

settled, or like to settle there, I have not heard. At the foremen-

tion'd meeting of the Correspondents, I used some Arguments [-]

to induce them still to promote to reestablish Mr. Brainerd in his

former Employ with his Indians, and to send them to Onohquau-

ghee. But I soon found it would be fruitless to urge the matter.

what was chiefly insisted on as an insuperable Obstacle to Mr.

Brainerd's going with his Family so far into the Wilderness, was

Mrs. Brainerds very infirm state. whether there was indeed any

sufficient Objection to such a removal at that Time divine Provi-

dence has since that Time laid insup so ordered the State and

Consequences of the War subsisting here in America, that insupera-

ble Obstacles are laid in the Way of their removal, either to

Onohohquaughee, Wawwomung or any other of th Parts of A-

merica that way. The French by their indefatigable Endeavour

with the d Nation of the delawares (so called from their antient

seat, about delaware River, tho' now chiefly residing on susque-

hannah River & it's Branches) have stir'd them to make war

on the English; and dreadful have been the Ravages & desola-

tions they have made of late on the back-Parts of Pennsylva-

nia and N. Jersey. They are the principal Nation inhabiting

the Parts about Susq---nah River, on which both Wioming &

On---ghee stand. the latter indeed is above the Bounds of their

Countrey ; but yet not very far from them ; and the delaware

Indians are frequently there, as they go to & fro. On which Ac-

count, there is great danger that Mr. Hawley's Mission & mi-

nistry there will be will be entirely broke up. Mr. H---y

came from thence about two months ago (with one of my sons, of

about 10 years old,2 who had been there with Him near a Twelve-

month, to learn the Mohawk Language) He has since been

to Boston to consult the Commissioners for Indian Affairs, that

have employed Him, And returned : and yesterday went from

my House to meet some of his Indians, at an appointed Time

& Place in the Mohawk Countrey, to determine with them

whether it be will be safe for him to return to abide with

them. [-] If not, yet He will be under the Pay of the Com-

missioners till next Fall, and the Issue of the two Expedi-

tions now unde in Prosecution, one again Crown-Point,

the other against Fort the French Forts at Frontenac & Nia-

gara near Lake Ontario ; which may possibly make a great

Alteration as to the state of the War with the Indians. If

Mr.

Hawley determines not to return to O---ghee this Spring, He will marginal

Probably go as Chaplain to the Indians in General Shirley's Army,

in the Expedition to Lake Ontario.

You speak of the vast superiority of the Numbers of the English in

America to those of the French, and that some therefore think the Colo

settlements of the former are in no great danger from the latter. ----

Tho' it be true that the French are 20 times less in than we are in

Number ; yet it may be a Question, whether other Things they exceed us

in, when all jointly Considered, wont more than Countervail all our ex-

cess of Numbers. They vastly exceed us in subtilty & Intrigue, in vigi-

lance and Activity, in speed and secrecy ; in Acquaintance with

the Continent of North-America, in all Parts west of the British

settlements, for many hundred Leagues, The Rivers, Lakes, Mountains,

the Avenues & Passes ; And also in the Influence they have among

the various Tribes & Nations of Indians; and their constant Care, di

skill & indefatigable diligence in managing them; to alienate 'em

from the English, attach 'em firmly to themselves, & employ them as

their Tools : Besides the vast Advantage they have in Time of War,

in being all united under the Absolute Command of one man, the

Governour of Canada ; while we are divided into a great many distinct

Governments, independent one of another ; and in some Respects of clash-

ing Interests : which unspeakably clogs & imbarasses our affairs, & makes

us, tho' a great, yet an unwieldy unmanageable Body, and an

easy Prey to our vigilant, secret, subtil, swift & active (tho' compara

tively small) Enemy.

As to a description of the situation of those Parts you mention, I can

give you no better than you have in maps that abound in great Bri-

tain. --- With Respect to the situation of Stockbridge ; it is

not in the Province of N. York as you have been informed, but

in the utmost Border of the Province of New the Massachusetts,

on the West, next to the Province of New York; about 40 miles

from west of Connecticut River, and about 25 miles East of Hudson's

River, and about 35 miles south-East from Albany. A Place

exposed in this Time of war. 4 Persons were killed here in the Be-

ginning of Septem. 1754 by Canada Indians; which occasioned a great

Alarm to us & great Part of N. England. since this we have had many

Alarms ; but God has preserved us. I desire your Prayers that we may

still be preserved, and that God would be with me & my Family

& People, and bless us in all Respects. - My Wife & Family join with

me in their Respects to you & yours. I am dear sir;

uour affectionate Br. & servt

Jonath. Edwards

Mr. McCulloch.

1. David Imrie, A Letter from the Reverend Mr. David Imrie, Minister of the Gospel at St. Mungo, in Annandale. To a Gentleman [William Hogg] in the City of Edinburgh (Edinburgh, 1755; rep. Boston, 1756). ↩
