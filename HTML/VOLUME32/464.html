C73. Timothy Edwards to Abigail Edwards Metcalf and William Metcalf, July 11, 1745 C073.TE-A & WMetcalfe 7-11-45 Timothy Edwards

C073. TE to William & Abigail Edwards Metcalfe,7/11/45. BRBL, f. 1552. Fragment.

Windsor July 11 th, 1745.

Dear Son & Daughter

By a Letter I receiv’d from your Sometime Since, wherein you Inform me that God has Taken from you your Dear Little Son by Death, 1 which Information I receiv’d with Sorrow. I question not but that you will both readily Own, that God both actually and rightfully Governs the workd as an Absolute Sovereign, and That is the perfection, and Glorious Excellenccy of his Nature, that he is Righteous in all his wayes, and holy In all his works and that he never did, Or possibly can, do the Least wrong to any of his Creatures, and that as he is Infinitely not only the highest but also the best of Beings, so it is most fit that in all his dispensations to= wards Mankind his Glory Should be his Great end. and as God only knows what is most for his Glory, and So What is best to be done, So it most agreeable to Religion and Reason, that the most Should do with all the works of his hand whatsoever he pleases. Now if you receive these things as true and in the Love of them, you do that which is pleasing to God. We have Many, yea very many passages in Gods holy word very agreeable to what I have now written Concerning the ab= solute Sovereignty and Glorious Excellency of God, Some of which (and but a few) I Shall now Mention, viz. Dan ll: 4. 35 and All the Inhabitants of the world are Reputed as Nothing; and he doth According to his will &c — Among the Inhabitants of ye Earth, and none can Stay his hand or Say unto him what doest thou? Hab: 2. 20. But the Lord is in his holy Temple, Let all The Earth keep Silence before him. 1. Sam ll: 3. 18. and he Said, it is the Lord, Let him do what Seemeth him Good. Zach: 2. 13. Be Silent O all Flesh before the Lord. Psal. 39. 9. I was dumb, I Opened not My Mouth, because thou didst it. Lam: 3. 28. 29. he Sitteth alone, and keepeth Silence, because he hath born it upon him, he putteth his Mouth in the dust. —— These Texts and Many others hold forth to us, the Infinite Greatness, Majesty, Absolute Sovereignty, Spot less Righteousness, and Glorious Excellency of God. If therefore from the high thoughts and Apprehensions of him they are therefore pleasing to you, and your Souls close with them, as God is honoured and Exalted by them, you give to God In Some Good measure the Glory that is due to his Name. heartily and practically to acknowledge the Sovereignty of God, to ascribe Righteousness to our Maker to entertain high and honourable thoughts of him, in a time of Great and Sore affliction, and So with humble Silence and Submission, patiently to bear his afflicting hand, acknowledging that he afflicts us far Less that we deserve, and that we have Still great cause to bless his Name, with Much of a Spirit of Love and thankfullness, for the Mercies that we yet enjoy, this is to put Singular honour upon God, and to Shine like Gold brightly when Lying in the Furnace. In a due manner Acknowledge God, as the Great King and So- vereign of the world, and So to walk humbly with him, and to have Exalting, and Adoring thoughts of him, and that when he dispenses things that are very Grievous and Bitter to us, If these things be the things that we Love, if we delight in them after the Inward man, and that because God is hereby honoured, and Glory is given to him, and the Contrary to them even in the Least degree, as it is against God, and to his dishonour is the evil, or are the things that we hate, our Souls with in us So acknowledging the Glorious Perfection, and Infinite Exellency of the Blessed God, that the Least mean Low thought of him, is that which we abhor; This is Surely pleasing and Acceptable to God. Such a Spirit as I have now in Some measure describing, I have comfortable hopes God hath Given to both of you to whom I am now writing, and that he hath enabled, and will enable you, humbly to walk before [...] 2 under that Bereaving stroke of his holy hand wherewith he hath Lately Sorely afflicted you: and If it be So you have Great cause to be thankful to God, for this is a mercy Greater, yea Much greater, then your affliction in the final departure (as to the Life that now is) of your Dear Child, though that be very great: for So to do is to act graciously, and So an Evidence, not only of Grace in Truth, but also of Grace in Exercise, as realy, as believing in Christ, and Mourning for Sin with Godly Sorrow, or of the Souls Going out after God or the Lord Jesus Christ in Desires, from a Spirit or principle of Divine Love is So And that you may See that what I have here Last written is Agreeable to the Judgment not only of ministers of a more Com= mon and ordinary Rank, but also of them that have been Esteem’d very Eminent both of New-England and Old, as may appear by what I shall next write, which is but a Small part of what I have had for many years Lying by me, chiefly Transcribed from the Printed Works of Several of the Ministers I have Respect to in what is Just above written; Some thing of it I receiv’d from two of them in Discourse, with which I shall begin what I here Intend.

"I once heard the Renowned Mr Samuel Willard Say thus or to this effect, viz, That a humble acknowledgement of, and "Submission to the Sovereignty of God, was a Sign or Mark of a mans being truly Converted, or an act and Evidence "of true Grace. Old Mr Woodbridge of Hartford, a Faithful Minister of Christ, and one of Great wisdom and Knowledge, once as we were Riding together as I Remember between Hartford, and Farmington, told me, or Said to me in Discourse, "that for "A Christian quietly to Submit himself under the hand of an afflicting God was a Great Act of Grace, and a Great At= "tainment in Christianity, or to that effect; he also Said to the Same purpose Lately when we were Sitting together at "his house also in his Last Lecture which was Feb: — 1718/19 — from Prov. 16. 7. when a mans wayes please "The Lord, — he Said that for a Christian to acknowledge the Glorious Atributes of God &c— to acknowledge him "In his Government of the world [...] 3 things pleasing to God - and that Submission and Resignation to God under "his afflicting hand So as not to Murmur &c— is thatwhich is very pleasing to God.

"When the Saints patiently bear affliction as coming from God, they Exercise Grace and holiness, they actually Submit "to Gods hand Subject themselves to him, and So are made more meet to enjoy him; when we patiently bear afflictions we "Subject our Selves, and this is the way to Live, and to Live Eternally with God. Our Patient Suffering is acceptable to God. Pres: t Wadsworth in his Resignation to God under afflictions p. 72. ††

†† under -"Thy Sutable Carriage under affliction, is one Good medium to know thou art of Child of God; this is a Good way to Evi= "dence thy Sonship. —— The more meek, humble, patient, and Long Suffering you are under troubles, So much the more you "have of your being Gods Children. ———— In So doing we greatly Glorify God &c ——— by our Submission and re "signation, we practically do our utmost to Set God on the Throne of his Sovereignty, we Glorify him by Obeying that pre= "cept, Psal. 46. 10. be Still and know that I am God. - We Glorify Gods Justice and Righteousness, we practically Justify "God in all he does, Saying his throne is Guiltless, no fault is to be found with any of his proceedings &c — marginal "God in all he does, Saying his Throne is Guiltless, no fault is to be found with any of his proceedings &c- "Mr Wadsworth in his Resignation to Gods will under afflictions. 91. 92. 93 &c ——

"The Sea when it is calm is as Still and quiet as any River, but Let the winds Once Rise and you Shall See a difference; "Then you Shall See Nothing but Raging, and Storming, out mire and Dirt. In peace wicked mens Corruptions Ly hid, then "they will carry themselves to God and Man More Moderately: but Let God Lay affliction on them, and then (as Satan falsely Charged Job) See if they will not Spit the very poyson of their blasphemy, in the face of God himself. ——— "———— Therefore doth James Call afflictions Temptations, because they Serve to try us &c— how was the tryal of Abra= "hams Sound heart, when God Laid that Cross upon him, that Grievous Command, &c —— now I have Given thee a thorow "Tryal of the Integrity of thine own heart. here was also the tryal of Jobs Good heart &c - —— how meek and patient was "he? No Murmuring, No Gruding, how meek and patient was he. the Lord hath Given and taken, blessed be his Name.

Mr Daniel Dyke of the Deceitfulness of Mans heart; Concerning the discovery of the heart by affliction. 328. 329. 330. ‡ vide marg t 4/p>

"A Holy Silence is that Excellent precious Grace, that Lends a hand of Support to every Grace: Silence is the keeper of all "other vertues; it Lends a hand to faith, hope, Love, humility, and Self denyal. A holy Silence hath its Influence upon all other "Graces that be in the Soul: it causes the Rose-buds of Grace to Blossom and bud forth. Silence is a Grace that keps a man "Gracious in all Conditions: Silence is a Christians Right hand. ——— It bears up the Soul in Adversity. A quiet Silent Spirit is "of Great Esteem with God. &c —— 1. Pet. 3. 4. even the Ornament of a meek and quiet Spirit, which is in the Sight of God of great "price. It is a Spark of the Divine Nature, a Beam of Glory, it is a Heav’n born Spirit. —— This is a Flower of Paradice; it is "a precious Gem that God makes Great Reckoning of. Mercy is nearest, Deliverance, and Salvation is at hand, when a "Christian Stands Still, when he Sits quiet and Silent, under his greatest troubles, and Sorest tryals. No man honours God, "Nor No man Justifies God at So high a Rate, as he who Lays his hand upon his Mouth, when the Rod of God is upon "his Back. Mr Brooks his Mute Christian under the Smarting Rod, p. 123. 124. 125 &c ————

———— "But then the Last thing to be Inculcated upon us is this, my Brethren, Let us be very Studious to "follow the Blessed Jesus; ——— to Conform to that Glorious Example which our Lord hath Given us of a pati "ent Submission to the will of God in every thing being a mighty Article in our Conformity to our Saviour.

"Take pains with your own hearts, Renounce your own will, cry to the Glorious One, that he would Subdue the Re= "bellion of your own will &c —— Give not Over till you can Say, &c — O my God If this be the Cup, which thou hast ap= "pointed for me, I wil Strive to Submit unto it, thy will, thy will Shall be Mine O my God! Make Choice of Spiritual "Blessings, which Shall be an Abundent Compensation for the Loss of the Temporal ones of which your Sufferings may de "prive you; and then Say If the Lord, and Such Blessings of the Lord, may be the portion of my Cup, I have a Goodly he= "ritage. It will be Impossible for you to Gratify Heaven More then by Such a Devotion.

"Doct r: Cotton Mather in his Sermon, of the will of the Father Submitted to, from Jn o: 18. 11. the cup that my Father "hath Given me to Drink, Shall I not Drink it? p. 25.—27.-

"We cannot Glorify the Great God more then by Submission to his holy providence. There are Some who read [...]5 Silence is praise to thee O God. Wenever parise our God, So Significantly, So Effectually "So acceptably as in Silence under his afflictive dispensations. Let us with an humble Resignation Sweetly Submit "unto the will of God in the Death of our friends, and Let us as the Genuine Children of Abraham, offer up unto God, "The very Isaacs of our Tenderest Love. ——— The Angels of Heaven will be the Glad Spectators, of this our hum "ble Resignation, and Say, Gen: 22. 14. Now I know that thou fearest God.

"Doct r: Cotton in his Piety quickned at a Funeral, from Eccl. 7. 2.

"I am to tell you over and over again, that you never gratify the Glorious God So by any thing as by this Resig= "nation unto him. No Devotions of a Godly Life do more Glorify God then this one Article of it.

Doct r: Cotton Mather in his Sermon of the Divine Sovereignty displayed and Adored, from Job 9. 12. behold he Taketh away, and who can hinder him.

"If I Take with a patient Submission the Cup that is appointed for me, tis as Good, as Rich, as Bright a Token for "Good as can be found upon me. t’will be aToken of thy Sincerity to Take it Sutably. perhaps t’is the Cup of "A funeral. that which was the Desire of thine eyes, the Delight, the Darling of thy Family must have its fu= "neral, to part with thy Isaac is the Cup that is Given thee, take it, and hear those acclamations of Heaven over "thee, —— Now know I that thou fearest God. ——— We Read 2. Tim: 2. 12 If we Suffer with him we Shall "Also Reign with him. If we Take our Cup as the blessed Jesus took it, and if we tread in the Steps of his patient "Submission to the Condition appointed for him, then we Shall be with him, and Sit with him and Sup with him, and "and Drink of the fruit of the vine in his Kingdom, with him, and that Lamb of God will feed us, and Lead us "to Fountains of Living Water for evermore.

Doct r: Cotton Mather, in his Sermon, The will of a Father Submitted to, from Jn o: 18. 11. the Cup which my Father [hath given me, Shall] 6 I not Drink it.

" — When Aaron had his Sons taken from him after an awful manner tis Said Aaron held his peace, Levit: 10. 3. — he "did not Murmur against God. When Eli was Informed by Samuel &c—— how humbly and Graciously did he Ex= "press himself, Submitting to the will and providence of God. 1. Sam ll: 3. 18 he said it is the Lord, Let him do what Seemeth him "Good. If God Shall Withhold from us those things which our hearts are most Set upon, we must humbly Submit to his holy provi= "dence. So did David, when he knew not whether ever he Should See the ark of God again, which he much desired; with what re= signation to the will of God doth he Express himself. ——— Behold here am I, Let him do to me, as Seemeth Good unto him. "———— Passive Obedience is the most difficult: If the Lord will take from our Estates, and bring us to penury, or If the "Lord will take from us our health, and afflict us with painful diseases; to Say the will of the Lord be done, is a hard Lesson; If the "Lessons in the School of Christ, &c. — Doct r: Increase Mather in his Sermon Occationed by the Death of his wife from that text, Mat: 6. 10. thy will be done. p. 13. 14. 15. &c. —— 36. 37.

"Concerning Crosses afflictions, Troubles &c— A patient Submission unto, and fruitful Exercise under marginal "Gods visiting hand is an Infallible demonstration that thou art a Son, and not a Bastard. again he Speaks thus, or to s "This purpose that Such a Submission unto God will, and Exercise under his afflicting hand are evident Marks of a persons "being in the right way to Heaven. Mr Robert Bolton in his Book of a Comfortable walking with God. p. 368. 369.

"To be above the Stroke of passion is a Condition equal to Angels; to be in a State of Sorrow, without the Sense of "Sorrow is a disposition beneath beasts; but duly to Regulate our Sorrows, and Bound our Passions under the Rod, "Is the wisdom, duty, and Excellency of a Christian. he that is without Natural affections is deservedly Ranked among "The worst of Heathen, and he that able rightly to Manage them, deserves to be numbred with the best of Christians. "Though when we are Sanctified we put on the Divine Nature, yet till we are Glorified we put not off the Infirmities "of the humane Nature. whilest we are within the reach of troubles, we cannot be without the Danger, Nor ought we "to be without the fear of Sin. how apt are we to transgress the Bounds of Reason and Religion under a Sharp afflic= "tion, appears in most mens Experience.

"Whatever God doth with us or ours, Still we Should maintain Good thoughts of him. A Gracious heart cleaves near= "er and Nearer to God in affliction, and can Justify God in his Severest Strokes, acknowledging them all to be Just and "holy, Psal: 119. 75. and hereby the Soul may comfortably Evidence to it Self its own uprightness, and Sincere Love to God; "yea it hath been of Singular use to Some Souls to take right measures of their Love to God in Such tryals: to have "Lovely, and well pleased thoughts of God, even when he Smites us in our Nearest and dearest Comforts, argues plain "ly that we Love him for himself, and not for his Gifts only, and that his Interest in the heart is deeper then Crea= "ture Interest is. And he observes that very great hath been the Comfort that hath Resulted to Some from Such dis= "coveries of their own hearts by close Smarting afflictions.

"The more Lovely and Engaging your Relation was, the more Excellent will your patience and Contentment with "the will of God in its death be: the more Loveliness, the more Self-denyal, and th emore Self denyal, the more grace. "It may turn to you for a Testimony of the Truth and Strength of Grace, that you can like Abraham part with a Child, "whom you So dearly Lovein Obedience to the will of God.

"Mr Flavel in his Token for Mourners, 1. 2. — 34. 35. 122. 123.

I have Comfortable hopes (as I think I have already Said) that you have both of you that in your hearts that is very agreeable to what is above-written, of the Judgment of Several Ministers of Christ, of a Superiour Character, (Some of which were men of fame and Renown in their day, above most of their order,) Concerning a patient Bearing of† 7 Affliction as the hand of a Sovereign God, and a humble Submission to his will therein.

Now if Such a Question Should Arise, viz Suppose we have hope from as it Seems to us we find and feel in our hearts That we have (and do experience) this patient hearing of affliction as the hand of a Sovereign God, and that from the high apprehensions we have of him we can freely Justify him in it, and humbly Submit to his will in it as is above Expressed [...] 8 but that we therein act Sincerely, and with the Excercise of True Grace.

[ . . .]

Blood and there be with him, and receive from him, the most Endearing Expression of his Love for evermore, and there marginal as our portion we Shall See God as our Father, and fully and everlastingly enjoy him in the [———————] brightest displays of the Glorious Excellencies and perfections of his‡ 9 Blessed Nature, and of his Love to us as Some of his Dear Children, and in a State of perfect holiness, and with most high and Glorious actings of Grace towards Our God and Redeemer, and tha forever And ever.

If we Esteem these things the Chief Good, the highest happiness that we can enjoy or desire, and So Set our hearts upon y m, to prize and Seek them above all other things whatsoever, we certainly Act Graciously. This is an act and Evidence of Grace † 10 viz. Supream, and So of Sincere Love to God and our Lord Jesus Christ.

It is very clear from Gods own word, that none but them that are truly Gracious, being Inwardly Renewed and Sanctified by the ho= ly Spirit in their Regeneration, can have Such high thoughts and Apprehensions of the Glorious Excellency of that Infinitely Glorious and Excellent being, the Blessed God, Being One God in three Divine persons, the Father Son and holy Spirit, as freely, and with their whole Souls to Chuse him to be their God, and accordingly to Serve and Glorify him as God and their God while thy are in this world, and to place their highest happiness in the full Enjoyment of him as their portion for ever in the Life to come. 6. and Lastly If we humbly hope in the Mercy of God in Christ for pardon and Salvation, and this hope be Accompanied with Such a fear of God that we are careful in all things to Avoid Sin, that So we may not Offend and dishonour him; In a word If it be much our Desire, delight and Care, dayly So to walk before God that we may please and honour him, and humbly acknowledging our great unworthiness as Sinners, and yet taking Encouragement from the Greatness and Riches of Gods mercy to Sinners in through the great Mediator, hope and trust in the Mercy of God in Christ for forgiveness of Sin and Eternal Salvation, this is truly to fear God, and truly to believe in the Lord Jesus Christ. To fear God, and not to hope in his Mercy in Christ, is to be either a dispairing Sinner, or but a Self-Righteous Pharisee. Again to hope (after a Sort) in Gods mercy in Christ, and not to Sanctify him in his heart and to make him his fear and his Dread, So as carefully to avoid Sinning against him, or so as to allow himself in any Course or way of known Sin, is to be a presumptuous Sinner, and one whose hope is but the hope of the Hypocrite; but So to fear God, and humbly to hope in his mercy in Christ, &c- as is above expressed, is to be a True believer, a Sound Christian, And So one that God Loves and takes plea= sure in. Psal. 147. 11. The Lord taketh pleasure in Them that fear him, in those that hopein his Mercy.

I am Sensible that I have been very Long, in If not too Long in this Letter; when I first begun it, I Intended it Should have been very Much Shorter then Now it is, but as it is, it is my hearts desire that God would make it not only profita= ble, but also comfortable to you both in this Day of your Mourning. I am also very Sensible that at Such a time as this you might well have Expected a Letter from me Some weeks ago, and Indeed I Intended to have Sent you one Long before now And I have been for Some time under considerable uneasiness that it has not been done, but by reason of the more then ordina= ry business that has ddayly Lain upon Me, that I am far from being able to as I would. Neither have I written this Letter without Considerable Difficulty, by resaon of Dayly want of Time. for many moneths together, (I am very apt to think) my work, every week, has been very near, if not full as Much again as the ordinary Work of my ministry in preparing and preaching two Sermons, Constantly every Sabbath &c— would Amount to. Before I conclude, or finish what I have further to Say in what I am Now writing, I would Say Something for your Comfort concerning your dear departed Little Son, and it is this viz that by what I have heard and Read, I am Inclin’d to think that Such Parents that have Good hopes Concerning themselves Respect their Eternal State may upon Scripture Grounds have the Same hopes for their Chil= dren that die in Infancy, Especially if they die Baptiz’d; and this by what I have met with in Discourse and Reading of the great est and most Judicious of our Divines, yea I have no Reason to question but that it is So of Divines in General, which I think
