A104. Letter to Thomas Foxcroft, November 21, 1749 104.JE-T.Foxcroft 11-21-49 Jonathan Edwards

A104. JE to Foxcroft, 11/21/49. BRBL, f. 1290.

Northampton Novem 21. 1749.

Rev & honoured Sir,

I condole you on the awful Breach God has made in your Family, Since I last Saw you, in the death of Mrs Foxcroft. By what I have heard of the the amiable Qualities & Endowments she was possessed of, your Loss, & the Loss of your whole Family must needs be exceeding great. The Breach made in a Family by the death of such an one, in such a Place and Relation, must be great, & as it were wide as the Sea. Yet those excellent Qualifications that render your Loss very great, must in a peculiar manner yi afford you Comfort in your Affliction; as They give you confidence that death has been an happy Change to Her, and that now she is in blissful Circumstances, tho' she be taken from you, & her Body moulders in the dark & silent Grave. so that altho' your sorrow be great, yet you mourn not for Her, but for your self; And in mourning for your self, as left by her alone in this evil world, your sorrow has this alleviation, or rather blessed Consolation attending it, that your separation from her is not final & perpetual; for those that sleep in Jesus will God will bring with Him; And then you shall again meet her in joyful Circumstances, & be caught up together with her to be meet the Lord in the Air, and shall both together be ever with the Lord. In the mean Time, dear sir, may you have much of the Presence of her G & your God marginal & saviour, who is all in all; and who is the unfailing Fountain of all that is amiable and comfortable & profitable in the most desireable of Creatures.

My Afflictions & Troubles are also very Great; Tho' of another Kind. The Difficulties in this Church relating to my opinion about the Qualifications of Communicants are come to great Height indeed. Our Circumstances are not easily conceived by those that are at a distance. On the Sabbath in Octob. 15 15 I proposed that a day should be set apart by the Church for solemn Fasting & Prayer, to seek to God for his direction & help under our present difficulties, and that the services of the day should be carried on by the neighbouring ministers; which I proposed, as supposing that their services would be more acceptable than mine: and it seemed to be unanimously agreed to. And there being several Persons in the Town that desired to come into the Church, who are hopefully converted, and stood ready to make a Profession of Godliness, who had hitherto been kept out because a Committee of the Church had refused to consent That They should make any publick Profession of Godliness, & co & chose rather that They should not come in at all, than come in in that way; I proposed that the neighbouring ministers that assisted at the Fast should be applied to for advice in that matter to see if They could not direct us marginal into some Method wherein These Persons might be admitted consistent with a good Conscience in both Pastor & People. This was objected against. then it was proposed by one of the Brethren that these ministers should be applied to for Advice with Respect to a Method of the Churches being informed of the Reasons of my opinion; because it was said the People had not generally read my Book, nor were like to; & that great Part of the Church were never like to be informed Unless they were directed into some other way by ministers. There was then considerable discourse of the Peoples hearing my Reasons delivered from the Pulpit, seeing it was confessed that a very great [part] of the Church had not read, [JE's om.] & were not like to read my Book. But this also was objected against, & nothing was determined. Therefore the Meeting was adjourned 'till the next Sabbath for further consideration of these Matters; & the Fast was appointed to be on the Thursday following that Sabbath. The People went away, as appear'd, many of Them in a great Ferment of Mind. On Monday, the next day, a Considerable number of Them met together, & subscribed a writing, directed to the Committee of the Parish or Precinct, demanding a Precinct Meeting, the next Thursday, to take into consideration my Opinion respecting Qualifications of marginal Members of the Church; and to apply to me, and desire me to recede from my Principles; and if I refused, to see if the Precinct would not use Endeavours for a separation between the Pastor & People. Accordingly the Precinct Meeting was warned and Convened on the next Thursday. But some At the meeting it was moved and insisted upon by (I suppose) two or three Persons that I should be desired to it should be put to vote whether I should be desired to deliver the Reasons of my Opinion from the Pulpit, and it pass'd in the negative. Then some of the more considerate objected against the Precincts proceeding to the Business for which the meeting was warned before the Fast, which we had appointed to seek direction & help from God, accordingly the meeting was adjourned for a Fortnight.

The next Sabbath the Church was stayed according as had been determined the Sabbath before; & we entered on some further discourse on things that had been discoursed on the sabbath before; And it was in the first Place put to vote whether the Church would consent that such amongst as were able & willing to make a publick Profession of Godliness should be admitted into the Church with such a Profession fo (rather than not be admitted at all) for the present, 'till our unhappy Controversy could be brought to an Issue: and there was but a very few votes for it. Then it was put to vote whether the neighbouring ministers that were expected at the Fast should marginal not be sought to for their Advice in this matter, to see if they could not find out a way that these Persons whom all agreed to be qualified, should be admitted in some way that might be safe & satisfying to both sides. for this also there was but very few votes. Then it was put to vote whether the Church would not consent to hear the Reasons of my Opinion from the Pulpit on Lectures appointed for that End (for They would look upon it Profaneness to hear 'em on the sabbath) seeing it seemed to be a thing beyond dispute, that a great Part of the Church had not been informed, nor were like to be informed of my Reasons any other way. But this also pass'd in the negative. Then I proposed that it should be refer'd to the neighbouring ministers whether It was not reasonable that I should be thus heard, all things considered. very much was said against it, [-] with much warmth, and some injurious Reflections; and when it was put to vote and passed in the negative.1

The next Thursday we had our Fast. And the Thursday following Novem 2. The Precinct met according to adjournment, and Chose a Committee of nine men to consider what Course to take, and confer with me, & make Report to the next meeting; & then adjourned Themselves for a week. The Committee chosen were many of them some of those that were most violently engaged in this Controversy. The marginal next day this Committee came to me, and told me that They had all agreed to make me this Proposal or offer; which if I would consent to, they would endeavour to bring the Precinct to yield to; viz. that the People should hear me preach in defence of my Opinion, provided I would first draw out my sermons at Len each sermon at Length, in a legible Character, & give it to Them, and give Them opportunity to carry it to some minister, that He might see it, & prepare an Answer to it, before I delivered it; and that then I might deliver it, provided I would consent that He should deliver his Answer immediately after it. I told Them that at present I could not think it to be my duty to comply with this Proposal, unless it were also provided that I should also see the discourses of my Antagonist, as He was to see mine. I then told Them what I thought of the Irregularity of the Precinct's taking this Ecclesiastical affair into their Hands. but they strenuously justified it. And after long continued then the Committee went away without concluding any Thing, after long continued uncomfortable disputing, and some harsh Reflections & injurious Charges. And several of Them afterwards did very industriously endeavour to make me appear in the highest degree ridiculous by much misrepresenting my talk at that Time. --This Committee met afterwards at another House, by Themselves; and drew up a Report, Wherein they declare their O it to be their Opinion that the Precinct should endeavour that there should be meeting of the Church to see if the Church would not apply to some of the marginal neighbouring ministers, for their Advice what was to be done to Issue the dispute between me & the Precinct, respecting the Admission of members into the Church.

On Nov. 9. the Precinct met to receive their Report & having understood what the report was, I sent a Letter to the Precinct, informing Them that if I was not against warning a Church meeting if They desired it, to consider of proper measures to be taken secure & promote the Interest of Religion & the Churches wellfare under its present Circumstances; But did not Think it was proper for me to call the Church together, to take measures to issue disputes of the Precinct, as was proposed ; and that I did not think the Church should be limited to an or directed by the Precinct in the management of its affairs. But my Letter was taken in very ill Part, and wholly disregarded. They therefore Accepted the Report of the Committee, & appointed two of the deacons to come to me to desire a Church meeting, 1 to determine a vote whether the Church were of my mind with Respect to Qualifications &c. 2 To see if the Church will apply to some neighbouring ministers for Advice what Course to Take. & then proceeded to add ten more to the Precinct Committee, but without g giving them any Instructions, or appointing Them any Business; B which I suppose was an oversight. But I conclude their design that was that this large Committee of ni 19 should in general take care of their affairs, & see that they were managed effectually. Then They adjourned their meeting for four weeks.

The next day, the two deacons came to me with a Copy of the vote of the Precinct, desiring me to warn a Church meeting &c as aforesaid.

The sabbath following I warned a Church meeting to be the next day, to consider what Course ought to be taken by this Church under its present difficulties, with Respect to the Admission of members into the Church. The Church accordingly met. The meeting was open'd by Prayer. Then one of the Principal Brethren shew'd Himself greatly displeased that I had used such general Terms in my warning of the meeting, and had not warned it in such Terms as the Precinct had directed me; and uttered several very injurious Reflections & Charges; and declared that If I would not warn a Church meeting for the Purposes the Precinct directed, They would warn Them Themselves2. After I had given some Reasons of my Conduct, I told the Church that I thought the state of the Church did greatly demand the assistance of a Council; and that we needed the advice of a large Council, that should look into our Circumstances and give us Advice in general, what we should do for our Peace and wellfare; and insisted that I should be allowed to nominate half the Council. After much debate upon it the meeting was adjourned for a week; and a Committee of five Persons chosen to consider of the Matter, & confer upon it with the Pastor, & report their Opinion at the next Meeting. The Committee ag all excepting one agreed to my Proposal of a Council mutually marginal chosen.

Yesterday the Church met according to adjournment, and after Prayer, the Report of the Committee was read; & then the dissenting Committee-man got up, & gave the Reasons why he did not agree to it, with some very reproachful charges on me. And suggested that there was some snare laid to entrap The People in my Proposal of a Council mutually Chosen. which seemed to alarm the People and They greatly insisted that the Council should all be of neighbouring ministers. and a very uncomfortable meeting we had. after long debating They a determined to add ten to the Church Committee & Chosen the warmest spirited men They could find, & would accept no others: when men of Calm Tempers were nominated, They would not vote Them. this Committee are further to consider what to do, and the meeting is adjourned for a Fortnight; after They had voted That the sacrament of the Lords Supper should be discontinued, & no more administer'd during the Continuation of the present Controversy.

Thus Things have gone on. The Alienation of the People is exceeding great. They have had, not a little to increase the Flame from some Gentlemen abroad; and particularly from Col. Wms of Weathersfield & Mr Ashley of deerfield. A great Part of my People seem to be industriously engaged to reproach me at Home & abroad; marginal & represent all that I say & do in the darkest Colours. and seek my separation from 'em very much as a man would strive to gain his Point, in a great Law-Suit. And indeed I have not the least Expectation of continuing their minister; tho' I believe my People think I am struggling to my utmost for it. I think the People seem to be in awful Circumstances, and in the high way to Ruin, for the Present & future Generations; And I durst not leave them without first using all proper Endeavours that They may be saved from Ruin, in the Use of suitable means, As by the Advice of an able & proper Council &c. And then besides, since I am in so many Respects reflected upon and reproach'd for my Conduct, I t & so many misrepresentations are continually carried abroad in the Countrey, I think it greatly concerns me, before I leave this People that our Affairs should be looked into & Judged of by an Impartial Council. And indeed I greatly need the Advice of such a Council to know how to conduct my self in so difficult a situation. I earnestly desire your Prayers, & desire you would lay my Case before other ministers, as that you think are friendlily disposed, & desire their Prayers for me, that I may be have Gods Presence & be guided by his Counsel in these great trials marginal w & Temptations. And wish you would write me your Thoughts.

And I desire you would lend me Mr Baxter, & Langley & any other3 good Book you have on this Controversy, & commit Them to the Care of Col. dwight; who will convey Them safely.

And There is another affair I would say something of, and that is concerning the Indians

& stockbridge4. ---I wish that the Commissioners would now take Care that there may be a man sent Them of sound Principles, & a pious Character. There has been so much contributed towards an Indian school there, that if the affair may be under the Care of a Missionary of Good Character, there there seems to be the best door for gradually propagating the Gospel among the Indians that is open'd at Present. If the school flourishes 'tis very likely some of the six nations would send their Children there to be instructed, & that the good Effects would be very extensive : I hope sir you will accept these Hints with Candour and ever remember with Christian Charity & Compassion before God,

your Respectful son & ser obliged servt

Jonathan Edwards.
