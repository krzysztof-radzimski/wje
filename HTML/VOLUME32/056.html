A46. Letter to Elnathan Whitman, February 9, 1744 046.JE-E.Whitman 2-9-44 Jonathan Edwards

A046. JE to Elnathan Whitman, 2/9/44. Draft or copy. ANTS f. 1743, #2.

To Mr Whitman of Hartford N.H. Feb. 9. 1743, 4

Rev. & d. s.

Mr Potwine was here this week. & among other things that were the subjects of our Conversa. he gave me some account of the Circumstances of a number of Persons that had absented thems. from your meeting & had attended the p. w. at W.1 & desired my sentimts I told him I should give no J. of their partic. Case because I was not under a Capacity of Knowing it but gave him my thoughts of some things in general & on further reflecting on the matter am come to a determina. to express the same sentiments that I signified to him in a Letter to you I think not from any Inclination to meddle with other folks Business nor from any dis- position to Justify those pernicious principles tending to promote & fo- ment Censoriousness & division that have prevailed in many parts of the L& of late which I have ever born a Testimony a- gainst But as it has been a time wherein the state of things has been very extraordinary attended in some Respects with ge- neral Confusion & the Remedying the mischiefs that have been being an affair of general Concern & the proper methods to be taken in order to it being that about which there is a great di- versity of sentiments I thought it might not be amiss for me in general to express my Th. to you as a Fr. & Relation leaving it with you to make what use of them & lay what weight to them you see Cause

as to differences among Professing Xtians of opinion & Practice about things that appertain to Relig. & the w. of G. I am ready to think that you & I are agreed as to the general Principles of Lib. of C. & that mens using Coercive methods with their Neighb. to oblige them to a Conformity to their sentimts or way is in nothing so unreasonable as in the w. of G. because that is a B. wherein each P. acts for hims. with his Creatour & supream Judge as one Con- cerned for his own acceptance with him on which depends his own & not his neigh. & Et. Happiness & salvation from Everlasting Ruin & it is an affair wherein each m. is infinitely more concerned with thi his Creatour than he is with his neighbors & so I suppose it will be allowed that each man ought to be left to his own C. in what he Judges will be most accepta- ble to G. or what he supposes is the will of G. as to the Kind or manner or means of w. or the society of worshippers he should Join with in w. not but that a great abuse may be made of this doc. of Lib. of C. in the w. of G. I know that many are ready to Justify every thing in their own Conduct from this doc. & I don't suppose that mens pretence of C. is alwaies to be regarded marginal when made use of to Justif. their Changing the society of worshippers they Join with or the means of their w. or indeed the Kind & manner of their w. men may make this pretence sometimes with such Circumst. that they may be worthy of no Credit in what they pretend it may be so manif. from the nature & Circumstances of the Case & their own manner of Behaviour that it is not C. but Petulancy & malice & willfulness & Carnal views that Influence them . & theref. it seems to me evid. that when such Pleas are made Those that are Especially con- cerned with them as persons peculiarly obliged to take care of their souls have no other way to do but to Consider the nature & Circumstances of the Case & from thence to J. whether the Case be such as will admit of such a plea. or whether the nature of things will admit of such a sup. that the men act Con- scientiously in what they do considering all things that appert. to the Case. & in this I conceive many things are to be con- siderd & laid together . as the nature of that thing that is the subject of Controv. -- or wherein they diff. from others or have Changed their own Practice. The degree in which it is disputable or how it may be supposed liable to diversity of Opinions one way or the other as to its agreableness to the w. of G. & as to the Import. of it with Regard to mens salva. or souls Good The degree of Kn or Ign. of the persons the Advantages they have had for Informa. or the disadvantages they have been under & what has been in their Circumst. that might mislead their Judgm --- The Princip. that have been Instilled into them. The Instructions they have had from those that they have had an high opinion of for wisd. & Piety that might misguide the Judgm. of P. of real Honesty & sincerity & tender C. The Examp. of others the diversities of Opinions among ministers the General state of things in the L& The Charac- ter of the Persons thems. & the manner of their Behaviour in the partic. affair in debate Now sir I imagine with Regard to those P. that have gone from you to W. that however you look upon their Behav.r herein as very disorderly yet if you suppose (the case being considered with all its Circumstances) that there was any Room for Charity that it might be through Infirmity Ign. & mistakes of Judg. marginal so that they might be truly Conscientious in it i.e might Really believe it to be their d. & what G. required of em to do as they have done . you would by no means think that they ought to be proceeded with in the use of such means as are proper to be used with Contumacious offenders or those that are stub. & obst. in sc&alous vice or willfull wick. or that you would think it prop. to proceed with P. towards whom there is this Room left for Charity that Possibly they may be honest & truly Conscientious acting as P. afraid to offend G. so as to cut them off from the Cong. of the L. & cast em forth into the visi. K. of S. to be as H. & Pub.2

Now sir I would Pray that it may be Consid. whether it can positively be determ. when all things are Consid. with Respect to those P. that have Absented thems. from your Assembly that it could not be in their Case that that might really be their J. that it was their d. so to do & that G. req. it of them & that they should greatly exp. the wellf.3 of their own souls in attending no other p. w. but that in your Cong. I suppose those P. are not much versed in Casuistical div. They are of the Comm. P. whose Judgm. - in all nations & ages are ex- ceeding easily led & sway'd They are not very capable of viewing things in the Extent of their Conseq. & of Estimating things in their true weight & Imp. & you know d. sir the state that things have been in in the Countrey. you Know what opinions have lately Prevailed & have been maintain'd & propag. by those that th have been lifted up to H. in their reput. for piety & great Kn. in div. matters with a great part of the P. of N. E. I dont pretend to Know what has influenced these P. in partic. But I think under these Circumst. it would be no st. thing if great numbers of the Comm. P. in the C that are really Conscientious & concerned to be accepted with G. & to take the best Course for their souls Good should really think in their H. that G. req. em to attend the ministry of these that are called New L. ministers & that it would be dangerous to their s. & what G. approved not of ordinarily to attend the ministry of others yea I should think it strange if it were otherwise It ought to be Consid. marginal how public Controv. & a great & general Cry in matters of Relig. strongly influences the minds of mult. of the Com. P. how it blinds their minds & wonderfully misleads their J. & Xtian Rules & the Example of the Apostles most certainly requires that great allowances be made in such Cases. and partic. the Examp. of the Ap. Paul with regard to great Numb. of Prof. Xtians in the Chh of Corinth who in a time of great & gen. Confu. in that Chh. through the evil Instruc. of Teachers that they admired that mis- lead & blinded their Jud. run into many & great disord. in their w. & woful schisms & div. among thems. & par- ticularly with regard to min. & with Reg. to the Ap. Paul hims. whom many of them seem for a time to have for- saken to follow others that set up thems. in Op. to him tho as he says he had been their F. who had beg. them through the Gosp.4 yet with how much gentleness does the Ap. treat them still acknowledging them as Br. and tho' he req. Chh. Censures to be used with Reg. to the Incestuous P. yet there is no shad. of the Ap. taking any such Course with those that had been mislead by those false Teachers or with any that had been guilty of these disord. except the f. T. thems. but as soon as they are brought off from following these false Ap. any longer he embraces 'em without further ado with all the Love & Tenderness of a F. bu- rying all their Censoriousness & schisms & disord. at the L. sup. -- & ill treatm. of him the extra. messenger of X to them & indeed the Ap. never so much as gave any direc. for the susp.5 of any one member from the L. sup. on account of these disord. or from any other part of the p. w. of G. but instead of that, gives em direc. how they shall go on better to attend the L. s. & other parts of w. & hims. without suspension or Interrup. goes on to call & treat them as beloved B. Ch.6 sanctif. in X J. called to be s. & praises G. on their behalf for the Gr. that is given them by X J.7 & often & abundantly ex- presses his Ch.8 towards them in innumerable Exp. that I might mention & nothing is more appa. than that marginal he don't treat them as those with respect to whom there lies a bar in the way of others treating them with the Charity that belongs to saints & good & H.9 members of the Xtian Chh. till the Bar be removed by a Chh. Process & indeed the insisting on a Chh Process with every memb. that has behaved hims. disorderly in such [-] like state of general Confusion is not a way to build up the Chh of G. (which is the end of Chh d.), but to pull it down it will not be the way to Cure a diseased member but to bring a disease on the whole body

I am not alone in these sentim. But I have reason to think that Col. St.10 from the Conv. I have had with him is in the like way of think. There came hither the last fall a couple of Sc.11 belonging to the C. at N. H. that had been M. of Mr N. Chh.12 but had left it and had incorporated with the sep. Chh & entred into Cov. with them when that Chh was embod. This was looked upon as a Crime that ought not to be passd over by Mr N. & the R.13 They manif. thems. willing to Return to Mr N. meet ing . but a p. Confes. was Req. of em in the m. H.14 acordingly each of em had off. a Confes. but it was not thought suff. but it was req. of them that they should add some things that they thought hard of & they consulting me about it I acq. Col. St. with the affair & desired his thoughts He said he looked upon it unreas. to req. any Confes. at all & that Considering the general st. of Confus. that had been & the Instruc. & Examp. these y. m.15 had had it might well be looked upon enough that they now alter their Practice & Returned again to Mr N. meeting not that you are obliged to think as Col. S. does yet I think Consid. his Charact. & Rela. his J. may well be of so much weight as to engage you the more to attend to & weigh the Reasons he gives marginal

The Objections that these Persons may have had against ordinarily attend. your M. may perhaps be very triv. but yet I app.16 that through Infirm. the Case may be so with truly Honest & Conscien. Xtians that triv. things may have great w.17 in their Consc. so as to have fast hold of them till they are better En- lightend as in the former time of the Count. it with18 with Resp. to Controv. between Presb. & Cong -- It was I as I have heard in those days real matter of Quest. with some whether a Presb. living & dying so could be saved some Irish P.19 that have lived with us have desired B. for their Ch.20 that yet lived in neglect of the ord. of the L. s. because of a diff. in some triv. Cir. of the admin. --- from the me- thod of the Chh. of Sc. This matter being discoursed of it was thought by Col: S. in partic. that their neglect ought to [be]21 born with and they ought to be looked upon as Xtians and their Ch. recieved to B. because however triv. the Founda. of their scruples were yet through Ign. they might be Hon. & Consc. in them

As to the Chh cov. that these P. have Entred into wherein they have obliged thems. ord. to Join in the W. of that Ch. I suppose none inter- pret the Promises of Ch. Cov. in such a sense as to exclude all Reserves of Lib. in case of an altera. of the J. in the affairs of Consc. & Relig. in one Respect or another as if a P. after incorp. with a Cong. Chh. should become a Consc. Chh man22 or Anabap. or should by any Chh Change of J. come to think the means or manner of w. unlawf. & so in other Resp. that might be mention'd & if it be so that these P. in some of their talk & Behav. have manif. a Contentious froward marginal sp. at the Time of their withdrawing from your Chh. I pre confess this gives greater Ground of suspicion of the sincerity [of]23 their Profess. of Consc. -- yet as to this I humbly concieve allowances must be made It must be consid. that it is possible that Persons in an affair of this nature in the thing it self may be Consc. & yet in the Course of the managemt of it may be guilty of very Corrupt mixtures of Passion & may many Evil disp. as indeed is Commonly the Case with men in long Controv/ of whatever nature & even with Conscientious men . Amd theref it appears to me that if P. in such a case are not obstin. in what is amiss in them in this Resp. & don't stand to Justif. their frowardness & unXtian speeches they notwithstanding may deserve Credit when they profess thems. Consci- entious in the Affair in General .

Thus dear sir I have freely sig. to you some of my th. with regard to some of the Concerns of this diff. day which prove a trouble to you not with any aim at Controuling you I am sensib that you & not I are the Past. of the 2d Ch. of H I only desire you would impartially consid the Rea- sons I have offered . Begging of X our Comm. L. that he would direct you in your th. & pract. to that which will be aceptable in his sight I am
