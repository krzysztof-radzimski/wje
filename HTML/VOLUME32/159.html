A139. Letter to Andrew Oliver (Draft), February 18, 1752 139.JE-A.Oliver 2-18-52 Jonathan Edwards

A139. JE to Andrew Oliver, 2/18/52. Draft. ANTS, f. 1752, #6-7. Partly written on back of Yale College commencement broadside for 1750.

To And. Oliver Esq. Feb. 18. 52.

In my Letter that I wrote to Your Honour he last fall & also Latter End of Last after the Interview with the Mohawk Chiefs the last August I earnestly & also in my Letter to Mr. speaker Hubbard I earnestly insisted on the necessity of some Persons being on the spot or very near appointed by the Commissrs & Gen. assembly. a sort of Curatours to have the immediate Care & Inspection & Care of our affairs -& in those Lett & concerning this affair I wrote That used those words " What renders it more necessary that things here should be under the Care immediate Care of trustees is the misunderstanding & Jealousies that here subsist between some of the Chief of the Pres. English i Present English Inhabitants of the Town which is one of our greatest Calamities. Things on this account do much more need Constant Inspection & theref the Gentlemen intrusted ought to be such are as are perfectly impartial no way interested in or related to those Contending Parties." In I then re commended Col. d. of Brookfield. But then knew not of what now appears that He would be the furthest of any Gentleman whatsoever of from having that most necessary Qualification I mentioned by his being about like to be the son-in-Law of one of these Contending Parties. which match by his marrying his daugher Mrs sergt. which match I there now appears the is Reason (as now abundantly appear if not Reason to apprehend (from what already appears plainly appears) will be extremely hurtful Conseq. to the impor tant affairs of stockbridge. This There was the last year a very formal & solemn Pacification between Col. W. & deac. Woodbridg. [-] with solemn Promises made by Col. W. that He would henceforward live quietly & friendlily with deac. W & no more attem speak ill of him or attempt any thing against him or in any wise molest Him but this new alliance. with with the News of Col. wof W---ld being yet alive and & his daughters being the mistress of the female school & & Col. W. of W-d's being yet alive & He [-] hims. being [-] now joined with the Commissioners in the Care of that school have so el elevated Him that He has now very much thrown off all appearances of Peace with deac deac. W. & in a very open manner revived the misunderstanding with Him by strangely affronting Him in a very open manner. And there appears of late a very sudden & strange alteration in Col. d. the temper & Conduct of Col. d. who has had ever since the appointmt of the Committee by the general assembly the sole managemt of all our affairs so that nothing has been done but He is the doer of it as for Col. P. he is hardly to be seen Here he stays at Home: & this is much the Case with Capt. Ashly & when He is here he leaves things wholly with Brig. d. I know not what the late alt great alteration in Him can be owing to unless it [be] female Counsels together with the Influ ences of his He now appears to approve that which Col. The Indians here have a de very ill opinion of Col. W. & the deepest Preju dice against Him. He having often molested em with Respect to their Lands & other affairs and as they think done very unjustly to 'em & Mad. s--t Partakes of no small degree of the same Prejudice. This has been app appeared by things they have said of her at my House of their own accord without any Body saying any thing to lead em to it They say that Mr. s. did very well till He married her but after h afterwards there was a great Alteration of in Him & He became quite another man They speak of her as Proud & Cove tous & not to be trusted There is affection of the Indians in the latter part of his Life this among other Evid. of their Prejudice against M. s that tho great Pains have been taken she to get Chil. for her Boarding female school. she has not been able to get above 4. Chil. 3. stockbridge Indians & 1 Mohawk. And there are already have already been loud Complaints by which justly or not I will not determine But if one of the Parents of their T her not it shews the Effect of their Prejudice and what Treatmt of her Children it may be expected will be the Effects of it. marginal

Mrs. sergt is undoubtedly a woman of very Considerable Abilities and her natural Temper disposes her much more to dominion than subjection she loves mu to have a Hand in the managemt of publick affairs & she had a vast Ascendant over Mr. sergeant & it to act appearance is but to have as great over Col. d. and she had very much the disposal of Mr. Hollis' monies. at least in she made bold with them in some Instances to dispose of em according to her own discretion in some In stances. as to the Justice of her manner of disposing Them I will not Assert that she is not [-] at all wronged in Representations that are made by some that know but this is a witho past doubt with me that she made more bold with his money than I should have dared to have done & with the publick Benefactions of some others than I should have dared to have done. or if she is wronged in the Representations that are made I could wish that they

And I could wish that the manner of spending Mr. Hollis's money since Mr. sergeants death by another Person into whose Hands They have fallen were thoroughly known. The longer I continue here & the more insight I get into the managemt of such affairs These affairs in stockbridge the more am I filled with astonishmt. when one Considers the noble sp. of Charity Mr. Hollis has shewn & the Large Benefacti ons that have been made by hi Remittances [which] have been made by Him to [JE om.]marginal this Place & to how little Good Purpose at least of late especially of late & how ignorant of He is of the true state of his affairs here & how far his conceptions of them probably are from the Truth it is enough to make one sick. But was informed by one of the late Committee from another of the same Committee that had seen one of them that He was extremely dissatisfied with it. and as to th Mr Hollis's Boys or those that He has pretended to teach & maintain at Mr. Hollis's Cost Mr. Hollis's T the particular design that Mr. Hollis had in view by his in his most generous Benefaction & which he supposes is pursued to good & Happy I mean the Educating the Boys that Capt. Kellogg has pretended to teach & maintain at his Cost T this money has been entirely lost at least it has been so if his way of managemt before was as it has been since I have been here I dont mean that his scholars have learned nothing at all by Him. But I mean they have had no better Education nor near so good as they might and probably m would have had without marginal any of this Cost. so that in Effect I believe Mr. Hollis's money has rather prevented & Clog'd his great design & prevented it. Capt. Kellogg has enticed away a number of stockbridge Boys from deac. Woodbridges school where they were regularly taught & governed Instructed & governed & taught good manners for the sake of making up [--] number of Mr. Hollis Boys by which he might draw his Pay for the Boarding school where they were much more neglected & have have grown more wild & disorderly & vicious. which is a mere Cheat to Mr. Hollis. Woodbridge taught were neglected. & then He has been absent near half his time since the begin ning of Last August leaving the Care of his Boys to one body or the other. And none The Irregularities and disorderly manner of [-] Capt. Kelloggs managemt of the B Boys has been a great what the Mohawks have found Great fault with & has been a chief discouragemt to em

Kellog is there still & not calling th the Boys He formerly had his Boys still & looking upon hims Them as under his Care & looking on hims. a superintendent with Respect to them than has been no & whether He intends to draw the Mr. Hollis's pay others can judge as well as I. He has nothing to do there as a master or teacher. tho He may t still take some Care of their Clothing & in some measure do the Part of a steward. In the way we are in we are like to have sup superintendents multiplied here drawing great salaries & [ ] & expecting great honours & having but little Business Capt Kellogg looks on Hims. a sort of su a superintendent of the whole school & mad. s. expects to be s. of the Female school.

Brig. d. had his Eyes opend as open to see Capt Kellogs defects as any Body & used to be abundant in speaking of his useless [-] ness his exceeding unfitness for the Business he he was in The disorder & nastiness in which things were kept under his Care & how that it was high time He was gone &c &cbut now hi suddenly his Tone is turned. Col. W. & Mrs. s. were alwaies in the same Box with Capt. Kellog & have ever appeared his great Friend. But still Col. Pynchons Eyes are open who tho He has been but little here can inform Your Honour something of these Things if H enquired of [he] should be enquired of. marginal

The managemt has been something strange with Respect to Mr. Hawley. Before He marginal Came Representating most [ ] were carried Representations were carried to him from some misrepresentating of things tending greatly to discourage his meddling at all with the Business. for what Reason I know not unless because it was supposed He was one of my Nominating & so it was supposed he would he would would naturally fall under my Influence & not be governed by them. when Col. d. came the Condu which was soon after Mr. Hawley [--] had begun his school the Conduct was as if He was at a loss whether to smile or Frown upon it whether to discourage or en& it was given out by some that are Conversant with Col. W & courage H & if it had not been for his daughter that it was doubtful whether Mr. Hawley would not soon Mr. Hawley's courage, prudence, and steadiness be removed. of temper He w in so difficult a situation He would undoubtedly have been laid under some lasting unsp unspeake & I feel still it will be the Endeavour of some to make his able disadvantages. finall it Just bework difficult to that End that they may discourage Him fore Col. d. went away He discourses unless they can draw Him over to such an attachment to em with Mr. Hawley in Capt. Kelloggs Presence. & [ ] him Come as Mr. Hawley Hims. informs me and warns Him [ ] Him to Mr. Hawley to be use a great deal of Caution on becoming to any [ ] & not to have his dependency too much on me & to be very Cautious in [ ] taking advice of me & offeres his own friendship & Assistance tells Him He is able that he might see Cause hereafter to alter his opinion of some Persons & assures him that he is nothing able to support him & assist Him than I two to one that He had Authority had Authority to direct him and order how he should conduct in the school. & that I had no Authority at all to intermeddle in his Concerns in the school & that when I introduced him into the school I did it without Authority that the Commissioners had no authority to order the in the affair but that the Authority was all vested in the Committee of the General Assembly & he had their Authority for they expected He should act for the Rest. & conti adds repeated Charges that He should not tell me what He had said by no means tell me of these Things that he had said to Him but without getting any promise from Mr. Hawley.

Now I Never Here appears a sudden wonderful Change in Col. d with Respect He has Constantly for many years professed the highest Respect for me esteem of me & Respect to me [ ] far beyond what I could with any modesty expect. He expressed an Esteem of me beyond any other Minister in N. England & has very lately expressed his great desires of Living under my ministry & I never have had one word with Him nor with Mad. sergt & Col. W. n or Capt Kellogg. Tho they greatly opposed my Coming to stockbridge before I came marginal But since have all manifested themselves to me well pleased with it tho I have heard They have said Contrary things behind my B Back. Mrs. Serg. has been proceeded to a great Extravagance in her testimonies of Esteem & Regard to my Face. tho from time to time I have found by what she has said to others her heart was not at all in it.

I suppose Col. W. of W---d has greatly Established the Character of his uncle & [ ] Cous. sergt in London & also the Cha and also that he has established the Confirmed my [ ] raised & Confirmed Mr. Hollis's good opinion of Capt. K. who has alwaies been his great Friend. And it may be expected that when Col. W. comes he will be exceeding Busy & active in our affairs agreable to his natural Temper. but I cant think his Influence on our affairs will be very happy. The society in London if they had any true view of the Case would never have appointed Mserg. to be the mis tress of the Girls school. & her own Father & near Kinsman the m Commi to receive & tee to Look after her & her Accounts. But things are like to [be] situated more preposterously still. if they according to the Course Things are in are as tis Expect viz. that Mad. sergt should be the mistress & her own own Husb. & if in Effect the whole Committee of the General Assembly to to receive her accounts make Representations to his Constituents & to [be the] ma [-] sole m & sovereign manager of that affair & all all other affairs here or rather this Power will be in Mad. sergts Hands with the here As he intends to settle here Family of the Williamses for her Counsel & her husband her Instrufor the sake of his wife's being the ment to execute her designs [-] mistress of the female school. as wish the Governmt of all our affairs are have to be in the Fam one Family of the Williams's tis the more Col. W. is a was that the of Go to Elisha W. Col. E. his daughter & Husband & I confess I have no hopes of seeing the affair Prosper if things are carried thus I expect nothing but perpetual [ ] or [ ] which probably wi & dissensions undermining and counterworking between one Family among the Inhabitants of the Town. which probably will & probably They will think themselves so strong that they will atsoon bring all to the Ground. I shall appear tempt nothing short of Purging the Town of all that stands in their no other than as they will soon go to work way. For my part, I have no reason to expect that they do thus [ ] because they [ ] except me from such an attempt the Issue to purge the Town of me as a nuisance as They well know & know already that I am not for & They doubtless & [ ] only Think me a great nuisance here their Turn. The affair at stockbridge being great & being much taken notice of in the Θ makes some People very fond of having a Hand therein and eager to engross the whole manage engross the managemt of it to Thems. and so impatient of every Person & thing that stands in their way. The Brigd is exceedingly engaged about his wifes being the mistress & therefore pushes that affair with great vigour. has already pur bought a great number of Boards & brought em on her Land to Build a school-House there. But marginal how imprudent will it be to set up a publick Building on a particular Persons Property.

I write these things Honoured sir because I am satisfied you have not heretofore been enlightend in the true state of Things as you ought to have been. It was my knowl. of some of these matters tho' but little in comparison that [-] occasioned me when last in Boston so earnestly to press the Commissioners frequently visiting the Place. I have been slow to speak. my disposition has been entirely to have suppress'd what I know that would be to the disadvantage of any of the People here. But I dare not hold my Tongue any longer. you doubtless will own sir that tis but doing you Justice for those that have been some body or other to Let you know the true state of things in a matter of such vast importance that is under your Care which you, being at so great a distance never can know but by the Information of some that live here. And I know of none you can more reasonably expect it from than from your missi the missionary you have have put here to have a the special care of the religious affairs of the care of the Interest of Relig. among the Indi ans. I did not intend to have intermeddled with the Affair of Mrs. S. being the mistress or to say any thing that should tend to hinder it. & therefore avoided every thing of such a Tendency in my Letter to sir WP.1 but being now applied to again by the honble Commsrs & seeing the Tendency of her Influence more & more Evidently appearing I thought this was the Time that G. called me to speak & that if I should hold my Tongue now I should perhaps Lay a Foundation for Great [--] uneasiness of Conscience to my Conscience all my Life after when I might groan under a contin the view of the Continued[--] in C Consequences of any silence & when it might be too late to speak.
