A114. Letter to Thomas Gillespie, April 2, 1750 114.JE-T.Gillespie 4-2-50 Jonathan Edwards

A114. JE to Thomas Gillespie, 4/2/50. ANTS, f. 1750, #2. Printed in Edinburgh Quarterly Magazine 1 (1798).

Northampton April. 2. 1750.

Rev. & dear Sir,

I received your Favour of Septem. 19. 1748. the last summer. and would now heartily thank you for it. I suppose it might come in the same ship with Letters I had from my other Correspondents in Scotland, which I answered the last summer ; But it did not come to Hand till a long Time after most of the other and after I had finished and sent away my Answers to Them, and that Opportunity for Answering was past. I have had no Leisure or Opportunity to write any Letters to Scotland 'till now from that Time 'till now, By Reason of the my Peculiar & very extraordinary Circumstances, on Occasio Account of the Controversy that has arisen between me & my People concerning the Profession that ought to be made by Persons that come to Christian sacraments, which is likely speedily to Issue in a seperation between me & my Congregation. This Affair, in the Progress of it, has proved not only a Controversy between me & my People, but between me and a great Part of New-England ; There being many far & near that are warmly Engaged in it. This Affair has unavoidably engaged my Mind, & fill'd up my Time, and taken me off from other Things. --I need the Prayers of my Friends that God would be with me, and direct & assist me in such a Time of Trial, & mercifully order the Issue. As to the Epistolary Controversy, dear sir, between you & me, about Faith & doubting, I am sorry it should seem to be greater than it is, through misunderstanding of one anothers meaning, And that the real difference between us is so great as it is in some Part of the Con troversy.

As to the dispute about believing without spiritual Light or sight, I thought I express'd my meaning in my last Letter very plainly : But per I kept no Copy, and it might perhaps be owing to my dulness that I thought so. However I perceive I was not Understood. I cannot find out by any Thing you say to me on this Head, that we really differ in sentiments, but only in words. I acknowledge with you that "all are bound to be lieve the divine Testimony & trust in Christ, --and the want of spiritual Light or sight does not loose from the Obligation one is laid under by the divine Command to believe constantly on Christ and at all seasons, nor excuse Him in any degree for not believing; -Even when one wants the Influence and Grace of the spirit, still He is bound to believe : Ability is not the Rule of duty " I believe the obligation to believe lies on a Person at th who is remaining without spiritual Light or sight," or being in the darkness: No darkness, no blindness no carnality, or stupidity excuses him a moment from having as strong marginal & lively a faith & Love as ever was E exercised by the Apostle Paul. or rrenders it not sinful in Him that he is at that same moment without such a Faith & Love. And yet I believe it is absurd, and of very hurtful Consequence to urge Persons to believe in the dark, in the manner & in the sense in which many hundreds of done have done in America ; who plainly intend a believing strongly with such a sort of strong faith or great Confidence as is consistent with continuing still, even in the Time of these strong Acts of Faith, without spiritual Light, carnal, stupid, careless & senseless. Their doctrine evidently comes to this, both in sense & Effect, That it is a mans duty strongly to believe with a Lightless, sightless Faith, or to have a Confident but yet a blind, dark & stupid Faith. And such a Faith has indeed been promoted exceedingly by their doctrine ; and has prevailed with its dreadful Effects answerable to the Nature of the Cause. We have had, & have to this day, multitudes of such strong believers, whose bold, proud & stupid Confidence, attended with a very wicked Behaviour, has given the greatest wound to vital Religion the Cause of Truth & vital Religion that ever it suffered in America.

As To what follows, in your Letter, concerning A Persons believing Himself to be in a good Estate, Its being properly of the Nature of Faith ; In this there seems to be some real difference between us. But perhaps there would be none, if distinctness were well observed in the use of words. If by a mans believing that He is in a good Estate, be meant no more than his believing that he does believe in Christ, does love God, &cI think there is nothing of the nature of Faith in it. Because I knowing it or believing it depends on our own immediate sensation or Consciousness, & not divine Testimony. True believers, in the Hope they entertain of Salvation, make use of the following syllogism; He that whosoever believes shall be saved. I believe. Therefore &cAssenting to the major Proposition is properly of the nature of Faith; because the ground of my Assent to that is divine Testimony. But my Assent to the minor Proposition, I humbly conceive is not of the nature of faith, because that does not is not grounded on divine testimony, but my own Consciousness. There The testimony that is the proper Ground of Faith is in the word of God. Rom. 10. 17. Faith cometh by hearing, & Hearing by the word of God. There is such a Testimony given us in the word of God, that He that Believes shall be Saved ; But there is no such Testimony in the word of God as that such an Individual Person, in Scotland such a Town in Scotland or N. England, believes. There is such a Proposition in scripture as that Christ loves those marginal that love Him ; and therefore that every one is Bound to believe; and a firm believing it on divine Testimony is properly of the nature of Faith; and for any one to doubt of it is properly the heinous sin of unbelief : But there is no such proposition in scripture, or that is any Part of the Gospel of Christ, that such an Individual Person in Northampton Loves Christ. If I know I have Complacence in Christ, I know it the same way that I know I have Complacence in my Wife & Children; viz. by the Testimony of my own Heart, or inward Consciousness. Evangelical Faith has the Gospel of Christ for its Foundation ; But that I love Christ is a Proposition not contained in the Gospel of Christ.

And therefore, that we mayn't dispute in the dark, 'tis necessary that we we well explain what we mean by a Persons believing He is in a good Estate. If thereby we mean only believing the minor of the foregoing syllogism. I believe, or I love God, 'Tis not of the nature of faith; But if by a man's believing Himself to be in a good Estate, be under stood not only the minor, but the Consequence. Therefore I shall be saved, or Therefore God will never leave nor forsake me, then a mans believing his good Estate partakes of the nature of Faith ; for these Consequences depend on divine Testimony in the word of God & Gospel of Jesus Christ. Yea I would observe farther, that a man's judging of the Nature h the Faith or Love he finds in Himself, whether that sort of Faith which He finds, be saving, may depend on his Reliance Reliance on scripture Rules & marks, which are divine Testimonies But his judging that He has those Individual inward Ex arises Acts of understanding & Exercises of Heart that he has, depends on Con inward sensation, & not on the any Testimony of the word of God: The Knowing present Acts depends on immediate Consciousness, & the knowing past acts depends on Memory. And therefore the Fulness of my satis faction that I have now have such an Inward Act or Exercise of mind, depends on the Strength of the Sensation; and my satisfaction that I have had heretofore, depends on the Clearness of my memory, and not on the strength of my Reliance on any divine Testimony. And so my doubting whether I have or have had such individual inward acts is not of the nature of unbelief ; tho' it may arise from unbe lief indirectly ; because if [-] I had had more faith, the Actings of it would have been more sensible, and the memory of them more clear, & so I should have been better Satisfied that I had them.

God seems to have given Abraham's servant a Revelation that The damsel in whom He found such [-] marks, should be Isaacs wife viz. coming to draw water, to with a Pitcher to that well, Her readiness to give Him & his Camels drink &c. should be Isaacs wife. And therefore his Assenting to this was of the nature of Faith, having divine Testimony for its foundation. But his believing that Rebekah was the damsel that had those Individual marks, marginal his knowing that she came to draw water, & that she let down her Pitcher &c. was not of the nature of Faith ; His knowing this was not from divine Testimony, but from the Testimony of his own senses.

you speak of a saints doubting of his good Estate, as "Part of unbelief," & the opposite of Faith considered in its full compass & Latitude, as one Branch of unbelief, one Ingredient in Unbelief;" And of Assurance of a mans good Estate, as "one thing that belongs to the Exercise of Faith;" I don't know whether I take your meaning in these Expressions ; If you mean that a Persons believing himself to be in a good Estate is one thing that Appertains to the Essence of saving Faith, or that saving Faith in all that belongs to its Essence, yea & Perfection, can't be without it I must humbly ask leave to differ from you. That it a believing that I am in a good Estate is no Part or Ingredient in the Essence of saving Faith, is evident by this ; That The Essence of saving Faith must be compleat in me before it can be true that I am in a good. If I han't as yet acted faith, yea if there be any thing wanting in me to make up the Essence of saving Faith, then I am not as yet in a state of salvation, and therefore can have no ground to believe that I am so. Every thing that belongs to the Essence of saving Faith is prior in the Order of Nature to a mans being in a state of salvation, Because 'tis saving Faith that brings him into such a state : And therefore believing that He is in such a state, can't be one Thing that is Essential or necessary in order to his being in such a state ; for that would imply a Contradiction ; It would be to suppose a mans believing that He is in a good Estate to be prior in the order of nature to his being in a good Estate. But a Thing can't be both Prior & Posterior, Antecedent & Consequent, with Regard to the very same Thing. The Real Truth of a Proposition is in the order of nature first, before its being believed to be true. But 'till a man has already all that belongs to the Essence of saving Faith, that Proposition That He is in a good Estate, is not as yet true. All the Propositions contained in the Gospel, all divine Testimonies that we have in Gods word, are true already, are already laid for a Foundation for faith, and were laid long ago ; But that Proposition I am in a good Estate, not being one of them, is not true 'till I have first believed: & therefore this Proposition can't be known to be true ti believed to be true 'till saving faith be first compleat Therefore the Compleatness of the act of saving Faith won't make it take in a beliefve of this Proposition. nor will the strength or Perfection of the Act cause it to marginal imply it. If a man in his first act of Faith has ever so great a Conviction of Gods sufficiency and Faithfulness, & let his Reliance on the divine Testimony in the Gospel be never so strong & perfect, all will have tendency to make Him believe, this proposition I am in a good Estate, to be true, 'till it be true; which it is not till the first act of Faith is compleat, & has made it true : -H A belief of divine Testimony in the first Act of Faith may be to any assignable degree of strength & Perfection, without believing that Proposition; for there is no such divine Testimony then extant; nor is there is [sic] any such Truth extant, but in Consequence of the first act of faith. Therefore, (as I said) saving Faith may be with all that belongs to its Essence, & that in the highest Perfection, without implying a belief of my own good Estate. -I do not say it can be without having this immediate Effect; But 'tis rather the Effect of faith, than a Part, Branch, or Ingredient of Faith. And so I don't dispute whether a mans doubting of his good Estate may be a Consequence of unbelief (I doubt not but it is so in those who are in a good Estate) Because if men had the Exercise of Faith in such a degree as They ought to have, It could not but be very sensible & plain that They had it. But yet I think this doubting of a good Estate is entirely a different Thing from the sin of unbelief it self, & has nothing of the nature of unbelief in it ; i.e if we take doubting ones good Estate in the sense in which I have before explained it, viz. for doubting whether I have such individual Principles & Acts in my soul. Take it in a complex sense, & it may have the sin of unbelief in it : As thus; If, altho' I doubt not I have such & such Qualifications, I yet doubt of those Consequences for which I have divine Testimony as if when a Person that doubts not that he loves Christ, yet doubts whether He shall receive a Crown of Life. The doubting of this Consequence is properly the sin of unbelief.

You say, dear Sir, " The Holy Ghost requires us to believe the Reality of his work in us in all its Parts just as it is." & a little before, " The Believer his doubting whether or not He has Faith is sinful, because it is belying the Holy Ghost, denying his work in Him ; so there is no sin to which that doubting can so properly be reduced as Unbelief."

Here I would ask leave thus to express my Thoughts in a diversity from yours. --I think if it be allowed to be sinful for a Believer to doubt whether he has faith such That doubting is not the sin of unbelief on any such account as you mention, viz. as belying marginal or denying any Testimony of the Holy Ghost. There is difference between doubting of a the being of some word of the Holy Ghost, and doubting denying the Testimony of the Holy Ghost. as there is a difference between doubting concerning some other words of God and denying the Testimony of God. 'Tis the work of God to give a man great Natural Abilities; And if we suppose God requires such a man to believe the Reality of his work, in all its Parts, just as it is; and so that 'tis sinful for Him to at all to doubt of his Natural Abilities being just as good as They are; yet this is no belying any Testimony of God (tho' it be doubting of a work of God) and so is diverse from the sin of unbelief. So if we suppose a very eminent Saint is to blame in doubting whether He has so much Grace as He really has, He indeed dont believe the Reality of Gods work in Him, in all its Parts, just as it is ; Yet He is not therein guilty of the sin of unbelief, against any Testimony of God, any more than the other.

I acknowledge that a true saint, in a carnal & careless frame, to doubt of his good Estate, is sinful, as it were mediately & indirectly, as the Cause of it is sinful, viz. the Lowness & Insensibility of the Actings of Grace in him, and the Prevalence of carnality & stupidity. 'Tis sinful to be without Assurance, or (as we say) 'tis his own Fault, he sinfully deprives Himself of it or foregoes it. As a servants being without his Tools is his sin, when He has carelessly lost 'em, or as 'tis his sin to be without strength of Body, or without the sight of his Eyes, when He has deprived Himself of These by Intemperance ; not that weakness or blindness of Body in their own Nature are sin, for they are Qualities in of the Body & not of the mind, the subject in which sin is inherent. ---'Tis indirectly the duty of a true saint alwaies to rejoyce in the Light of God's Countenance, because sin is the Cause of his being without this Joy at any Time And therefore it was indirectly david's sin that He was not rejoycing in the Light of Gods Countenance, at that Time when He was in the Bed of Adultery with Bathsheba. But yet 'tis not directly a believers duty to rejoyce in the Light of God's Countenance when God hides his Face, But it rather then becomes Him to be troubled and to mourn: so there are perhaps many other Priviledges of saints that are their duty indirectly, & the want of 'em is sinful not simply but complexly marginal Considered. of this Kind I take the want of Assurance to be of ones good Estate to be.

I think no words of mine either in my Book or Letter implied that a Persons deliverance from a bad frame don't begin with renewed Acts of Faith or Trusting in God : If They did, They implied what I never intended. doubtless if a Saint comes out of an ill frame, where in Grace is asleep it mus & inactive, it must be by renewed actings of Grace : 'Tis very plainly impossible That Grace should begin to cease to be inactive in any other way than by its beginning to be active : It must begin with the renewed actings of some Grace or other : And I know nothing that I have said to the Contrary, but that the Grace that shall first begin sensibly to revive shall be Faith, and that this shall lead the way to the renewed acting of all other Grace, and to the farther Acting of Faith it self. But a Persons coming out of a carnal careless dead frame, by the reviving of Grace in his soul, is quite another Thing from a saints having a strong Faith strong Hope exercise of Faith, or strong hope, or strong Exercise of any other Grace, while yet remaining in a carnal Careless dead frame ; or in other words, in a Frame wherein Grace is so far from being in strong Exercise, that tis asleep & in a great measure without Exercise.

There is a holy Hope, a truly Christian Hope, that the scripture speaks of, that is reckon'd among the Graces of the spirit : And I think I should never desire or seek any other Hope but such an one ; for I believe no other Hope has any holy or good Tendency. Therefore this Hope, this Grace of Hope only, can properly be called a duty : But 'tis just as absurd to talk of the Exercise of this holy Hope, the strong Exercise of this Grace of the spirit, in a Carnal, stupid, secure fr careless Frame, such a [-] frame yet remaining, as it would be to talk of the strong Exercises of Love to God, or Heavenly-mindedness, or any other Grace in remaining in such a Frame.

--Tis doubtless proper earnestly to exhort Those who are in such a Frame to come out of it, by the strong Exercises of all Grace; But I should not think it proper to to press a man earnestly to entertain maintain strong Hope, a strong Holy hope (that Grace of the sp.) NOTWITHSTANDING the prevailing & continuance of great carnality & stupidity (which is plainly the sense of the People I opposed) For this is plainly to press People to an unholy Hope, a strong Hope that is not a no Christian Grace; and that is strong wicked Presumption. And the promoting of this has most evidently been the Effect of such a method of dealing with souls, In innumerable multitudes of awful Instances. marginal

You mean sir, to suppose t p. 6 & 7 of your Letter that 'tis Gods manner of dealing with his saints, while in a SECURE CARELESS Frame, first to give em Assurance of their good Estate, while They remain in such a Frame, and to make use of that assurance as a mean to bring 'em out of such a Frame. Here again I must crave leave to differ from you, and to think that none of the Instances or Texts you adduce from scripture do at all prove the Point. I think it is Gods manner first to awaken their Consciences, & to bring 'em to reflect upon Themselves, and bring 'em to feel their own Calamity which They have brought upon Themselves by so departing from God, (by which an End is put to their Carelessness & security) and again earnestly & carefully to seek Gods Face, again before They find Him, and before God Restores the comfortable & Joyful sense of his Favour. And I think this is abundantly evident both by scripture and Experience.

You much insist on Jonah as a Clear Instance of the Thing you lay down. you observe that He says Chap. 2. I said I am cast out of thy sight; yet will I look again towards thy holy Temple & v. 7. when my soul fainted within me, I remembered the Lord, and my Prayer came in unto Thee, even into thine Holy Temple. you speak of these words as "demonstrating an assurance of his good Estate and of Gods Favour" (I will not now dispute whether They do or no.) And you speak of this Exercise of assurance &cas his Practice in an Evil Frame, and in a careless Frame; for He slept securely in the sides of the ship , manifesting dismal secu rity, awful Carelessness, in a carnal Frame. That Jonah was in a careless frame secure Frame when He was asleep in the sides of the ship, I don't deny. But, dear sir, does that prove that he was remained still in a careless secure Frame when He uttered Those words Chap. 2. v. 4, 7. in his Heart He said Those Things in the Fishes Belly Chap. 2. v. 4, 7? does it prove that He remained Careless after He was awaked, & saw the terrible storm, & owned it was the Fruit of Gods Anger towards Him for his sins? & does it prove that He still Remained Careless when after the Whale had swallowed Him, when He seem'd to Himself to be in the Belly of Hell? when the waters compassed Him about, even to the soul; & all G as he says, all Gods waves & Billows passed over Him, & He was ready to despair; when he went down to the Bottoms of the mountains,1 was ready to think God him had [-] cast him out of his sight, & imprisoned marginal in a Prison that He could never escape (The Earth with her Bars was about me forever) And his soul fainted within Him.2 He was brought into this Condition after his sleeping securely in the sides of the ship, before He said I will look again towards thine Holy Temple &c-He was evidently first awakend out of carelessness & security, before & brought into distress, before He was comforted.

The other Place you also much insist on, concerning the Church of Israel, is very much like This : Before God comforted them Church with the Testimonies of his Favour, after their backslidings, He first, by severe Chastisements, together with the awakening Influences of his spirit, brought 'em out of their Carelessness & carnal security. It appears by many scriptures that this was God's way of dealing with that Pleople. so Hos. 2 Chap. 2. God first Hedged up her way with Thorns, and made a wall that she could not find her Paths. ---And took away her Corn & wine & wool & Flax, destroyed her vines & FigTrees, & Caused her mirth to cease, & b by this means brought her to her self, brought out of her security, carelessness & deep sleep, very much as the Prodigal son was brought to Himself; God brought her first into the Wilderness, before He spake Comfortably to her, & by gave opend to her a door of Hope; By her distress first brought her to say, I will go & return to my first Husband : & then when God spake Comfortably to Her, she called him Ishi, my Husband.3 and God did as it were renewedly betroth her unto Him. That 2 of Hosea is very parallel with Jer. 3d One serves well to illustrate & explain the other, And that it was Gods way of dealing with his People Israel, after their Apostacy & carnal security, first to awaken it Them, & under a sense of their sin & misery & before He gave Them sensible Evi- dence of his Favour and not to awaken em ou to bring them sollicitously to seek his Face, before He gave Them sensible Evidence of his Favour, & not to awaken out of security by first making manifest his Favour to 'em, is manifest by many scriptures as Levit. 5 26. 40---42. deut 32. 36---39. 1 King. 8. 47---51. Jer. 29. 12, 13, 14. Chap. 30. 12---22. Chap. 50. 4--8. Ezek. 20. 35, 36, 37. Hos. 5. 15 with Chap. 6. 1---3. Chap. 13. 9, 10. Chap. 14. throughout. marginal

And besides I would Observe concerning that in Jer. 3. The Prophecy is not concerning the Recovery of backsliding saints, But or the mystical Church, which tho' she had corrupted herself, still continued to be Gods wife ; But concerning Apostate Israel, that forsaken and renounced her Husband, and gone after other Lovers, & whom God had renounced, put away & given her a bill of divorce (v 8) so that her recovery could not by giving her assurance of her good Estate, as still remaining his wife, and that God was already married unto Her; for that was not true, and is not consistent with the context. And whereas it is said v 14. Return O backsliding Children, saith the Lord, for I am married and I will take you one of a city &c. Indeed I am married, in the Hebrew, is in the preterperfect Tense ; But you know sir, that in the Language of Prophecy the præterTense is very commonly put for the future. And whereas it is said v 19. How shall I put Thee among the Children? ---& I said Thou shalt call me Father. I acknowledge this here, my Father, & that Rom. 8. 15 Is the Language of Faith; 'Tis so two ways. 1. 'Tis th such Language of the soul as is the immediate Effect of a lively Faith : I acknowledge that the lively Exercises of Faith do naturally produce satisfaction of a good Estate, as their immediate Effect. 2. 'Tis Language which in another sense does properly & naturally Express the very Act of Faith it self, yea the first act of faith in a sinner, before which He never was in a good Estate ; As thus, supposing a man in distress, pursued by Enemies that sought his Life, should have the Gates of several Fortresses set open before Him, and should be called to from each of Them to fly thither for Refuge, and viewing Them all, & seeing one appearing to Him strong & safe, but the othe rest insufficient, He should accept the Invitation to that one, & fly thither with this Language, This is my Fortress : this is my Refuge : In vain is salvation looked for from the other : Behold I come to this. This is my sure defence. Not that He means that He is already in the Fort within the Fortress & so in a good & safe estate, But this is my chosen Fortress, to which I betake my self for safety. so if a woman were at once sollicited by many Lovers, to give her self to Them in a married state, and beholding the superiour Beauties & Excellencies of one far above all the Rest, should betake her self to Him, & cast her fly into his Arms with this Language ; This is my Husband : behold I come unto Thee : Thou art my spouse. Not that she means marginal that she is already married to him, but that He is her chosen Husband &c-Thus God offers Himself to sinners as their saviour, God & Father ; And the Language of the Heart of Him that accepts the offer by a true Faith, is, Thou art my saviour; in vain is salvation hoped for from others that offer Themselves Thou art my God & Father. Not that He is already his Child, but He chooses Him, & comes to Him that He may be one of his Children. as Jer in Jer. 3. 19. Israel calls God F her Father, as the way to be put among the Children and be one of Them, and not as being one already. & in v. 21, 22, 23. she is not bou brought out of a careless & secure state by knowing that the Lord is her God ; but she is first brought to consideration, & sense of her sin & misery, weeping & supplica tions for mercy, & conviction of the vanity of other saviours & Refuges. Not only before she has assurance of her good Estate, but before she is brought to fly to God for Refuge that she may be in a good Estate.

As To the Instance of Job; I would only say that I think He, was far f while in his state of sore affliction, Tho' he had some great Exercises of Infirmity & Impatience under his extreme Trials, yet was very far from being in such a Frame Frame as I in tended, when I spoke of a secure, careless, carnal Frame &cI doubt not, nor did I ever question it that the saints hope & Knowledge of their good Estate, is in many Cases of excellent Benefit to help Them against Temption, & Exercises of Corruption.

with regard to the Case of gre extraordinary Temptation & Buf feting of satan, which you mention, I don't very well know what to say further. I have often found my own Insufficiency as a Counsellor in such-like Cases, wherein Melancholy & bodily distemper have so great a Hand, and give satan so great Advantage, as appears to me in the Case you mention : If the Lord do not help, whence should we help? If the Christian Friends of such afflicted, and (as it were) possessed Persons, would from Time to Time pray & Fast for them, it might be a proper Exercise of Christian Charity, and the likeliest way I know of for Relief. ---I kept no Copy of my former Letter to you, & so don't remember fully what I have already concerning this Case. But this I kno have often found with such Melancholy People, that the greatest difficulty don't lie in giving Them good advice, but in perswading marginal them to take it. One thing I think of great Importance, which is that such Persons should go on in a steady Course of a the Performance of all duties, born of the general & Particular calling , without suffering Themselves to be diverted from it by any violence of Satan, or specious Pretence of his whatsoever, properly ordering, proportioning & Timing all sorts of duties, duties to God, publick, private & secret, & duties to man, relative duties, of Business & Conversation, Family duties, duties of Friendship & good Neighbourhood, duly proportioning Labour & Rest, Intention & Relaxation, without suffering one duty to croud out or Intrench upon another. If such Persons Could be perswaded to this, I think in this way they would be best guarded against the devil, & He would soonest be discouraged, and a good state of Body would be most likely to be gained, & Persons would be act most as if They trusted & rested in God, and would be most in the way of his help & Blessing.

with Regard to what you write concerning immediate Revelations, I have thought of it, & I find I can't say any thing to any Purpose, without drawing out this Letter to a very extraordinary Length : And I am already got to such Length, that I had need to ask your Excuse : I have written enough to tire your Patience. -It has indeed been with great difficulty that I have found time to write so much : And if you knew any extraordinary Circumstances, I doubt not you would excuse my writing any more. I acknowledge the subject you mention is very important : Probably, If God spares my Life, and gives me opportunity, I may write largely upon it. --I know not how Providence will dispose of me: I am going to be Cast on the wide world, with my large Family of 10 Children. ----I humbly request your Prayers for me under my difficulties & Trials.

As to the state of Religion in this Place & this Land it is at pre sent very sorrowful & dark. But I must for a more particular Account of Things refer you to my Letters to Mr. McLaurin of Glasgow & Mr. Robe. so asking a Remembrance in you Prayers I must Conclude by subscribing my self with much Esteem & Respect your obliged Br. & servt

Jonathan Edwards

P. S. July. 3. 1750.

Having had no Leisure to sir finish the Preparation of my Letters to Scotland till before this Time, by Reason of the extraordinary Troubles, Hurries & Confusions of my Unusual Circumstances; I can now inform you that the Controversy between me & my People, that I mention'd in the Beginning of my Letter, has issued in a seperation between me & my People. an ecclesiastical Council was called on the Affair ; who sat here the week before last; who, by a Majority of one voice, determined an immediate seperation to necessary ; and accordingly my the Pastoral Relation to my People was dissolved on June 22. If I can procure the printed Accounts from Boston of the Proceedings of the Council, I will give order to my Friend there to inclose them with this Letter, & direct them to you. ----I desire your Prayers that I may take a suitable Notice of the Frowns of Heaven on me & this People, (between whom was once so great an union) in the bringing to pass such a separation between us; and that these Troubles may be sanctified to me; that God would overrule this Event for his own (which doubtless many Adversaries will rejoyce & Triumph in) that God would open a door for my future Usefulness, & provide for me & my numerous Family, in our present unsettled, uncertain Circumstances, being cast on the wide world. J. E.
