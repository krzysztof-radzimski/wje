A130. Letter to Thomas Gillespie, July 1, 1751 130.JE-T.Gillespie 7-1-51 Jonathan Edwards

A130. JE to Thomas. Gillespie, 7/1/51. ANTS, f. 1751, #6-7.

Stockbridge July 1. 1751.

Rev. & very dear Sir,

I am very greatly obliged to you for your most kind, affectionate, comfortable and profitable Letter, of Feb 2. 51. I thank you sir for your sympathy with me under my Troubles, so amply testified, and the many suitable & proper Considerations you suggest to me for my comfort & Improvement. May God enable me to make a right Improvement of Them. 'Tis not to be wondered at dear sir, that you are shock'd and surprized at had what has happened between me and the People of Northampton. 'Tis very surprizing to all impartial and considerate Persons that live near, and have the greatest Advantage to know the Circumstances of the Affair, and the Things that preceeded this Event & made way for it : But no wonder if it be much more so to strangers at a distance. I doubt not but that God intends his own Glory, and the Prosperity Safety & Prosperity of Zion, and the Advancement of the Interest of Religion, in the final Issue of this Event ; And, I trust also, my Good. But it is best that the true state of the Case should be known, and that it should be view'd as it is, in Order to receiving that Instruction, which divine Providence holds forth in it, & in order to the proper Reflections & a right Improvement:

As There are is a difference among particular Persons as to their natural Temper, so there is some difference of this Kind to be observed in diffe rent Countreys, and also in different Cities & Towns. The People of Northampton are not the most happy in their Natural Temper. They have, ever since I can remember, been famed for a high-spirited People, & close, & of a difficult turbulent Temper. However, tho' in some Respects they have been a stiffnecked People, yet the God has been pleased in Times past to bestow many special & distinguishing Favours upon. The Town has stood now near an 100 years. Their first Minister Mr. Eleazar Mather (Brother to dr. Increase Mather of Bother,1 & Mr. Samuel Mather of dul dublin in Ireland) was a very eminent man of God. After Him came Mr. stoddard, my Grand father; a very great Man, of strong Powers of Mind, of great Grace, and great Authority, of a Masterly Countenance speech & Behaviour. He had great success in his ministry. there being many seasons in his day of general Awakening among his People. He continued in the ministry at Northampton about 60 years. But God was pleas'd especially to manifest his Power in the weakness of his sucessor; marginal there having been a [--] more remarkable Awakening since his death that Ever had been before that. Altho' since that also, a greater declension, and more awful departures from God in some Respects than Ever before : and so the last minister has had more to humble Him than any of his Predecessours. May the Effect be answerable to God's just Expectations! ---The people fr having from the Beginning been well Instructed ; have had a Name, for a long Time, for a very knowing People ; And many have appeared among Them Persons of good Abilities ; and many that have been born in the Town have been promoted to Places of publick Improvement: They have been a People distinguished on this Account. -These things have manifestly been abused to nourish the Pride of their Natural Temper . an which has made made2 'em more difficult & unmanageable. There were some mighty Contests & Controversies among Them in Mr. Stoddards which were managed with great Heat & violence ; some great Quarrels in the Church, wherein Mr stoddard, great as his Authority was, knew not what to do with them.

In one Ecclesiastical Controversy in Mr. stoddard's days, wherein the Church was divided into two Parties, The Heat of spirit was raised to such a Height, that it came to hand-Blows ; a number of one met [--] the Head of the Opposite Party, and assaulted Him and beat Him unmercifully. In later Times the People have had more to feed their Pride : They have grown a much greater & more wealthy People than formerly, and are become more extensively famous in the world, as a People that have excelled in Gifts & Grace, and had God extraordinarily among them. Which has insensibly nou ingendered & nourished spiritual Pride, that grand inlet of the devil into the Hearts of men, and Avenue of all manner of Mischief among a Professing People. spiritual Pride is a most monstrous Thing. If it ben't discern'd and vigorously opposed in its firs Beginning, it very often soon raises Persons above their Teachers, & supposed spiritual Fathers, & sets 'em out of the Reach of all Rule and Instruction ; as I have seen in innumerable Instances. And therein this Inconvenience attends the publishing of Narratives of a work of God among a People. such is the Corruption that is in the Hearts of men, and even of Good men, that there is great danger of their making it an Occasion of spiritual Pride. --- There is great Reason to think that Northampton People have provoked God greatly against them by trusting in their Priviledges and attainments; & the Consequences may well be a warning to all Gods People far & marginal near, that hear of Them. ---- Another Thing Which probly has contributed in some measure to the unhappiness of the Peoples Manners, was that Mr. stoddard, tho' an eminently Holy Man, was naturally of a dogmatical Temper ; and the People being brought up under Him, and with a high veneration for Him, naturally were led to imitate Him : Especially their officers & leading men seem'd to think it an excellency to be like Him in this Respect.

--- It has been a very great wound to the Church of Northampton that there has for fourty Forty or fifty years been a sort of settled division of the People into two Parties; something like the Court & Country Party in England (if I may compare small Things with great) There have been some of the chief men in the Town, of Chief Authority & wealth, that have been great Proprietors of their Lands ; who have had one Party with Them. And the other Party, which commonly has been the greatest, have been of Those who have been Jeasou Jealous of them, apt to envy 'em, and afraid of their having too much Power and Influence in Town & Church. This has been a Foundation of Innumerable Contentions among the People from Time to Time ; which have been exceeding grievous to me ; and by which doubtless God has been dreadfully provoked, & his Spirit grieved & Quench'd, and much Confusion, and many evil works have been introduced. --Another Thing that Evidently has contributed to our Calamities is, that the People we had got so Establish'd in certain wrong notions & ways in Religion, which I found Them in and never could beat them out of. Particularly it was too much their Method to lay almost all the stress of their Hopes on the particular steps & method of their first work, i.e. the first work of the spirit of God on their Hearts in their Convictions & Conversion, & too look but little at the abiding sense & Temper of their Hearts, and the Course of their Exercises & Fruits of Grace, for Evidences of their good Estate. Nor had They learn'd, and many of Them never could be made to learn, to distinction between Impressions on the Imagination, and truly spiritual Experiences. And when I came among Them I found it to be a Custom among Them without discretion or distinction of Occasions places or Companies, to declare & publish their own Experiences; and oftentimes to do it in a light manner, without any air of solemnity. This Custom has not a little contributed to spiritual Pride, and many other Evils. when I first settled among the People, being young & of marginal but little Experience, I was not thoroughly aware of the Ill Consequences of such a Custom ; & so allowed it, or at least did not testify against it as I ought to have done. ---And here I desire it may be observed that I would be far from so laying all the blame of the sorrowful things that have come to pass to the people, as to suppose that I have no Cause of self-Reflection & Humiliation before God on this Occasion. I am sensible that it becomes me too look on what has lately happen'd, as an awful Frown of Heaven on me, as well as on the People. God knows the wickedness of my Heart and my great & sinful deficiencies and offences which I have been guilty of in the Course of my ministry at Northampton. I desire that God would discover them to me more & more, and that now He would effectually humble me & mortify my Pride and self-confidence, and empty me entirely of my self, and make me to know how that I deserve to be cast away as an abominable Branch, and a vessel wherein is no Pleasure. and if it may consist with his holy will sanctify me, & meet make me a vessel more meet for my masters Use, & yet improve me as an Instrument of his Glory & the good of souls the souls of mankind.

One Thing that has contributed to bring Things to such a Pass at Northampton, was my youth & want of more Judgment & Experience, in the Time of [-] that extraordinary awakening above sixteen years ago. Instead of a Child, there was want of [a] Giant in Judgment & discretion among a People in such an extraordinary state of Things. In some Respects doubtless my Confidence in my self was a great wrong to me ; But in other Respects, my diffidence of my self injured me. It was such that I durst not act my own Judgment, and had no strength to oppose received notions & established Customs, & to testify boldly against some glaring ap false appearances of Reli & Counterfeits of Religion, 'till it was too late. And by this means many things got Footing which have been a dreadful source of spiritual Pride, and other things that are exceeding contrary to true Christianity. If I had had more Experience and Ripeness of Judgment & Courage, I should have guided my People in a better Manner, and should have guarded Them better from satans devices, and prevented the great spiritual Calamity of many souls, and pl perhaps the eternal Ruin of some of Them, and should have done what marginal would have tended to lengthen out the Tranquility of the Town. ---- However, doubtless at that Time there was a very glorious work of God wrought in Northampton, and there were numerous Instances of saving Conversion ; tho' de undoubtedly many were deceived, and deceived others ; and the number of true Converts was not so great as was then imagined. many may be ready from things that are lately come to Pass to determine that all Northampton Religion is come to Nothing, & that all the Famed Awakenings & Revivals of Religion in that Place prove to be nothing, but strange Tides of a melancholy & whimsical Humour. But They would draw no such Conclusion if they exactly knew the true state of the Case, & would judge of it with full Calmness & Impartiality of mind. -- There are many things to Considered in the Case of Northampton.

1. That many of those who have been most violently engaged, and have chiefly led[-] & excited others in it, Tho' they have been Leading men in the Town, and have been esteemed considerable for their Knowledge, Estate and Age, and have been Professours of Religion, yet have not been the most famed for Piety.

2. The leading men, who have been most engaged in this matter, who have taken vast Pains to stir up others that are inferiour have had this great Advantage in their Hands, That the Controversy was a Religious Controversy ; That which I opposed was what They alwaies had supposed to be a Part of divine Truth, a precious & important doctrine of the word of God, & that the Cause of my opposers was the Cause of God. This has led the more ignorant & less considerate People to look on their Zeal against me a vertue, & to giv Christen even their Passions & Bitterness in such a Cause with sanctified names, & to let 'em loose, & prosecute the views of their Bitterness & violence without Check of Conscience.

3. They have also had the great Advantage of the vast veneration the People had for Mr stoddard's ministry memory; which was such that Many of Them looked on Him as a sort of deity. They were all (i.e. the except the young People) born & brought up under his ministry, and had been used from their Infancy to esteem his sayings all as Oracles. And He, they knew, maintain'd that doctrine which I oppose, with great Positiveness & Zeal, & opposed the Contrary as which I maintain, as an exceeding pernicious doctrine. Under these Cir cumstances I naturally appear as a dangerous opposer of the Cause of God, marginal and my teaching & insisting on the doctrine which Mr. stoddard opposed appears to 'em a sort of horrid Profaneness.

4. Crafty designing men have abundantly fill'd the Ears of the more ignorant with suggestions that my Opinion tends to overthrow all Religion, and to ruin the present & future Generations, and make all heathen, shutting them out of the Church of Christ.

5. Not only many of the Leading men in Northampton have used their utmost Endeavours to engage the minds of the Common People in this Controversy ; But They have also been put forward by the neighbouring ministers all round. my [--] opposers have also been assisted and edg'd on by some at a greater distance, Persons of Note ; And some great men in Civil Authority have had a great Hand.

6. It is to be considered, That the Contrary opinion to mine had not only long been established in Northampton, without so much as one opposer to it, but it had also been fully & quietly established for a long Time, in all the neighbouring Churches & Congregations, & in all the Countrey round, even to a great distance. so that my opinion, when first broach'd, appeared to the People exceeding singular. Their views being very narrow, It appeared to Them that all the world do almost was against me. And my most crafty Opposers have improved this advantage, and have abundantly im Represented me, as ex all alone in my opinion.

7. many of the People, who at length came to have their spirits much raised, and were brought to join in violent measures, yet came slowly into it, after beving long Practiced with, & indefatigable Endeavours used to engage and inflame 'em.

8. There about 20 Heads of Families, who besides others, that were women & young People, ever appeared openly against the Proceedings of the Town ; & many others have appeared friendly to me. And there is not a little Reason to think, that there are many more, especially women & youth, that would appear so, if They dare. For a Person, by appearing my Friend at Northampton, so much as to discountenance my being turned out of the Pulpit, exposes Himself to Immediate Persecution of his neighbours, and perhaps of his nearest marginal Friends. I mean He is falls under their Great Resentment, looses all their Friendship, is every where the Object of Reproach. & ________________

9. Tis to be Considered that these things have happened, when God is greatly withdrawn, & Religion was very low, not only at Northampton but all over N. England. _________

10. I believe the devil is greatly alarmed by the Opposition made made to Mr. stoddards doctrine, & a Practice the agreable Practice, which had been so long established at Northampton, & so extensively in the Countrey : In which He found his Account, & hoped for greater Consequences, mo & more agreable to Him. And God for wise Ends, has suffered Him to exert Himself in an extraordinary manner in Opposition, as God ordinarily does when Truth in is in the Birth.

But I am drawn out to an unexpected Length in my observations on these Things, and have not left my self Room nor Time for some other Things, that I would willingly write . & must therefore refer you to my Letters to my other Correspondents in scotland, particularly Mr. McLaurin, Mr Robe, Mr. McCulloch, & Mr. John Erskine. To some of Them I have been sending a particular Account of my present Circumstances, & things which have lately pass'd relating to 'em. In a I would only say in general, that I have had a Call to settle at stockbridge, a place in the western Borders of New-England, next to the Province of N. York, about 36 miles from Albany, & The Place where Mr. sergeant was minister & missionary to the Indians. I am both called by the Church here constituted partly of English & partly of Indians, and am appointed missionary to the Indians by the Commissioners for Indian Affairs in Boston : Agreable to what you suggest in your Letter, as tho' you had been able to foresee future Events, when you Perhaps you are to be employed where the Gospel has been little understood,

or attended to. I suppose this Place will for the future be the Place of my Ordinary abode. Tho' it will be some months before I can remove my Family.

I have no Leisure at present to write on that subject you speak of, viz. Impressions & im supposed immediate Revelations ; marginal tho' I own the vast Importance of the subject. I had begun to write something against the Arminians, before the late Controversy broke out with my People ; which broke me off from it, and obliged me to write on that Controversy. & now lately Mr. Williams of Lebanon has wrote a Book in answer to mine on that subject, which I think my self obliged to answer, if God gives opportunity.

I have much to p teach me to behave as a Pilgrim & stranger on the Earth. But in the midst of Troubles & difficulties, I receive many mercies. Particularly I have great Reason, with [-] abundant Thankfulness, to take Notice of the great Kindness of Friends in Scotland. Blessed be God who never forsakes those that trust in Him, and never wants Instruments for the Conveyance of his Goodness & Liberality to those who suffer in his Cause.

- I shall take Care that there be conveyed with this Letter to you, one of my Farewell sermons, and the Result of the Council, that sat at Northampton the last may.

Remember me dear sir, at the Throne of Grace, with Regard to all my Trials. and with Regard to the important my new Circumstances, & the important service I have un undertaken in this Place. --And please in your next to inform me of what Family you & of their state.

I am sir.

your most affectionate Friend & Brother, Jonathan Edwards. Mr. Gillespie
