Stockbridge in N England Febry 24. 1752.

Sir:

I have been so favor'd & honor'd by some of the Commissioners (in Boston) of the Honble Society for propagating Christian Knowledge in N England and Parts adjacent, that they have sent me Copies of three of Your Letters to Sir William Pepperell, written chiefly on the Subject of Schools for the Instruction of Indian Girls, with three other Papers drawn up by You, & Sent to him on the same Subject, with Letters sent me by the said Commissioners, proposing to me to write to You on our Indian Affairs, encouraging me, that probably a Letter from me would not be taken amis Sir William himself has also Honour'd me with a Letter, proposing my Writing to You which, together with a hope that my so doing might serve to promote the Great & Important Ends & designs of the Settlement of this place, is what Encourages & Induces me to write, & is what I have to offer in Excuse for my Troubling you with this.

And to begin with what should be uppermost in my heart -I thank God that has excited such Concern for the Enlargement of the Kingdom of Christ in America among the Poor Indians in some at so great a Distance, so able to help us who are immediately concern'd in their Instruction; I think the concern that is manifested by some in Great Brittain about these things, & the Zeal & Activity that appears in them to Spread the Gospel in the Dark Parts of this Continent, may well Shame us that are the Inhabitants of the Brittish America If we regard only our Temporal Interest & Safety, without any Respect to the Enlargement of Christ's Kingdom & of the Good of the Souls of those unhappy People, it certainly greatly Concerns us to do our utmost to Propagate the Protestant Religion among the Indians. The English have not only greatly failed of their Duty in so Neglecting the Instruction of the Indians, but we have been Extremely Impolitic, & by our Negligence in this matter have brought the whole Brittish America into very difficult & Dangerous Circumstances, which I am much more sensible of since I have Lived in this place, where I have been now for about a Twelvemonth, during which Space I have had very frequent Conversation, & much acquaintance with many of the Iroquois or Six Nations (as we Call them) I have often had free & Large Conversation with some of their Chiefs, who have a great acquaintance with the State of those Nations, & Nations beyond them --Whilst we have been asleep, our most dangerous & Inveterate Enemies the French have been awake; they have discerned & taken the advantages which we have overlook'd & Neglected, to our unspeakable, & it is to be feard, Irreparable Damage. The English have heretofore had vastly greater advantages in their hands to gain the Six Nations, & most of the other Nations, & Establish them in their Interest, than the French, as the Brittish Settlements are abundantly more numerous & populous than the French, & Better Situated.

as we Possess the sea Coasts, & the more powerful Indian Nations are much Nearer to us, & might have a Better market for their Commodities, & be much more Easily Supply'd with such Goods as they need, & at a much cheaper rate from us than from the French; & the Six Nations or Iroquois (which are the most Powerful body of Indians in these Northern Parts of the Continent) have ever since the Settlement of the European Colonies, 'til of late, been much more inclined to the English than the French; & though the French have been Indefatigably Labouring to draw them over to their Interest for perhaps a whole Century past, & we all the while very Negligent of them, yet they have made but slow Progress 'til very Lately. Instead of our Improving the advantages Divine Providence has put, & so long continued in our hands, to consult our own Safety & Strength, by using proper means to secure the Friendship & Alliance of these Nations, we have on the Contrary been constantly taking Courses to Alienate them -Tis true we have traded with them a great deal, but our trade has been carried on in such a way as has naturally tended to Beget & Establish in them a Distrust & Aversion to us. most of our Indian Traders being Persons of little honesty or Conscience, the Indians have been abundantly Defrauded, & commonly that has been given 'em in Exchange for their Skins which has done 'em no Good, but has been their Greatest Plague viz Rum in vast Quantities, which, thô Pleasing to their Appetites, they are Sensible is their Bane, & serves nothing to gain their Affections, but the Contrary -they themselves give it as a reason why they are under a necessity of deserting us that we destroy them --

Nothing has been more common than for our Traders first to make 'em Drunk, & then to marginal trade with them & defraud them of their Goods, when they have lost the use of their Reason and are unable to defend themselves. Tho the King annually gives £500 Sterling as a Present to the Six Nations, yet (I suppose it to be very apparent) that the Greatest Part of the money is Embezzel'd, & used to advance the Private Gain of those into whose hands it falls; & as to that part of it which is bestow'd on the Indians, much of it is given in Spirituous Liquors, which not only does 'em no good, but is the occasion of their more foolishly Squandering away the more valuable goods that are given them immediately after they have received them so that very little is Carried home with them --And as to Instructions in Religion, but little has been done by the Missionaries of the Society for Propagating the Gospel in Foreign parts. That Society have been vastly imposed upon by the Representations of their Missionaries, as appears by the Printed accounts Published by the Society which come over hither ---The Iroquois themselves have often in my hearing Complain'd greatly of the Negligence of those Missionaries --the money of that Society, instead of being used to Propagate the Gospel among the Heathen, is Chiefly spent to Promote the Church party in opposition to those of the Presbyterian or Congregational persuasion in N England; those that are of the Church of England here, especially their Clergy, being almost universally wholly High Church men, & great Bigots, using all manner of methods to promote their own party, & to encroach upon & root out the N England Churches, & engross every thing to themselves; wherein they are perhaps no Less active, than the Papists are to build up themselves here upon on the Ruins of the Protestant & Brittish Interest in America, which is a thing worthy to be Consider'd in any projection for a Coalition of the Church of England & Dissenters in the Affair of the Instruction of the Indians, & the Establishment of Schools for the Education of their Children & Youth.

The French here in America have been more faithful to those by whom they have been intrusted than the English --the French Government in Canada have been more faithful to their King & the French Missionaries more True to the Interest of their Religion they have had their Missionaries Constantly for many Years amongst the most powerful of these Nations of North America, who have been great in their Labours among them, using all the arts & Subtile Devices of the Romish Clergy, prejudicing them against the Protestants & English by Innumerable Jesuitical Falsehoods; & by Accounts we have from the Mohawks, the French fail not to make great use of our Negligence as to their Instruction, as an advantage to fix an Ill opinion of us in their minds; Telling them it is Apparent that the English care not for their Good as they take no Pains to Instruct 'em in Religion; at the same time offering their own Assistance & Labours for their Eternal Salvation, if they will be their friends.

The Vast difference of the Temper of the French & English Nations, as it has appear'd in the Conduct of each towards these People, is very Strange while they have been so Exceeding Vigilant, we have been like men in a Lethargy, so as to make us the objects of the Mirth of the French, & of late of the Contempt of the Indians -they often, in my hearing, have made mention of the Remarkable Difference, & have told me how the French Laugh at our Sluggishness & Stupidity ---We have seen the Advantages the French were Taking, in one instance & another & have stood by, & let 'em go on Quietly 'til it has been too late, & then we have Lamented that we did not seasonably bestir ourselves to prevent them Thus, when they were about building their Fort at Crown Point on our own Land (which has been our greatest annoyance in the Late War) it was known of before, & our anticapating them talk'd of, but all Ended in Talk, & nothing was done; & in a Little time we most severly Suffer'd for our Sloth & Neglect -Besides our Grievous Distress from thence, & Loss of Bloud, & Diminution of Territory, that Fort has already Cost us more to Defend ourselves against it, perhaps Ten times so much as the Charge of Building & maintaining a Fort there ourselves would have been.

The Great advantage the French have by their Interest in the Indian Nations never so marginal sensibly appear'd as in the Late War, for by means of them they were much too hard for these Brittish Colonies, tho the French in Canada are but a handful in comparison of us. The things we Suffer'd in the War have rais'd the Indian's Opinion of the Strength of the French, & fix'd in 'em Notion of our Impotence; many things that happen'd in the War served to render us Contemptable in their Eyes, & to disincline 'em more & more to a Strict Alliance with us; especially our making such a Pretence of our Invasion of Canada, & such great preparations for it, & treating with them so much about it, & at last Failing without putting any thing in Execution, & so, greatly disapointing those of the Indians that we had Engag'd, & who had prepar'd to assist us -& then after that, making another Pretence of Besieging the Fort at Crown Point, & also making Great Preparations for that, & doing much to Engage the Indian's Assistance, & finally dropping that Design also, without going against the Place -Besides some Notable instances of unfaithfulness in the English toward the Mohawks, who proved themselves our Faithful Allies in the Late War, of which they have Loudly & often Complain'd -When these things are Consider'd, 'tis no wonder they have been follow'd with such sad Effects, in so great an alienation of most of these Northern Tribes from the Brittish Interest -Especially when it is also Consider'd, that the French stand ready to aggravate these things to the Indians, to put their own Constructions upon them, to heighten the Contempt, & Blow up the Resentment which they saw naturally rising in their minds on these occasions.

The French have been mightly Encouraged by these things; & this Encouragement, together with the Experience they have lately had of the advantage of the Alliance of the Indians to them & the Disadvantages which they have seen us Suffer by it, have Exceedingly Engag'd them to Lay out them selves to finish the Business of Alienating from the English all the Nations of Indians in North America, & fixing them strongly in a Friendhip with them & Dependance upon them I Believe the People of England are in no measure Sensible how much the Court of France have Exerted themselves, & what Expence they have been at for this purpose since the Conclusion of the Peace. The Presents that are made them at the French King's Cost are very Large, & such as we can have little Hope to Vie with them in; and the Mohawks here tell us of Great things they have been doing in the Two Past Years to the West of us; in building Forts at the Carrying Places between the Lakes & Rivers, & in the most Important Places; In Treaties they have had with the Nations; Messages that have been sent to them, & presents that have been made 'em -They have so Extended their Plantations & Fortify'd Places, that they have now in Effect join'd their settlements in Canada & those on the Missisippi River, & hemm'd us all in; & have had Great Success in gaining over the Greater Part of the Iroquois, & almost all the Nations about the Great Lakes, by which they are become very Formidable to the few Indians that are yet our Friends, who Blame the Brittish Governors for their Negligence, & commend the Governor of Canada for his Superior Wisdom. the French have Left no stone unturn'd, no method neglected to gain their Purpose they have not only sent forth their Missionaries & other Emissaries, but they have of Late, on several occasions, gone with Armies to do their Business the more thoroughly --Their way is, when they have gain'd the Friendship of a Nation, to persuade some of em to Come & Settle in Canada with presents & many fair promises, & to Inveigle 'em to Permit their Building Forts in their Territories, whereby their Countrys are in Effect Annexed to the French Dominions -So they are constantly Enlarging the French Empire in America, & more & more Curtailing the Brittish Dominions ---In time of War they are far more active & thorough in their Business than we; & they are no Less Active to Establish & Enlarge their Interests & undermine ours, in time of Peace, when we are soundly asleep -In time of War they Drive us in 'tis found by Experience that we cant maintain our Frontiers against them & their Indians; but where they once get Footing, They hold their own, & never give back.

What will be the Consequences of these things in Future times God only knows but, if another War should Break out whilst things are in their Present situation, we may well Cry The Lord have Mercy on us! unless the Most High should remarkably appear for us, there would marginal be Great Probability of our being brought into unexperienc'd Distress -Indeed, if Things continue in the Course they are now in, there is a Great Prospect of the French King's having the Greatest Part of North America in his Possession --Tho the King of Great Brittain has much the Greatest Number of Subjects here, & has had heretofore vastly the Greatest advantage to Enlarge his Dominions in these parts of the World --It would have been an Easy thing in the Time of the Late War (unless Heaven had remarkably appear'd against us) to have taken Possession of the whole of this Northern Continent --Certainly the French in the Like Case would not have Neglected the opportunity -But we must Look to God, & not to Ministers of State for Defence --That things are brought to this Pass is both the Natural Consequence, & also a Just Punishment, of our so much Neglecting the Eternal Welfare of the Indians.

Divine Providence seems now to have Left us but one way to help ourselves, or to do any Thing considerable towards regaining these Nations, & that is applying ourselves now at Length (tho it be Late in the Day) in Good Earnest to our Duty, in Instructing them in the Principles of True Religion, cultivating the minds of their Children, & Storing them with the useful Knowledge -There seems still to be an Opportunity for us to do much in this way towards attaching them to Our Interest, as well as promoting their own True Happiness, if we have but a Heart to improve it, & a Prudence Sufficient for the Conduct of the Affair ---The Indians, Wild as they are, have some Sense of the shamefulness of Vice, & of the Value of order Virtue, Order & Civility -they are also in a Degree sensible of the Worth of Knowledge -If anyone among them is able to Read & Write, it is look'd upon as a Great attainment, & they Esteem it a thing much to be Valu'd to be able to Read & Understand the Bible --------& therefore many among them are fond of their Children's Learning the English Tongue to that End, that they may understand what they Read ---Therefore, herin We have an advantage to out do the French -For how =ever Great Expence they are at in the Presents they bestow upon 'em, yet, agreeable to the Genius of their Religion & Maxims of Their Church, they keep 'Em in Ignorance, they Forbid em the use of the Bible, nor do they teach 'em to Read & Write If we should go on & prosecute the Design of Instructing them to Good Effect, & many among them by Our means should have these Attain =ments, & it were seen by the Nations that there was such a Difference between such as have been under Our care & those under Theirs, it would be seen that the Greatest Benefit was receiv'd by those that are on Our side; & their observing the Difference, & taking notice that the French keep Their Indians in Ignorance, & refuse to Enable them, or allow 'em to read the scriptures, would naturally Excite in the Minds of this Jealous People a Suspicion of the French, as not truly Seeking their Good, but as having some Ill design in their pretences of Kindness to 'em; & that the rather, because they have so much Discerning as to be sensible the French are a Designing, Crafty Treacherous People; & thô our Presents of Cloathing, Arms & Ammunition should not Equal those of the French, yet they would see the Advantage they receive from Us to be more substantial, & of Greater Value than any thing the French bestow upon them --we should also have also this Advantage, that our Religion would recommend it self more to their Reason & the Light of Nature in 'em, thô Less to their Corruptions. As This way of dealing with these Nations would be in itself the most Christian & Benevolent, so therein we[should have most Reason to Expect God's Blessing, & might hope that his Anger for our Past Neglects & Ill Treatment of them would be Turn'd away, from whence we have otherwise Reason to fear that he will use them as a sore scourge, to inflict on us a just Punishment of our Cruelty to their Souls & Bodies, by our withholding the Gospel from them, Defrauding them of their Goods, Prejudicing them against Christianity (the Religion we profess) by our Unchristian Behavior, & more than Heathenish Wickedness; & our Killing multitudes of them, & Greatly wasting their numbers with Strong Drink --And not only would this method be most becoming Christians, & so most Pleasing to the Great marginal Governor of the World, but if we look only to the Natural Tendancy of it in the present Situation of things, I am persuaded there is no Course in the world that can be Devised by any Policy or art, that would be so likely to Gain these Nations & attach them to us I think this is Evidently the Voice of Providence, & the Intimation which God gives us in the present State of things, that if Ever North America is regain'd from an Adherence to our Antichristian Enemies, it must be more by Spreading the Light of the Gospel, than by any Policy, Wealth or Arms of the Brittish Empire ---If the King's Annual Bounty of £500 Sterling (the Greater part of which it is to be feared is Embezzel'd, & great Part of the remainder Spent to make the Indians Drunk) were Lay'd out for their Instruction, it would be Probably ten times so Effectual to gain the End that is Aimed at. There now seems to be a Door opened in a Remarkable manner in This Place

for the successful Prosecution of this method of making the Indians our Friends by Instructing them; giving us better opportunity than we have had for it many Years. Here they see the Congregation of Houssatunnock Indians, now consisting of above 250 souls,

who have been some of them under the Care of the English above 16 Years, & have been constantly well Treated by us -they see the many happy Effects of our Care of them, & kindness to them in things which Concern their Temporal Interest, & none of those Ill Consequences which those Suspicious People are Exceedingly apt to apprehend. -They have before their Eyes, in these Indians, a Confutation of that which commonly is their Greatest Objection against putting their Children under the Care of the English, viz that the English will make Slaves of 'em, & the Eyes of many of the Iroquois seem now to be Turned this way; & there appears some special influence on the minds of many of them at this Time, inclining to such Greater Degrees of Light than they have hitherto Lived in --Besides those that are of the Tribe of the Cayreeyenkeys or proper Mohawks, there are Lately come to us above 20 Old & Young of the Oneiutas, another of the Six Nations, from a Place called Onohquanga, (about 200 Miles South West from Albany, on Sasquehannah River near the head of it,) on an Invitation that was sent to 'em from hence the Last summer -There is something wonderful in these Onohquanga Indians -of Late Years, thrô some Remarkable Influ=ence on their minds, they have had a Strange turn of mind; several of their Chief men, that have been Notorious Drunkards have wholly Reformed; & they have Generally left their former inclination to War & Bloudshed, & Turned their Minds to Religion, & have been seeking after Instruction --Those of 'em that are Come hither Shew the Best Disposition of any of Our Indians -they are peculiarly Serious, Disposed to Civility, & manifest a Thirst after Knowledge and Delight in Instruction: they are Well Pleas'd with what they find here, And the Chief of 'em, who is a very Serious man, intends to go home in the Approaching Spring, & invite others of his People hither, & thinks it probable that he shall bring many with him. We begin now to get into more happy Circumstances as to the method & means of Instruction -Heretofore, Things have been in no small Degree of Confusion, For want of a Proper person to be the Schoolmaster of the Iroquois; after many abortive Endeavors, one at length is obtain'd, who appears well Qualify'd for the Business Mr Gideon Hawley; a young Gentleman of Liberal Education, about 24 Years of Age; he has now set up a Regular school, Greatly to the Satisfaction of the Indians -His Character is that of a Person of Good Abilities, solid Piety, remarkable Prudence & Steadiness of Temper for one of his Years, a happy Talent in Teaching, Good Resolution & Spirit of Government and has Long had an Inclination to the Business of Instructing Indians.

He has a School of about 36 Iroquois Children & Youth, which indeed is too Great for One marginal Master, considering the manifold Business he has to do: he applies himself with Great Zeal & Diligence to Instruct the Children in reading & writing & the English Tongue, & in the Principles of Religion & Good manners; himself Labouring at the same time to Learn the Iroquois Language; Besides teaching a Couple of English Boys, who are put into the School by the Government to learn the Indian Tongue, & other things necessary to fit them to be serviceable to the Indians, either as Missionaries, or school Masters in Due time There is already a happy Alteration among the Indians since Mr Hawleys Coming, tho he has been among them but about 3 Weeks; he has already got a Considerable Interest in the Affections of the Indians, & their Chiefs have Diligently apply'd themselves to assist Him, & Strengthen his hands in Governing their Children, & Bringing them to Good order -Some of the Chiefs of the Mohawks having been here of Late, who have manifested Great Satisfaction in these New Regulations, & will Doubtless, carry home a Good Report of what they have seen. Another thing That appears very Favorable to our Design, which is the Happy Union that Subsists between those Iroquois & the Stockbridge Indians the latter making the other Welcome, & doing all that they can to Encourage them in seeking Instruction & coming hither for it --The Stockbridge Indians had a Meeting the Last week, of their own accord, without any Motion from any English Person, to consider whether they had done all that it was Proper for them to do to Encourage the Iroquois coming hither, & to Promote the Design of their Instruction; and Determined that they had not yet done their Duty, but that something farther was incumbent on them & after Consultation they concluded & did as follows ---They gave Notice to the Iroquois that are here present, that they Desired a Conferrance with them, & after all were Convened, they Told them, that they observed the English had done a Great Deal to Promote their being Instructed in the Christian Religion, which made them to Reflect on themselves, & Consider whether they had not failed of doing their part; they told them, that as to themselves, they now for sometime had Experienc'd the Great Benefit of the Instructions of the English that once they were in a State of Great Ignorance & Darkness, but now were brought into the Light, & knew that it was Good; & therefore Earnestly Desir'd them to give heed to the Instructions the English gave them, & Not to Listen to any objections against them; & Particularly not to give way to any Suspicions or Jealousies of any Ill Design in the English, fearing that they intended to make slaves of them or their Children, or any thing of that Nature; & told em, that they themselves at First were full of such Jealousies, but now they had been so many Years under the Care of the English, & found that there was no thing at all in their Suspicions, no Foundation for 'em & told them for their Encouragement, they might have the use of those Lands that were round about the Boarding School where their Children were Instructed as long as they pleas'd & concluded with observing, that Time in this World is but short, saying to this Purpose, "we have but a Little Time to Continue here; we had therefore Best to Improve our time, Give Diligent heed to the Instructions we have, & do our utmost to Practice accordingly; & it may be, if we Do so, we may hereafter meet together in Heaven" -I had this Account of the Conferrance from Abraham Caunaugh stansey, who is the Chief man of the Nation of the Mohawks in all Matters of Religion, a very serious Devout & Upright man

So that now if the English dont fail of their Duty, & if Private Views & Designs, & some unhappy marginal misunderstandings which Subsist between the Principal English Inhabitants of Stockbridge, (which is the Greatest Calamity that attends the Affair): I say if these things don't Prevent, there is a most hopeful Prospect of much Good being done in Promoting the Truest Welfare of these People, & also much being done for the British Interest in America If things go on under a Wise Conduct, it is to be hoped, that instead of one Male School and one Female, there may, after some time, be several Schoools for each sex, & means found for giving some of the Children of Best Genius (for there are some very Forward Genius's among 'em) a superior Education, to fit them to be Instructors, School Masters or Missionaries to their own people -and here might be Erected in time a kind of an Academy to this end.

and if this should take Effect (as I see not at present why it may not) It will probably be found necessary with these People to go out of the Usual methods of a Scholastic & Academical Instruction, & particularly not to begin with Teaching the Boys the Learned Languages, but to begin with more Entertaining & Rational studies, which will better Suit the Genius of these People, instructing them something in Geography & History, & some of the Easier parts of the Mathematics, & the First Rudiments of Astronomy & after that, something of Logic & Natural Philosophy withal setting apart a fit Proportion of Time every Week for the Study of Divinity -& perhaps some that have a peculiar Genius for Learning Languages may soon be initiated in the study of the Learned Languages == but no Particular method can be fix'd on before some Trial -The Best method must be Left to be found, by the United help of Discretion & Experience, in such as have the Care of the Business == Doubtless more happy methods might be found for instructing both the Girls & Boys than are commonly gone into among the English in New England -----I should think it were no matter how soon a Couple of Good Terrestial Globes, & some Good Maps of Places mention'd in the Holy Scriptures, were procured for the use of the school here. If such things as these should take Effect, as I know nothing why they may not if it be Judg'd Best that they should & we Properly Exert ourselves in order to it, & our Misunderstandings & Private designs dont Prevent; it probably would have a Powerful Influence to Draw & Unite the Nations of Indians to us Possibly it may be thought I am in too much haste too forward in my Projections; & that it is time Enough to talk of an Academy &ca: many years hence --But certainly, if we Consider how active the French are, & what Amazing Progress they have made of Late, & are daily making, it may be Sufficient to convince us that we had need not to be Dilatory == not that I suppose the State of Things is already Ripe for the putting all these things in Execution --Things are not Sufficiently settled & Certain with respect to the Iroquois but, if Things remain with their Present Good appearance one Twelvemonth longer, I should think it high time to begin to Lay New Foundations for a more Enlarged Design --Some of the Boys & Young men amongst the Iroquois have made Pretty Swift Progress in Learning the English Tongue; & it is to be hoped Mr Hawley in time will be able to get as acquaintance with Their Language & he is disposed, so soon as he can get any Considerable Knowledge of it, to go up into the Countrys of the Six Nations, taking some of the Best of the Indians with him, to Endeavour to persuade the People there to bring their Children hither for Instruction; Abraham Caunaugh Stansey has notify'd himself willing to go with him, which it is to be hoped will have an Happy Effect

I dont suppose that instructing the Indians is all we should do to secure em in the British Interest -marginal undoubtedly many other things might be done, which Join'd with this might serve greatly to forward the Design. Beside the Kindness shewn to their Children, in Boarding, them Cloathing & Instructing them, Kindness might be shewn to those Parents who bring their Children, & Presents might sometimes be made to some of their Chiefs that assist in the Affair --And still Forts might be Built & maintained by the Government, in some Important Places not yet Possess'd by the French, which if we Neglect much Longer, we may Expect will not be long Neglected by them. If our Old Obstinate Indolence be Continu'd still, we may Depend upon it that our Enemies will come Closer & Closer upon us with Their Fortifications; our supineness will not only Embolden them, but as it were Tempt & Draw them on ---We might to Good Effect set up Trading houses, from whence the Indians might be Supply'd with Goods at a much Cheaper Rate than the French can Afford them; & care might be taken that they should be Treated with inviolable Honesty & Fairness, which would have a mighty Influence upon 'em; and no Rum should be sold to 'em. Before the Full Copies of Your Three Letters to sir William were sent to me by some of the Commissioners in Boston, Andrew Oliver Esqr. their Secretary & Treasurer was pleas'd to send me an Extract of the First of those Letters, & to propose that I should send my Thoughts concerning a Female school to sir William, accordingly I did so. what I Drew up may recieve Improvement from Your Thoughts Contain'd in one of the Papers sent sir William with Your Letters ----I think with you that one school of Girls should not consist of more than 15 or 20, only I Conceive it to be Necessary, as to the Children of the Iroquois at Least, whose Parents at present have no Lands or Tenements in New England, that they should be Boarded & Lodg'd under the Care of their Mistress, in an house built for that purpose -which wil give the Mistress much greater advantage to change their manners, & Form their minds, & bring 'em to a Civil, Orderly & Christian way of Living -I agree with you sir, that one Mistress, with a Female Assistant who shall be subject to the Mistress, will be Sufficient for One school -But then there will be Need that the Mistress be generally Present in the school, & Devote herself to the Business of Teaching, which, in order to be done with Good Effect with respect to such as are taken out of such Wildness & Barbarity, will require the Constant Application of a Woman of Superior Talents & therefore I Differ from you in supposing that Missionaries Wives should be Preferr'd for the Business, it being ordinarily Impossible, that Women who are married, & have the Care of Families of their own, should be sufficiently Devoted to this Business.

But I fear I have Quite tired your Patience --I have been Drawn on to so Great a Length in my Writing by the Vast Weight of the Subject, & the many Important things Pertaining to it which present themselves to the view of one in my Situation together with a View of your Concern about the Affair, tho' you are at so great a Distance, & have so much less to Excite & Oblige your Concern than we that are on the spot -Hoping, that some Good Improvement may be made of the Particular Informations I have given, & for your Candid Acceptance of what I have written, I remain

Sr

Your most huml Servt

Jonathan Edwards.
