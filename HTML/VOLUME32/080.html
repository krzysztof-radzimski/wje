A066. JE to Unknown Correspondent, after 3/13/46. Draft fragment. ANTS, f. 1744-46, #1.

[...] the faith & henceforth so also there will be laid up for me a Crown of Righteousness and those words Chap. 1. 121 I know whom I have believed & I am persuaded . ---are Interpreted thus If I keep my self through an approaching Trial upon supposition that I keep my self through my approaching trial & so approve my self a believ true believer I do Know that I have believed & in X & have committed my soul to him & that he is able to keep it through all against that day. & that in Philip. 1. 21. for me to live is X & to die in is gain is Interpreted on supposition I live to X to the End of my days then [-----------] to live conform to live will be & to die will be gain I shall live to X & to die will be gain. Therefore tho I am uncertain whether I shall either live to X & to die will be gain I am in a strait between Two two, not knowing which to Choose whether to depart to be with X & or to live here to live to X. I wont Charge upon you these absurd Interpre tations of SS. possibly I have misunderstood you when you speak of Persons being undeter uncertain whether they shall not become Persevere through future Trials & about the Apostles speaking with in assured strain on h on supposition tho I am not able to find out how I should misunderstand you

* Last paper last Col.]2 And if it be as you say that the apostle Paul [--] expressed Himself as uncertain [-] about the state of his soul in the Texts you cite to this Purpose you your self have proved that a saint may sometimes [--] have an absolute assurance & yet afterwards be without for This apostle before he uttered those words in Philip 3 12, 13. had spoken as one having the hi most Positively and assuredly and as one that Knew was certain of his own sincere Xtianity & future Reward . 2 Cor. 4. 8 & v. 14. to the End with Chap. 15 5. 1---8 & v. 16, 17 & Chap. 6. 1---11 Chap 1. 12, 21, 22. 1 & in I Thes. 2. 1---10 he most solemnly appeals to G. Concerning his sincerity & Holiness.

Concerning that Text in Gal. 2. 20. you say I know that the scripture speaks of his X loving & giv dying & giving himself for those who yet are supposed finally to obtain the Benefits of his death It would be an endless I shall not undertake to in this Letter to go through with the Controversy about universal Redemption & about the Interpretation of those scriptures that speak of Xs dying for the all &c--But I think if we find the very same words of Xs Loving & giving himself for our elsewhere joind in like manner it will be [ ] to Interpret an Joind in likel manner at any where else unless it be in Eph. 5. 25. where they are used concerning that Church of saints that are Xs spouse & members of his Body of his flesh & his Bone. And tis certain that the Apostle in this Place in Galations dont speak of Christs Loving him & giving himself for him only in the way that as he does those that have no saving Benefits of his death because in the same sentence he speaks of saving Benefits of & Consequences of Xs Loving him & giving himself for him Just as Xs living in him & his living by the faith of the son of God. & speaks of [ ] with those Benefits with as much positiveness as much positiveness as of the other as to that Text 2. Pet. 1. 10. I shall not now pretend in this Letter to begin finish the Controversy between Calvinists & Arminians about the meaning of the word Election as it is used in the New T. whether it signifies a particular Election as the Calvinists suppose or only something General extending to all that live under the Gospel as you suppose the word is used here. Let that be as it will I think it is plain that [-----] [------] as it is possible any thing should be by other words in the sentence that what the Apostle exhorts to make sure in this is something special special & saving. you say the word ϐεβαιοs3[-] is always in SS. & in all other writing used to signify the certainty & stability of the thing it self & never used to signify the Assurance of knowledge. I am far from pretending to such an universal & perfect acquaintance not only with the Greek Testament but all oth other Greek as to assert positively as you do that this word [-------] is used thus & thus alwaies in em all & never any otherwise I know the word Bebaios signifies firm or sure But I dont suppose there is any thing in the wo greek word that hinders it being used for any thing the firmness of assent or evidence or Assent or the firmness & of a thing in a Persons with Regard to its Evidence in the mind Kno to the mind & its Assent than any any more than to signify the firmness [------------------] and however it [-] is with all other Greek writings yet if you had Exam more fully Examined the Greek Testament alone you would never have asserted the Contrary with Regard to the use of the word there . In 2 Cor. 1. 7 . Tis expressly used for the firmness of Hope . our Hope of you is stedfast Bebaia. in mark 16. 20 The word is used [ ] being made [ ] as to their Evidence. In Col. 2. 7. tis used for the firmness to of Endeavour of Faith & so of the judgment as it is also in Heb. 2. 3. & in 1 Cor. 1. 6. & Luke 6. 16. & in Tit. 3. 8 dia διαϐεβαιουσθαι is put for affirming a thing as evident & certain. And the nature of the thing makes it evident that the Apostle Peter in this very Text4 wherein he exhorts those he wrotes to when he exhorts those he writes to to make their calling & Election sure not to make the thing firm & & established in it self but su firm & sure as to the Evidence of it For their calling nor Election nor the saving Benefits of them could not be made more firm in themselves than they were already if they were true saints as the Apostle supposes (see v. 1 & Chap 3. 1.) unless we hold falling from Grace . But if you hold this doctrine & this be the Ground of your objection I think you ought to have explained your self on this Head that I might have known how to have Replied to you But yet you not only say that this Text is not to my Purpose but that it plainly makes against me here I confess my self extremely at a loss what you should intend or which you supposed the words not only did did not make for me but against & that plainly. As to the great objections you make against my saying that marginal the spirit of God in his saving Operation Communicates himself to the soul in his own proper Nature that this is the same thing as to say that implying as you suppose [-] Gods Communicating his Essence after all that is said the objection is only about the use of Language & propriety of Expressions. It cant be any thing Else for I have particularly explained my meaning and expressly declared what I do not mean [----] that I don by his proper nature I dont mean his Essence & have also declared particularly what I do meaning viz that the spirit of Gods communicating Himself in his proper nature I mean communicating something of his nature Holiness & this Explanation I give immediately when I a fir in the Place where I first use the Expression. so that the Exceeding great objection is that I in your opinion and then further to explain myself say that the Grace which is in the Hearts of the saints, is of the same Nature as much as it is possible for that to be which is infinitely less in degree.5 so that the Exceeding great objection is that I in your Opinion signify this that I declare to be my meaning ny an improper Expression & if that be still a grand difficulty with you I hope you will consider the matter more fully I confess my skill in the English Tongue does not extend so far as to discern the great impropriety of the word as I have used it.

The word nature is not used only to signify the Essence of a thing but is commonly used many other ways is used very variously. Chambers in his dictionary observes on this word as follows "Nature Natura φυσις is a Term very variously used Aristotle has a whole Chapter wrote expressly to enumerate the various acceptations of the Greek word φυσις renderd into English nature and among Latin writers the different acceptations are so many that a certain author reckons up fourteen or fifteen Mr Boyle in a precise Treatise of the vulgarly received notion of nature gives us eight principal ones."6 That Property which is natural to any one & is eminently his Character I think is without abuse of Language or going cross to the common use of it called his proper nature tho' is not Just the same with his Essence thus we say concerning an exceeding good natured man that Ingenuity is his very nature. I think Holiness may [ ] to be without absurdity be said to be the proper nature of the Holy spirit on two accounts. 1 as Tis his peculiar beauty & Glory as Bright & so may in a special manner be called his nature as Brightness may in a Peculiar manner be said to be the nature of the sun 2. Tis the proper Character of the spirit above all other things in that office & work of his wherein we are concerned with him This in is that in his nature which he especially manifests & exercises in his own office acts & operations towards us & therefore this in his nature is singled out from all other things to denote him by as he is Revealed us Hence his name by which he is called in SS. is the HOLY Ghost. & This is that is that in his nature which he communicates something of to to his the saints They are made Partakers of his Holiness as the SS de expressly declares Heb. 12. 10. & that without Imparting to them his Essence Light & Heat may in a special manner be said to be pr the proper nature of the sun. and yet none will say that every thing to which the sun communi cates a little of its Light & Heat has therefore communicated to it the Essence of the sun & becomes the same Being wi is sun'd with the sun or becomes the same being with the sun or becomes equal to that Immense Fountain of Light & Heat A diamond or Chrystal that is held forth in the sun beams may properly be said to have some of the [----] suns Brightness communicated to it for tho it hant the same Individual brightness with that which is Inherent in the sun yet & be infinite immensely less in degree yet it is same nature. I dont Know that I have exceeded the due bounds of a writer [--] in divinity in expressing [--] that communication that God makes to his saints of his Holiness in the very Terms that God himself does in his word in calling it a communicati their being made Partakers of the divine nature and I think the Representation I have made [-] of the matter is such as the scriptures in innumerable Texts that might be mentiond do we d d does abundant ly warrant in innumerable Texts that might be mentiond with Regard to what the objections you make against what I say about a new spiritual sensib sense given to the Hearts of the saints &c and spiritual sensation &c & calling it a new Principle of anture & a new ability to exert the Faculties & calling it a new ab Principle of nature & a new Habit giving a Person ability & disposition to exert the Faculties in Exercises of a new Kind.
