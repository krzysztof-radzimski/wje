C119. Memorial of Stockbridge Inhabitants to Spencer Phips and General Court, [After November 1752] C119.Woodbridge-Phips aft11-52htm Timothy Woodbridge, Stockbridge Inhabitants

C119. Timothy Woodbridge & Inhabitants of Stockbridge to Spencer Phips, n.d. [after 11/52]. Mass. Archives. In hand of Timothy Woodbridge, with original signatures of English and Indian inhabitants.

To his Honour Spencer Phips Esqr. Lieut Governour, & commander

in chief, in & over his Majesys Province, of the Massachusetts Bay in New-

England. And to the Honrbl his Majesties council, & house of Repretes

in general court convened at Boston.

The memorial of the inhabitants of Stockbridge, Humbly sheweth that about

Sixteen years ago, the great & general court of his province, made a grant of land

in this wilderness to us; of about twenty three thousand acres, two thousand & four

hundred of Sd land was allow'd for the english Settlements; and four hundred more

was purchased of a proprietor of upper housatonnock, whose right fell in that part

of Said Upper housatonnock, (viz) the north part that was taken of from Sd Town-

ship, to make part of the Township of Stockbridge, this quantity of land was original-

ly granted to Six english Settlrs, which are now augmented to twelve, exclu-

sive of the present ministers family, near one half of Stockbridge belonged the

proprietors of upper Housatonnock, about three Square miles of Said half, the

Indians procured by exchanging their lands, which they reserv'd in the

Township formerly called the lower Housatonnock, on which they liv'd in the

time Stockbridge was granted. The other half, (viz) the north part of

the Town was claimed by several Indians, by a native right, whom to

make easy the Indians, and english, in order to bring the whole into

a common propriety, was obliged to advance a Sum of money ---

Sixty pounds our old Tenr., to satisfy these original claimers, So that

about two thirds of the Town was procured, by the Inhabitants by the

exchange of land, & the payment of money, before we could peacably

possess the grant made us by the great & general court.-------

These things being amicably settled, among us with considerable cost &

pains, we hop'd a foundation was laid for future peace, being sensible that

our Situation & circumstances in a peculiar manner obliges us

hereto. And as we have been Sedulous & carefull, to promote peace,

and harmony, among ourselves, so we could with ease & pleasure

maintain it, if we were not in a very extraordinary manner broken

in upon, by some persons who have of late taken up their residence in this

Town. When the Late Revrd Mr. Sergeant propos'd to us, who are the chief

proprietors of this Town, his scheme of setting up a free boarding school

in this place, and his desire tha twe would provide a tract of Land ac-

comadable for the Same, his arguments to obtain so valuable and large

a tract of our land was, that our children should have the benefit

of it, and that Mr Hollis would Support & educate a number of our

children. On such a Proposal, we yielded up the land The Said Mr Sergeant

unhappily for the peace & comfort of this Town, introduced one Mr

martin Kellogg to be the School master, in Said boarding school.

Both Mr Sergeant, & we, were at the first introduction of Said Kelloggmarginal

unacquainted with his qualifications, temper & disposition, tho we

have had opportunity Since, to determine that he is not a Suitable

person for the business he is employ'd in any of the before men-

tioned respects: and we have good evidence that Mr Sergeant viewed

him in the same light, for Some of us know very well, that

he was looking out for another master, a little before his

death, and had actually had conversation with a young Gen-

tleman, of a liberal education, to undertake the school.

Several of your memorialists put their children into this New school

upon Mr Sergeants desire, but not we apprehend to their advantage

Especially since Mr Sergeants death, those few of our children that

have been at Mr Kelloggs school at turns sometimes having

five or six, sometimes two or three, & Sometimes none; have

been left at very loose ends as to their education they have

neither been dieted, clothed, or instructed, in industry or any

other usefull knowledge, as we espected when And as we had

assurances of when we deliver'd our children to be instruct-

ed by Sd Kellogg. Excepting two or three that have had

considerable part of their instruction in the Town school; that

have made some proficiency in reading only there is but little

effected by the large sum Mr Hollis has remitted here from

year to year. The children tha thave been instructed at the boarding

school, have appear'd to make no progress, in civility & vertue

but have rather declin'd, living an idle life, and much without

government, has tended much to their being more [---------]

uncivil and mischievous in their conduct, & behaviour. we have foundmarginal

this to be the case with our children & the Mohawks begin loudly to

complain of he increase of unrulyness in their children.

And we cant but express our wonder, at the odd measures that are gone

into by Sd Kellogg, in order, we must think, to impose on mak-

kind; which is when any persons of distinction, come into the Town

to set these two three young men to read a lesson, pretending that

others that have been instructed for Sixteen, or Seventeen, years are

equal in their proficiency to these, whereas many ten years

ago as forward as they be now. But this being the produce, of thousands

of pounds; the best must be made of altho the world be impos'd

upon thereby. And we begg leave further, to express our Surprize, at

the

Strange report, prefer'd to the great and general respecting the affairs marginal

and people, of, Stockbridge, by Coll Dwight and Capt Ashley which

they prefer'd to the great & general court, at their Session in Novem-

ber last. These gentleman have often express'd their sense of Capt

Kelloggs conduct, in a very different manner, from what they

have done in the Said report. When and by what means these gen-

tlemen, made the discovery, of Capt Kelloggs great knowledge,

of the value of the country of the western tribes of Indians; his

extraordinary talents, & prudence, in the management of Indian

affairs, his Zeal for the crown, and the good of this province.

We must confess ourselves at an intire loss: and must remain so

till Something appears to manifest this late to others, as well as to

them. Tis manifest that both these Gentlemen have view'd Mr Hollis's

cause in a suffering condition, and have often expressed teir dissatisfac-

tion with the Sd Kelloggs conduct, relating hereto. Indeed Coll

Dwight of late has chang'd his voice; but in as much as Capt

Ashley has ever Since he has had concern at the boarding school,

from time to time declar'd himself dissatisfied both with the cond

conduct, & accomps, of Sd Kellogg, we must charitably beleave

that he remains so still. And it is certain that Capt Ashely; has

manifested his disapprobation of setling the female boarding

school, on Mrs Dwights land, repeatedly and long since Coll

Dwight has carried the materials for building the same to that

place. The representation made to the great & general court as if the

inhabitants of this town, or any of them, had been guilty of crimi

nizing, contradicting, counteracting, and violently opposing the gene-

ral courts Committee: especially Capt Kellogg; That endeavours

have been us'd with workmen to desist from their labour, that by

multiplied managements of this kind, together with tattlings and evil

surmisings, which have tended to defeat the charitable designs of Mr

Hollis, & the political views of the government. These [-----] are

very harsh, they sound very ill, And we apprehend no manner of

foundation for such a representation, but we satisfied it was done

with an intent to Stir up the resentment of the court, without cause against

us: which we look upon as the most injurious treatment; Since it

is manifest to us us that these Gentlemen endeavoured to palm these

and many other things upon the great and general court, that have

no foundation, in truth; to gain the courts interposition that they might

the

The better, and more Successfully, carry on their personal, & intrigu[?]marginal

ed Schemes. Their representing a design premidat[at]ed, & concerted,

by certain parsons, to break up the Hollisian school, is a Mere

fiction: much like the school itself. The account they gave the court

of a hundred Mohocks, residing here, and fifty children in

the school, is a mere chimera; a thing that we never had any

reason to believe existed. Thare was in the beginning of Mr

Hawleys Setting up the school, for a little space something more

than thirty, including several grown persons that attended

the school: but this appearance was soon ended: but not as is

unjustly represented, by means of an opposition by the people

of this Town, to the Comtee & Capt Kellogg. And their tens was

never reduced to units by any such methods. But if it ever came

to pass; it was by other means. If the gentlemen have not mistook

in their calculation, it is most like, their number was reduced

by the impolitick and rangling conduct, of the managers: for

we affirm it as a truth, that we have not oppos'd the Comtee in

any thing, respecting their business as Such, neither is thare one

Mohock the less here by means of any thing that we have done

nor has our conduct had, (that we know of,) the least tendency to lessen

their number. [----] we have reason to fear, the conduct of these

Gentlemen, will prove a reason, why thare will not be any here

in a little time. We hope we are not behind hand with the Comtee

themselves, and Capt too. in revering honouring and obeying

the supreme authority of this province. We, esteem their protection

a high and valuable previledge, and their resentment a very

great calamity, and we never intend justly to deserve it:

and we cant but account them our enemies, who would

bring this misery upon us. We confess our ignorance to be such

respecting Capt Kellogggs being here, we did not know that

thare was any order of government relating to him:

and Still apprehend thare is not; but conclude Mr Hollis to be

his principal & Sole improver, and on whose account only

he resides here. And we being so Scituate, as continually

to observe the state and condition of his affairs: And it has ap-

pear'd to us for some years pass'd it has been under such poor

cir

circumstances, so miseraly managed, that there has been a great mis-marginal

applycation of that Gentlemens money, and his Noble and generous

designs almost, if not quite defeated; thereby we under this view of things,

veryly tho't it our duty, as friends to that Gentleman, who is at so extraordina-

ry a charge, to promote Christian knowledge in these parts, to give him

some notice of what we knew of the bad condition of his affairs, which

we did in the following words. -- -- -- -- -- -- -- -- --

'Revd & Honoured Sr. Considering the right you have to proper information

'of the State of your affairs, in this place, for the support of which, you are

'with so extraordinary a charity & generosity, at so great expence, and that

'it is impossible you should have any information, but what must come

'originally from some tha live on the spot. We the subscribers who have

'been a long time inhabitants of the Town of Stockbridge, have thought our-

'selves called in providence, to inform you, that it has been very manifestly

'our observation, that your money that has been expended at the boarding

'school here, since Mr Sergeants death, has not in any good measure answer-

'ed your generous, & pious, intentions, of promoting knowledge, religion,

'civility, & industry among the ignorant, & barbarous Heathen: and we have

'reason to think from what has been very observable, of late, that it has been

'so improved, as rather to be a great hindrance to those good designs, and even

'to the great wrong, of the religious interests, of this place in general; And the

'affair of Capt Kelloggs continuance here, at your charge, since Mr Hawleys

'coming, which has been without any visible, Stated, employment, which

'Has to our observation, been matter of great offence to good people in gene

'ral, in these parts of New England, wo have occasion to observe it.

'And tis very manifest many ways, that you cannot be safe in the ex-

'pence of your moneys here, without committing them to the care

'of the Commisrs in Boston, who are wise & faithfull men, and not

'interested in the misunderstandings that subsist between some per-

'sons of distinction in this western part of New England, about our

'affairs. Or in committing them to some other faithfull trustees.

'We say these things with much regret, having no disposition to injure

'any perticular persons Character, either as to their prudence or fidelity,

'our giving this information, we assure you, Sr, is not from any such

'disposition, but as we hope from a regard to the honour of God,

'& justice to you, and from regard to the interest of the important

'affair of the instruction of the Indians in this place, which di-

'vine providence has of late given the most fair, and happy oppor-

'tunities for And which we hope might still be gloriously ad-

'vanced, if proper measures were taken.

We give the honourable court the trouble of this transcript, because we

understand that some of the Honrble members appear dissatisfied

at this conduct of ours. In this we had no design of intermeddling

in the affairs of government, or doing any thing that should prove marginal

a disadvantage to the design of the instruction of the Indians

for that is a thing we have a disposition from our hearts, to promote

And as none can tax us with a conduct contrary to such a pretence,

it was mere clamour to represent us, the people of this Town as

being any ways instrumental, of confounding the affair of Set

tling the Mohocks in this government. But on the contrary, we

have on all occations manifested, a forwardness to promote it, we

have Shewn ourselves friendly, to the strangers that come among

us, nor have we endeavoured to disaffect them to any, and if they

are so, it is from their own observations. And notwithstanding

we aprehend, we have a just & legal title to the fee of the land in

this Town, we have been willing to suffer Several inconvenien-

ces, in opening a way to receive the Mohocks, till the govern-

ment should provide for them, they have had the use of the

School lands, for two years, that we & our children fitted for

cultivation, and other lands we have provided Since their com-

ing among us, that they have had the use of, and we have

often assisted them in labour, to get something for their sup-

port. But still we must be traduc'd as the authors of ruining

the design; we think this treatment very ill & Severe, and impos'd

on us, the better to answer anotherr design, those gentlemen have

in view. For we can as well account, for their Threatning to give us

a blow on one side, when they intend to strike us on the other, as

we can conceive, how Coll Dwight, & Capt Ashley, have occation to

change their Sentiments about men & things so Suddenly; one while

applauding The Revrd Mr Edwards, as the most Suitable, & capable

person, that could be found for the business he is employed in.

And Capt Kellogg as utterly unfit for the service that he is

employ'd If these gentlemen, pass'd this judgment before they

were acquainted with the merit of the men, this proves that

they were at least too hasty, & precipitant in their judg-

ment. But if it be as Coll Dwight has declar'd, that his high

opinion of Mr Edwards, was founded on a twenty years

acquaintance, we are left at an utter loss to know, why all at

once, he Shifts sides, and becomes an enemy to Mr Edwards.

And now he is an unfit person of no service, nor like to be

Serviceable, here in the work of the Ministry in this place.

Nor any pains, or representations, must be spar'd now, to render

him of a character unsuitable for this service, representing him verymarginal

unjustly: without any reasonable foundation that we can imagine as

a troublesome person, a busibody in other mens matters, one whom

the Indians disaffect, whose preaching is Short, and unintelligible,

and that the Indians are not like to gain much knowledge from

his instructions. Altho he stands approv'd by the Honrble & Revrd

Commisrs whose, approbation, of the qualifications of their

missionaries, have ever been esteem'd Sufficient till now.

And is esteem'd by the inhabitants of the Town, both english, and

Indians, as a faithfull and good minister, in whom we take in-

tire satisfaction. And we think it a very extradinary piece of

conduct, in Coll Dwight, Capts Williams, & Kellogg, that they

should so resolutly undetake to deprive us of a minister, that

we have the highest value of.

Two of these Gentlemen have no real esate in the Town, and

are but just inhabitants among us, and Neither of them have

been at cost or pains, in procuring or Settling the Minister.

And it looks very ill to us, that Coll Dwight, and Capt Kellogg,

should take so much upon them, respecting this church and

congregation, as to determine against our choice of a minister,

seeing, they don't properly belong to either. We think this is an

unreasonable, and violent breaking in upon our previledges

ecclesiastical previledges, and has a great tendency to per-

petuate our disturbances, and to bring us into a broken,

and miserable circumstances. And we have great and just rea-

son to complain to the Honourable court, of the conduct of these Gentle

men, whose restless haughty and Selfish conduct, we veryly think to be

the foundation of all our difference, and the calamity we Suffer thereby.

They are offended, because we don't view them in the same light that they view

themselves: proper persons to lord it over us, whose conduct must never be tho't

amiss by any of us; and like lame dupes not only quietly to suffer them

to break this church in pieces, & to thro' this Town & church into the

utmost Confusion, by removing the Minister; which we have so

lattely Settled to the Satisfaction of the whole Town, but would

have us yield up our judgments in the same manner.

We have no controversy with those Gentlemen with respect to the business

that they are employ'd in, but only as they trouble the Society in taking

upon them to act those things that don't belong to them, as e

know of in any capacity at all. Indeed they are the men that are

medlers, in other mens matters, tho they endeavour unjustly to render us

odious by charging this upon us, allowing themselves in that, which they

condemn in another. Indeed we judge, and as things appear to us, & we marginal

cant judge otherways, that with all the great expence of the province;

and [--] what Mr Hollis has been at too, thare is very little effected

in carrying into execution the designs of either. We who gave the lands

for the encouragement of the school, have yet seen no appear-

ance, of any thing that was promised us, when we were apply'd

to for Said lands, but has hitherto serv'd rather for a retreat of

persons that set themselves against the good of the Inhabitants

of the Town, some commanding it as the agents of the Govern-

ment, and others as Mr Holliss. Tho we never heard of its being ap-

propriated in either of these ways. Nor ever gave the Lands on

other conditions then that if we did not find matters conducted

as was promised, the Land should revert to us again, which

Mr Sergeant gave us his promise. Mr Woodbridge has propos'd

to us the governments expectations of Sd lands being se-

cur'd for the use of the school, we are willing to comply with

this, provided it be not a place of contention, & confusion, as it

has hitherto been, and is like to be, So long as the persons that

are upon retain their dispositions. which are we apprehend to

deprive us of our liberty, and priviledges, as free Subjects of the

government, and rather than not bow & buckle us to their schemes,

would overturn & bring to an end, all the designs of promoting

christian knowledge among us. We don't pretend to any right to

order in the affair that belong to these Gentlemen, much less would

we do any thing tha tshould hiner the good design intended. They

are accountable to their principals, and we only think we have

a right to inform them, and to defend ourselves against any

ill contrivance, they may be carrying on against us.

We are tired with contention, and the calamities that attend it

we waited on Coll Dwight soon after his return from Boston,

desir'd him to give His reasons for engaging to Zealously

to remove the minister we are he has no right to do and that

it cant be effected without injuring his Character, & usefull

ness in the world more than would be done conistent with

justice, we propos'd to him if he had reasons that were Sufficient

we would attend to them, but if he had not we hop'd to convince

him, that he was acting an unreasonable part, but he

treated us with contempt; and told us that we came in an odd

manner, and upon an errand as odd, that he was not oblig'd to give

us his reasons, but let us know, that he did not think Mr Edwards

would be usefull among us, Tho he might have been he told

If we had perswaded our minister, (not his) timely to have fallenmarginal

in with his Schemes, which if he had have been perswaded into, we en-

tertaining the same opinion of that Gentlemans Schemes, that

we have hitherto done, we Should be as willing for his dismission

as he is. We know not how Coll Dwight, would make him more usefull,

he gift of tongues or any other miraculous power, he has not to be-

stow. And he stands in no need of his assistance, to be an able, faith-

full, & painfull minister: for he justly Sustains that Character already.

But we think the Gentleman must mean if anything, that if Mr

Edwards had but have prostituted his judgment, at all perad-

ventures to that Gentlemans, & Capt Kelloggs Schemes, he

would have given him a different to the world, then what he

has lately done. Thus we have as truly and plainly as we can

laid before the Honourable Court, the miserable State and Con-

dition of the Town by reason of these unhappy contentions.

Humbly praying that the great & general Court would

be pleas'd according to their great wisdom & goodness to in-

terpose for our relief, that we may with peace & quietness,

enjoy our rights, & previledges, both civil and religious,

according to our Excellent constitutions; And your

memorialist Shall as in duty bound Ever pray &c
