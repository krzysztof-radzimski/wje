A78. Letter to Thomas Gillespie, September 4, 1747 078. JE-T.Gillespie 9-4-47 Jonathan Edwards

A078. JE to Thomas Gillespie, 9/4/47. ANTS, f. 1747, #1. Addressed on cover "To the Rev. / Mr Thomas Gillespie / Minister of the Gospel / at Carnock in / Scotland."

Northampton Septem. 4. 1747.

Rev. & dear Sir,

I received your Letter of Novem. 24. 1746. tho' very long after it was written. I thank you for it, & for your offering me a Correspondence with you: such an offeri I shall gladly embrace, and esteem it a great Priviledge; more especially from the Character I have received of you from Mr Abercrombie,1 who I perceive was intimately acquainted with you.

As to the Objections you make against some Things contained in my late Book on religious Affections, I am sorry you did not read the Book through before you made Them; if you had, perhaps the difficulties would not have appeared quite so great. As to what you is contain'd in the 74 & 75 Pages, I suppose there is not the least difference of Opinion between you & me, unless it be concerning the signification & Propriety of Expressions. I am fully of your mind, and alwaies was, without the least doubt of it, "That every one, both saint & sinner, is indispensably bound, at all seasons, by the divine Authority, to believe instantly on the Lord Jesus, And that the Command of the Lord 1 John. 3. 23. That we should believe on the name of his son Jesus Christ, as 'tis a Prescription of the moral Law, no less binds the sinner to immediate Performance, than the Commandment not to kill, To keep the Sabbath day, or any other duty, as to the Present Performance of which, in way of duty, all agree the sinner is bound; -and that men are bound to Trust the divine Faithfulness, be their Case, with Respect to Light or darkness, sight &c. what it will; and that no Situation they can be in looses them from Obligation to glorify the Lord at all seasons, and expecting Fulfilment of his word"2 And that while the sinner that is without spiritual Light or sight is bound to believe, & that it is a duty that is at that very Time incumbent on Him to believe. But I conceive that there is a great deal of difference between these two things, viz. its being a mans duty that is without spiritual Light or sight to believe, & its being a his duty to believe without spiritual Light or sight, or to believe while he yet remains without spiritual Light or sight ; Just the same difference that is between these two Things, viz. Its being his duty that has no Faith to believe, and its being his duty to believe without Faith, or to believe without believing. I trust there is none will assert the latter, because of the contradiction that it implies. As 'tis not proper to say 'tis a mans duty to believe without Faith, because it implies a contradiction, so I think it equally improper to say 'tis a mans duty to believe without marginal those things that are essentially implied in Faith, because that also implies a Contradiction. But a spiritual sight of Christ or Knowledge of Christ is essentially implied in the very nature and notion of Faith; & therefore 'tis absurd to talk of believing in Christ without spiritual Light or sight. 'Tis a the duty of a man that is without those Things that essentially belong to faith, to believe; and tis the duty of a man that is without those things that essentially belong to Love, to Love God ; because 'tis an Indispensable obligation that lies on all men, at all Times, & in all Circumstances, to Love God : but yet 'tis not a mans duty to Love God without loving him, or yet continuing without those things that essentially belong to his Love. 'Tis the duty of those that have no sense of the Loveliness of God, & have no Esteem of Him, to Love him; & they bent in the least excused by the want of this sense & Esteem, in not loving Him one moment ; but yet it would be properly nonsense to say 'tis their duty to Love him without any sense of his Loveliness, or any Esteem of Him ; Tho' it be his duty this moment to come out of his disesteem & stupid in wicked insensibility of his Loveliness, & Love Him. I made the distinction, I thought, very plainly, in the midst of those sentences you quote as exceptionable; I say expressly p. 74 "It is truly the duty of those who are thus in darkness, to come out of darkness into Light and believe; but that They should confidently believe and trust, while They yet Remain without spiritual Light or sight, is an Antiscriptural and absurd doctrine.["] The misunderstanding between us, dear sir, I suppose to lie in the different Application of the Particle without, in my use of it, and your Understanding of it, or what we understand as spoken of & supposed without spiritual Light or sight : As I use it, I apply it to the Act of believing, and I suppose it to be very absurd to talk of an act of Faith without spiritual ; wherein I suppose you will allow me to be in the Right of it. As you understand it it is applied to duty or Obligation; and you suppose it to be not at all absurd to talk of an Obligation to Faith without spiritual Light or sight, but that the Obligation remains full where there is no spiritual Light or sight ; wherein I allow you are in the right of it. I think sir, if you read what I have said in my Book on this Head again, it will be exceeding apparent to you, that 'tis thus that I apply the Proposition without, & not as you before understood it. I thought I had very plainly manifested that what I meant by being in darkness, was a being in marginal spiritual Blindness, & so in a dead, stupid, Carnal & unchristian Frame and way, and not what is commonly called a being without the Light of Gods Countenance, under the Hidings of his Face &c. we have a great number of People in these Parts that go on that supposition, in their Notions & Practice, that there really is such a Thing as a such a manner of believing, such a Kind of Faith as this, viz. a confident believing & firm Trusting in God in the dark, in the sense mention'd, That is to be sought after, and is the subject matter of divine Prescription, And which many Actually have. And indeed there are inummerable Instances of such as in are apparently in a most senseless, careless, Negligent, Apostate, & every way, unChristian and wicked Frame, that yet, on this Princi encouraged by this Principle, do retain an exceeding strong Confidence of their good state, & count that herein they do their duty, & give much Glory to God, under the notion of trusting God in the dark, & Hoping against Hope; & not trusting in their own Righteousness; and They suppose it would shew a legal spirit for 'em to do other wise. I thought it would be manifest to every Reader that I was arguing against such a sort of People.

You say "It merits Consideration whether the Believer should ever doubt of his state, on any Account whatever, because doubting, as opposed to believing, is absolutely sinful." Here sir, you seem to supposng that a Persons doubt ing of his own good Estate, is the proper opposite of Faith, & these & some other Expressions in your Letter seem to suppose that doubting of ones Good Estate and Unbelief is the same Thing, & so that being confident of ones good Estate and Faith are the same Thing ; which I acknowledge I dont understand ; I don't take Faith, and a Persons believing that They have Faith to be the same Thing : nor do I take unbelief, or being without Faith, & doubting whether They have it, to be the same Thing, but entirely different. ----I should have been glad either you had taken a little more notice what I say on this Head p. 76, 77 or said something to convince me that I am wrong in this Point. The Exercise of Faith is doubtless the way to be delivered from darkness, deadness, Backsliding &c. or rather is the deliverance; as Forsaking sin is the way to deliverance from sin & is the deliverance it self. The Exercise of Grace is doubtless the way to deliverance from a Graceless Frame, that consists in the want of the Exercise of Grace. But as to what you say, of a or seem to intimate of a Persons being confident of his own good Estate, as being the way to be delivered from darkness, deadness, & Backsliding & prevailing Iniquity, I think, whoever supposes this to be Gods method of delivering his saints, when sunk into an evil, careless, Carnal & unchristian Frame, first to assure 'em of their good Estate & his Favour, & by that means to marginal while they yet remain in such an Frame, & so to make that the means of their deliverance, does surely mistake Gods method of dealing with such Persons. among all the multitudes I had opportunity to observe, I never knew one dealt with after this manner. I have known many brought back from great declensions, that appeared to me to be true saints, but it was in a way very diverse from this. In the first Place Conscience has been awaken'd, and They have been brought into great Fear of the wrath of God, having his Favour hid; and They have been the subjects of a kind of new work of Humiliation, brought to a great sense of their deservings of Gods wrath, even while They have yet feared it, becau before God has delivered Them from the Apprehension of it, and Comforted Them with a renewed sense of his Favour.

As to what I say of the necessity of Universal obedience, or one way of known sin, being Exception enough against a mans salvation I should known better what to have said further about it, if you had briefly shewn how the scriptures that I mention, & the Arguments I deduce from them are insufficient for the Proof of this Point. I confess they appear to me to prove it as fully as any Thing concerning the necessary Qualifications of a true saint can be proved from scripture.

You object against my saying p. 276. "nor can a true saint ever fall away, so that it shall come to that this, that ordinarily there shall be no remarkable difference in his walk & Behaviour since his Conversion from what was before" what I say, I think implies no more that his walk & behaviour over the same Ground, in like Circumstances, & under like Trials, will have a remarkable difference. As to the Instances you mention of david & Solomon, I don't know that the scrip tures gives us any where so much of a History of their walk & Behaviour before their Conversion, as to put us into any proper Capacity of Comparing their After walk with their Former. These Examples are uncertain ; But I think those doctrines of the scripture are not uncertain, which I mention in the Place you cite, to confirm the Point which teach that Converts are new men, new Creatures, that they renewed not only within, but without, that old Things are pass'd away, and all things become new, that They walk in newness of Life, that their Members of their bodies are new, that whereas they before were the servants of sin, & yielded their members servants of Iniquity to Iniquity, now They yield Them servants of Righteous ness unto Holiness. marginal

As to those doubts & Cases of difficulty you mention, I should think it very needless for a divine of your Character, to Apply your self to me for a solution of [---------] difficulties, However since you are pleased to insist on my giving my mind upon Them, I would observe, as to the first Case you mention, of a Person incessantly harassed by satan &c. You dont say of what nature the Temptations are that Je is harassed with; Nut I think it impossible to give proper Advice and directions without knowing this. satan is to be resisted in a very different manner, in Case of different Kinds of Onsets. when Persons are harassed with those strange, horrid Injections, that melancholick Persons are often subject to, He is to be resisted in a very different manner from what is proper in Case of vio lent Temptations, to gratify some worldly Lust. In the former Case I should by no means advise a Person to resist the devil by entering the Lists with Him, and vehemently engaging their Minds in an earnest dispute & violent struggle with the grand Adversary ; But rather by diverting the mind from his frightful suggestions, by going on steadfastly & dili gently in the ordinary course of duty, without allowing Themselves Time & Leisure to attend to the devils sophistry, or viewing his frightful Representations, committing Themselves to God by Prayer in this way, without anxiety about what had been suggested. That is the best way of resisting the devil that crosses his design most; & He more effectually disappoints Him in such Cases, that treats Him with Neglect, than He that attends so much to him, as to engage in a direct Conflict, and goes about to try his strength and skill with Him, in a violent dispute or Combat. The latter Course rather gives Him Advantage, than any Thing 'tis what He would: if he g can get Persons thus engaged in a violent He gains a great Point : He knows that melanchollick Persons are not fit for it: By this He gains that Point, of diverting & taking off the Person which is one great Thing He aims at : and by this, having now having gain'd the Persons attention to what He says, He has opportunity to use all his Craft & subtilty: & by this struggle, He raises melancholick vapours to a greater degree & further weakens his mind & gets Him faster & faster in his snares, He increases the Persons Anxiety of mind, which is the very thing by which mainly He ful fils all his Purposes with such Persons.

Concerning the other difficulty you mention relating to the verifying of Rom. 8. 20. All Things shall work together for Good &c. in a saint that falls under Backslidings & spiritual decay &c. It seems to be a matter of some difficulty to understand exactly how this is to be taken, & how far it may from marginal hence be infer'd that the Temptations the saints meet with from satan & an evil world, and their own declensions & sins shall surely work for their Good. However, since you desire my Thoughts, I would express Them, such as They are, as follows'

In order rightly to state this <atter, there are two Things may be laid down, as {ositions of certain & indubitable Truth concerning this doctrine of the Apostle.

I. The meaning cannot be that Gods dispensations & disposals towards each saint are the best for Him, most tending to his Happiness of all that are possible; or that all Things That are ordered for Him, or done by God with Respect to Him, are in all Respects better for Them Him than any Thing else that God could have ordered or done, issuing in the highest Good & Happiness that it is possible He should be brought to. For that would be as much as to say, that God will bestow on every one of his Elect as much Happiness as he can, in the utmost Exercise of his Omnipotence. and this sets aside all these different degrees of Grace and Holiness here, & Glory hereafter, which He bestows according to his sovereign Pleasure.

All Things may work together for Good to the saints, All may be of Benefit to them, & may have concurring Tendency to their Happiness, & may all finally issue in it, & yet not tend to, or issue in the highest degree of Good & Happiness possible . There is a certain measure of Holiness & Happiness, that each one of the Elect is eternally appointed to; And all Things that relate to Him work together to bring to pass this appointed mea sure of Good. The Text & Context speaks of Gods eternal Purpose of Good to the Elect, predestinatingting Them to a Conformity to his Son in Holiness & Happiness; & the implicit Reasoning of the Apostle leads us to suppose that all Things will surely concur to bring to Effect Gods eternal Purpose. And therefore from his Reasoning it may be infer'd, that all Things will tend to, & work together to bring to pass, that degree of Good that God has purposed to bestow upon Them, & not any more. And indeed it would be in it self unreasonable to suppose any Thing else but this. Inasmuch as God is the supream orderer of all Things, doubtless all Things shall be so ordered, that with one consent, They shall help to bring to pass his Aims, Ends, & Purposes : But surely not to bring to pass what He does not aim at, & never intended. God in his Government of the world is carrying on his own designs in every Thing ; but he is not carrying on that which is not his design. And therefore there is no need of supposing that All the Circumstances, Means & Advantages of every saint, are the best in every Respect that God could have ordered for Him, or that there could have been no Circumstances or means that He could have been the subject of, that would, with Gods usual Blessing on means, have issued in his greater marginal Good. Every saint is as it were a living stone, that in this present State of Preparation, is fitting for the Place appointed for Him in the heavenly Temple. And in this sense all Things undoubtedly work together for the Good to every one that is called according to Gods Purpose, He is all the while he lives in this world, by all the dispensations of Providence towards Him, fitting for the particular mansion in Glory that is appointed and prepared for Him, or Hewing for his appointed Place in the heavenly Building . And

II. Another Thing which is no less certain & demonstrable than the Position that has been already laid down, & indeed follows from it, is this; when it is said All Things work together for Good &c. Thereby cannot be intended All Things both Positive & negative Or that it is so universally, that not only every Thing positive Thing that the saints are the subjects of, or are concerned with will work for their Good, but also that when any Thing is absent or witheld from Them by God in his Providence, that Absence or witholding is also for their Good, or better for Them than the Presence or Bestowment would have been. For this would have the same absurd Consequence that was mention'd before, viz. that God makes every saint as happy as possibly He can. And besides, if so, it would follow that Gods witholding greater degrees of the sanctifying Influences of his spirit is for the saints Good, & that it is best for Them to live and die so low in Grace as They do. which would be as much as to say, that 'tis for their Good to have no more Good, or that it is for their Happiness to have no more Happiness here and hereafter. If we take good notice notice of the Apostles discourse in Rom. 8. it will be apparent that his words imply no such Thing. All Gods Creatures, And all that God does in disposing of Them is for the Good of the saint. But it will not thence follow that all Gods Forbearing to do is also for his Good, or that it is best for Him that God does no more for Him.

Therefore the following Things I humbly conceive to be the Truth concerning the sins & Temptations of the saints being for their Good.

1. That All Things whatsoever are for the Good of the saints, Things negative as well as positive, in that sense, that God intends that some Benefit to Them, shall arise from every Thing. so that something of the Grace and Love of God, will be hereafter to be seen to have been exercised towards Them in every Thing; altho' at the same Time, the sovereignty of God will also be to be seen, with Regard to the measure of the Good or Benefit Aim'd at; & some other Things, if God had seen Cause to order Them, would have produced an higher Benefit. And with Regard to negative disposals, consisting not in Gods doing, but forbearing to do, not in giving, but witholding, some Benefit, in some Respect or other, will ever accrue to the saints, even from These : Tho' sometimes the Benefit will not be equal to the Benefit marginal witheld, if it had been bestowed. As for Instance, when a saint lives & dies comparatively low in Grace, There is some good Improvement shall be made, even of this, in the eternal state of the saint, whereby he shall receive a real Benefit; Tho' the Benefit shall not be equal to the Benefit of an higher degree of Holiness, if God had bestow'd it.

2. God carries on a design of Love to his People, and to each individual saint, not only in all Things that They are the subjects of while They live, but also in all his works & disposals, & all his Acts ad extra, from Eternity to Eternity.

3. That the sin of the saints in general, is for their good, & for the best in this Respect, That it is a Thing that, through the sovereign Grace of God, & his infinite wisdom, will issue in a high Advancement of their Eternal Happiness, that They have been sinful fallen Creatures, & not from the Benef Beginning perfectly innocent & holy, as the elect Angels; And that They shall obtain some additional Good on occasion of all the sin they have been the subjects of, or have committed, beyond what They would have had if They never had been fallen Creatures.

4. The sin & Corru of the saints in this sense cannot be for their Good, that it should finally be best for Them, that while They lived in this world, their Restoration & Recovery from the Corruption They became subject to by the Fall, was no greater, The Mortification of sin & spiritual vivification of their souls carried on to no greater degree, that They remained so sinfully desti deficient, as to Love to God, Christian Love to men, Humility, Heavenly-mindedness &c. And that They were so barren, and did so few good works; And consequently, that in general, They had They much sin & of the Exercises of it, & not more Holiness & of the Exercises of that & Fruits of that; (for in proportion as one of These is more, the other will be less, as infallibly, as darkness is more or less, in Proportion to the diminution or Increase of Light.) It can't finally be better for the saints, that in general, while They lived, They had so much sin of Heart & Life, rather than more Holi ness of Heart & Life. Because the Reward of all at last [-] will be according to their works, And He that sowed sparingly shall reap sparingly, & He that sowed bountifully shall reap also bountiful ly; & He that builds wood Hay & stubble shall finally suffer Loss, & have a less reward, than if he had built Gold, silver & precious stones, Tho' He himself shall be saved. But notwithstanding This,

5. In The sins & Falls of the saints may be for their Good, & for the better, in This Respect, That the Issue may be better than if the Temptation had not happen'd, & so the occasion not given, either for the sin of yielding to the Temptation, or the virtue of overcoming it ; And yet not in that Respect (with Regard to their sins or Falls in general) that it should be better for Them in the Issue, that They marginal have yielded to the Temptations offered, than if They had overcome. For the fewer victories They obtain over Temptation, The fewer their good works, and espe particularly of that Kind of Good works to which a distinguished in Reward is promised in Rev. IId. & IIId. & in many other Parts of scripture. The word of God represents the work of a Christian in this world by a warfare; and 'tis evident by the scripture that He that acquits Himself as the best soldier shall win the greatest Prize. Therefore, when the saints are brought into Backslidings & decays, by being overcome by Temptations, The Issue of their Backslidings may be some Good to Them, They may receive some Benefit by Occasion of it, beyond what they would have received if the Temptation & never happen'd; & yet their Backslidings may be a great Loss to them in that Respect, That They shall have much less Reward than if the Temptations had been overcome, & They notwithstanding had persevered in spiritual vigour & diligence. But yet this dont hinder but that,

6. T It may be so ordered by a sovereign & Alwise God, that the saints Falls & Backslidings, through their being overcome by Temptation may in some particular Instances, may prove best for Them; not only that the Issue may be greater Good to them than they would have received if the Temptation had not Happen'd, but greater, than in that Instance, than if the Temptation had been overcome. It may be so ordered that their being overcome by that Temptation, shall be an occasion of their having greater strength, & in the whole, obtaining more and greater victories, than if they had not fallen in that Instance : But it cannit be so that in the general, it should prove better for Them that They are foil'd so much, & overcome so little, in the Course of their Lives, & that Th finally their decay is so great, or their Progress so small. From these Things it appears,

7. That the saying of the Apostle All Things work together for Good to that love God, Tho' it be fulfill'd in some Respect to all saints, & at all Times, & all Circumstances, yet is fulfilled more especially & eminently to the saints continuing in the Exercise of Love to God, not faili falling from the Exercises, or failing of the Fruits of [-] divine Love in Times of Trial : Then Temptations, Enemies & sufferings will be every best for them, working that which is most for their Good every way ; And They shall be more than Conquerors over Tribulation, distress, Persecution, Famine, Nakedness, Peril & sword Rom. 8. 35-37.

8. As God is carrying on a design of Love to each individual saint, in all his works & disposals whatsoever, as was observed before, so the particular design of Love to them that he is carrying on, is to fit marginal them for, & bring 'em to their appointed Place in the Heavenly Temple, or to that individual, precise Happiness & Glory in Heaven that his eternal Love designed for Them, & no other; (for Gods design of Love, or of Happiness to Them, is it only just what it is, & is not different from it self;) And to fulfil this particular design of Love, every Thing that does, or in any respect disposes, whether it be positive, privative or negative contributes. because doubtless every thing that God does, or in any Respect orders, tends to fulfil his Aims & designs. Therefore undoubtedly

9. All the while the saint lives in the world, He is fitting for his appointed mansion in Glory, & hewing for his Place in the heavenly Building : And fo All his Temptations, Tho' they may occasion, for the present, great spiritual wounds, yet at last, They shall be an occasion of th his being more fitted for his Place in Glory. And therefore we may determine, that however the true saint may die, in some Respects, under decays, under the decay of Comfort, & of the Exercise of some religious Affections, yet every saint dies at the Time when his Habitual Fitness for his Place in the heavenly Temple is most com pleat. because otherwise, all things that happen to Him while He lives, would not work together to fit Him for that Place.

10 God brings his saints at the End of their Lives, to his greatest Fitness for their Place in Heaven, not by diminishing Grace in their Hearts, but by increasing it, & carrying on the work of Grace in their souls. otherwise that cannot be true, that where God has begun a good he will perform or it, or carry it on to the day of Christ. for if they die with a less degree of Grace than they had before, then it ceases to be carried on before the day of Christ comes. If Grace is finally diminished, then satan so far finally obtains the victory, He finally prevails to diminish the fire in the smoking Flax. and then is that promise verified, that God will not Quench the smoking Flax, till He bring forth Judgment unto victory? so that it must needs be, that altho' [-] saints may die under decays in some Respects, yet They never die under a real decay of the work of Grace in General. If They fall, They shall rise again before They die, & rise higher than before; if not in Joys, & some other Affections, yet in greater degrees of spiritual Knowledge, self-emptiness, trust in God & solidity & Ripeness of Grace.

If these Things that have been observed are true, then we may infer from Them These Corollaries.

1st. That notwithstanding the Truth of that saying of the marginal Apostle Rom. VIII. 28. The saints have Cause to lament their Leanness & Barrenness, & that They are guilty of so much sin; not only, as it is to the dishonour of God, but also as that which is like to their own eternal Loss & damage.

2dly. That nothing can be infer'd from the foremention'd Promise, tending to set aside, or make void the Influence of Motives to earnest Endeavours to avoid all sin, to increase in Holiness, & abound in Good works, from an view to aim at an high & eminent degree of Glory and Happiness in the eternal world.

3dly. That Tho' it is to the eternal damage of the saints, ordinarily, when They yield to, & are overcome by Temptations, yet satan & other Enemies of the saints, by whom their Temptations come, are alwaies wholly disappointed in their Temptations, & baffled in their design to hurt the saints, Inasmuch as The Temptation, & the sin that comes by it, is for the saints Good, and They receive no a greater Benefit, in the Issue, than if the Temptation had not been, & yet less than if the Temptation had been overcome.

As to Mr Boston's view of the Covenant of Grace, I have had some Opportunity with and I confess I did not understand his scheme deliver'd in that Book. I have read his fourfold state of man, & liked exceeding well. I think He therein shews Himself to be a truly great divine.3

Hoping that you will accept my Letter with Candour, & remember me in your Prayers, I subscribe my self, your affectionate & obliged Brother

& servt,

Jonathan Edwards.
