A228. Letter to John Erskine (Draft), August 3, 1757 228.JE-J.Erskine 8-3-57 Jonathan Edwards

A228. JE to John Erskine, 8/3/57. ANTS, f. 1756-59A, #8, 10. Draft. Cover for JE's letter to Erskine of July 25, 1757.

Stockbridge, August 3, 1757

Rev. & dear Sir,

In June last I received a Letter from you, dated January

22. 1757. with Mr. Anderson's Complaint verified, and Ob-

jections to the Essays examined.1 For these Things I now re-

turn you my hearty Thanks.

The Conduct of the Vindicator of the Essays, agai from Objecti-

ons made against them, seems to be very many things are

produced from Calvin, & several Calvinistic Writers, to defend

what is not objected against : his Book is almost wholly taken up

about that which is nothing to the Purpose ; perhaps only to amuse

& blind the Common People. According to you Proposal I have

drawn up Something, Stating the difference between my Hypothe-

sis & that of the Essays ; which I here send to you, to be printed

in Scotland, if it be thought best ; or to be disposed of as you

think proper. I have written it as a Letter to you : and if it be

published, it may be as Letter from me to a Minister in Scot-

land. Lord Kaims's Notion of God's deceiving mankind by

a Kind invincible or natural Instinct of or feeling, leading them

to suppose that they have a Liberty of Continence & se Contin-

gence, and self-determination of will, in Order to make 'em

believe themselves Themselves & others to be blamed or praised

for what they do, is a strange Notion indeed ; and it is hard

for me to Conjecture what his views could be in publishing

such Things to the World. However, by what I have heard, In

an Some others seem to be so far of the same Mind, that they

Think, that if it be really true that there is no self-determin-

ing Power in the Will, as opposed to any such moral necessi-

ty as I speak of, Consisting in a certain Connection between Mo-

tive & volitions, 'tis of mischievous Tendency to say any thing

of it, that it is best the Truth in this Matter should not be

known by any Means. I cannot but be of an extremely diffe-

rent Mind. On the contrary I think the Notion of Liberty,

as consisting in a contingent Self-determination of the will, as

necessary to the morality of Men's dispositions & actions, as an

is almost inconceivably pernicious ; and that the contrary Truth

is one of the most important Truths of moral Philosophy that

ever was discuss'd & the most necessary to be known; And

that for want of it Those Schemes of Reli Morality & Religion,

which are a Kind of Infidel schemes, entirely diverse from the

Virtue & Religion of the Bible, and wholly inconsistent

with & subversive of the main things belonging to the Gos-

pel Scheme, have so vastly & so long prevailed, & have stood

in Such Strength. And I think whoever imagines that he,

or any body else shall ever see the doctrines effectually main-

tained against these Adversaries, till the Truth in this Matter be

settled

settled, imagines a vain thing. For allow these Adversaries marginal

what they maintain & this Point, & I think they have strict

demonstration against us. And not only have these Errours

a most pernicious Influence in the publick religious Controversies

that are maintain'd in the World, But such sort of Notions

have a most fatal Influence many ways on the souls of men

all Ranks in all Transactions between God & their souls.

The longer I live, & the more I have to do with the souls

of Men in the work of the Ministry, the more I See of this.

Notions of this sort are one of the main Hindrances of the success of

the preaching of the word, & other means of Grace, in the Conver-

sion of Sinners. This especially of appears when Sinners the

Minds of Sinners are afected with some Concern for their souls,

& they are stir'd up to seek their Salvation. Nothing is more neces-

sary for men, in such Circumstances than thorough Conviction & Humi-

liation, that their Consciences should be properly convinced of their

real Guilt & sinfulness, in the Sight of God, & their deserving of

his God's wrath. But who is there, that has had Experience of the

work of a Minister, in dealing with souls in such Circumstances

that don't find, that the Thing that mainly prevents this,

is mens excusing themselves with their moral Inability? & the

moral necessity of Those Things wherein their exceeding Guilt

& sinfulness in the sight of God, most fundamentally & mainly

Consists : such as living from day to day without one spark

of true Love to the God of Infinite Glory, & Fountain of all

Good. their having greater Complacence in the little vile

Things of this World than in Him , their Living in a Re-

jection of X, with all his Glorious Benefits, & dying Love;

& after all the Exhibitions of his Glory & Grace, having their

Hearts still as Cold as Stone towards Him. their living

in such Ingratitude for that[-] infinite mercy of his laying

down his Life for sinners. They, it may be, think of some

Instances of Lewd Behaviour, Lying, dishonesty, Intemperance,

Profaneness, &c-- But the Grand Principles of Iniquity, con-

stantly abiding & ap reigning, from whence all Proceeds

are overlook'd ; Conscience don't Condemn 'em for these things,

because they cant Love God of themselves, they cant believe of

thems. & the like . they rather lay the blame of these

Things, & their other wicked reigning wicked dispositions

of Heart to God, & secretly charge him with all the Blame.

These things are very much, for want being thoroughly instruc-

ted in that great & important Truth & certain Truth, that

a bad will, or an evil disposition of Heart, it self is wickedness;

'tis wickedness in its very Being, Nature & Essence ; without Con-

sidering & not only the Occasion of it, or the determining

Influence, that it was at first owing to. some it may will

Say, they own 'tis their Fault that they have so bad a Heart,

that they have no Love to God God, no true Faith in Christ marginal

& Gratitude to him, because they ha have been careless, & slothfull

in Times Past, & have not used means to obtain a better

Heart, as they should have done : and it may be they are

taught that they are to blame for their Wickedness of Heart,

for they brought as it were brought it on themselves, by in Adam,

by the sin which he voluntarily committed, which sin is justly

Charged to their Account ; which perhaps they don't deny:

but how far are these from being a proper Conviction of

their Wickedness, in their Enmity to God & Christ? To be

Convinced of the sin of something that long ago was the

Occasion of this Enmity & to be convinced of the Enmi-

t Wickedness of the Enmity it self, are quite two Things.

And if the sinners under some awakening find the Exercise

of Corruption of Heart, in as it appears in a great many ways

in their meditations, Prayers & other Religious duties, and

on Occasion of their Fears of Hell &c. &c. still this Notion

of their Inability to help it excusing them, keeps 'em from

proper Conviction of sin herein . Fears of Hell tend to

convince men of their Hardness of their Hearts. But then when

they find how hard their Hearts are, & how far from a pro-

per sensibility & Affection in things of Religion, they are

kept from properly condemning themselves for it, from th

the moral necessity For the moral very Notion of Hardness

of Heart implies moral Inability: the Harder the Heart

is, the more dead is it in sin, & the more unable to Regard

Thus the strength of sin is made the Excuse for sin.

And thus I have known many under Fears of Hell justifying

or excusing themselves, at least implicitly, in horrid workings

of Enmity against God, in blasphemous Thoughts &c.

'Tis of great Importance that they that are seeking their

Salvation should be brought off from all dependence on

their own Righteousness : But these Notions, above all

Things prevent it : They justify themselves in the Since-

rity of their Endeavours. They say to themselves, they do

what they can, they take great Pains; & tho' there be

great Imperfection in what they do, & many evil workings

of Heart arise, yet those they can't help: Here moral

necessity comes in as an excuse. Things of this kind have

visibly been the main hindrance of the true Humiliation &

Conversion of sinners, in the Times of awakening, that

have been in this Land, every where, in all Parts, &

as I have had an opportunity to observe in very many

Places. When the Gospel is preach'd, & its offers & Invita-

tions & motives most powerfully urged, & sinners Hearts

stand

out, here is their strong hold, their sheet Anchor : were it not marginal

for this, they would either Comply, or their Hearts would con-

demn them for their horrid Guilt in not complying.

And if the Law of God be preached in its strictness & spiri

tuality, yet Conscience is not properly convinced by it ; &

they justify themselves with their Inability; & the design &

End of the Law, is as a schoolmaster to fit for Christ, is

defeated. Thus both the Law & the Gospel are prevented

from having their proper Effect.

The doctrine of a self-determined will, as the Ground of all

moral Good & Evil, tends to prevent any proper Exercises of

Faith in God & Christ, & the so in the Affair of Our salvation,

as it tends to prevent all dependence upon them. For instead

of this, it teaches a kind of absolute Independence in Tho all

those Things that are of Chief Importance in this Affair.

our Righteousness depending originally on our own Acts, as

self-determined. Thus our own Holiness is from our selves

as its determining Cause, & its original & highest source.

And as for imputed Righteousness that have any merit

at all in it, to be sure, there can be no such thing : for

self-determination is necessary to Praise & merit : But what is

imputed from another is not from our self-determination or

Action. so that all [ ] & truly, in this scheme man is not

dependent on God; but God is rather dependent on man in

this affair : For he only acts Consequentially, in Acts in which

he depends on what he sees we determine & do first.

The nature of true Faith implies a disposition to give

all the Glory of our salvation to God & Christ. but

this Notion is inconsistent with it : for in Effect gives the

Glory wholly to man. For that is the very doctrine that is

taught, that the merit & Praise is his, whose is the de

determination of what is de the Praiseworthy deed. so that,

on the whole, I think it must be a Miracle indeed, if ever

Men are Converted that have imbibed Such Notions as

these, and are under their Influence in their religious Concerns.

Yea these Notions tend effectually to prevent Men's ever

seeking after Conversion with any earnestness. 'Tis manifest that

Men never will be in earnest in this Matter, 'till they are Awa-

k their Consciences are awaken'd, and they are made sensible

of God's Anger, and their danger of suffering the Terrible Effects

of it. But that Stupidity which is opposed to this Awakening,

is upheld chiefly by these two Things : Their Insensibility of

their Guilt in what is past & present; and Flattering them-

selves as to the future. These Notions of Liberty of Contingence,

Indifference and Self-determination, as Essential to merit Guilt

or Merit, tend to preclude all sense of Gui any great Guilt for

past & present wickedness. As has been observed already,

all Wickedness of Heart is excused, as what, in it self considered,

brings no Guilt. and all that the Conscience has to recur to,

to

To Mr. Erskine

To Mr. Erskine marginal

to find any Guilt, is the first wrong Determination of the will,

in some ill before that wickedness of Heart existed, to that

was the Occasion of Introducing or continuing that wickedness

of Heart. Which determination arose contingently, from a state

of Indifference. And how small a matter does this at once

bring Mens Guilt to, when all the Main Things wherein

their wickedness consists are passed over? And indeed the

more These Principles are pursued, the more & more must

Guilt vanish, 'till at last it comes to Nothing, as may easily

be shewn . And with Respect to Self-Flattery as to what

& Presumption, as to what is future, nothing can possibly be con-

ceived more directly tending to it, than a Notion of a Liberty,

at all times possessed, consisting in a Power to determine ones

own will to Good or evil ; which implies a Power men have

at all Times to determine them to repent & Turn to God. And

what can more Effectually encourage the sinner in present de-

lays and to embolden him to go on in sin, in a Presumption

of having his own salvation at all Times at his own Command.

And this Notion of self-dependence & self-determination,

tends to prevent or enervate all Prayer to God for Converting

for why should men earnestly cry to God for his Grace to de-

termine their Hearts to that, which they must be determined

to of themselves. And indeed it destroys the very Notion of

Conversion it : there can properly be no such Thing, or any thing

akin to what the scripture speaks of as Conversion, Renova-

tion of the Heart, Regeneration &c. If growing good by

a number of self-determined Acts be all that is

requisite required or to be expected.

Excuse me sir, for troubling you with so much on this Head.

I speak from the Fulness of my Heart. What I have long seen

of the dreadful Consequences of these prevalent Notions every

where, and what I am convinced will still be their Con-

sequences, so long as they Continue to prevail, fills me

with great Concern. I therefore wish that the Affair were

more thoughly looked into, & searched to the very Bottom.

I have reserved of a copy of this Letter, & also of my other to you

dated July 25. intending to send them to Mr. Burr, to be by him

Conveyed by the way of N. York or Philadelphia. Looking on

these Letters as of special Importance, I send duplicates, lest one

Copy should fail. The Pacquet, in which I enclose this, I cover

to Mr. Gillies, & send to Boston, to the Care of Mr. Hyslop,

to be conveyed to Mr. Gillies. But yet have desired him, if he

has a more direct Opportunity, to convey the Pacquet to Edin-

burgh, by the way of London, then to put a wrapper over the

whole inscribed to you; and to write to you, desiring you

to

to break open the Pacquet, & take out the Letters which belong marginal

to you. I have left the other Letters, & as well as those to

you open, excepting the wrapper to Mr. Gillies, that he that

first receives the Pacquet, either you or he, may read the

Letters to the Rest, & then seal & convey them. And I should

be glad if Mr. McCulloch might also see all the Letters as

well as Mr. Gillies.

You'll see sir, something of our sorrowful State on this side

of the Water by my letter to Mr. McCulloch. O Sir pray

for us; & pray in particular for

Your affectionate & obliged Friend & Br.

Jonath. Edwards.

P.S. Please for the future to direct your Letters to me, to be

left with Mr. William Hyslop Mercht in Boston, a Scots

Gentleman, who married a Kinswoman of mine, & who main-

tains a Correspondence both at London & scotland, & will

be very faithful & friendly.

Mr. Erskine
