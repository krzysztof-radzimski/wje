71. Rom. 2:16 (edit)

 

 

The Secrets of Men Must All Come Into Judgment Before God

 

 Romans 2:16.
 In the day when God shall judge the secrets of men by Jesus Christ according to my gospel.

 

The Apostle is speaking in the foregoing verses, beginning with the sixth verse, how God will judge all sorts of men universally according to their deeds; that he will render indignation and wrath, tribulation and anguish upon everyone that doth evil, whether he be Jew or Gentile; and on the contrary will render glory, honor and peace to every soul that works good, whether he be Jew or Gentile, for there is no respect of persons with God [Rom. 2:9-11]. God won’t favor the Jews because they are the seed of Abraham, nor shall the Gentiles be excused because of their ignorance. For says the Apostle, “as many as have sinned without law shall perish without law” [Rom. 2:12].  That is, they shall perish for their breach of the law of nature, written in their hearts; as the Apostle afterwards explains himself, “and as many as have sinned under[i] the law shall be judged by the law,” as in the twelfth verse. What follows in three next verses comes in by way of parenthesis, and the words of our text are to be connected with the twelfth verse. There, he told by what rule they should be judged; in our text he tells the time when: “in the day when God shall judge the secrets of men according to my gospel.”

 


This day is deciphered,

1. By the business of the day, which is judgment. It will be day appointed for this very end and business; it is a day to be noted for the great work of it. And therefore it is called the great and notable day, Acts 2:20.
2. The judge, who is Jesus Christ, that crucified man[ii] that the Apostle preached to the Romans.
3. The things judged, namely, the secrets of men; things that are out of the way of other judgments that human judges can have nothing to do with, because they are secret and hid from the world.
4. The revelation which God has made of it, which the Apostle calls his gospel, that is, the gospel which he had preached. As if he had said, “It will be on that day that I have told you of and preached to you, and which has been so clearly revealed in that gospel that I have preach[ed] to you and to other Gentiles.”


DOCTRINE.
The secrets of men must all come into judgment before God.
Under this doctrine we shall,
I. Show what is implied in a thing’s coming into judgment before God.
II. [Show] what secrets of men will thus come into judgment before God.And then make improvement.

I. There are these things implied in the secrets of men coming in to judgment before God:
 First. That God will bring ‘em to light and call men to an account concerning ‘em. They must appear before God and be inquired of about ‘em, as it was with our first parents. They hid themselves, and endeavor to keep themselves and what they had done secret; but God calls ‘em out of their lurking place and inquires, “Hast thou eaten of the tree, whereof I commanded thee that thou shouldst not eat?” [Gen. 3:11], and, “What is this that thou hast done? [Gen. 3:13].
 There are innumerable things concerning every man that are hid from the eye of the world they know nothing about, therefore they are called to an account by nobody concerning 'em. They are not cognizable by parents, or by the civil magistrates, or by the church. He escapes punishment and reproof, and the man is no otherwise looked upon than if they had not been: but they are open and naked to the eyes of God. And he therefore, in due time, will call to an account about 'em. They must come and stand before God’s seat, must stand at his bar. God won’t want witnesses to make them evident, for they were seen by his own all-seeing eye.
 Now God says nothing to ‘em about it, though he sometimes executes judgments upon men for their secret wickedness in this world, and speaks to men by his word and by providence and by their own consciences; yea,[iii] speaks once, yea, twice, but they perceive it not. But there will a time come, when God will apparently and immediately plead with them about their secret things.
Things that were secret must be brought to light before God, they must be made public; they were known to God before, but they shall be made known to angels and men in God’s presence, and the persons themselves being present. Luke 12:2-3, “There is nothing covered, that shall not be revealed; nor hid, that shall not be known. Whatsoever ye have spoken in darkness shall be heard in the light; and that which ye have spoken in the ear in closets shall be proclaimed upon the housetops.” Men won’t be called to an account before God for his information, but for the manifestation of their guilt—an inexcusableness. He won’t ask sinners the reasons of their actions that he may be informed, but that it may appear that they have no reasons nor any excuse, or if they have obtained satisfaction and pardon. They must be called to an account, that their freedom from guilt may appear by the good account which they have to give. Rom. 14:12, “So then every one of us shall give account of himself to God.” Matt. 12:36, “Every idle word that men shall speak, they shall give account thereof in the day of judgment.” It is called a reckoning with men. Matt. 25:19, ”After a long time the lord of those servants cometh, and reckoneth with them.” So God will reckon with men, with every man, about all their secret disposition and actions, that are included in[iv] judging of ‘em.
 Second. A determining the nature of them. Whether they are agreeable or disagreeable to the divine law, whether they are agreeable or contrary to the light of nature and the light of divine revelation, whether the actions are good or sinful, to that end the books of God’s law will be opened, Rev. 20:12.
 It will be determined to what degree they are bad; the aggravating circumstances will be reckoned[v] up and every one will have its due weight given to it. Everything, whether it before was secret or open, shall be made to appear just as it is, shall be set forth in its true light and proper labors.[vi]
Therefore judging is called weighing in the balances. Dan. 5:27, “Thou art weighed in the balances, and art found wanting.”
 Third. There is a determining the recompense that is due by the divine law, and sentencing of him accordingly. It shall be determined, according to those books which shall be opened, what reward or punishment belongs to him upon the account. The same rule, that is the rule for us to live by, will be the rule for us to be judged by hereafter.
And the powerful sentence shall immediately be passed; they shall be doomed to receive the appointed recompense. And the eternal reward or punishment shall be irreversibly fixed.
 Fourth. There is included in divine judging, executing the sentence; for the same person who passes sentence by his almighty power, executes the same. The sentence does as it were procure the execution. As God by his word created the world—he said, “Let it be,” and it was so—by his word or sentence he will as it were procure the execution of it. God’s executing his decree of judgment is frequently called “judging” in scripture, as you may see, Job 36:31, and in many other places. We shall in the

II. [Second] place show what secrets of men those are, that shall come into judgment before God. He will thoroughly try men’s state. The eye of this judge will search me[vii] and discern [Matt. 7:1-2].
 First. God will hereafter judge the state and condition that men are in in this world. The state of men’s souls is a secret thing in this world. We see the outward behavior of men, some of it, but we see not the heart; the principles and spiritual disposition, and state of the soul is out of sight.
 Concerning[viii] some men, indeed, we may have reason from our constant observation of their vicious lives, of their allowed, high-handed, presumptuous, wicked course of life, to be confident and to conclude that they are wicked men. Though we can’t be assured what state they die in, however they have lived.
 From the contrary, holy, Christian walk and behavior of others, we may have great reason to hope that they are truly godly, and sometimes we may be very fully satisfied; but we can’t be certain concerning others. Though there be a possibility of persons’ obtaining assurance concerning themselves, yet they can’t be sure concerning others. And in the general, the spiritual condition of men’s souls in is[ix] one of the secrets of men. It is a hidden, invisible thing; the life of [a] Christian is a hidden life. Col. 3:3, “Your life is hid with Christ in God.” A saint is said to receive “a new name, which no man knows but he that receives it,” Rev. 2:17. The heart is called “the hidden man,” I Pet. 3:4. The most skillful persons, and they that have had most experience, can’t certainly tell, and very of ten persons are ignorant concerning themselves. They don’t know the condition of their own souls; they frequently are pure in their own eyes, when indeed, they are not cleansed from their filthiness; they think they are rich and increased with goods, and know not that they are wretched and miserable, and poor and blind and naked [Rev. 3:17]. There are multitudes that are mistaken about their condition; they know not their own hearts. And there are many that are truly godly that don’t know it; they hope they are, but they have also many doubts and fears, and sometimes conclude themselves wrong when they are indeed right. So that the state and condition of men’s souls is in the general a secret thing in this world.
 But there is a time coming when it will appear what every man is. His heart, which lay hid and out of sight, shall be laid open and plain to view, so that all may see what his spiritual condition is. Then it will be tried, whether the man had any sincerity in him or no. And then it shall appear, concerning multitudes that thought themselves truly gracious and the friends of God and his children, that they are in the gall of bitterness and the bond of iniquity. All the false colors and painting of hypocrites will then be taken off, and it shall appear what they be. Of many that have made a great show of religion and have been counted eminent saints, it shall appear that they never had one dram of grace in their hearts. There will be many dreadfully disappointed, when God comes to try them, that expected to be received and embraced in the arms of God’s eternal love; but God will say to them, “Depart from me, I know you not, you workers of iniquity” [Luke 13:27].  They thought they were friends, but they'll appear strangers and aliens, and it will signify nothing for 'em to plead that they have eaten and drunk in [his] presence, that he has taught in their hearts, that they have taught in his name, and in his name have done this or that.
 Then the cloak of man’s own righteousness shall be taken off. It will appear then as filthy rags indeed and not sufficient to cover their nakedness. Rev. 16:15, “Behold, I come as a thief. Blessed is he that watcheth, and keepeth his garments, lest he walk naked, and they see his shame.”
 And on the other hand, there will be many persons that have been hardly thought of, and censured as wicked men and called hypocrites, that then shall shine forth in the righteousness of Christ. God will commend them with a “well done, thou good and faithful servant [Matt. 25:21].” And many that have had many doubts concerning themselves and were afraid, and very like many that were afraid upon their deathbeds, when they come to be tried by the touchstone, shall be found true gold; and grace, which was small and almost invisible before, shall now be brought to perfection at that time. God, who searches the hearts and tries the reins, will examine concerning everyone, whether or no he ever with his whole [heart] received Jesus Christ, committed his soul unto him and with all his heart complied with the gospel; whether or no he chose Christ for his own sake and received [him], because of his own excellency and worthiness. God will try the state of every man by the same rules of his Word that he gives us, to try ourselves. Every one will be judged out of those things that are found written in the books. Thus, God will judge the secret state of the souls of men.
 Second. Men’s thoughts and desires and counsels of their hearts must come into judgment before God. I Cor. 4:5, “When the Lord comes, he will bring to light the secret things of darkness, and will make manifest the counsels of the hearts.”
 Men’s thoughts and the workings of their hearts are the most secret things that belong to men. ‘Tis one of the distinguishing attributes and prerogatives of God, to know men’s thoughts. Others may guess at them by men’s words and behavior, but God alone sees them. Rev. 2:23, “I am he which searcheth the reins and hearts: and I will give unto every one of you according to your works.”
This province is quite out of the limits of all human judgments. Though men may sin exceedingly in their thoughts—they may do things only in their hearts, that are equal to many heinous sins in the outward behavior—yet such things are out of the reach of human view, and therefore out of the reach of human judgment.
 Many men that have a fair outside and are orderly in their behavior that is seen of men, are yet licentious in their thoughts. There are many things that are restraints upon carnal men as to their outward carriage that may keep them tolerably orderly, such as fear of the punishment of human laws and fear of disgrace, being reputed as a vicious person. A respect to their own outward interest and common shame restrains, but these restraints take no hold of 'em, here. They may think what they please and indulge a sinful disposition in their thoughts as much as they please, and lose no credit or respect of men, nor be in any danger of it. They are quite secure in external acts of wickedness, let ‘em be never so secret, yet there is some danger of their being discovered. But here they are out of danger, except as sinful thoughts lead to sinful actions. And men are ready to think they may take a liberty in their thoughts, that they may indulge themselves there as long as they don’t proceed to the outward act.
 But, however, all these secret hidden workings of heart must come into judgment; they must give an account of them. Though they can indulge their lusts in their thoughts as they go about the world and be secure from mortal eyes, yet their thoughts are written down in God’s book; they are not forgotten, but are written with a pen of iron and the point of a diamond. They must give an account of all their lewd, unclean thoughts, their vain thoughts, their covetous thoughts, their malicious and revengeful thoughts, and their thoughts about worldly and profane things upon the Sabbath.
There is abundance of sin in men’s thoughts. Many are continually working wickedness and contracting guilt by the inward meditations and desires of their hearts. They will find a dreadful account in God’s book another day, when the thoughts of their hearts shall be laid open, and they shall be recompensed according to these secret works of theirs.
 The aims, designs and ends of men in all that they do must all come into judgment before God. The wickedness of actions arises very much from the wicked aims and designs that men have in them. Now other men can’t certainly determine this any otherwise than they infer it from the outward act; but God, by his own perfect knowledge, will determine with what aim and view every action was done, and precisely with how great a degree of wicked inclination and desire every action was performed, and so will proportion the punishment.
 On the other hand, the gracious workings of the hearts of the godly must come into judgment. God will declare all their holy aspirations of soul after God and Jesus Christ, their gracious desires and longings of hearts, their secret acts of love, of holy delight, of submission and resignation; as also their charitable and benevolent thoughts towards their brethren, and all their good designs and views in what they did, and their good intentions and desires, where they han’t had opportunity to do according to their intentions. Those works that are done in secret, he that seeth in secret will openly reward [Matt. 6:4]. Much of the work of a Christian is heart work, consisting in the acting’s of the soul. But those works which none sees but God and the soul in which they are, shall be seen by heaven and earth in the day of judgment.
 Third. God will judge men’s secret behavior. God’s eyes are everywhere, beholding the evil and the good; darkness is no covering to him. Roofs and walls that hide from the view of the world don’t hide from God; everything is transparent to his eyes, that are as a flame of fire. We know not how much sin there is committed in secret, but God knows, and will make known to all. “There is nothing hid that shall not be revealed, nor covered that shall not be known [Luke 12:2].”
Sin loves darkness. As it loves spiritual darkness, so it flies from the light of the sun. Therefore, wicked works in general are called “the works of darkness.” How much secret fraud and cheating is there in the world? How much lasciviousness and uncleanness, in the several degrees of it, are committed in secret? How much slander and evil-speaking and lying are men guilty of in secret? How many malicious and revengeful acts do men secretly perpetuate against their neighbors? See Job 24:13-17, “They are of those that rebel against the light; they know not the ways thereof, nor abide in the paths thereof. The murderer rising with the light killeth the poor and needy, and in the night is as a thief. The eye also of the adulterer waiteth for the twilight, saying, No eye shall see me: and disguiseth his face. In the dark they dig through houses, which they had marked for themselves in the daytime: they know not the light.For the morning is to them even as the shadow of death: if one know them, they are in the terrors of the shadow of death.”
Dreadful will be the account which we shall hear at the day of judgment of men’s secret sins and works of darkness. And dreadful will be the doom which shall be pronounced, and the punishment which shall be inflicted.
 So the secret good works of the godly shall also be openly rewarded; their secret prayer, and praises, and humiliations, and their secret alms, and deeds of charity, Matt. 6:3[-4], “when thou dost alms, let not thy left hand know what thy right hand doeth: that thine alms may be in secret: and thy Father which seeth in secret, himself shall reward thee openly.” All the secret piety, the secret diligence and watchfulness, and that holy commerce between God and him, though the world knows nothing of it, now shall be declared before men and angels, and God will honor them and glorify them in the sight of them all. Thus the secrets of men must be judged by God.

 

APPLICATION.

I. Use of Reproof to those who take God’s work upon them in judging the secrets of men. There is a proneness in men, ever since the fall, to take God’s work upon them, to put themselves into God’s place, and to act the deity one towards another as far as they can. They will be each other lords and gods, and will take upon them to be the judges of others, and will not only judge those things that are apparent and open and liable to the cognizance of men, but they also assume to themselves to judge the secrets of men, those things that are invisible, and that it is God’s peculiar province to judge.
 So they are forward to pass their judgment about the states of others’ souls, to determine that such and such visible Christians are hypocrites, that they have no true grace, but that they are wicked men. And they determine upon very slight grounds: as that they have done such and such things that ben’t right, and contrary to the rule, and so they at once conclude they are wicked men; or that they don’t tell of such particular experiences as some others do; they imagine they don’t talk like saints: and from many other things that ben’t sufficient grounds to judge a man a child of the devil.
 There are many very forward to judge the hearts of others upon particular occasions and to censure ‘em, as if they could see their thoughts, intentions and designs. They are ready to put the hardest construction upon others’ actions, to judge severely of the motives and ends of such and such actions. They are especially ready to do it if they themselves have been displeased by ‘em, or if they have fallen into any sin, or have met with any great calamity. This is a very unchristian spirit, though it be a very common one; ‘tis exceeding contrary to the gospel rules. Rom. 14:4, “Who art thou that judgest another man's servant? to his own master he standeth or falleth”; as if he had said, “Who art thou[x] that you take upon you to act as lord and judge of your fellow creature and fellow servant?” So Jas. 4:[11-]12, “Speak not evil one of another, brethren. He that speaketh evil of his brother, and judgeth his brother, speaketh evil of the law, and judgeth the law: but if thou judge the law, thou art not a doer of the law, but a judge. There is one lawgiver, that is able to save and to destroy: who art thou that judgest another?”
 When it is thus, it is a manifestation of a proud disposition, a disposition to exalt themselves above their fellow creatures and set themselves as their judges. It shows a very high opinion men have of themselves, and a great ignorance of their own failings and weakness, and a great deal of hypocrisy. Matt. 7:3-5, “why beholdest thou the mote that is in thy brother's eye, but considerest not the beam that is in thine own eye? Or how wilt thou say to thy brother, Let me pull out the mote out of thine eye; and, behold, a beam is in thine own eye? Thou hypocrite, first cast out the beam out of thine own eye; and then shalt thou see clearly to cast out the mote out of thy brother's eye.”
 ‘Tis a base spirit and hated by mankind, and even those persons that are most censorious will soonest be put into rage when others show the same spirit towards them.
Christ tells us that this is the way to be judged ourselves. If we are forward in judging and censuring others, we may not only expect to meet with the same from other men, but from God the great and rightful Judge. Matt. 7:1-2, “Judge not, that ye be not judged. For with what judgment ye judge, ye shall be judged: and with what measure ye mete, it shall be measured to you again.”

II. Use of Exhortation to sinners to reform their secret sins.
 [First.] The sins that I speak of, are such, are hid from the eye of the world, either known only to you or known only to some that are concerned in the same sins. I fear there are many that now hear me whose hearts and consciences witness that they live in some secret, vicious practice. ‘Tis an encouragement to you to allow yourself in those ways of sin, that it is hidden from the world. But you must consider that that is not your advantage, but an advantage that Satan has to ruin and destroy you forever. When you can sin with the greatest security then Satan can work your ruin with the greatest ease. When you are secretly, indulging your sinful inclination, then Satan has the best opportunity to indulge his malice and inclination to destroy you. When he sees sinners bite securely at his bait, he then has the greatest hope of making a prey of ‘em, and has much the greatest advantage for it.
Consider what you have now heard: that all your secret sins that you from time to [time] commit are had in remembrance, everyone of them, and you must give an account to God about ‘em. They will all be brought to light, and will stand before you and stare you in the face, when you stand before God and before men and angels.
Consider also that, if you are got into a way of secretly gratifying any lust, your case is exceeding dangerous; there is great danger that you will so provoke God, and so harden your own heart, that you never will be effectually turned from sin to God. God often punishes men that live in ways of secret wickedness, especially under gospel light, by giving of them up forever to their own hearts’ lusts, Rom. 1:24-26. There is danger that you will provoke him, that he'll resolve never to give you conversion, if you han’t done it already. Doubtless there are a great many persons that seek to be converted that never obtain it in all their lives’ time, because they have so provoked God by allowed ways of secret wickedness, that he lets them alone and never will make a saving change in them. Is. 59:2, “your iniquities have separated between you and your God, and your sins have hid his face from you, that he will not hear.”
If you indulge yourself in sin, upon a presumption that God will show mercy to you hereafter, that is the way to have God resolve that he will not show you mercy, and that he will order matters for your ruin and destruction. The Apostle speaks of those that have abused the grace of God, and have taken encouragement from it to indulge themselves in lasciviousness, as you may see, Jude 4. And speaking of the great danger of their destruction, he compares, as you may see in the following verses, to those that were overthrown in the wilderness, that God swore should never enter into his rest, and to the fallen angels, and the cities of Sodom and Gomorrah [Jude 6-7].
O, therefore, beware! Go not on in that dismal road that you are in. Forsake those ways immediately, if you would not run yourself down so far towards eternal destruction, that you never shall be able to recover yourself.
Second. Let all be exhorted to judge themselves, that they may not be judged of God hereafter. There are two parts of self-judging:
1. Considering our ways and condemning ourselves for our sins. We must thus judge our own secret sins. Judge therefore now, before that great Judge comes. Hearken to the judgment of conscience; be often in reflecting on your secret sins, and confess, and lament, and bewail them before God. Condemn your former sinful practices, not only in words but in heart, not only in saying that they are vile and odious but in hating and abhorring of them; and even that you deserve God’s wrath, that it would be just for God forever to deny you any mercy; and don’t only say so, but see and be deeply sensible of it.
2. Another part of self-judging is examining ourselves about our state, that we may judge what condition we are in, whether in a natural condition. This doctrine offers the strongest motive to us to do thus. We have heard that this is one of the secrets that is to be tried, and judged, and brought to light at the day of judgment, and according to which our state is to be determined forever. ‘Tis worth the while, therefore, to take the greatest care, and use the[xi] utmost diligence, that you may not be deceived and find yourself deluded when you come to be judged. Take heed you don’t flatter yourself that you are rich and increased with goods, while indeed you are wretched and miserable, and poor and blind and naked. Watch and keep your garments, lest you then walk naked and they see your shame. You can’t be too careful, too strict and exact in your inquiries about your own state and condition, because ‘tis a matter of great difficulty and of the greatest consequence. Never give over this work of trying and examining yourself, and using all possible proper means to find out the state of your own soul, till you have obtained full satisfaction. Never fix it down with yourself that your condition is good, so as to make it no more a question, till you are sure you can’t be deceived. The judgment that you are to be tried by hereafter will be most strict; let your trial of yourself be also very strict and severe.

[III. The] Last Use is to encourage godly men in their secret piety. For God, who sees in secret, will bring it into judgment and will reward it openly. ‘Tis the temper of sincere Christians; they don’t desire to publish abroad their good deeds and the acts of piety between God and their own souls. ‘Tis sufficient to them that God knows; it, they seek not so much the praise of men, as of God.
And God will honor them in the sight of the many millions that shall be present at the last day. You may take encouragement, therefore, from hence, to be more diligent and careful and fervent in spirit in your secret service of God, whatever reproaches and hard censures you may meet with from men. You may comfort yourself in this, that God is to be your judge, who is a righteous judge, and will reward you and everyone according to his works.
God don’t forget none of your prayers, nor none of your tears, none of your secret praises, none of your holy conversation that is in secret between God and you. He remembers every passage, and lays up a reward for you accordingly against you come into his presence.
What great encouragement is it to diligence, frequency and exactness, in walking with God in secret, that every gracious thought and every gracious act shall have an eternal reward; that your crown of glory, which shall be set upon your head at the day of judgment, will be proportionally bright and glistening to the exercises of that holy life that is hid with Christ in God.
