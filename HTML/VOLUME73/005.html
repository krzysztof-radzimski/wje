1 Corinthians 12.31 Scripture 1 Corinthians 12.31 1 Corinthians XII. 31 But covet earnestly the best gifts: and yet shew I unto you a more excellent way.

___________________________________________

The first age of the Christian Church, i.e. the first hundred years after the birth of Christ is by divines often called the Age of miracles, and sometimes the Apostolick age, for it was about so long before all the apostles were dead.. And during that age there were doubtless more miracles, or miraculous influences of the Spirit of God, than in all other ages together.

The miraculous influences of the Spirit of God here had ceased from about the time of Malachi 's time, till a little before the crucifixion birth of Christ; when they began to be restored to make prepare the way for the coming of Christ, first and immediately to introduce John the Baptist, Christ's forerunner into the world. Many miracles were wrought during Christ's mortal life, especially in the latter part of Christ's life, during the three years and an half of his public ministry; and after his death the spirit of God was poured out more abundantly than ever before on in his miraculous influences on the christian church; and it was a common thing in that age, not only for the apostles, but for ministers in general and also for innumerable multitudes of private christians every where in the Christian world to be endowed not only with the saving influences of God's spirit on their hearts, ? faith and love, and other graces on them but also with extraordinary and miraculous influences of the Spirit, some in one way, and some another,. Some had the gifts of prophecy, and foretelling before events, some of tongues, some of healing. , tc.

These extraordinary gifts are the subject on which the apostle insists on p. 2 throughout this chapter, wherein is the text, and he enumerates many of them in the three verses immediately preceding the text. "And God hath set some in the church, first apostles, secondarily prophets, thirdly teachers, after that miracles, then gifts of healings, helps, government, diversities of tongues. Are all apostles? are all prophets? are all teachers? are all workers of miracles? have all the gift of healing? do all speak with tongues? do all interpret?" and then the apostle concludes the chapter with the words of the text; in which may be observed three things:

1. Here are There Two kinds of the influences, or fruits of the Spirit of God are here spoken of: the one common, and the other saving. These common gifts that are spoken of were thosee miraculous gifts that were conferred in those days, such as the gifts of prophecy \, and tongues and others of the same nature; for though they were extraordinary and wonderful, yet they were common both to good and bad men, they were not saving gifts. To have The gift of prophecy was no not a saving gift, Balaam and Saul had it; and so to have the gifts of tongues, and working miracles were common and not saving gifts, for men might have those and yet that is not not have charity, or love and so be nothing; as is supposed in the two first two verses of the next chapter. "Though I speak with the tongues of men and of angels, and have not charity, I am become as sounding brass, or a tinckling cymbal. and though I have the gift of prophecy, and understand all mysteries, and all knowledge; and though I have all faith, so that I could remove mountains, and have not charity, I am nothing."

But besides those common gifts there is another kind of influence p. 3 and fruits of the Spirit spoken of in the latter part of the verse, and that is what consists in saving grace: "and yet shew I unto you a more excellent way." By this more excellent way, the apostle means, seeking charity or saving grace, as he explains himself in the words that immediately follow in the beginning of the next chapter. So that both the common and saving influences of the Spirit of God are spoken of in the text.

2. We may observe how these gifts or influences of the Spirit of God are compared among themselves.

First. There is a difference among the common gifts of the Spirit: some of them are better than others, and therefore some are called the best. When the apostle speaks of the best gifts, he has respect to those miraculous gifts mentioned in the forgoing verses as prophecies, gifts of healing, diversities of tongues, &c.

2. Secondly. The saving grace of the Spirit, summarily consisting in in charity, is said to be more excellent than any of them. He speaks of the best gifts in the former part of the verse, but "yet," he says he, "I shew you a more excellent way." I recommend something to you more excellent than the very best of all those common gifts; charity is a better gift of the Spirit of God than the very best of his miraculous gifts.

3. Here is an exhortation in the text, earnest to seek the best influences and fruits of the Spirit. He would have them earnestly seek the best of the common gifts of the Spirit, but yet it is plainly implied that he would have them pursue that saving gift of charity more earnestly still, than any of them, which also appears in the beginning of the XIV Chapter fourteenth chapter, where after he had been extolling p. 4 charity, and shewing how much better it was is than the best of miraculous gifts of the Spirit, he says, "follow after charity, and desire spiritual gifts, but rather than you may prophesy.

Doctrine

Christians should earnest seek the best experiences.

As to those miraculous influences of the Spirit that were experienced in the apostolical age, we are not concerned with them; they have long since ceased or failed, as the apostle foretold that they would.. I Cor. XIII. 8. "Whether there be prophecies, they shall fail; whether there be tongues, they shall cease; whether there be knowledge, it shall vanish away." Therefore when the apostle exhorts to covet earnestly the best gifts, we are not concerned in the exhortation as it relates to those miraculous gifts. But the apostle's exhortation manifestly extends further; in as much as he would not only have the christian Corinthians covet earnestly the best miraculous fits, but would have them much more covet the charity; which is more excellent still than any of them, and which is a gift or fruit of the Spirit that never fails, but is bestowed still on all that who are true christians. So that the exhortation of the apostle in its full latitude is to be taken thus; that we should earnestly covet the best influences and fruits of the Spirit of God. As the apostle would have the christians in those days covet earnestly the sense best of those several kinds of gifts or influences, of the Spirit, that were more common then; so, though the same gifts of the Spirit are not bestowed now as then, yet the reason is the same, that we should covet earnestly the best of the several kinds of those influences and fruits of the Spirit, that God now bestows in the christian church; Now and this doubtless is a duty pointed forth out to us by this exhortation of the apostle, and, on which,I that I shall p. 5I shall now insist upon from the words. text. Here

First. I. Professing christians should not content themselves with those religious experiences that are common but should seek saving experiences. There are many influences of the Spirit of God that which persons do experience, that are common, ie that which natural men have as well as those that who are regenerated. Such are common convictions. The Spirit of God does operates oftentimes on the minds of natural men to convince them of the dangerous nature of sin, to give them some sense of the shortness and uncertainty of life, and some convictions of the being and holiness, and terribleness of God, and leads them to some sense of their own sinfulness in God's sight, and exposedness of their exposure to God's wrath. The Spirit of God helps men's natural reason the better to exert itself, and so may give em them some sense of the reasonableness of many divine truths; may help them to see the rational evidence of the being of God and a future state, and that the scriptures are the word of God. God's Spirit assists natural conscience the better to do its office, to bring sin to rememberance, to shew the heineousness and guilt of it, and the punishment to which it exposes to; and does often helps the consciences of men the more thoroughly to search and discern the inward wickedness that is rooted in them, and that is putting forth itself from time to time in the secret workings of the heart.

The Spirit of God does often helps men's natural apprehensions and imaginations, to have a more full and clear idea of the things of religion, whereby they become much more affecting; it often gives a lively idea of that the natural good that there is in the things of religion for divine things have not only a spiritual goodness and beauty in them, but there is a natural good in them, that which tends to gratify p. 6 a principle of self love. So in the attributes of God's mercy there is a great deal that is pleasing to nature, as also in the love of Christ in dying for sinners. So there is a great deal much in the promises of God's word, in the promises of heaven; there is much in the general notion of that honour and ease, and pleasure that is which are in heaven, that is pleasing to men's natural self love. So there is a great deal much in many duties that are required in the work of God, which is pleasing to the same principle; in the duties of justice and liberality, and kindness and forgiveness; and so in almost all other moral duties, there is something in them or in their fruits that is considered in others gratifying to natural self love.

The Spirit of God does often help the apprehensions of men to have a lively idea of that natural good there is in those things whereby they have many affecting views of them.

Now though these things impart no real goodness to those that who experience them, yet as they are from the influences of the sSpirit of God, they are good, and are of a good tendency. And some of them are better than other;. Some are so as they are greater in degree, so and thus those convictions of sin and the misery of a natural condition that are great and deep, are better of a much better tendency than those that are small and light. Some are better than others in their kind, as some as are more inward. Some convictions that natural men have, lead them more down into their own hearts than others, it is better to have a sight of the heart than it is to have a sight of hell.. Some of the common influences of the Spirit have more of a resemblance of what is spiritual than others. There are some common influences of the Spirit that work much by sorrow for sin, affection in religious duties, and some by joy and delight and. p. 7 Some have a much greater influence on the conversation than others, and some are more pleasing than others, and some do very much raise up the heart for a season in joy and praise.

But professing christians should see to it that they do not content themselves with neglect of the common gifts and influences of the Spirit of God of whatever kind or in whatever degree. They should content themselves with no common conventions, however deep they are; they should content themselves with no legal repentance, which is but a common gift of the Spirit; they should content themselves with no common light revealed, though it be to that such a degree as to know all mysteries; they should content themselves with no common gift faith, though it should be to that such a degree as to remove mountains; they should be content themselves with none of those moral and religious dispositions and actions that are common, though they go to that such a degree as to give all their goods to the poor, and their bodies to be burned. They should content themselves with no common impressions on their imaginations, though it be to the gift of prophecy, though they should have immediate revelations from heaven. They should content themselves with no common affections, though they should repent of sin with Judas, and walk softly in sack cloth with Ahab, and be lifted up with joy with the stony ground hearers, and be brought to say, O what a blessing is it, it is, with the foolish Galatians, and to sing God's praises with the revolting stifnecked Israelites and to glorify God for the wonders that he has done with the Jews that saw Christ's miracles; and though they should be brought to take up great form strong resolutions that they will follow Christ wherever he p. 8 goes, with the scribe that of whom we have account of, Math. VIII.19. "And a certain scribe came and said unto him, Master, I will follow thee whithersoever thou goest." Though they should not only keep all the commandments of the law as to the external duties of the law, but also come knocking to Christ saying, "Good master,"------and should as to------the righteousness which is of the law be blameless, and should have a seal of God, as Paul had before he was converted. ---I say, professing christians should content themselves with none of these common gifts of the Spirit, even the most excellent of them, but should earnestly seek those experiences that are spiritual and saving; that they may not only have those influences of the Spirit of God that which assists nature, but those that are supernatural, not only those that help natural reason and conscience, but those that imparts a principle that is entirely above nature, that gives that life light of which men are naturally wholly destitute of,, and that principle of divine love to God for the bounty of his nature and not only his goodness to us which is an heavenly principel..

This professing christians should do, because unless they obtain those fruits of the Spirit that are above all that are common gifts, they cannot be christians indeed; with all the most excellent and dazzling of, of common gifts they will be but painted sepulchres, will be as loathsome in the sight of Him who searches the heart, and sees what is within them as a rotten dead carcass hid under a shining monument. However common gifts of the Spirit may please them, and puff them up with pride, and may sooth and quiet conscience for the present, and fill the heart with vain hopes and p. 9 expectations, and may also get them a name to live among men; yet all will be swept away at last as a spiders webb, all those painted garments shall be stripped off, and they shall walk naked. This foundation though a strong one, yet is not a strong one, and will prove but weak--at last, and will be carried away with by the flood, and let the building fall. And all the great hopes and----------expectations that have been built on them, with it will be followed with the most dismal disappointment and confusion. Math. V.20. "For I say unto you, that except your righteousness shall exceed the righteousness of the scribes and Pharisees, ye shall in no case enter into the kingdom of heaven." Job XXVII. 8. "For what is the hope of the hypocrite, though he hath gained, when God taketh away his soul?"

As particularly thate grace of charity, as or divine love. And it is observable that once an occasion of a remarkable exercise of his meekness, even when Aaron and Miriam themselves, from whom he might least expect such treatment rose and opposed him and spake against him Numb XII at the beginning. And Miriam and Aaron spake against Moses, because of the Ethiopian woman whom he had married: And they said, hath the Lord indeed spoken only by Moses? hath he not also spoken by us? and the Lord heard it. Now the man Moses was very meek, above all the men which were upon the face of the earth. And the Lord spake suddenly unto Moses, and unto Aaron, and unto Miriam, Come out ye three unto the tabernacle of the congregation and they three came out. And the Lord came down in the pillar of the cloud & God did remarkably reward him for his meekness by p. 38 extraordinarily testifying his peculiar favour to him immediately upon it, and promising that he would manifest himself apparently to him in a more remarkable manner than to any other prophet 6.4.8. verses. And he said, Hear now my words: if there be a prophet among you, I the Lord will make myself known unto him in a vision, and will speak unto him in a dream. My servant Moses is not so, who is faithful in all mine house. With him will I speak mouth to mouth, even apparently, and not in dark speeches; and the similitude of the Lord shall he behold: wherefore then were ye not afraid to speak against my servant Moses? So that a meek behaviour as it is an excellent behaviour, so is also doubtless the way to excellent experiences, and there is no question to be made of it, that one great reason, why many christians have no more experiences of the several and excellent discoveries of God, and joys of holiness, is, that they are so defective in such behaviour; there is in them so much roughness and bitterness in their spirit and carriage, so much resentment, so many fierce speeches, so much speaking evil of others, that they are kept lean, and dark, and doubtful, they go long without excellent experiences, they have not one quarter so much of the presence of God, as otherwise they might have. They are low, and are kept low, and and poor, the party of anger and resentment, and bitterness of spirit eats out the sweetness and life of spiritual experiences in them.

3. Thirdly. Another kind of practices that which are peculiarly excellent and acceptable in the eyes of God, and are more to be attended and rewarded with the most excellent experiences; are such as are attended with p. 39 labour and suffering in the cause of Christ, and in the way of duty. For such holy practices as those there is a peculiar exercise of patience. Patience is the nature virtue that endeavors endures and goes through sufferings; but patience is a nature virtue that does especially produce excellent experiences, as you may see, in Rom. V. 3,4,5. "And not only so, but we glory in tribulations also: knowing that tribulation worketh patience; and patience, experience, and experience, hope: and hope maketh not ashamed; because the love of God is shed abroad in our hearts by the Holy Ghost, which is given unto us." To live a life of suffering and bearing the cross for God, of suffering in the denial of our appetites, and suffering in our ease and worldly comforts, and our names, and the good will of others as we are called; is the way to have such experience of the love of God shed abroad in our hearts, as shall beget the sweetest comfort, and most joyful hope, that will enable us to triumph and glory in tribulation. This is one common fruit of suffering for God, that the Spirit of God is given to such persons as do suffer, as a spirit of joy and glory. I Peter IV.14. "If ye be reproached for the name of Christ, happy are ye; for the Spirit of glory and of God resteth upon you: on their part he is evil spoken of, but on your part he is glorified."

This is remarkably confirmed in the instance of the apostle Paul, who was one that abounded above all other mear mere men that we read of in scripture, in labours and sufferings for Christ; and he was rewarded by answerably great and remarkable experiences. Oftentimes God is pleased to reward his people that willingly suffer for him, with wonderful experiences in the time of their sufferings. Christ comes to them, and manifests himself remarkably at that p. 40 individual time, as Christ came to Shadrach and his companions in the furnace and walked there with them; and as Stephen while they were stoning him, saw the heavens opened, and Jesus Christ standing on the right hand of God, and his enemies beheld his face, as it had been the face of an angel.

Therefore let one advise all such as desire to have the most excellent experiences, to labour, to abound in the most excellent practices. The Psalmist says in Ps. CXIX. 56. "This I had, because I kept thy precepts." This I had, He does not say what it was it was, but it was doubtless something remarkable, some manifestation in token of the favour and love of God, that was peculiarly sweet and excellent; and possibly the reason why it is not expressed what it was, was because it was inexpressible. If you also will keep God's precepts, and will be persuaded to abound in excellent practices, especially in deeds of charity, meekness, and suffering for Christ; you also shall have cause to say with the Psalmist, "This this I had, because I kept thy precepts." This excellent and precious gift I received, This this sweet season of communion with God, this blessed communication of the divine love to my soul."

So much for the second direction in order to obtaining the best experiences, or to labour to obtain abound in the most excellent practices

I now proceed to a

3. 1 Direction in order to it, viz., So Endeavor to make progress in holiness, in something of a constant, steady manner. If you do so, you will have more and more excellent experiences. Though your beginning may be small, yet your latter end shall greatly increase. Your experiences would will become more and more spiritual, having less p. 41 mixture of what is common and natural. Standing water is not soon purged from its filthiness, but running water soon purges itself. The experiences of christians, that do who in a steady and constant manner grow and make progress in holiness, is are like the fruit of a tree, which, though at first it be green and crude, yet the longer it hangs, the riper and better it grows. If your growth be in something of a steady and constant manner, your progress will be far more glorious than if it be very unsteady. the growth that is steady though it be slow yet produces great things at last. The progress increase of a little child in knowledge and speech seem to be very slow, the difference in a few days or a weak is seen perceptible, but yet if we compare it at the distance of three or four years, how vast is the increase?

If you are very unsteady in the progress that you make, and here be great interruptions and intermissions, sometimes for a while seem to go forward steadily in the ways of God, but then by getting into public or private strife, or into some other ill way, knocks all in the head again, and then after some time get into a forberable good frame again, and then decline thy same great reward again. I say if you go on thus it is the way never to arrive at any thing very eminent. Those repeated words and intermissions do, as it were, discourage the growth of grace. If a tree be planted in a fruitful soil, and seems to flourish very well for a while, yet if from time to time there be a blow struck at the root or if soon after it begins to bud and put forth leaves and flowers, and looks like flourishing, is the token and the leaves and the buds are eat off with worms, the growth of that tree will be as it were discouraged p. 42and it will by degrees pine and languish.

Therefore be exhorted to seek that you may make some constant progress in grace, in order to which there is need of constant watchfulness, and diligence. Your labour and your care, and prayer to God must be kept up, your hands must be continually lifted up if you would not have too much prevail against you. And especially watch over your own iniquity, the sin that does most easily beset you, the sin of your natural temper, or the sin to which you are especially under tempted to by your circumstances.

4. Another direction I would give you in order to an obtaining the best experiences is from time to time is to Examine the experience which you have, and evidences to endeavor distinguish what is spiritual in them from what is common and natural. If you have a wrong notion of your experiences, and take them those to be of the most excellent kind of experiences that which are not, you will not be likely to obtain those that are indeed the most excellent; you will be likely to rest in what you are, not being sensible of the imperfection of it, and the need you stand in of something further.

Examine those affections, those joys, and those fears that you have, and labour to discerne how far they are spiritual. Examine how far that great emotion that which you feel, may arise from natural hunger, and how far ever novelty and self love, causes; observe how far what you have, is from impressions made on your imagination; How how far those exercises of the love of God that you feel, are for the sake of what God is himself. the excellency of his nature.

Examine your joy, and inquire how far it is a rejoicing in Christ, and how far a rejoicing in yourself; when you have had great joy, p. 43 inquire whether or no what you have been rejoicing in is what you have seen in God and Christ, or is it what you have seen in yourself. Has your eye in that joy looked chiefly forwards, and not of yourself or has it not been very much employed in the mean time in viewing yourself, and the goodness of your own experiences. When you have had great joy, enquire inquire whether your joy has been attended with levity, with a kind of a light dispostion in you, and and air of talking and behaviour; or has it been a solid joy, that not raising you up with levity, but casting you down low in the dust, in humility and purity of spirit, and has it been attended with a salautary sense of the majesty of God, and your own nothingness and unworthiness.

When at any time it seems to you, that you have had great discover discov-ies, examine and try in what degree those discoveries have been attended with humility, with a disposition and longing to cast yourself down, and lie low, and in the dust before God; --- and examine how far your experiences have been attended with self denial, or have had that effect upon you to excite a real willingness in you to forsake all for Christ, in case you should be called to it. --- And consider what the effect is that is left upon the soul, whether or no it be for spiritual pride, or being lifted up with what you have met with as though you was were better than others; and if you are not sensible of this yet, inquire whether or no it has left any sensible discoveries that which you call so great, of a more sweet, christian, and heavenly disposition than you was before? Does it appear by your lamblike spirit, that you have been with Christ? Are you more of a spirit of christian love and meekness, and gentleness of spirit? If so, then it is a very good sign p. 44 that the experiences which you have had, are excellent experiences indeed.

Again. Enquire Inquire what fruits your experiences you have been taken with have brought forth. Have you found yourself since more engaged in your heart to inquire, what you shall render to the Lord for all his benefits? Have they made you much more earnest and thorough, have they enabled you to more to overcome the evil the great notion of your heart, and your temptations that you are under.

Enquire whether Have your experiences have given a greater strength to your mind in running the christian race? Have they increased your uneasiness with in sin, and made indirectly sin a greater burden to you, and increased the earnestness of your warfare against it?

And after Are your experiences that you are ready to be taken with. Enquire who ? or no they are such as are far from being contented with themselves, but on the contrary do they stir on your prompt you to great shiftings strivings after further experiences, after greater degrees of the knowledge of God, after more love to God, and to serve God better?

By thus examining your experiences from time to time you will be less liable likely to mistake dross for gold, and so not likely to rest in dross instead of gold, and will be more likely to seek after and obtain those experiences that are purely spiritual.

5. Pray to God that you may never have any kind or degree of hope or comfort, but what is attended with those three effects; viz., humility, reverence, and obedience.

Ask it of God, that he would not suffer you to entertain any other kind of hope or comfort, but what has that the effect to humble the soul. There is a sort of comfort that puffs? the soul up, but it is better to be without it. Also pray to God, that you may never p. 45 experience any other kind of comfort than that which begets reverence, that which is attended with solemnity and an holy awe ? God. It is not the design of the gospel to remove any reverential ?, though it be any delight of it is its design to cast out slavish fear. In heaven where joy is carried to the greatest height, to the utmost perfection, yet their rejoicing does not exclude reverence, but causes it; their reverence is to an higher, degree in proportion as their joy is greater. The angels that do always behold and do continually rejoice, yet at the same time are represented as veiling their faces. Their rejoicing, though it be great, yet is humble, and solemn, and reverential? reverential. And this is the way that we are called upon to rejoice. Ps. II.11. "Serve the Lord with fear, and rejoice with trembling."

Pray to God that you may never have any other sort of comfort, but what engages your you to serve God, and makes you more careful and diligent in the duties of religion. that if you had not that comfort If there be any comfort that tends to make you more slack than when you was were under fears of hell, pray to God that all such comfort may be taken and kept from you, and that you may be kept under the fears of hell till you have some other principle that will have that effect to stir you up to your duty more than fear will.

If God guides you in this path, and never suffers you to lay hold of any comfort or hope any further than it has these effects, then you will be safe, this is a sure saving never to be deceived, and to be you will be always kept from mistaking what is common for them which what is spiritual. If you seek and desire hope and comfort no further than it p. 46has these effects, that is the way to when you have comforts, to have those that are of the best, and most excellent kind.
