324. Cant. 6:1 324.Cant. 6'1(5_34) (ed.).xml edited Jonathan Edwards
THAT WE MAY SEEK HIM WITH THEE Scripture THAT WE MAY SEEK HIM WITH THEE CANTICLES 6:1 Whither is thy beloved gone, O thou fairest among women? whither is thy beloved turned aside? that we may seek him with thee.

THAT WE MAY SEEK HIM WITH THEE1

The spouse of Christ in the foregoing chapter gives an account how that Christ knocked at her door and called to her to let him in, but that she refused parenthetical. Afterwards she repented and rose to open to him, but he was gone. In the text is represented how she sought him but could not find him, and how she called him, but he gave her no answer Canticles 5:6. And then she charges the daughters of Jerusalem, "If ye find my beloved, that ye tell him, that I am sick of love" Canticles 5:8.

Whereupon the daughters of Jerusalem ask her, what is her beloved more than another beloved. Whereupon she describes her beloved, sets forth his beauty and excellency. Whereupon the daughters of Jerusalem, being moved by her description of the excellency of her beloved, ask her, as in the text, "Whither is thy beloved gone? that we may seek him with thee."

These daughters of Jerusalem are represented as being hitherto strangers to Christ. They inquire, what he is more than another2 beloved that she so charges them. And then, after the spouse has described him, they ask her, "Whither is thy beloved gone? that we may seek him with thee".

Observe in the words,

1. Who are the persons here making inquiry, viz. daughters of Jerusalem . . . For they are the same she was speaking to in the preceding chapter, which was "daughters of Jerusalem," as appears by the eighth verse. By "daughters of Jerusalem" are meant persons belonging to visible church of Christ. Jerusalem is the church parenthetical. They are persons living under means of grace.

2. Who is the person inquired of. She is here called the fairest among women, which signifies the church or spouse of Christ, or those that are converted. The true invisible church of Christ is made up only of converted persons. They are the fairest and most excellent and beautiful of mankind. They are the excellent of earth, Psalm 16:3

3. Who is the person inquired after, viz. her beloved, which is Jesus Christ. She, by conversion, comes to stand in that relation to Christ.

4. What is the inquiry that they make: where he is gone. They ask counsel and direction of those that are converted, and have found Christ. They ask how to seek, they ask in what way he is wont to be found. "Where is he gone?" i.e. in which way is he wont to be found?3

5. The reason why they make this inquiry: "that we may seek him with thee."

Doctrine

When some are converted, this should stir up others to seek to be converted also.

There are some that are converted, brought out of darkness . . .4

This is a work God is carrying on in the world.

God is wont to bless his ordinances and make them effectual for the conversion of the elect.

Some have been long under darkness and difficulties but God at last brings them out of darkness.

Some of all sorts are converted.

And when some are converted, this should stir up others to parenthetical, for the following reasons:

First. Others may see hereby that there is such a thing as conversion. Sometimes the conversion of others is evident and apparent to spectators, so at least, that there can be no just cause to doubt of it. Though it ben’t absolutely certain that there has indeed been such a thing as conversion in them, yet men can’t act rationally to call it in question.

Many are ready to doubt whether there be any such work or no. They doubt whether such work is not delusion or imagination . . . or hypocritical pretence.

No reason to question it, only because they never felt it themselves. ’Tis far from them.

If persons doubt whether there be such a work, no wonder they don’t earnestly seek it . . .

But in that this work is carried on in the world, and there are from time to time instances of it, this may convince others that there is such a work. For these instances are oftentimes very visible. Their conversion is accompanied with many evident manifestations of it that others may behold. The godly’s profession of it may be just ground for others to believe that they have experienced it. For though there may be some that may lie and profess to have experienced what they never did, yet ’tis most unreasonable to think all do. And oftentimes unreasonable to think this of a particular person that otherwise is a person of truth and honesty, and it is evident by his whole behavior, to suppose that he would go to frame a story of such and such experiences out of nothing, and pretend such great things without any foundation.

And if it be supposable that there may be one such instance, yet ’tis unreasonable to suppose that all that pretend such great things are guilty of such falsehood.

And then oftentimes ’tis visible not only by their professions but by the apparent alteration there is made in them.

Though the godly ben’t ostentatious of this goodness, yet it oftentimes evidently discovers itself. It is so apparent it can’t be hid. There is an evident alteration in their knowledge and sense of things, and in their dispositions and tempers. They are apparently new persons. Something has made a great change in them. Their mouths are sometimes strangely opened and their tongue is loosed, and there is a wonderful alteration in their carriage. The wolf is as it were turned into a lamb. There is such an alteration, none can reasonably doubt but that there has indeed been such a thing as conversion in them, and that their natures are changed and renewed. ’Tis apparent there is such an alteration as no man can make, and that they could not make themselves. There evidently appears the finger of God in it.

This verifies what the Scripture says about a work of conversion, and what ministers preach about it, and makes it evident that there is such a thing, and that ’tis no fable.

This should stir persons up earnestly to seek parenthetical, that ’tis evident that there is such a thing. For if there be such a thing in reality, then it is a great thing. Doubtless we all stand in necessity of it.

Second. When some are converted, this should stir parenthetical, as it is an evidence of the truth of the whole of religion. The whole Christian religion is evidenced by the conversion of one soul. That we sometimes see instances of others’ conversion, ’tis a demonstration that religion is no delusion, but that ’tis all a reality.

It shows it to be a reality, because it has such effect. If there was no truth in religion, it would be in vain, it would have no effect on men to change and renew them. It would not be effectual to change men’s nature; and therefore, that it is effectual, shows it to be true.

Conversion is a great work that God does, whereby he bears testimony to the truth of religion. If God should continually work miracles before our eyes, such as were wrought when Christ was on earth, such as healing the sick . . . or raising the dead . . . and we should see ’em, should we not think it a great evidence of the truth of religion? But when God works this work, ’tis a greater work than such miracles. As Christ himself testifieth, John 14:12, "Verily, verily, I say unto you, He that believeth on me, the works that I do shall he do also; and greater works than these shall he do; because I go unto my Father."

’Tis as great an attestation to the truth of religion, as if God should raise persons from the dead to bear testimony parenthetical.

’Tis an evidence of the truth of religion, because in conversion men are made to see the truth of religion. They see that Scripture is the Word of God, and all the great fundamental doctrines of the gospel certainly true. They can bear witness to it.

If there be such a thing as conversion, then there is a God. The cause is evident from the effect.

And if there be such parenthetical, then Scripture is the Word of God. For ’tis by that word that this effect is wrought . . . is fulfilled in it. This shows it to be true, because the great things the Scripture tells us about conversion and what belongs to it, are5 accomplished.

If there be conversion, parenthetical there is an heaven and an hell. Scripture tells us that all are by nature children of wrath Ephesians 2:3. They are in danger of everlasting perdition, and stand in absolute need of change of nature, in order to the being saved.

If there be parenthetical, then there is way of salvation by Christ. parenthetical, then that is true that he that believes shall be saved Mark 16:16, and . . .6 All those things will follow, if there be parenthetical.

Therefore, when others are converted, it should stir persons up parenthetical, as it gives them such an evidence of the truth of these great things of religion, which show the infinite benefit, and absolute necessity of, conversion.

Third. When any are converted, this should stir parenthetical, because there are the more to bear testimony of the excellency and sufficiency of Christ and his worthiness to be received. To be converted is to come to Christ and to accept of him and choose him and trust in him for salvation. When any are converted, they are able to bear testimony to others from their own knowledge that Christ is an excellent and glorious and lovely person, more excellent than all earthly things, better than all other beloveds, worthy that they should accept of him. And they are able to testify and declare to others that Christ is sufficient, that there is enough in him for all the world, for greatest sinners . . .

They have seen him to be so, and have found him to be so; they can assure others from what they know that Christ is excellent and sufficient.

Thus we find in the context that when the daughters of Jerusalem inquire of the spouse, "What is thy beloved more than another beloved, O thou fairest among women?", then she tells them how excellent he is. And they are lively stirred up to seek an interest in him too, when they had heard what she could testify of the excellency of her beloved. They say unto her, "Whither is thy beloved gone, O thou fairest among women? whither is thy beloved turned aside? that we may seek him with thee."

So that others have seen the excellency of Christ should stir persons up themselves to seek after Christ.

Fourth. When some are converted, this should stir parenthetical, because there are the more to bear testimony to the happiness of a converted state. Men are ready to think that ’tis not a happy state.

But when any are converted, they can bear testimony to the happiness of such a state.

How well worth the while to seek and strive, to take utmost pains to hold out. Though it be never so long, how richly it will pay ’em.

They can tell by their experience that it is a happy state. They have tasted and seen that God is gracious Psalm 34:8; 1 Peter 2:3. They have experienced the sweetness of religion.

They know the excellency of the benefits obtained in conversion. Peace of conscience. How contemptible the world and worldly things are in comparison of what is to be obtained by conversion.

They can tell how they were mistaken formerly, and how they see their own folly in thinking as they7 do. Psalm 73:22, "So foolish was I, and ignorant: I was as a beast before thee."

Fifth. There is a good example set them. He that is converted forsakes all his sins. He mortifies all his lusts. He takes up the cross of Christ. He gives himself up to God and devotes himself to his service and glory. Embraces all God’s commandments to walk in them forever. Renounces himself and seeks the glory of God.

These are things that are matters of great difficulty with men, and that they have a great opposition of nature to. But here is an example set them of it which should stir them up to it. Example has a great influence, and surely it ought to influence persons in a thing of this nature.

Sixth. When some are converted, there is great encouragement in it for others, to seek to be converted also. If persons would make a right improvement of it, they might find great matter of encouragement in it to seek conversion, and that in many ways. I shall mention some.

1. This shows that conversion is attainable, that ’tis not too great a blessing to bestow on such poor miserable creatures as we are.

Though so great a blessing, so infinite a blessing, yet we hereby see that persons do obtain it. God is willing to bestow it on men.

2. ’Tis matter of great encouragement to see that this is a work that God is still carrying on in the world. Persons are sometimes ready to be discouraged at the conversion of others. But ’tis strange that men should make such an improvement of it. And ’tis through the devil’s influence that they do, who is so subtle as to discourage persons with that which in its own nature is most encouraging.

If there were none converted, if this was not a work that God continued to carry on in the world, that would be discouraging. Persons would doubtless improve that to discouragement.

And so if conversion was much rarer then it is parenthetical, that would doubtless be discouraging. Persons would say within8 themselves, "’Tis a vain thing to seek conversion. There are scarce any that attain it." If there was not above one conversion in a town in twenty years, how discouraging.

Therefore the more are converted, the greater encouragement is there to others to seek.

When God is pouring out his Spirit for the conversion, God is so much the nearer to us. When our neighbors are converted, then ’tis a time for us to put in for a share.

3. ’Tis an evidence of the sufficiency of Christ for us. They were of the same nature, same sorts of hearts, same corruption;Proverbs 27:19, "As in water face answereth to face, so the heart of man to man."

We see Christ was sufficient for them. God’s grace was sufficient for them. That same grace that was sufficient for them, is for us too.9 They were thus and thus sinful and wicked and provoking, yet they obtained mercy. 1 Timothy 1:13, "Who was before a blasphemer, and a persecutor, and injurious: but I obtained mercy, because I did it ignorantly in unbelief." 1 Corinthians 6:11, "And such were some of you: but ye are washed, but ye are sanctified, but ye are justified in the name of the Lord Jesus, and by the Spirit of our God."

4. This may encourage parenthetical, for there are the more to pray for the conversion of others. When persons themselves are converted, they have a longing desire for the conversion of others; they don’t desire to have the benefit only themselves, to be above others in it. They desire others should share with them, and will pray for it. And this may be a just encouragement parenthetical, for we are told, James 5:16, that "the effectual fervent prayer of a righteous man availeth much."

And there is the more reason to hope that the Spirit of God will be poured out, and the work of conversion carried on, the more there are to pray for it. The prayer of the godly may have a great influence to bring down the Holy Spirit in his converting influences.10

Application

I. Use. Hence awakened concerned sinners ought not to be sorry when they hear of others’ conversions, but to rejoice. Such oftentimes, through the influences of Satan and evil workings of their own hearts, are grieved when they hear of others’ conversions. It is not agreeable news to them when their neighbors or acquaintances or near relations have received comfortable hopes concerning their state. They sometimes are guilty of envying them.

This is dreadful wickedness and very provoking to God, and is unreasonable; for they han’t any reason to be sorry, but to rejoice. For then, as the doctrine teaches, they have something now to stir them up to seek salvation.

Sinners need to be stirred up. The heart is very hard, and prone to dullness and slothfulness; and mercy through this never obtains.11 They have reason therefore to wish and pray for means of quickening and further awakening, and to be glad when they are granted.

The conversion of others may be a great help to them to give them further awakenings, and oftentimes is so; and therefore they should be glad when any such things come to pass. It is very frequent that the conversion of one is an occasion of the conversion of another. And then they have reason to rejoice, because, as has been observed, there is so much matter of encouragement in it. Persons are sometimes discouraged by such things, but that is unreasonable, for there is great encouragement in it.

They would be discouraged with that, if there were scarce ever any such thing as conversion. And therefore they ought to rejoice when there is. ’Tis for their interest that the Spirit of God should be poured out and the work of conversion should be abundantly carried: and they have reason to wish and pray for such times, for then they would be a great deal more likely to obtain a share of converting grace. For God is on the giving hand at such times, and ordinarily conversion is more easily obtained.

II. Second Use is of Exh. to sinners. Let the conversion of others stir you up earnestly to seek conversion. When others have found the Lord Jesus Christ, let this stir you up to seek him, that you may also obtain an interest in that glorious and lovely person. Inquire, as the daughters of Jerusalem do in the text, "Whither is thy beloved gone? whither is thy beloved turned aside? that we may seek him with thee."

There have been some instances of this glorious work amongst us of late, as we have reason to hope. Let it stir you up to get in the like blessed and happy state and condition that they are got into. Let it encourage you to seek it. God is carrying on that work, and he has poured out his converting grace round about you. There is the more hope that you may obtain a share of it. You see that ’tis a thing attainable. The same grace and the same Savior that was sufficient for them, is sufficient for you also. It is as sufficient for you as it was for them. There is encouragement that you may find Christ in the same way that they have found him. Therefore wait upon God in those ordinances and in those duties, for you see that he has blessed them for the conversion of others. If you would find him, go in the footsteps of the flock, Canticles 1:8.

And especially let young people be stirred up hereby to seek conversion. And to that end,12 consider a few things.

First. ’Tis not all that are to be converted. There are many of you now here present together. But ’tis not to be supposed you will be together in another world. There will be a great distinction made, and vast difference in your state then. Some of you in a happy and glorious parenthetical, some of you with God and Christ and glorious saints and angels, but some of you in a miserable state. There will be a great gulf fixed between you parenthetical, however now you are together here on earth. You are taught that but a few, but a remnant, shall be saved parenthetical.

Second. If persons are to be converted, the time of youth is most commonly the time. God appoints who shall be converted, and he appoints the time when. And we find by experience that13 if persons are ever to be converted, the time of youth is most commonly the time of it.

Third. It will look most hopefully in those that are most stirred up by the conversion of others. We shall have most reason to hope that they also may obtain the like blessing, that God has determined to bestow that blessing upon them, and that they will follow those that have already come to Christ.

Fourth. If you ben’t stirred up by such things, it will look very dark and dangerous upon you. It is a sign that you are very hard and for the present very destitute of needed influences of God’s Spirit. This is a great means and great tendency.
