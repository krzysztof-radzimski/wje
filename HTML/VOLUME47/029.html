257. Heb. 12:29(a) 257.Heb 12'29(a) edited Jonathan Edwards
GOD IS A CONSUMING FIRE Scripture

-- 362 --

GOD IS A CONSUMING FIRE Hebrews 12:29 For our God is a consuming fire.

In the former part of the chapter we have a comparison made between the legal and evangelical dispensations of the covenant of grace under the types of Mt. Sinai and Mt. Sion. The Apostle tells the Christian Hebrews that they were not come to Mt. Sinai; formerly, their fathers that were under the legal dispensation were brought by Moses to Mt. Sinai, but now Jesus Christ had brought them to the heavenly Mt. Sion. Mt. Sinai, that they were brought to, is described by the terrible appearances which attended it when the children of Israel came to it: "it burned with fire [and was covered with] blackness, and darkness, and tempest" [Hebrews 12:28].1

God, when he appeared on Mt. Sinai, appeared in a very terrible manner; he appeared as a consuming fire, as we read, Exodus 24:17, "And the sight of the glory of the Lord was like devouring fire on the top of the mount in the eyes of the children of Israel." The Apostle observes that the discoveries which God makes of himself on Mt. Sion are much fuller of grace, and mercy, and God's more mild and gentle attributes, in that here is "Jesus, the mediator of the new covenant, and the blood of sprinkling that speaks better things than the blood of Abel" [Hebrews 12:24]; but yet informs us, that here is no encouragement to sin at Mt. Sion more that at Mt. Sinai, but that on the contrary, here is more to deter from sin than there. As the Apostle, when he had finished the description of Mt. Sinai and Mt. Sion, subjoins in the twenty-fifth verse: "See that ye refuse not him that speaketh," that is, he that speaketh from Mt. Sion. There is a voice that speaks to us from both

-- pagenum --

those mountains, Sinai and Sion. The children of Israel, they heard a voice speak from Mt. Sinai, and we under the gospel have one that speaks to us from Mt. Sion. "For," says the Apostle, "if they escaped not who refused him that spake on earth, much more shall not we escape, if we turn away from him that speaketh from heaven" [Hebrews 12:25]. God spoke to the children of Israel from Mt. Sinai,2 spoke on earth; the voice that was then heard was from Sinai, an earthly mountain. But the Mt. Sion that Christ speaks to us from, is heaven. And if they did not escape then, when they refused what was said on earth, how shall we, if we refuse him who speaks from Mt. Sion, which is heaven: whose voice then shook the earth only, for he spoke on earth, but now speaking from heaven, his voice is more powerful and shakes both heaven and earth?

Wherefore we that are come to Mt. Sion have a twofold powerful motive to obedience. We have the gracious promises of the gospel to allure [us]; we have the promises of a "kingdom that cannot be moved" [Hebrews 12:28]. The voice that speaks from Mt. Sion is abundantly more full in promises than that which spoke from Mt. Sinai; wherefore says the Apostle, "we receiving a kingdom that cannot be moved, let us have grace, whereby we may serve God" [Hebrews 12:28]. Here is motive from love and gratitude. And then, here at Mt. Sion, we may also find that what tends also to move us to serve God from a principle of holy awe, and reverence, and fear of him, to be careful to serve him acceptably, to endeavor in everything to please him, and be accepted of him; and this we have in the text, viz. that our God is a consuming fire. The Christians' God is a consuming fire, as well as the God of the Israelites. God is a consuming fire in Mt. Sion, as well as upon the top of Mt. Sinai; he is a consuming fire under the evangelical, as well as under the legal dispensation.

-- pagenum --

Doctrine

God is a consuming fire.

For the clearing up of this doctrine, I would

I. Show what is implied in the expression of his being a consuming fire.

II. What he is a consuming fire to.

III. What attributes in God render him a consuming fire.

I. I would inquire what is implied in the expression, "a consuming fire." And in the,

First place, the expression denotes a fire of a very great and extraordinary heat. The expression is metaphorical, and God therein is compared to a fire of an exceeding fierce and terrible heat: that was what was meant by the expression of consuming fire, or devouring fire, in the Jews' dialect. When it is said that the sight of the glory of the Lord was like devouring fire on the top of the mount,3 doubtless the meaning is a fire that seemed to be of a very dreadful heat and fierceness.

Second, the expression seems to denote a fire that there is no withstanding, but that consumes whatever stands in its way, a fire that nothing can stand before: the stoutest and strongest are not able to withstand this fire, are consumed by it when they stand in its way. This is a fire that will "consume the earth with her increase, and set on fire the foundations of the mountains," Deuteronomy 32:22.

Thus God encourages the children of Israel in the beginning of the ninth chapter of Deuteronomy, that though they were going to possess nations greater and mightier than they, and cities great and fenced up

-- pagenum --

to heaven, and the people that were giants, the children of Anak - of whom they used to say: "Who can stand before them?" - yet God tells 'em for their encouragement that he would go over before them, a consuming fire, and would destroy them, and bring them down before them [Genesis 9:1-3]. Though the inhabitants were giants, yet they could not stand before this consuming fire; and though their cities were walled up to heaven, yet their walls would be no security from it.

Strength and weapons of war will defend against almost anything better than against fire. If a strong man be beset with devouring flames, his strength does not avail him there; he can't defend himself any better than a weak one; and if he is never so well armed, his armor will be to no purpose to fight with flames. So none can stand before God, when he sets himself against them; no creature, however strong, can resist him. The strength of men or angels will be to no purpose, when opposed to God; or if there be the united strength of multitudes, and never so well armed, it will do [no] more to defend against God than against devouring flames.

God is a consuming fire that will burn all up that resist him. When Ahaziah sent his captains with fifty armed soldiers to apprehend Elijah, what did their strength or armor avail them, when, at the word of Elijah, "fire came down from heaven and consumed him and his fifty" [II Kings 1:10]? To what purpose is it for any to set themselves in battle array against devouring flames? It is like briars and thorns: their sharp points may defend 'em from man and beast, who would choose not to meddle of 'em, but they are no defense against the fire. Isaiah 27:4, "Who would set the briars and thorns against me in battle? I would go through them and burn them together." This is a fire that will quickly and utterly consume whatever stands in its way.

Third. God is a consuming fire, as he is a fire that there is no hiding from, or getting out of the way of. God is a fire that will burn them that he opposes, and they cannot fly from it, but the flames, wherever they are, will pursue 'em. If they hide themselves in caves and

-- pagenum --

dens of the mountains, the fire will search them out and burn them, for it sets on fire the foundation of the mountains and burns to the lowest hell [Deuteronomy 32:22]. If a man be accursed of God in this world, why, his curse will follow him wherever he goes, and whatever means he uses for his own prosperity: God's curse will consume him like fire, and hereafter there will be no avoiding being utterly consumed by the flames of the fire of his wrath. We proceed,

II. What God is a consuming fire to. God is not a consuming fire to all. To God doth not belong the character of an universal destroyer, but on the contrary, he hath the character of the fountain of being, the fountain of life and all good, a Savior and everlasting rock.

First. He'll be a consuming fire to all with whom he is not at peace. If there [be] any opposition between God and any creature, and that opposition continues, God will certainly at last prove a consuming fire to that creature. If there be anything of difference or controversy between God and any other being, if an end ben't put to it, if the difference ben't healed, mark that other being, and you will certainly see that his end is to be utterly consumed. God will most certainly be a consuming fire to him.

If there be any that presume [to] deny God that honor that is his due, or that cast any contempt on his infinite majesty to disobey his commands, or any way to displease him or affront him, they hereby stir up the anger and wrath which will burn like fire, and will surely consume those that are the objects of it at last, unless it be appeased, and they by some means be reconciled to him. Jeremiah 7:20, "Mine anger and my fury shall burn and shall not be quenched"; so Chapter 21:12.

Whosoever he be that has in any wise displeased God, whether great or small, angel or man, God will be a consuming fire to him, unless reconciliation to God by some means be obtained. God will be a consuming fire to all his enemies. Psalms 37:20, "But the wicked shall perish and the enemies of the Lord shall be as the fat of lambs they shall consume; into smoke shall they consume away"; Psalms 97:3, "A fire goeth before him

-- pagenum --

and burns up his enemies round about"; Isaiah 5:24, "As the fire devoureth the stubble, and the flame consumeth the chaff, so their root shall be as rottenness, and their blossom shall go up as dust: because they have cast away the law of the Lord, and despised the word of the Holy One of Israel."

There is a day coming wherein everyone that is found with the guilt of sin upon him shall be consumed in the fire of God's wrath. 'Tis sin that makes them the proper fuel for this fire, as dry wood or the fat of lambs, or as stubble is what easily takes fire and burns. Sin makes the sinner to be as the dry tree fit for the fire and apt to burn. Luke 23:31, "If they do these things in the green tree, what shall be done in the dry?" By the dry tree there is meant the sinner.

Second. God is a consuming fire to all spiritual filth and defilement. Fire is of a purifying nature, and of a searching nature; it will consume the filthiness of anything. So God is of an omniscient, searching and pure eye; he sees all the spiritual filthiness that there is in the world; and his pure eye cannot endure it, but he will consume it. The wrath of God will search out all the filth of the universe. When the angels that fell defiled heaven with their filthiness, the fire of the wrath of God soon purged it out. So the fire of wrath will find out all wicked men4 who are not cleansed from their filthiness, and will consume them. They whose filthiness is not purged away by the spirit of judgment and the spirit of burning, and by being baptized with the Holy Ghost and with fire in this world, shall be burnt in the fire of wrath in the world to come.

How full is this world of filthiness, what abominations are from day to day breaking out, and appearing in the world of mankind! God will be a consuming fire to all this filthiness. There is the great furnace of the wrath of God prepared on purpose to burn this filth. God is now daily casting in the filthy and abominable into it, and has been doing

-- pagenum --

so for above five thousand years.5 Wicked men are in Scripture compared to the scum of a pot that shall be consumed by the fire, Ezekiel 24:6, 9-13:

Wherefore thus saith the Lord God; Woe to the bloody city, to the pot whose scum is therein, and whose scum is not gone out of it! bring it out piece by piece; let no lot fall upon it. Therefore thus saith the Lord God; Woe to the bloody city! I will even make the pile for fire great. Heap on wood, kindle the fire, consume the flesh, and spice it well, and let the bones be burned. Then set it empty upon the coals thereof, that the brass of it may be hot, and may burn, and that the filthiness of it may be molten in it, that the scum of it may be consumed. She hath wearied herself with lies, and her great scum went not forth out of her: her scum shall be in the fire. In thy filthiness is lewdness: because I have purged thee, and thou wast not purged, thou shalt not be purged from thy filthiness any more, till I have caused my fury to rest upon thee.

The city of Sodom was like a pot full of such filth, and it was a pot that God het6 hot in the fire of his wrath, and consumed the scum thereof. So as it was with Jerusalem, as the prophet Ezekiel says. And so this wicked world is like such a pot whose scum is therein, and the day is coming wherein God will heat this also with the fire of his wrath; the day is coming that shall burn as an oven, the wicked world shall be as a fiery oven in the day of God's anger, Psalms 21:9.

There is a day coming when God will come down from heaven as a consuming fire. He will be as a refiner's fire and as fullers' soap, to cleanse away, and burn up, filthiness, Malachi 3:2. This filthy, polluted

-- pagenum --

world shall not always stand in God's sight, but 'tis reserved unto fire against the day of judgment and perdition of ungodly men.

Third. God is a consuming fire to all that is false and unsound in religion. If any cover themselves with a false covering, whereby they make a show, as though they were real Christians, and are not, if they are wolves in sheep's clothing, God will be a consuming fire to their covering, and will discover their nakedness and show their filthiness, and make them to appear what they be.

This consuming fire will try every man and every man's work.7 If any man's work be only wood, hay, and stubble, it shall be burnt up, however fine a show it made; when the day comes, this consuming fire will utterly abolish it; but he whose work is gold, silver, and precious stones, his work shall endure the fire, I Corinthians 3:12-13. God will try men as a furnace tries metals. Pure gold is a thing that will endure the fire, unto which the sincerely godly are often compared; hypocrites shall be like lead and tin and the baser metals, as like dross that the furnace will consume. Jeremiah 6:29-30, "The lead is consumed of the fire; the founder melteth in vain: the wicked are not plucked away"; i. e. the founder puts 'em into the fire, but he takes nothing out, they are not plucked away, but prove base metal, and so are consumed of the fire. As it follows, "Reprobate silver shall men call them, because the Lord hath rejected them." See also Ezekiel 22:18, etc., "Thou hast defiled thy sanctuaries by the multitude of thine iniquities, by the iniquity of thy traffick; therefore will I bring forth a fire from the midst of thee, it shall devour thee, and I will bring thee to ashes upon the earth in the sight of all them that behold thee."

When God comes at the day of judgment, then will he be as a refiner's fire [to] all. Then will he try every man and all false appearances of religion shall be burnt up; that fire will search out all hypocrites in the world: every man shall be tried, what he is made on. "Christ's fan is in his hand, and he'll thoroughly purge his floor, and

-- pagenum --

gather the wheat into his garner, and burn up the chaff in unquenchable fire" [Matthew 3:12, Luke 3:17].

III. Inq. What attributes of God do especially render him thus a consuming fire?

First, his omniscience. Fire [is] of a searching nature. God sees all the wickedness men are guilty [of]. As a fierce fire will penetrate, search, and distinguish, will try gold and silver, and will distinguish what is pure from what is counterfeit, will distinguish and separate between the dross and pure metal, so doth God's all-seeing eye: it searches and tries men, it penetrates into the most inward closet of men's hearts, it distinguishes the hypocrites from the sound convert, it tries the ways and works of the children of men, it distinguishes what is sincere and hypocritical. It discerns all the filthiness that is covered with darkness and that lies hid in men's hearts; it doth as it were pierce men through and through. This makes him a fire that men can't hide from. Again,

Second, his holiness. As he is a being of an omniscient eye, so he is of a pure eye. As fire is of a pure and purifying nature, so such is the holy nature of God, that he will [not], and cannot, endure spiritual filthiness. He is infinitely holy, and hates sin with perfect hatred. 'Tis because of his holiness, that the wicked shall not stand in his sight: if you bring filthiness into God's presence, which [he] so hates and which his holy nature cannot endure, this stirs up his fierce indignation. 'Tis because of the holy nature of God, that sin is so displeasing and provoking to him, so that his displeasure against [it] is as unquenchable fire.

Third, his jealousy, whereby he is jealous for his own honor. And his wrath is excited by those that contemn him or give to any other that respect and honor that is due to him alone. In the fourth [chapter] of Deuteronomy, 24th verse, there God's being a consuming fire is explained by that, that he is a jealous God: "For the Lord thy God is a consuming

-- pagenum --

fire, even a jealous God." God's jealousy is often compared to fire in Scripture; Ezekiel 36:5, "Surely in the fire of my jealousy have I spoken"; or Zephaniah 1:18, "But the whole land shall be devoured by the fire of his jealousy," and 3:8, "All the earth shall be devoured with the fire of my jealousy." So we read of God's jealousy smoking against ungodly men: "The Lord will not spare him, but then the anger of the Lord and his jealousy shall smoke against that man, and all the curses that are written in this book shall come upon him" [Deuteronomy 29:20]; Psalms 79:5, "How long? wilt thou be angry for ever? shall thy jealousy burn like fire?"

Fourth, the justice of God. God is an infinitely just God, and as he is the judge of the world, will surely execute deserved vengeance on all ungodliness; he will in no wise clear the guilty. By reason of this attribute of God, it is impossible but that [he] should be a consuming fire to those who continue ungodly.

Fifth, and lastly, the power of God. As we have observed already, the expression of a consuming fire denotes a fire of a very powerful heat, and that none can resist or stand before. The power of God executes what the holiness, and jealousy, and justice of God calls for, and his power is equal to those other attributes. God is infinite in power, whereby he is able to fulfill the purposes of his wrath, and utterly to destroy and consume all his enemies. His power was such that when the mighty angels, a great number of them, and some of the mightiest of them, combined against him and united their strength, yet he quickly sent them all like lightning from heaven, flaming, into everlasting torments. He is able to make as miserable as he pleases, and none shall be able to stand before him.

And God exercises his great power in the vengeance that he executes on his enemies, in showing his wrath; he will make his power known: hereafter the power of God's anger will be known, but now who

-- pagenum --

knows its power?8 Psalms 2:12, "When his wrath is kindled but a little" – such is God's power that when a fire is kindled in his anger, it will burn to the lowest hell, Deuteronomy 32:22; that is, it will burn the earth to the lowest and most inward parts of it. So God in the execution of his anger will manifest such a mighty power that [it will burn to the lowest hell].9 What an awful description have we of the effects of God's power, when he executes wrath, Nahum, first chapter at the beginning, ["The mountains quake at him, and the hills melt, and the earth is burned at his presence, yea, the world, and all that dwell therein"]; so again the 18th Psalm, ["Then the earth shook and trembled; the foundations also of the hills moved and were shaken, because he was wroth"].10

The truth of these things will appear at the end of the world, when there will be such manifestations of the amazing dreadfulness of his power and wrath against the ungodly, even at the first appearances of Christ in the clouds of heaven, that all the kindreds of the earth shall wail because of him. And we are told that the kings of the earth, and the great men, and the rich men, and chief captains, and every bondman and every free man shall hide themselves in the dens and in the rocks of the mountains, and cry to the mountains to fall on them, and the rocks to cover them, and hide them from the face of him that sits on the throne, and from the wrath of the Lamb, Revelations 6:15-16.

The apprehensions of the wrath of God, when they come to see how great and awful a being he is, will make the stoutest heart to tremble. There is no heart can endure, nor hands be strong, in the day when God riseth up [Ezekiel 22:14]. The hearts of the devils do tremble now at the thoughts of the future wrath at, and after, the day of judgment, though they are such strong and proud beings. But if they tremble when they think of it at such a distance, how much more will they tremble when

-- pagenum --

they come to stand before God at that day? The apostle John gives this account of his vision of the day of judgment, Rev. 20:11, that "before his face the earth and the heaven fled away," that is, he appeared clothed with such awful and tremendous majesty, and power, and justice, that heaven and earth were as it were frightened, and could not endure the sight, but fled away with fear. But if the foregoing appearances of God's terrible anger shall be so dreadful, how terrible will the execution of it be: what a consuming fire will he be to his enemies, after that sentence has been pronounced of, "Depart, ye cursed, into everlasting fire, prepared for the devil and his angels" [Matthew 25:41].11

Application

I. Use of Awakening to ungodly men. That that God is a consuming fire may be a very terrible consideration to you, considering the circumstances you stand in with respect to him: that you are not at peace, ben't upon good terms with such a God; that you live in sin against him, daily provoking him to wrath. It may well terrify a wicked man to think that he lives, and moves, and has his being12 in a God that is a consuming fire. If God be a consuming fire, what will become of those men that have no care how they walk before, and whether they please or displease him, whether they honor or affront him, don't much care what they do in his sight, whether he likes or dislikes it; nor is it a thing they much trouble themselves about, whether God loves 'em or hates 'em? What is like to become of such men, if God verifies what he has said of himself in his word, where he has once and again told us that he is a consuming fire?

Consider what we observed in the doctrinal part, that you can't flee or hide yourself from this fire: it will search you out and consume

-- pagenum --

you, though you hide yourself in the wilderness, or ends of the earth, or on the tops, or under the bottoms of mountains; and you are not able to resist it: you will [be] but as stubble before the flames. How great is the power of fire when it gets head! There is nothing can resist it or stand before it: it is terrible to see the raging of devouring flames when a city or only an house is on fire. But if a material fire, that we use in our kitchens, be so powerful, so awful to behold, when it gets head and makes such dreadful devastation, how powerful then, may we conclude, this consuming fire is, which we have heard of, which is the infinite and almighty God! Who can stand before this fire? How awful would it be to see, and how much more awful to feel, omnipotence in flames!

If God be a consuming fire, this teaches us how terrible the misery of those is, that are consumed and destroyed by the wrath of God. It is very terrible to burn alive in our culinary fire; the thought of it gives a very dreadful idea of misery. But how exceedingly more miserable must they be, that are consumed in the devouring flames of that fire that we have heard of! If God be a fire, doubtless he is a very dreadful [fire]. We may argue justly that if God be love, that love is a very great and extraordinary love, infinitely surpassing all other love. If God be light, he is doubtless a very bright and glorious light indeed. If God be a sun, he infinitely surpasses the material sun in brightness. If he be food, he is very sweet, dainty, and satisfying food indeed. So doubtless, if God be a fire, he is a very dreadful fire indeed.13 Because whatsoever God is, he is like a God, like an infinite being. God is a being infinitely surpassing all things in every property

-- pagenum --

of his nature, and therefore infinitely surpassing all things in whatever he is. If he be a light, he is infinitely surpassing all lights in brightness; and if he be a consuming fire, he must be a fire infinitely surpassing all fires in heat and fierceness. And therefore it must be infinitely dreadful to be consumed and tormented by this; it must be infinitely dreadful to have the flames of this fire take hold upon one.

Why therefore are you not afraid to expose yourself to those flames? Men are wont to be very careful how they are catched by the flames of our common material fire. If there be an house on fire, all the inhabitants will make haste to get out of it, lest14 they should be burnt; yea, men will be careful that they don't so much as burn their fingers in our ordinary fire. But yet you are not afraid every day to expose yourselves exceedingly to the flames of this consuming fire: not only to being slightly touched and scorched by them, but the being all over swallowed up of them in the midst of the furnace, in the very hottest of it, and there lying and being tormented to all eternity. Men act as though they contrived to get into this fire, they are rushing upon it, as though they strove who should get into it first, and who should get into it deepest. It is an awful consideration that by far the greater part of mankind, and by far the greater part of those that lived under the gospel, have it their portion to dwell with those everlasting burnings; and that 'tis because men will against all warnings that can be given them - by what they do willfully, and have no need to do - plunge themselves, thrust themselves into it. Are there not some here present, that do daily expose themselves to it by the ways that they willfully and allowedly walk, and don't seriously intend to do any other still, notwithstanding this warning? And if it be so, don't this verify what I say, that they act as if they were set upon it, to be burnt in this fire?

-- pagenum --

God's being a consuming fire does very strongly argue, and clearly show, the inexpressible extremity of the torments of the damned. Does it not give a dreadful idea of misery, to conceive of such a thing? If it could be of a man's remaining alive in such fires as men can make with earthly fuel, as for instance in the furnaces that are used for the refining of metals, to lie in the midst of the glowing heat of such a furnace for a considerable time, full of quick sense: I say, what a dreadful idea does this give one of misery! And then I ask again, don't it seem reasonable that it should be inconceivably more dreadful than that, to be swallowed up alive by the consuming fire of the omnipotence of God in flames? or to have God himself, with his almighty power, and infinite holiness, and justice, and jealousy to be forever a consuming fire to you? Is it not reasonable to think that there is a great difference between the terribleness of God, when showing himself as a consuming fire, and any fire that men can make of earthly fuel? If the case be so, is not your case very sad, who are every day exposed to this, are in such a state that you are more likely to suffer this misery than not, in that by far the greater part of those that are in your case do suffer it in all ages of the world?

Here 'tis very probable that some will evade the force of what has been said, by saying within themselves that they doubt of the truth of it. And there are two things that I shall mention that it is probably the minds of some may object.

First. It seems to them so strange a thing that the greatest part of all mankind should eternally suffer such inconceivable, inexpressible torment, that they can't believe it is so, or that the misery is so great. I answer: it is unreasonable to think any other, if you believe the being of a God.

If there be a God, doubtless he is the lawgiver of his creatures; and if so, there must be some punishment for the breach of his laws. And it's unreasonable to think any other than that he should reveal to men, what punishment they must expect for the breach of his laws. How

-- pagenum --

unreasonable is it to suppose that God would conceal from his subjects, and never tell them, how great a punishment they must expect for their disobedience! 'Tis unreasonable to suppose any such thing, as that God would be a lawgiver to us, and yet never tell us what are the sanctions of his laws; but if God has told us, then I would ask where, if not in the Scripture? Where else in the world has God told men how great a punishment they must expect for the breach of his laws, if not in the Bible? If this ben't the revelation, where is it? And if it be, then surely 'tis reasonable to believe it, however dreadful it seems to be.

But possibly some may say it may be, we don't understand the Scriptures. Perhaps the Scriptures don't mean such a dreadful degree of misery as ministers tell of? But it is impossible we should be mistaken: the dreadfulness of the misery of the damned is spoken of in Scripture so often, and set forth so many ways, and so particularly described; and we are told that it is God's design in their punishment to show his wrath, and make his power known, and that none knows the power of his anger, and that 'tis according to his fear or majesty, and the like, that we can't be mistaken.

And then we have no reason to scruple our understanding God's meaning, because the punishment, as we understand it, is so dreadful. For as dreadful as it is, it appears to reason to be no way unjust, nor no way contrary to the mercy of God. The dreadfulness of hell punishment is no argument against the truth of it, for 'tis not the design of God to show mercy to them; they are vessels of wrath: 'tis no way God designs in any wise to glorify the attribute of his mercy on such as have the punishment of his law executed on them. And therefore let the punishment be never so extreme, if the pains and torments should surpass the power of human arithmetic to measure, or give an account of, yet 'tis not incredible, if it ben't more than just. Again,

Second. Wicked men may be ready to say within themselves that it don't seem real to them that God is such a consuming fire to wicked men, as he is represented, because they have sinned against him all their

-- pagenum --

lives, and they never perceived that he hurt them at all. It seems strange that God should have such a terrible wrath against wickedness, and yet let men alone so long. All that I shall answer to this, is:

That this is not a time nor place for it. The present state never was designed by God for a state of retribution; it was always intended as a state of probation: and therefore you can't reasonably argue, that [God] won't prove as much of a consuming fire to you, as you have heard, when you are removed out of the world, though he lets you alone now. You read in the Scripture that God is a God of wonderful patience and long suffering; now you find that this world is a place for that. And you also read that he is a consuming fire, and hereafter you may find that hell is a place for the manifestation of that.

II. [Second] Use is of Exh. in several branches.

First. Is God a consuming fire? Let us not dare to oppose God, let us not set ourselves as enemies to him. Whoever we oppose, let us not oppose God, but evermore comply with him, and conform to him, and yield to his will, whatever it be. Let us not oppose God in any of his designs or ends, but evermore fall in with him, and put to our hand to endeavor to promote the same. God declares it to be his design to glorify himself. Let us not, if we have any regard to ourselves, by any means to be doing what tends to the contrary; but let us seek the promotion of the same thing, as much as in us lies. 'Tis God's declared design to advance the kingdom and honor of his Son. Let us [not] dare to oppose him, but let us also endeavor to promote his Son's kingdom too.

Whomsoever God favors or whomsoever he opposes, if we have any regard to ourselves, let us be of the same side. God opposes Satan and holds him for his enemy. Let us therefore take heed that we don't join with Satan against God. Whoever we join with, don't let us join with the enemies of God, for God is a consuming fire. God greatly favors the godly in all that he does. Let us fall in with him in this also: let us always be for them, and appear in their cause; for he that touches them, touches the apple of God's eye.

-- pagenum --

Let us take heed that we don't oppose God's authority, or submit to any other lords that are opposite to him. When God commands, let us not show an opposite or backward spirit to obey. Let us take heed that we don't show a careless spirit about a conformity to God's will: this will doubtless be displeasing and provoking to God. Let us take heed that we don't in anything cast a slight upon God or upon his authority, as promises or threatenings; for let us still remember that God is a consuming fire. If we han't a mind in a dreadful manner to be consumed, let us not dare to quarrel with God, to find fault with him about any of his dispensations towards us or any other creatures. Let us not suffer our hearts proudly to rise up against God. But let us with the greatest care and exactness endeavor in everything to please God, to fall in with him, to yield to him, and to comply with him, and to do what shall be acceptable to him.

What madness it is to go to contend with devouring fire! It is impossible that we should ever be successful in it. What signifies it for briars and thorns to set themselves with their sharp points in battle array against a consuming fire that will go through them and burn them together! If a company of armed men, when a city is on fire, should go forth to withstand and fight against the flames with their swords and weapons of war, what would they get by it, but only to be burnt up alive? But this folly would not be so great as theirs who fight against God; I Corinthians 10:22, "Do we provoke the Lord to jealousy? are we stronger than he?"

Second. Let us be exhorted to cleanse ourselves from filthiness. As we observed in opening the doctrine, God is a consuming fire to all spiritual filth, as a fierce fire will purge away and consume the filth of anything that is thrown into it. Our souls are naturally all over filthy. If we would not therefore be consumed in the fire of God's holy indignation, let us earnestly seek that we may be cleansed from our filthiness; that our souls may be baptized with the Holy Ghost, and with fire, and that we may be cleansed by the spirit of judgment, and the

-- pagenum --

spirit of burning. Let us labor to see our own filthiness; that is the first step to be taken in order to our being cleansed from it. Let us seek that God would give us a sight of that fountain of corruption and loathsome defilement that our souls are a sink of. And let us forsake all our filthiness of life. If we would not have God to be a consuming fire to us, we must mortify our impure lusts, we must cleanse ourselves from sensuality.15

Third. Is God a consuming {fire}? Then let us fly to the Lord Jesus Christ for defense and shelter. As we are naturally, we are all universally exposed to this devouring flame; for we all come into the world with the guilt of sin, and are not at peace with God; are born enemies to God, and with our hearts full of filthiness. And there is no safety for us as we are in, and by, ourselves. God, who is a consuming fire, is a God that we can't flee from, nor hide from: he is everywhere, and in him we live, and move, and have our being;16 the flames of this fire do fiercely rage against us every day: what therefore shall we do that we may be sheltered from it? There is one way, and but one, that we may be sheltered from [it]; there is one shelter that we may flee to, and be safe; there is a most secure and quiet and sufficient refuge provided for us: and that is in Jesus Christ. If we fly thither, we certainly may be safe; it will perfectly secure us, so that there will be no danger. We may see the flames rage and devour others, and hear the roaring of them, but they will not, they cannot, come nigh us. All that are found out of Christ, will be consumed. Every soul, let 'em use what17 methods they will to defend themselves, or let 'em hide themselves where they will, the flames will certainly find 'em out, and they will be swallowed up in the deluge of fire.

-- pagenum --

But if we fly to Christ for refuge, and are in him, there we may be unhurt: this refuge won't only secure us from being consumed by the flames, but they won't reach us at all; they shall not touch, nor affect us, in the least: there we may be as under a cool shadow, unmolested, and in the full enjoyment of our quietness and comfort. The blood of Christ has, as it were, quenched the flames for those that are in him; 'tis indeed fire unquenchable by anything else but Christ's blood. If we therefore are in Christ, the flames of this fire will have nothing to do to hurt us. God is not a consuming [fire] to them that are in Christ.

God is such a consuming fire that no created thing can be any defense from the flames thereof. But Christ is a divine person, a person partaking of the increated18 essence of God, and therefore he is a strong barrier against those flames. The walls of the Canaanites were no defense to 'em against the children of Israel, because God, a consuming fire, went before them, as in the ninth [chapter] of Deuteronomy, at the beginning;19 but the walls of this city of refuge are a good defense even from this. The consuming fire of God's wrath quite spends itself upon Christ, and never reaches those that are in him; the flames spent themselves upon him when he was crucified. They soon exerted all their fury upon him, and spent themselves, and that because he was a divine and infinite person. The flames of the wrath of an infinite God never can spend themselves upon any person that is created and finite: when they exert themselves upon any angel or angels, as they did, and do, upon some of them, they will burn against [them] to all eternity, and their fury will never be spent. But when Christ endured them, they spent all their heat upon him, and are gone out, so that there is nothing of them left to consume those that are in him.

If you are [within] this shelter, the flames will wholly spend themselves upon the walls of it; they won't at all reach those that are within. As in the time of the deluge, those were safe from the waters, that were within Noah's ark; so those that are in Christ, are safe from

-- pagenum --

the deluge of the fire of God's wrath. In him you may be safe at the day of judgment, when heaven and earth shall be all in a flame, and the elements shall melt with fervent heat, and the earth with all the works that are therein shall be burnt up, and all devils and wicked men shall be consumed. What the wicked can't find in the caves and dens of the mountains, the godly will find in Christ.

Fourth. If God {is a consuming fire}, then let us never presume to appear to God in our own names, but only in the name of Christ. We surely should take heed in what manner we approach to such a God, lest20 he break forth upon us, and we be consumed. It will be dreadful presumption in us to come before such a God, bringing nothing else with us but our own righteousness, having nothing else to cover our nakedness, and hide our filth: how shall we be exposed, if we come in such a manner to the fierceness of the wrath of an infinitely holy and sin hating God! The Apostle directs us, Col. 3:17, "And whatsoever ye do in word or deed, do all in the name of the Lord Jesus Christ."

Fifth. If God is a consuming fire, let us see to it that our religion be real and sincere, such as will bear the fire. Let us not content ourselves with a mere profession, or an outward show, and in doing the external part, or in transient affections that are like the morning cloud and as the early dew [Hosea 6:4, 13:3]: all such things as these one day will be burnt up by this consuming fire. Don't let us content ourselves21 with pretences to such and such discoveries, and the experience of a good work in the right steps,22 without a real change of nature, and a change of life: see to it that your conversion be sound. The day is coming that will burn up the religion of thousands of thousands of those that have made a fair show here in this life, and have passed for godly men, and had a high thought of themselves here in this world. What looked like gold, and silver, and precious stones in their own eyes, and it may be in the eyes of others, will appear to be

-- pagenum --

nothing but gilded wood, and hay, and stubble, when the fire comes to try it; it will burn up at once, and all vanish into smoke; there will be nothing of it left.

You had need, all of you, to see that you are sound; for this fire that you have heard of, will search you, and try you effectually, more thoroughly than ever anything was tried in a furnace; the fire, when that comes, will soon show what you be. Consider that there is a great deal of dross passes for gold nowadays; but when that day comes, it will presently decide what it is; there is a great deal that glistens now, that won't stand the fire.

And doubtless there are hypocrites here amongst us, as well as in other places. God knows who they are, and the fire will show who they are. Without doubt there are some of you that tell of a work of conversion, and whose experiences and religion will all vanish away in a cloud of smoke at once, as soon as ever Christ comes as a refiner's fire;23 and it will then appear that you never were truly converted.

Seeing therefore that we are to be tried as it were by a refiner's fire, it behooves us to be very thorough in trying ourselves now, and in pressing after something further in religion than ever yet we have obtained, that we may make sure; after the example of the apostle Paul, who doubtless had as little occasion for it upon any such account as most Christians in the world: he counted not himself to have apprehended, but this one thing he did, forgetting those things that were behind, he pressed forward towards those things that were before, that he might apprehend, Philippians 3:12-13.

I shall conclude by offering something to Consideration which may be an enforcement of each of the foregoing exhortations; which is this, viz.

What God will be to you if you are godly.

First. Instead of a consuming fire to burn you, he'll be a light to enlighten you. If a friend, if pure, if in Christ, if sincere, he who

-- pagenum --

is a fire to burn others, will not hurt you at all; he'll only be a bright and glorious light to you, whose beams won't burn you, nor be any way painful to you: they will be exceeding refreshing to you, they will be pleasant to your eyes, and sweet and rejoicing to your soul. He'll be such a sweet light to you, that it will be a joyful and glorious entertainment to you to behold it; you never will be weary of it. He'll be ten thousand times as glorious a light to you as the sun and his beams, immensely more pleasant and refreshing. That there will be such a difference, the prophet Malachi teaches us, in the fourth chapter, first and second verse. In the first verse, he tells us what God will be to the wicked: "For, behold, the day cometh, that shall burn as an oven; and all the proud, yea, and all that do wickedly, shall be stubble: and the day that cometh shall burn them up, saith the Lord of hosts, that it shall leave them neither root nor branch." In the second verse, he tells us what he will be to the godly: "But unto you that fear my name shall the Sun of righteousness arise with healing in his wings." As a fire to devour one, but as the sun to enlighten and refresh the other; his beams won't hurt them, but heal them. It is said, "with healing in his wings," by which is meant his beams; the bright rays going out from the body of the sun, it seems, the Hebrews in their language used to call the wings of the sun. [So the] difference in the influence of the pillar of cloud and fire towards Israel and Egyptians [Exodus 14:20].24

Second. God will be as a cool shadow and as a river of water to refresh you. Isaiah 32:2, "And a man shall be as an hiding place from the wind, and a covert from the tempest; as rivers of water in a dry place, as the shadow of a great rock in a weary land." The spouse tells us, Christ was to her as the apple tree among the trees of the wood, and that she sat down under his shadow with great delight, Canticles 2:3; Isaiah 25:4, "thou hast been a shadow from the heat."25 This which is a consuming fire to the ungodly, in [the] new Jerusalem is a pure river of

-- pagenum --

water of life, clear as crystal, and the tree of life that gives its sweet refreshing shadow on its banks [Revelations 22:1-2].
